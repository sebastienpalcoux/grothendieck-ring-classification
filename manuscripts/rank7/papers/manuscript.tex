\begin{abstract}
We apply general categorification obstructions to the pinned, complete
fusion-ring census of rank seven and multiplicity at most four. Every
asserted exclusion has an exact certificate or a cited theorem with
exactly checked tensor hypotheses; every asserted
realization has an explicit unitary fusion-category model and an exact
full-tensor identification. We include a rank-seven extension of the
formal-codegree test to noncommutative rings, a global-dimension-fifteen
obstruction, and complete oriented signed-sphericalization certificates
for 246 rings through multiplicity four. The remaining tensors are recorded as
\emph{undecided}. Three additional published AnyonWiki associators have
independent exact pentagon, invertibility, unit and rigidity checks.
Thus multiplicity one is complete, with thirteen realized rings;
the first undecided multiplicity is two.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and statements}
Let $R$ have distinguished basis $(b_0=1,b_1,\ldots,b_6)$ and multiplication
$b_i b_j=\sum_k N_{ij}^k b_k$. A \emph{Grothendieck ring} here means the
based ring of a complex fusion category; no pivotal, spherical, braided,
or unitary hypothesis is placed on a hypothetical categorification.
Its multiplicity is $\max_{i,j,k}N_{ij}^k$. The upstream
\emph{Fusion Ring Census} \cite{Census} provides the complete rank-seven
fusion-ring list through multiplicity four. We pin its source bytes,
source commit, row ordering, and their digest in the companion package.
This is a categorification study of that census; the list of undecided
tensors is not counted as a list of Grothendieck rings.
Multiplicities at least five lie outside the pinned source and the
claims of this manuscript.

\section{Necessary conditions and their order}
The checks in Table~\ref{tab:stages} run from top to bottom on surviving
tensors, and a tensor is charged to its first successful condition.
Near-group and small-dimension checks read a few coefficients; the
zero-spectrum and closed-subring passes are finite tensor scans;
exact Casimir factoring and subcategory-dimension arithmetic follow.
The complete oriented search ends this initial sequence; the further
theoretical exclusions are appended as a dated update. This order records precisely when
a criterion first contributes rather than hiding it in an unordered
union of tests. Multiplicity two is still undecided, so these stages
do \emph{not} establish a multiplicity threshold beyond which no new
criteria would be needed.
The oriented criterion first contributes at multiplicity one, so it
cannot be deferred to a higher multiplicity without losing a proof.

If $G$ is the group of invertible basis elements and the only other
object $X$ satisfies $X^2=\sum_{g\in G}g+\ell X$, then for $|G|=6$:
at $\ell=0$ the Tambara--Yamagami construction forces $G$ abelian
\cite{TY}; at $0<\ell<5$ in the commutative case,
Schopieray's near-group obstruction rules out categorification
\cite[Proposition~3.2]{Schopieray}. These statements apply to a
categorification without a pivotal assumption. The certificate records
the six group elements, the remaining object and $\ell$, and rechecks
every fusion-rule hypothesis.

The global-dimension-fifteen branch has six dimension-one simples and
one dimension-three simple: direct substitution into every product
establishes that $(1,\ldots,1,3)$ is its strictly positive dimension
character. Hence its Frobenius--Perron dimension is $6+3^2=15$.
The $pq$ classification \cite[Theorems~3.1 and~5.1]{EGO} makes a
complex fusion category of dimension $3\cdot5$ pointed in this case,
contradicting the dimension-three simple.

The zero-spectrum obstruction is the exact necessary criterion of
Liu--Palcoux--Ren \cite{LPR}; each certificate stores its nine basis
indices and the verifier rechecks all required zero/nonzero fusion
coefficients. A proper fusion-closed subset of basis elements would
generate a fusion subcategory. If its induced rank-four or rank-five
ring is excluded by the complete lower-rank classifications
\cite{Rank4,Rank5}, the ambient ring is excluded. The imported
model registries contain all fifteen rank-four and all seventeen
rank-five realized based-ring types, checked up to multiplicities
$128$ and $20$, respectively; all encountered subrings fall within
these ranges. The complete rank-six multiplicity-two classification
\cite{Rank6} supplies context and methodology, without being used as
an unstated rank-seven classification theorem.

\begin{proposition}[The formal-codegree test at rank seven]
\label{prop:dnumber}
Suppose that $R$ is a rank-seven fusion ring, possibly noncommutative.
Let $C=\sum_{i=0}^6 N_iN_i^{\mathsf T}$ be its regular Casimir matrix.
If an irreducible factor of the characteristic polynomial of $C$ is
not the minimal polynomial of a $d$-number, then $R$ cannot be the
Grothendieck ring of a complex fusion category.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the commutative case, the eigenvalues of $C$ are the formal
codegrees of one-dimensional characters and must be $d$-numbers by
Ostrik \cite{OstrikCodegrees}. In the noncommutative case,
semisimplicity, $\dim_\C(R\otimes\C)=7$, and the positive
one-dimensional character imply the unique possible decomposition
$R\otimes\C\simeq\C^3\oplus M_2(\C)$.
There is only one two-dimensional irreducible module; it is fixed by
every field automorphism, so its formal codegree is a rational
algebraic integer and hence an integer. The Casimir acts on this
matrix block by twice that integer. Every remaining nonrational
Casimir eigenvalue comes from a one-dimensional character and is a
formal codegree, so Ostrik's condition applies. The implementation
factors the exact integral characteristic polynomial and tests the
coefficient-divisibility characterization of $d$-numbers. The replay
checker recomputes and factors that polynomial.
\end{proof}

If $S$ is a proper commutative fusion subring of a hypothetical
categorification, the quotient $\FPdim(R)/\FPdim(S)$ must be an
algebraic integer \cite{ENO}. The subcategory-divisibility stage
computes this quotient in the exact Frobenius--Perron number field,
records its characteristic polynomial, and certifies a nonintegral
coefficient. Its certificate is rederived from the complete tensor.

\section{Complete oriented tests at rank seven}
The oriented-dimension-order row of Table~\ref{tab:stages} uses the general, \emph{oriented}
signed-sphericalization construction established in \cite{Rank6}.
For an arbitrary hypothetical category $\mathcal C$, its canonical
sphericalization has two lifts of each simple object, interchanged
by a sign object $\varepsilon$. Taking the quotient by
$(\varepsilon+1)$ produces integer coefficients $T_{ij}^k$ with
$|T_{ij}^k|\le N_{ij}^k$ and
$T_{ij}^k\equiv N_{ij}^k\pmod 2$.
Unit, duality, cyclic Frobenius reciprocity, and ordered associativity
further constrain the quotient. The self-dual positive-indicator
normalization imposes the established square-channel congruences
modulo four. These conditions are necessary and cover every
categorification, whether pivotal or not; a signed solution is not
itself a categorification.

The rank-six proof of the general construction and the
dimension-order obstruction \cite{Rank6} are rank-independent. Here
their enumeration engine is extended to seven basis elements. It
enumerates the independent cyclic/dual-reflection coefficient orbits,
prunes only exact associativity equations with checked integer bounds,
and constructs \emph{every} signed quotient. For each quotient the
checker verifies either its adjoint parity, its gauge equivalence
with the original ring, or the failure of every admissible spherical
character by codegree and dimension-order tests. In particular the
dimension-order test requires the central-induction dimension
$\delta/f_\chi$ to lie in the $\Z$-span of the signed simple
dimensions, as required by \cite{OstrikPivotal,Rank6}.

\begin{proposition}[Verified exhaustive branches]
The oriented-dimension-order criterion excludes four, fifty-one,
sixty-one, and one hundred thirty previously surviving rank-seven
rings of exact multiplicities one through four, respectively.
For $R7\_1\_5$, $R7\_1\_23$, $R7\_1\_33$, and $R7\_1\_38$,
the complete signed enumerations have respectively $2,4,4,8$
associative quotient tables. Every table in each of these 246
certified exhaustive searches is eliminated by necessary conditions
for arbitrary complex fusion categories.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The machine-readable proof tree for each of the 246 rings gives its full
dimension-character factorization, every obstruction, the ordered
signed-orbit domain, and one checked branch for every enumerated
table. The independent verifier rebuilds the domains from the source
tensor, repeats the bounded exhaustive enumeration, checks the
\emph{entire} list of surviving tables for equality to the recorded
list, and recomputes each branch witness. The search used to discover
these finite trees is logically separate from their exhaustive replay;
timed-out or unfinished searches generate no negative decision.
The enumeration returns the four displayed multiplicity-one branch
counts and no uncovered branch for any of the 246 certificates.
The rank-independent necessity of the search is the
sphericalization theorem cited above.
\end{proof}

\section{Further theoretical exclusions}
The local triangular-prism proof of Liu--Palcoux--Ren--Vercleyen
\cite[Corollary~6.2, Theorem~6.4 and Theorem~7.2]{TPE}
excludes $R7\_2\_185$ and $R7\_3\_635$ over $\C$.
The first is exactly their $\mathcal F_{210}$.
The second is a consequence of the same local proof, as follows.
In the source basis of dimensions $(1,5,5,5,6,7,7)$, their tensors agree
outside the cube on the two dimension-seven objects. Both have this
strictly positive integral dimension character, so any categorification
is pseudo-unitary and has the canonical spherical structure.
The cited proof uses the squares of two dimension-five objects $k,l$,
selected columns of dimensions one and five, their dualities and
categorical dimensions. The zero constraints are read from the squares
of those selected objects. Thus none of the differing coefficients on
the dimension-seven cube enters either local subsystem or its linking
equation. Both rings give precisely the inconsistent polynomial system
in the proof of \cite[Theorem~7.2]{TPE}. The supplementary checker
verifies all these tensor equalities and the positive dimension
character; it does not rerun the published Gr\"obner-basis calculation.

For $R7\_3\_900$ and $R7\_3\_991$, a different short argument gives a
general exclusion. Their dimension type is $(1,1,1,1,2,4,4)$ and their
Frobenius--Perron dimension is $40=2^3\cdot5$. Any categorification would
be solvable \cite[Theorem~1.6]{ENOSolvable}, hence weakly group-theoretical
and unitarizable \cite[Theorem~5.20]{GHR}.
For a unitary categorification, the positive $p$th-root limit of
\cite[Corollary~3.4]{Lim} requires, for every subset $S$ of basis labels
and $N_{ij}^l>0$,
\[
 \frac{\sum_{x\in S}N_{lk}^x d_x}{d_l}
 \leq \frac{\sum_{m\in T}N_{jk}^m d_m}{d_j},
 \qquad
 T=\{m:N_{jk}^m>0,\ N_{im}^x>0\text{ for some }x\in S\}.
\]
Taking $S$ to be the objects of dimension at most two, the exact
witnesses in the supplement give $2\leq1$ for each ring. The first
witness is $(i,j,k,l)=(1,1,2,1)$; the second is $(3,4,4,3)$, using
zero-based census labels. This unitary obstruction is a general one
here because the dimension theorem forces unitarizability.

\begin{proposition}[A half-partition criterion]
Let $R$ be an integral commutative fusion ring of dimension $4pq$, for
distinct odd primes $p,q$. Write $a_E=4pq/f_E$ for its formal codegrees.
Suppose an odd-prime-power $a_E>1$ occurs, and every submultiset of the
$a_E$ excluding the unit and summing to $2pq$ leaves an odd-prime-power
entry in its complement. Then any categorification is weakly
group-theoretical and has the Frobenius property.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrality gives the canonical positive spherical structure.
By \cite[Theorem~2.13]{OstrikRank3}, the induced unit $I(1)$ in the center
has distinct multiplicity-one summands of dimensions $a_E$.
An odd-prime-power summand implies a nontrivial symmetric subcategory
of the center \cite[Corollary~7.2]{ENOSolvable}. If this provides no
nontrivial Tannakian subcategory, it must be $\mathrm{sVec}$.
For its fermion $f$, \cite[Proposition~2.8]{OstrikRank3} gives
$s[I(1)]=4pq[I(1)]$. The fermion has twist $-1$, so it is absent from
$I(1)$, whose twist is trivial. Its row therefore gives
$\sum_E\epsilon_E a_E=0$, with monodromy signs $\epsilon_E=\pm1$
and $\epsilon_{\mathbf1}=1$. The negative signs form a half-partition.
By hypothesis an odd-prime-power summand centralizes $f$.
Its slightly degenerate centralizer consequently contains a nontrivial
Tannakian subcategory by \cite[Proposition~7.4]{ENOSolvable}.

Write this subcategory as $\mathrm{Rep}(G)$. Its de-equivariantization
has neutral dimension $(4pq/|G|)^2$, hence $|G|$ divides $4pq$.
If $p$ or $q$ divides $|G|$, the neutral category has at most two prime
divisors and is solvable. Extension and equivariantization reconstruct
the center; Morita invariance and subcategory closure then make the
original category weakly group-theoretical
\cite[Theorem~1.6 and Proposition~4.1]{ENOSolvable}.
Otherwise $|G|$ is two or four and supplies $\mathrm{Rep}(C_2)$.
The original category is a $C_2$-extension or $C_2$-equivariantization
of a category of dimension $2pq$
\cite[Propositions~2.9--2.10]{ENOSolvable}.
That category is integral: in the equivariantization case its simple
dimensions are rational quotients of integral dimensions by orbit
sizes and projective representation degrees, hence integers.
The integral squarefree-dimension theorem
\cite[Theorem~9.2]{ENOSolvable} makes it group-theoretical.
The Frobenius property follows from \cite[Theorem~1.5]{ENOSolvable}.
\end{proof}

For $R7\_3\_905$, the exact positive dimension vector is
$(1,6,6,1,1,1,8)$, and the formal codegrees are
$(140,20,20,7,4,4,4)$. Thus the induced-unit dimensions are
$(1,7,7,20,35,35,35)$. The only half-partition excluding the unit
consists of two dimension-$35$ summands, leaving both dimension-seven
summands. The proposition applies, but $6$ does not divide $140$.
This excludes every complex categorification. The checker verifies the
entire formal-codegree polynomial and all $64$ nonunit subsets exactly.

Finally, $R7\_4\_2727$ has dimension type $(1,1,1,3,6,6,6)$,
dimension $120$, and formal codegrees $(120,60,60,24,4,3,3)$.
The induced unit has simple summands of dimension two, so the center
contains a nontrivial symmetric subcategory. The invertible group has
odd order three; \cite[Lemma~2.5]{Dai120} therefore gives a nontrivial
Tannakian subcategory, and \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Dai120} makes any
categorification weakly group-theoretical and hence unitarizable.
The exact support inequality above gives $4\leq2$ for
$(i,j,k,l)=(3,4,3,3)$ and $S=\{6\}$, a contradiction.

\subsection{A double-equivariantization obstruction in dimension 52}
The ring $R7\_2\_75$ has dimension type $(1^4,4^3)$, invertible group
$V_4$, and trivial universal grading. Every invertible fixes every
dimension-four simple. These are exact tensor properties checked by the
supplement. Suppose $\mathcal C$ categorifies it. Dimension $52=4\cdot13$
makes $\mathcal C$ solvable \cite[Theorem~1.6]{ENOSolvable}. The last
step of its prime cyclic extension/equivariantization construction
\cite[Proposition~4.4]{ENOSolvable} cannot be an extension, since
$\mathcal C_{\rm ad}=\mathcal C$. Its character group must embed into
$V_4$, so the last step is
$\mathcal C=\mathcal D^{C_2}$, with $\FPdim(\mathcal D)=26$.
Each possible nontrivial character fixes the three dimension-four
simples and pairs the four invertibles. The equivariant-simple
orbit/stabilizer formula \cite[Proposition~4.26]{DGNO} therefore forces
$\mathcal D$ to have type $(1^2,2^6)$ and rank eight. A fixed simple of
the action has two lifts exchanged by the character; a free orbit has
one lift of twice the dimension. There are no higher-dimensional
projective representations of $C_2$.

The category $\mathcal D$ also has trivial universal grading. Equal
dimensions of faithful grading components \cite[Proposition~8.20]{ENO}
make the grading-group order divide $26$. Orders $13$ and $26$ exceed
the rank. Order two would give a neutral category of prime dimension
$13$, necessarily pointed and of rank $13$
\cite[Corollary~8.30]{ENO}, also impossible. Applying the same solvable
last-step argument gives
\[
 \mathcal D=\mathcal P^{C_2},\qquad
 \mathcal P=\operatorname{Vec}_{C_{13}}^\omega.
\]
The inner $C_2$ must act nontrivially on $C_{13}$, hence by inversion;
a trivial object action would give $26$ invertible simples in $\mathcal D$.

The canonical central $\operatorname{Rep}(C_2)$ in $\mathcal D$ is
unique over its underlying two invertibles. Indeed, the kernel of
$\operatorname{Inv}(Z(\mathcal D))\to\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal D)$
is the group of tensor automorphisms of $\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal D}$.
Such an automorphism is trivial on every summand of $Y\otimes Y^*$,
hence on $\mathcal D_{\rm ad}=\mathcal D$. Two lifts of the same
invertible differ by this kernel. The outer $C_2$ action thus preserves
the central $\operatorname{Rep}(C_2)$. Equivariantizing it gives a
central Tannakian $\operatorname{Rep}(G)$ with fully faithful underlying
embedding into $\mathcal C$, where
$1\to C_2\to G\to C_2\to1$.
Its symmetric fibre functor is the composite of the two forgetful
functors. Therefore $G$ is $C_4$ or $V_4$; its four characters exhaust
the invertibles of $\mathcal C$, forcing $G=V_4$.
De-equivariantization in stages now gives
$\mathcal C=(\operatorname{Vec}_{C_{13}}^\omega)^{V_4}$, with the
normal $C_2$ acting by inversion
\cite[Section~2.6 and Proposition~2.10]{ENOSolvable}
\cite[Theorem~4.18(i) and Proposition~4.19]{DGNO}.
These are central Tannakian constructions; no braiding of $\mathcal C$
is assumed.

The object action $V_4\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_{13})=C_{12}$ has image
of exponent at most two and contains inversion. Every nonidentity
object consequently has orbit size two and stabilizer $C_2$.
Each of the six nonidentity orbits contributes two dimension-two
simples, while the unit contributes the four characters of $V_4$.
This forces type $(1^4,2^{12})$, contradicting $(1^4,4^3)$.
Thus $R7\_2\_75$ has no complex fusion categorification, without any
pivotal or unitary assumption. The proof does not require $\omega$
to be trivial.

\subsection{An order-sixteen orbit obstruction in dimension 96}
The same method excludes $R7\_2\_252$ and $R7\_2\_315$.
Both have dimension type $(1,1,2,3,3,6,6)$, invertible group $C_2$,
and trivial universal grading. Their nontrivial invertible $h$ fixes
the dimension-two and dimension-six simples and exchanges the two
dimension-three simples. Solvability and the last-step argument force
$\mathcal C=\mathcal D^{C_2}$. The orbit/stabilizer formula then gives
$\mathcal D$ dimension $48$ and type $(1^3,3^5)$.

The universal grading of $\mathcal D$ is trivial. Otherwise its group
has order dividing $48$, is solvable by Burnside's theorem, and has a
prime cyclic quotient of order two or three. Equal component dimensions
\cite[Proposition~8.20]{ENO} make both impossible. For a $C_2$ grading
all three invertibles lie in the neutral component, requiring
$24=3+9n$. For a $C_3$ grading their group maps trivially or isomorphically
to the grading group, requiring $16=3+9n$ or $16=1+9n$.
Thus solvability forces $\mathcal D=\mathcal E^{C_3}$ with
$\FPdim(\mathcal E)=16$. Its unit accounts for all three invertibles
of $\mathcal D$. A dimension-three simple of $\mathcal D$ comes from
either a free orbit of three invertibles of $\mathcal E$ or a fixed
dimension-three simple, the latter giving three such simples in
$\mathcal D$. Counting the five dimension-three simples leaves only
types $(1^{16})$ or $(1^7,3)$ for $\mathcal E$. The second is impossible:
its seven invertibles would fix its unique noninvertible $Y$, so
$YY^*$ would contain all seven, leaving dimension two that cannot be
filled by copies of $Y$. Consequently
$\mathcal E=\operatorname{Vec}_H^\omega$ for a group $H$ of order sixteen.

As in the preceding proof, $\mathcal D_{\rm ad}=\mathcal D$ makes the
canonical central $\operatorname{Rep}(C_3)$ unique over its underlying
invertibles. The outer $C_2$ action preserves it. Equivariantization
in stages gives $\mathcal C=(\operatorname{Vec}_H^\omega)^G$, where
$1\to C_3\to G\to C_2\to1$ and the central
$\operatorname{Rep}(G)$ embeds fully faithfully into $\mathcal C$.
The cyclic group of order six would give six invertibles; hence $G=S_3$.
The identity orbit contributes dimensions $1,1,2$, exhausting the small
simples. Any other fixed orbit has projective stabilizer $S_3$, whose
irreducible projective degrees are at most $\sqrt6$, and would give an
extra small simple. An orbit of size two has stabilizer $C_3$ and would
give extra dimension-two simples. An orbit of size three gives two
dimension-three simples, and a free orbit gives one dimension-six simple.
Thus the action on $H$ must have orbit sizes
\[
 (1,3,6,6).
\]
Its normal $C_3$ acts without fixed points outside the identity; a
transposition fixes exactly two elements of $H$.

Here is a purely group-theoretic contradiction. For a fixed-point-free
order-three automorphism $\alpha$ of a group of order sixteen, every
characteristic subgroup has order congruent to one modulo three.
Thus the center has order four or sixteen. If it has order four, it
is $V_4$, and the noncyclic quotient $H/Z(H)$ is also $V_4$.
The commutator is an $\alpha$-equivariant alternating map
$\bigwedge^2V_4\to Z(H)$. Its source has trivial $C_3$ action, whereas
its target has no nonzero fixed vector. It vanishes, a contradiction.
So $H$ is abelian. The characteristic subgroups $2H$ and $4H$ exclude
$C_{16}$, $C_8\times C_2$, and $C_4\times C_2^2$, leaving
$H=C_4^2$ or $C_2^4$.

Every involution of either remaining group fixes at least four elements.
For $C_2^4$, $(S-I)^2=0$ gives $\dim\ker(S-I)\geq2$.
For $C_4^2$, if $S$ reduces to the identity modulo two, it fixes all
four elements of $2H$. Otherwise a lifted basis change gives
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with
$a,b,d$ odd and $c$ even. From $S^2=I$ one obtains
$bc=0$ and $b(a+d)=0$, hence $c=0$ and $d=-a$ modulo four.
Each of the four choices of $x$ has the unique fixed-vector completion
$y=-b^{-1}(a-1)x$; the second equation follows from $a^2=1$.
This contradicts the required two fixed points and excludes both rings
over $\C$. The supplement checks the exact tensor premises and also
provides an independent finite corroboration of this theoretical lemma.

\subsection{Two dimension-24 equivariantizations}
\label{sec:tyequiv}
Put $\mathcal T=\operatorname{TY}(C_3,\chi,1/\sqrt3)$, with
$\chi(a,b)=\exp(2\pi iab/3)$. Write $m$ for its noninvertible simple and
$|a|=0$, $|m|=1$ for its $C_2$ grading. Inversion $I(a)=-a$, $I(m)=m$
preserves $\chi$ and gives a coherent unitary $C_2$ action with canonical
channel identifications as tensorators and identity square coherence
\cite[Section~4A and Proposition~4.8]{GNN}.
The associators are phases, permutations or the normalized Fourier matrix.

First take $\mathcal E=\mathcal T\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(C_2)$ and
$F(X,e)=(I(X),e+|X|)$. Grading additivity and the trivial pointed
associator give the tensor structure, and $F^2=\mathrm{Id}$ as a tensor
functor. Thus $\mathcal E^{C_2}$ is unitary. Its two fixed simple objects
$(0,e)$ each have two equivariant lifts $t_e^+,t_e^-$; the other
invertibles form orbits $P_e=\{(1,e),(2,e)\}$, and the two odd objects
form $Z=\{(m,0),(m,1)\}$. In the ordered basis
\[
 (1,h,t,ht,P_0,P_1,Z)=(t_0^+,t_0^-,t_1^+,t_1^-,P_0,P_1,Z),
\]
the invertible group is $C_2^2$, $h$ fixes both $P_e$, $t$ exchanges
them, and all invertibles fix $Z$. The remaining products are
\begin{align*}
 P_eP_f&=t_{e+f}^++t_{e+f}^-+P_{e+f},& P_eZ&=ZP_e=2Z,\\
 Z^2&=1+h+t+ht+2P_0+2P_1.&&
\end{align*}
Here and below subscripts are modulo two. Fixed output channels are
exchanged in pairs and have one invariant vector of each sign; free
orbit outputs have a unique equivariant lift. These rules agree with
$R7\_2\_1$ under the model-to-census permutation $(0,5,1,2,4,3,6)$.

For the second construction, let $z$ generate $C_4$ and use the
normalized unitary cocycle
\[
 \omega(a,b,c)=i^{w(a,b,c)},\qquad
 w(a,b,c)=a\left\lfloor\frac{b+c}{4}\right\rfloor,
 \quad 0\leq a,b,c<4.
\]
Inside $\mathcal T\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(C_4,\omega)$ take the
full diagonal subcategory with simples $(a,z^{2e})$, $(m,z)$ and
$(m,z^3)$. It is closed under tensor products and duality because TY
parity equals the pointed degree modulo two. Define $F$ by inversion in
both factors. Its TY tensorator is that of $I$; on pointed degrees use
$J(a,b)=i^{u(a,b)}$, in the direction $F(X)\otimes F(Y)\to F(X\otimes Y)$,
where
\[
 u=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\0&0&0&3\\0&3&2&1\\0&1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Reducing arguments modulo four, direct substitution gives
\begin{align*}
 u(b,c)-u(a+b,c)+u(a,b+c)-u(a,b)
   &\equiv w(a,b,c)-w(-a,-b,-c),\\
 u(a,b)+u(-a,-b)&\equiv0\pmod4.
\end{align*}
The first is tensor-functor coherence; the second makes $F^2$ the
identity tensor functor. Identity multiplication coherence therefore
defines a genuine $C_2$ action. The supplement checks all $256$ pointed
cocycle equations, $64$ tensorator equations and $16$ square equations.

The equivariantization has the same simple-orbit pattern. The positive
lift $t$ of $(0,z^2)$ satisfies $t^2=h$, since $J(2,2)=-1$; hence its
invertibles form $C_4$. All other displayed products remain valid, with
$t_1^+=t$ and $t_1^-=t^3$. In $Z^2$, the degree-two pair of channels is
exchanged with coefficients $1,1$, and the degree-zero pair with
coefficients $i,-i$. Both involutions have trace zero and eigenvalues
$1,-1$, giving one copy of each fixed lift. The complete tensor matches
$R7\_2\_195$ under $(0,6,1,2,5,3,4)$. All scalars and tensorators are
unitary, so both constructions are unitary categories over $\C$.

\subsection{A quaternion equivariantization in dimension 48}
Let $Q_8=\{\pm1,\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}$ with
$e_1e_2=e_3$, $e_2e_3=e_1$, $e_3e_1=e_2$, and $e_a^2=-1$.
For $p\in S_3$, the rule
\[
 \rho_p(e_a)=\operatorname{sgn}(p)e_{p(a)}
\]
is a group automorphism, and $\rho_p\rho_q=\rho_{pq}$.
It gives a strict unitary action on $\operatorname{Vec}(Q_8)$, so
$\operatorname{Vec}(Q_8)^{S_3}$ is a unitary fusion category of
dimension $48$. Each fixed central grade $1,-1$ carries the three
ordinary irreducibles $1,\varepsilon,V$ of $S_3$; the six noncentral
grades form one free orbit, giving a simple $X$ of dimension six
\cite{BurciuNatale}. Label the first six simples $(z,a)$, with
$z\in C_2$ and $a\in\{1,\varepsilon,V\}$. Their products are those
of $\operatorname{Rep}(S_3)\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}(C_2)$ and
$(z,a)X=X(z,a)=\dim(a)X$.
In $X^2$, the six ordered pairs with product either central grade
form a regular $S_3$-set; each noncentral grade has four ordered pairs.
Consequently, in the order
$(0,1),(0,\varepsilon),(0,V),(1,1),(1,\varepsilon),(1,V),X$,
\[
 X^2=(0,1)+(0,\varepsilon)+2(0,V)
       +(1,1)+(1,\varepsilon)+2(1,V)+4X.
\]
This complete tensor matches $R7\_4\_2$ under
$(0,5,4,1,2,3,6)$. The replay checks the group action, computes the
central permutation characters and compares all $343$ coefficients.

\subsection{The twisted quaternion model from a Chern class}
\label{sec:chern}
The remaining dimension-48 ring $R7\_4\_2019$ has a unitary
group-theoretical model
\[
 \mathcal C(\Gamma,\Omega,S_3,1),\qquad
 \Gamma=Q_8\rtimes S_3,\qquad
 [\Omega]=c_2(V)-\operatorname{inf}c_2(R).
\]
The action is the signed-axis action above. To construct the class,
view unit quaternions inside $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and distinguish the
scalar imaginary unit $I$ from quaternion units $i,j,k$. Set
\[
 r=-\frac{1+i+j+k}{2},\qquad s=I\frac{i-j}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Then $r^3=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$; conjugation implements the signed
axis cycle and transposition. Thus $r,s$ define a unitary
representation $R:S_3\to U(2)$ with determinant the sign character,
and $V(q,p)=qR(p)$ is a representation of $\Gamma$.
The ordinary second Chern classes give a well-defined class
in $H^4(B\Gamma,\Z)\cong H^3(\Gamma,U(1))$
\cite{Atiyah}. Its restriction to $S_3$ is zero.

Put $z=-1\in Q_8$ and $K=\langle z\rangle\times S_3$. If
$x=c_1(L)$ for the sign line of $\langle z\rangle$ and
$y=c_1(\det R)$, the splitting principle gives
\[
 [\Omega]|_K=c_2(L\otimes R)-c_2(R)=x^2+xy.
\]
Writing elements of $K$ as $(a,p)$ and parity as $\epsilon(p)$, this
restriction has the explicit normalized representative
\[
 \omega_K((a,p),(b,q),(c,r))=(-1)^{abc+a\epsilon(q)\epsilon(r)}.
\]
Indeed the real logarithm $a(bc+\epsilon(q)\epsilon(r))/2$ has
integer coboundary $ab(cd+\epsilon(r)\epsilon(s))$, representing
$x^2+xy$. The replay checks all $12^4$ such identities exactly.
Choose a unitary global cocycle representing $[\Omega]$. Extend a
correcting normalized two-cochain from $K$ to $\Gamma$ to make its
restriction exactly $\omega_K$. Then $\Omega|_{S_3}=1$.
The group-theoretical category is the category of $\C[S_3]$-bimodules
in $\operatorname{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\Omega}$
\cite[Section~3 and Theorem~5.1]{GelakiNaidu}. The unitary cocycle and
standard positive separable subgroup algebra make it unitary.
This proves existence without constructing a table of $48^3$ cocycle values.

The central double cosets have stabilizer $S_3$. Over $z$, the multiplier is
\[
 \alpha_z(p,q)=\frac{\omega_K(z,p,q)\omega_K(p,q,z)}{\omega_K(p,z,q)}
              =(-1)^{\epsilon(p)\epsilon(q)}.
\]
It has the one-dimensional projective representation
$u(p)=I^{\epsilon(p)}$. Its lift $t$ satisfies $t^2=\varepsilon$:
the central-degree tensorator is trivial and $u(p)^2$ is the sign
character. Thus the six central simples are
$1,\varepsilon,v,t,t\varepsilon,tv$, with the $S_3$ representation
products and relation $t^2=\varepsilon$.
The noncentral double coset is free and gives one simple $X$ of
dimension six. Products $YX=XY=\FPdim(Y)X$ follow from support and
dimension. Each central product fibre of $X^2$ is a free transitive
$S_3$-set, so its projective monomial representation is projective
regular, with irreducible multiplicities $1,1,2$ regardless of the
cocycle phases. The noncentral fibres each have size four. Hence
\[
 X^2=1+\varepsilon+2v+t+t\varepsilon+2tv+4X.
\]
In this order the model-to-census permutation is $(0,6,5,1,2,3,4)$.
The supplement checks the representation relations, action,
characteristic-class restriction, projective lift, and all $343$
coefficients. The remaining $59$ tensors are undecided.
