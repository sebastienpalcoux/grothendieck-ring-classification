\section{Proof of the uniform bound}\label{sec:global-bound-proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-global}]
If $\C$ is spherical, Theorem~\ref{pivbd:main} gives
$D<1000$. Suppose therefore that it is not spherical.
Theorem~\ref{ns:thm:ns-spherical} shows that every simple object is
self-dual. If $D$ were rational, it would be an integer, and weak
integrality would give a spherical structure by
\cite[Proposition~8.24]{ENO}. Thus $D$ is irrational.

The field generated by the Frobenius--Perron character is a number-field
factor of the four-dimensional rational Grothendieck algebra. It has
degree at most four and contains $\Q(D)$. If $D$ had degree
three, that character field would have degree three as well;
Theorem~\ref{cub:thm:cubic-classification} would make the category
spherical. Only degrees two and four remain.

In either case Corollary~\ref{col:cor:rank-four-dimension-field-descent}
identifies the signed dimension field with $\Q(D)$.
In degree two, Theorem~\ref{qs:short-orbits} shows that the signed
rational algebra is the product of two copies of this field, with
complementary codegree product at least $100$.
Theorem~\ref{quad:opt:unconditional} therefore gives $D<1000$.
In degree four, both ordinary and signed rational algebras are the
common quartic field. Theorem~\ref{thm:quartic-uniform-bound} gives $D<950$.
This proves the dimension bound in every case.

By Lemma~\ref{core:lem:coefficient-bound},
\[
 N_{XY}^{Z}\le d_X<\sqrt D<\sqrt{1000}<32.
\]
Integrality gives the bound $31$. A multiplication table in a fixed
basis of four elements has only finitely many entries, all now bounded;
hence only finitely many based isomorphism classes can occur.
\end{proof}
