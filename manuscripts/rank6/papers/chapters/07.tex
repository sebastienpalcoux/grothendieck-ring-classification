\section{Certificates, research coverage, and the remaining frontier}\label{sec:certification}
\subsection{Automated allocation and verification}\label{sec:verification}
The computation has three separate layers: the frozen source census,
a discovery pipeline, and an exact categorification-certificate checker. Discovery is permitted to time
out or to use numerical linear programming to propose a functional.
Only the checker can validate a negative record. The final ledger
contains one of four disjoint reporting classes: generally excluded,
unitarily realized, spherically realized with no pseudo-unitary
categorification, or unresolved. Five separate existence columns record
the categorical structures, with no implication from spherical to
pseudo-unitary existence. The pipeline rejects any intersection
of its positive and negative sets.

Tables~\ref{tab:stages} and~\ref{tab:stages-high} give a sequential view: a tensor removed at one
stage is not counted as a new removal at any later stage. The order
places inexpensive structural and arithmetic obstructions before
larger finite branch searches. Effectiveness means the number of
\emph{additional} tensors decided at that point, rather than the number
that a criterion could reject when run in isolation. The table is a
transparent operational order, not a comparison of logical strength or
a hardware-independent timing ranking. Ineligible hypotheses, an
unfinished search and a feasible necessary relaxation all leave a
ring undecided unless another accepted certificate or model resolves it.

The allocation in Table~\ref{tab:allocation} is disjoint. It records
the certificate family stored for each exclusion, while
Tables~\ref{tab:stages} and~\ref{tab:stages-high} make the sequential accounting explicit.
Neither table asserts mathematical independence of the criteria. The signed and positive-dimension records call the
same low-induction and finite-indicator framework internally. When an
older unbalanced relative-trace certificate suffices, it is interpreted
as a certificate for Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced} with zero coefficients
on the added balance equations. Likewise every inherited Farkas vector remains valid after the Galois-forbidden columns of Theorem~\ref{thm:character-induction} are removed: the inequalities on retained columns are unchanged. The stronger framework therefore retains the old exact witnesses rather than treating their special cases as unrelated obstructions.

Every certificate is verified from the original ordered tensor. The
checker reconstructs number fields, verifies separating elements and
factorizations, recomputes low-column and signed-table possibilities,
and checks all integer inequalities. In particular it does not assume
that a stored list of branches is exhaustive. For Farkas records it
checks \eqref{eq:farkas} with Python integers. During certificate
verification the floating-point linear-programming entry point is
replaced by a function that raises an error if called.

The source revision and tensor hashes fix the counting input. No census
is regenerated for the categorification analysis. The positive check
reconstructs the listed products, the zesting tensor and scalar
coboundary, finite-group character rings, and group-theoretical
convolution tensors, including the twisted $S_4$ cochains and their
projective stabilizer factors. Exact Weyl characters reconstruct the
$G_2$ model. Every positive entry, including every multiplicity-one
entry, is matched by an explicit unit-fixing basis permutation; the
complete $\ConstructorCount$-record constructor catalogue is checked for omissions. Regression tests compare the native integer
routines with straightforward exact implementations, and deliberately
corrupted certificates must be rejected.

This is an exact computer-assisted verification, not a formalization in
a proof assistant. The categorical input theorems, their hypotheses,
and the mathematical completeness argument remain part of the written
proof. A checksum proves file integrity, not a mathematical theorem.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}]
Each negative record is an application of Theorems~\ref{thm:involution}
and~\ref{thm:automorphisms}, or of Proposition~\ref{prop:inputs},
Corollary~\ref{cor:subring}, or of an exhaustive signed/positive reduction
followed by Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} or Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced}
and the cited rank-four or rank-five tests, with the tensor-sign
reduction of Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-sign} and the extension of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rotation} where recorded. The stronger character-aware system of Theorem~\ref{thm:character-induction}, dimension-order test of Proposition~\ref{prop:dimension-order}, and signed refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-traces} are used where recorded. The spectral refinement of Proposition~\ref{prop:module-perron},
rank-three subring descent, Theorem~\ref{thm:morita-order6}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:biquadratic-trace-obstruction} supply the
additional continuation certificates. The second continuation also
uses the ramification criterion of Proposition~\ref{prop:ramification-trace},
Theorem~\ref{thm:formal-codegree-trace-ideal}, the signed saturation
corollary, and Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic-gauss-family}. The higher
stabilizer and ambient cyclotomic refinements use
Corollary~\ref{cor:stabilizer-average} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quartic-stabilizer}, with a complete general-to-pseudo-unitary
reduction whenever their inner certificate has only pseudo-unitary scope.
Theorem~\ref{thm:stabilizer-rotation-frontier} supplies the final general
multiplicity-three exclusion: its sphericalization reduction, full
Morita-action calculation, local rotation lemma and complete exact
indicator separation are all required. The exact checker validates
all hypotheses and exhausts the required finite alternatives. Thus no
excluded tensor has a complex categorification. Section~\ref{sec:positive}
constructs and tensor-matches all claimed positive examples, with the
additional models in Sections~\ref{sec:literature-models} and~\ref{sec:g2-e4}, the higher models of Section~\ref{sec:through7-models}, and complete
catalogue in Section~\ref{sec:model-catalogue}. In particular the spherical
existence and pseudo-unitary nonexistence for $\RR{2}{124}$ use the
full-tensor identification and the two cited results of Schopieray. No complement argument is used
to assign the positive records. The census and disjoint ledger establish the counts in
Table~\ref{tab:classification}. The other records are left undecided,
with no assertion of nonexistence or realizability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A realized boundary of the finite relaxations}\label{sec:frontier}
The ring $\RR{2}{4}$, realized in Proposition~\ref{prop:g2-e4}, has invertible group
$\langle g\rangle=C_4$ and two self-dual noninvertibles $X,Y$.
Its rules are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstresidual}
 \begin{gathered}
 gX=Y=Xg,\qquad gY=X=Yg,\\
 X^2=Y^2=1+g^2+2X+2Y,\\
 XY=YX=g+g^3+2X+2Y.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The dimensions are $(1,1,1,1,d,d)$ with $d=2+\sqrt6$ and
$D=4+2d^2=24+8\sqrt6$. This ring is not confused with the Klein-four
quadratic ring of the same dimension type: its invertible group is
cyclic. Nor is it the zesting example in Proposition~\ref{prop:zest},
whose noninvertible square has only one of the two noninvertibles.

\begin{proposition}[An exact finite feasible branch]\label{prop:frontier}
For the ring \eqref{eq:firstresidual}, the height-six balanced
finite-indicator system has a rational nonnegative feasible point after
fixing an integral high-column Gram decomposition. The associated
formal list has twenty-two central simples. Thus the necessary system
consisting of the chosen low data, Gram equations, algebraic-integrality
column bounds, height-six relative traces, duality balance, Galois
dimension-count balance, and integer column counts cannot by itself
exclude this ring.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The file \filename{data/frontier_feasible.json} specifies the complete
tensor, low matrix, allowed second indicators, nonnegative integral
column counts, and rational values of the trace variables. The checker
reconstructs the low-column list and confirms membership, computes every
high column, and checks \eqref{eq:integergram}. It then reconstructs the
balanced system and the fixed-count rows and verifies $Ax=b$ and
$x\ge0$ by exact rational arithmetic. The specified column counts sum
to sixteen and the low part has six distinct columns, giving twenty-two
formal simples. The exact entries, system dimensions, and a successful
verification report are included in the supplement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integral counts in the rotation relaxation]\label{prop:rotation-frontier}
For $\RR{2}{4}$, the height-six system of Theorem~\ref{thm:rotation}
has an exact nonnegative feasible point with integer high-center counts
and integer cyclic-character counts. Its system has $377$ equations in
$893$ variables, with $60$ nonzero coordinates and formal central rank
$27$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The file \filename{data/frontier_rotation_feasible.json} supplies its
low columns, second indicators and exact rational coordinates. The
checker independently regenerates all center and rotation rows,
verifies nonnegativity and every equality, and checks integrality of
all $798$ high-center and $55$ rotation-count coordinates. Only the
inequality slack coordinates are permitted to be rational. Summing the
high-center counts and the six low columns gives $27$.
\end{proof}
This is a different feasible point from the rank-twenty-two point in
Proposition~\ref{prop:frontier}; they must not be combined into one
claimed center. The stronger point shows that adding the displayed
tensor-rotation equations and integrality of these grouped counts
does not exclude the cyclic-four tensor, now positively realized in Section~\ref{sec:g2-e4}. A separate
height-twelve rational search also found no exclusion, but only the
height-six exact point is used for the proposition.

Neither proposition constructs a modular datum or a category.
In Proposition~\ref{prop:frontier} trace counts may be rational.
Even the grouped integer counts of Proposition~\ref{prop:rotation-frontier}
specify
averaged twist information rather than individual twists satisfying
Verlinde and balancing identities. These points are now positive controls for the realized tensor
$\RR{2}{4}$, not grounds for pursuing an exclusion of it. For unresolved
tensors, additional information such as individual twist sectors,
center fusion coefficients and multiplication compatibility can refine
the necessary systems. The distinct point in
Section~\ref{sec:modular-frontier} concerns $\RR{3}{215}$ and is verified
separately. No feasible system is promoted to categorical existence.

The higher-multiplicity residuals include both finite feasible relaxations and bounded signed searches that did not finish. Their diagnostics and complete
tensors are supplied separately. The verified feasible points for $\RR{2}{4}$ must not be silently assigned to other rings. Current
quadratic-category work such as \cite{MY} is relevant context, but
its Klein-four classification is distinct from the cyclic-four conformal-embedding construction above.

\subsection{Reproduction and counting data}\label{sec:reproduction}
The top-level command
\begin{center}\texttt{python verify.py --jobs 2}\end{center}
checks the frozen input correspondence, positive models, and allocated
negative certificates. The command with \texttt{python -O} checks
that correctness does not depend on Python assertions. Regression
controls exercise exact arithmetic, corrupted certificates and the
frontier witnesses. Precise dependencies, commands, report scope and
search-stop behavior are given in the accompanying verification guide.
These are classification checks, not a new enumeration of fusion rings.

The \filename{oeis/} directory contains an eight-term b-file, a CSV,
and proposed definition and provenance text. The definition is the
number of based-isomorphism classes of rank-six fusion rings of
\emph{exact} multiplicity $m$, without assuming commutativity,
self-duality, integrality or categorifiability. The terms are copied
from the pinned census. No accession number is assumed and no online
submission is claimed. Counting farther and classifying farther are
separate achievements.

\subsection{Research coverage and the next unresolved stratum}\label{sec:research-coverage}
The residual list is derived from the complete decision ledger, not
from a hand-selected sample. Multiplicities one through three are completely decided.
The smallest unresolved multiplicity and the exact residual tensors
are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}; the first such tensor is the
priority for further theoretical and automated work. The stage table
also shows whether difficulties reappear at larger multiplicities.
A criterion applied successfully at one bound remains the same
mathematical criterion at the next bound: additional computation alone
is not presented as a new theorem.

The module-Gram refinements and relative-center constructions are
necessary tests or model constructions with explicit hypotheses.
A failed eligibility check does not exclude a ring. An inconclusive
Gram factor does not supply a model. For the relative Fibonacci center
of $TT_3$, the categorical construction and exact tensor reconstruction
supply the positive conclusion; feasible Gram data by themselves do not.

Literature comparisons distinguish exact fusion-rule matches from
shared dimensions. Unitary-only classifications do not exclude arbitrary
complex fusion categories, and candidate modular data are not
categorifications unless the source supplies a construction. The same
principles govern new models and new necessary criteria: a model must
have a justified categorical source and an exact full-tensor match;
an obstruction must state its hypotheses and exhaust all branches
needed for the claimed categorical structure.

The former residual $\RR{3}{215}$ illustrates the distinction between
feasible necessary data and categorical existence. Its $230$-equation
induction witness satisfies the joint low-product Gram condition in
Section~\ref{sec:joint-low-product-gram}. Proposition~\ref{prop:cyclic-eight-center}
extends that rank-$22$ point to exact modular and full center fusion
data with a compatible restriction-ring map. These are historical
feasible controls for the specified relaxations. They do not satisfy
the additional local involution-rotation condition used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:stabilizer-rotation-frontier}, which now generally
excludes the target ring.

There is also an independent strengthening of the source-specific
cyclic-eight argument: the actual Grossman--Izumi center forces the
six twist-one constituents of canonical unit induction. Applying the
same observation to the involution Morita dual forces the missing
right-action trace. The earlier six-object module and second-algebra
argument therefore excludes the whole actual cyclic-eight Morita class,
without fixing a candidate restriction map. The proof and exact trace
check are retained in
\filename{research/round2_audit/actual_cyclic8_morita.md} and its
companion program. This argument does not assert rigidity of braided
categories from their modular data.

The Dong--Schopieray bound in
Section~\ref{sec:premodular-frontier-bound} separately excludes a braided
spherical categorification of this tensor. Its scope is narrower than
the final general obstruction. The Ng--Rowell--Wen comparison screens
all $\CensusCount$ source tensors against the rank-six ancillary data
and performs exact based-ring comparisons after invariant filtering.
It finds no new model; the source's largest fusion coefficient is two.
Failure to match that modular catalogue is not a general obstruction.

These computations are reproduced by
\begin{verbatim}
python research/m8_frontier/low_product_gram.py --check
python research/m8_frontier/premodular_bound.py
python research/m8_frontier/nrw_rank6_scan.py
\end{verbatim}
The programs retain exact certificates and scoped reports alongside
their source. They apply their conditions to tensor data rather than
using a desired classification outcome as input.

\paragraph{The frozen continuations.}
The original sixteen-stage classification campaign and its accepted
certificate file are retained unchanged. The first continuation used
forty-seven frozen residual inputs and accepted seven general exclusions.
Its bounded discovery attempts, including higher multiplicities, remain
in \filename{verification/continuation/}; its accepted file is
\filename{certificates/continuation_20260929.jsonl}. The rank-three
subring scan covered the whole census, while the module and order-six
Morita refinements used their recorded residual domains. The Morita
argument's pseudo-unitary step follows a proved two-dimension Galois
reduction on the dual category. Ineligible inputs, interrupted searches
and surviving necessary systems were not accepted as exclusions.

The second continuation starts from the forty complete tensors in
\filename{verification/round2/campaign_manifest.json}. This immutable
domain contains the former multiplicity-three frontier and all
thirty-nine remaining inputs at multiplicities four through eight.
Regenerating the smaller final residual export does not change the
continuation's input. Its accepted general records are frozen in
\filename{certificates/continuation_round2.jsonl}; the disjoint union of
the continuations with the original campaign determines the ledger.
A tensor may have several independently checked proofs but receives
only one exclusion allocation.

The local stabilizer-rotation theorem completes multiplicity three.
Its higher-dimensional and cyclotomic refinements retain all required
module branches, character alternatives and exact separating witnesses.
The ramification, biquadratic and formal-codegree trace-ideal criteria
are applied by uniform tensor-based scans, including every frozen
higher input; the higher stabilizer campaign separately records
eligibility, finite search outcomes and budget stops for that same
input domain. This coverage does not mean that every possible search
completed or that every criterion's hypotheses held for every tensor.
The all-orders Gauss obstruction has pseudo-unitary scope; its general
applications include the separately verified signed reduction.

Two independent full-census scans illustrate the distinction between
criterion coverage and new allocations. The biquadratic checker
excludes fifty-one of the $\CensusCount$ tensors in isolation, and the
trace-ideal checker excludes $1827$. These totals include already
excluded rings and overlap other criteria; they are not added to the
classification count. Their complete reports and positive controls are
retained in \filename{research/round2_external/}. The Gauss family scan
also checks the full census, identifying the applicable parameter
family by complete tensor comparison. The imported multiplicity-thirteen
example is a regression input, not a census record.

The command \filename{software/round2_release.py} checks all candidate
general records, including alternative proofs, replays the first
continuation, freezes the second accepted file and builds the ledger
in one process. \filename{software/continuation_controls.py} independently
replays every accepted record in all continuation files against that
ledger, with numerical optimizers disabled. It is an incremental check
and does not claim to rerun the unchanged original campaign. A full
current-ledger replay is available through \filename{verify.py}.
The verification guide gives exact commands and report locations.

The third continuation starts from the twenty-two complete tensors in
\filename{verification/round3/campaign_manifest.json}. Its accepted
general proofs are allocated separately in
\filename{certificates/continuation_round3.jsonl}, preserving all earlier
certificate files. The tensor-based criteria, full-census applicability
scans, positive controls and surviving relaxations are recorded in
\filename{research/round3/}. The new signed-spectrum arguments separate
general categorical necessity from the pseudo-unitary induction step;
the latter is used only after a complete Galois reduction.
\filename{software/round3_release.py} replays the accepted new proofs
with numerical optimization disabled before rebuilding the combined
ledger. It retains the unchanged earlier exact evidence and does not
claim to repeat the complete original negative catalogue.

The remaining $\UnresolvedCount$ tensors are unresolved by this release.
No literature-wide open-problem claim is attached to that word. In
particular, survival of all implemented tests means only that these
tests have not decided the input. The paper makes no prediction of
counts or categorifiability beyond multiplicity eight.
