\section{Pairings, connected algebras and Morita restrictions}
\label{sec:pairing-morita-20261027}
The two rank-six rings remain unresolved. The following obstruction concerns
actual module categories, not just nonnegative integral representations.

\begin{lemma}[Morita pairing parity]
Let $\mathcal C$ be pseudo-unitary and $A$ a connected separable algebra.
Give its module category the positive trace and $A$ the compatible symmetric
separable Frobenius structure. If a selfdual simple $Q$ of
${}_A\mathcal C_A$ has second indicator $\epsilon$, every selfdual
constituent of its underlying object having indicator $-\epsilon$ occurs
with even multiplicity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The forgetful functor is pivotal Frobenius monoidal. It transports the
chosen nondegenerate selfduality pairing, including its pivotal transpose
sign. On an isotypic block of opposite sign, the multiplicity space must
carry a nondegenerate alternating form; its dimension is even.
The positive normalization is essential:
$\dim_{{}_A\mathcal C_A}Q=\dim_{\mathcal C}U(Q)/\dim_{\mathcal C}A$.
Thus the induced spherical structure is the canonical positive one.
The categorical inputs are Schaumann, \emph{Traces on module categories
over fusion categories}, Proposition 5.8 and Theorem 6.6
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5716}), and Mulevi\v cius,
\emph{Condensation inversion and Witt equivalence via generalised orbifolds},
Example 4.18 (\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02611}).
This transports pairings; it does not assert preservation of numerical
Frobenius--Schur indicators by the forgetful functor.
\end{proof}

For $\RR{8}{1660}$ the source signs on $(1,g,X,gX,\sigma,T)$ are
$(1,1,1,1,-1,1)$. The mixed-sign type-III dual contains a selfdual simple
whose restriction is $\sigma+T$. The second mixed type-IV realization
contains one restricting to $X+gX+\sigma+T$. Either sign for that simple
would leave an odd opposite-sign block. Both module realizations are
therefore impossible. The other mixed type-IV realization passes this test.
The restrictions follow by internal-Hom reciprocity:
$[U(Q):Z]=(N_ZQ)_{mm}$, using the complete dual action matrices, not a
dimension match.

\begin{proposition}[The $1+X$ construction target]
In a pseudo-unitary categorification of $\RR{8}{1660}$, any connected
separable algebra with underlying object $1\oplus X$ has bimodule category
with based ring $R8\_3\_941$. The same holds for $1\oplus gX$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The distinguished free module is simple and has internal end $1+X$.
Its zero diagonal $\sigma$ coefficient makes it an Ising leaf.
Only type III in the complete module list has this internal end; types II
and IV have nonzero $T$ coefficient, while type I is regular. The first
diagonal sign is positive. The mixed second sign has just been excluded.
For equal signs the complete dual-action computation has two rank-eight
possibilities. The weighted second-indicator obstruction eliminates one.
The other tensor is $R8\_3\_941$: the source-to-census permutation
$(0,3,6,7,4,5,2,1)$ identifies all $512$ structure constants.
Relabelling $X,gX$ gives the other assertion.
\end{proof}

The inverse internal-end algebra in this Morita context is also $1+Y$,
with $Y$ selfdual of dimension $d=3+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6$.
Neither algebra has been constructed. The third rotation on $\operatorname{Hom}(1,X^3)$ has eigenvalue multiplicities $(2,1,0)$ or $(2,0,1)$ at $(1,\omega,\omega^2)$. A symmetric Frobenius form makes the trilinear multiplication functional cyclically invariant. Via the pairing on $X$, the $X^2\to X$ component lies in its two-dimensional fixed subspace. Together with $X^2\to1$, this leaves at most three linear coefficients before normalization and gauge. Its associativity
still depends on the unknown ambient associator. The six-simple connecting
module, consisting of two regular Ising blocks, is an exact construction
target, not an existence theorem. Equal global FP dimension alone would
not identify this partner: $R8\_3\_928$ has the same dimension but is
categorically Morita rigid.

For the negative spin representation, $(44,2)$ is impossible in low frame
zero: phase $12$ has a unique symmetric restriction column and hence no
negative charge-conjugation line there. In frame one the total asymmetric
column budget is eight. Phase-$36$ asymmetric columns cannot be discarded;
both overall spin alternatives remain. Fusion by the distinguished
Galois-unit object of dimension $d^2$ acts on the negative conjugation
space as a signed permutation: its integral symmetric matrix squares to
the identity, so every row has one entry $\pm1$.

\subsection{A nonsplit, s-simple fermion centralizer}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:r1660-fermion-centralizer}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a positive categorification of $\RR{8}{1660}$,
$h$ its nontrivial central invertible, $F(h)=g$, $\theta_h=-1$, and
$d=3+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6$. Put $D=4(1+d^2)$ and
$\mathcal E=\langle h\rangle'\subset Z(\mathcal C)$.
Then $\FPdim(\mathcal E)=D^2/2$, its M\"uger center is $\mathrm{sVec}$,
and its only fusion subcategories are $\Vec,\mathrm{sVec},\mathcal E$.
In particular it does not split as a nondegenerate category times
$\mathrm{sVec}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The integral-center and phase-mass theorems above say that $1,h$ are the
only integral central simples, and that every central simple containing
$\sigma$ has restriction $2X+2gX+\sigma+3T$. No nonintegral central simple
therefore restricts entirely to the proper Ising subcategory, and no
central simple has dimension $\sqrt2$. Each of $X,gX,T$ generates
$\mathcal C$. Every nonpointed fusion subcategory of $Z(\mathcal C)$
thus forgets dominantly to $\mathcal C$ and has FP dimension greater
than $D$. Equality would make the functor an equivalence, supplying a
central simple preimage of $\sigma$ of dimension $\sqrt2$.

If $\mathcal E=\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}$ with $\mathcal M$
nondegenerate, modular decomposition \cite{DGNO,DMNO} gives
$Z(\mathcal C)=\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathcal K$ with
$\FPdim(\mathcal K)=4$. The factor $\mathcal K$ contains $1,h$ but no
other integral simple. Its remaining squared dimensions sum to two,
forcing exactly one simple of dimension $\sqrt2$, a contradiction.

If a proper nonpointed $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal E$ contains
$\mathrm{sVec}$, its centralizer in $\mathcal E$ either equals
$\mathrm{sVec}$, forcing $\mathcal H=\mathcal E$, or is nonpointed.
Both factors would then have dimension greater than $D$, contrary to
$\FPdim(\mathcal H)\FPdim(\mathcal H')=D^2$.
If $\mathcal H$ does not contain $h$, its join with $\mathrm{sVec}$ is
$\mathcal E$. Its symmetric center is integral and hence trivial.
Modular decomposition would give
$\mathcal E=\mathcal H\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}$, already excluded.
\end{proof}

This proof assumes neither a Morita link to $R8\_3\_941$ nor existence
of $1+X$. The same four-dimensional-complement argument proves
nonsplitting for $R8\_3\_941$: its integral center is $\{1,h\}$, and
every nonpointed source dimension is greater than $\sqrt2$.

\subsection{Odd indicators in an actual Morita link}
Suppose positive categorifications of $\RR{8}{1660}$ and $R8\_3\_941$
are Morita equivalent; equal dimension alone does not imply this.
Put $a=2+\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt6$ and $b=a+\sqrt2$.
The rank-eight noninvertible dimensions are $a,a,b,b,d,d$.
For odd $n$, write their indicators as $u_1,u_2,v,v,w_1,w_2$, and put
$x=\nu_n(X),t=\nu_n(T)$ in the rank-six category. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r1660-r941-odd-indicators}
 u_1+u_2=t,\qquad v=t/2,\qquad w_1+w_2=2x.
\end{equation}
Indeed the dimension-weighted indicator is Morita invariant and
$\sqrt2d=a+b$, so
\[
1+a(u_1+u_2)+2bv+d(w_1+w_2)=1+(a+b)t+2dx.
\]
The elements $1,a,b,d$ form a basis of $\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, linearly
disjoint from $\QQ(\zeta_n)$ for odd $n$: each nontrivial quadratic
subfield has conductor divisible by four. Coefficient comparison gives
\eqref{eq:r1660-r941-odd-indicators}.
For $n=3$ the four $a,b$ vacuum spaces have dimension one, and the known
$x=2+\omega,t=2$ force all four rotations to be identity.
The two $d$ rotations have combined multiplicities $(4,2,0)$; their
unordered traces are $\{2+\omega,2+\omega\}$ or $\{3,1+2\omega\}$,
up to simultaneous conjugation. These feasible conditions construct
neither the Morita link nor either category.

\subsection{Complete algebra reduction for $\RR{4}{155}$}
Write the simples as $1,h,g,hg,X,Y$, with $X^*=Y$ and
\[
 XY=1+h+4(X+Y),\qquad X^2=Y^2=g+hg+4(X+Y).
\]
Put $d_0=4+3\sqrt2,D_0=72+48\sqrt2$.
The established unique central lifts have twists $1,-1,i,-i$.
Thus $h$ is a central fermion and the pointed associators on
$\langle g\rangle,\langle hg\rangle$ are nontrivial.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:r155-all-connected-algebras}
Every connected separable algebra in a positive categorification of
$\RR{4}{155}$ has one of the following underlying objects:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 1,\quad 1+h,\quad 1+X+Y,\quad 1+2X+2Y,\\
 1+h+2X+2Y,\quad 1+h+4X+4Y.
\end{gathered}
\]
Every indecomposable module category is represented by the regular algebra,
by $1+h$, or by a possible algebra with underlying object $1+X+Y$.
The latter statement asserts neither existence nor uniqueness of its
algebra structure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For invertible $z$, adjunction gives
$[A:z]=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_A(zA,A)\in\{0,1\}$, since $A,zA$ are
simple right modules. Their stabilizer is a subgroup on which the
pointed associator is trivial, hence $\{1\}$ or $\{1,h\}$.
The Frobenius pairing gives $A\simeq A^*$, so
$A=A_k=1+k(X+Y)$ or $B_k=1+h+k(X+Y)$.

For $A_k$, subtracting the four pointed free modules from the induction
Gram leaves residual multiplicities $(a_j,b_j)$ with
\[
 \sum a_j^2=\sum b_j^2=1+8k-4k^2,\qquad
 \sum a_jb_j=8k-4k^2.
\]
Thus $k\le2$. For $k=1$, a residual module underlying $X$ would have
internal-end dimension $d_0^2/(1+2d_0)=2+4\sqrt2/3$, not an algebraic
integer; the remaining modules underlie $2X+Y,X+2Y$.
For $k=2$ they underlie $X,Y$, with internal ends $1+h$.
The latter algebra structure is unique since
$H^2(C_2,\CC^\times)=0$, so $A_2$ is represented by $B_0$.

For $B_k$, the two pointed free modules are $B_k,gB_k$ and all other
simple modules underlie $a_jX+b_jY$. Their residual Gram satisfies
\[
 q=2+8k-2k^2,\qquad
 \sum a_j^2=\sum b_j^2=q,\qquad \sum a_jb_j=q-2.
\]
Thus $k\le4$. For $k=1,3$, put $c_j=a_j+b_j>0$.
Internal-end dimensions are respectively
\[
 c_j^2(13+9\sqrt2)/7,\qquad c_j^2(5+3\sqrt2)/7.
\]
Integrality in $\ZZ[\sqrt2]$ forces $7\mid c_j$ while
$c_j\le2\sqrt8<6$, a contradiction.
For $k=2$, $q=10$ and the dimension is $c_j^2(1+2\sqrt2/3)$.
Hence $3\mid c_j$ and $\sum c_j^2=36$.
The alternative $c_j=6$ forces $a_j=b_j=3$ and contributes nine to an
inner product of total eight, impossible. Four residual modules remain
with $c_j=3$; their total squared norm twenty forces $(a_j,b_j)=(1,2)$
or $(2,1)$, twice each. Their internal ends underlie $1+X+Y$.
For $k=4$, $q=2$ gives two copies each of $X,Y$, with internal-end
dimension $d_0^2/(2+8d_0)=1$, hence the regular module category.
Finally $A_0$ is regular and $B_0=1+h$ exists.
\end{proof}

The canonical central algebra for the pointed subcategory has a forgetful
augmentation splitting its algebra as $1\times A_2$. This forces $A_2$
in a hypothetical category, but returns to the $B_0$ Morita route; it
does not force $A_1$.

\subsection{Central fermions and complete Morita ring rigidity}
\begin{lemma}[Central fermion alpha induction]\label{lem:central-fermion-alpha}
Let $f$ be a central fermion in a fusion category $\mathcal C$ and let
$A=1\oplus f$ be its group algebra. The central half-braiding defines
a tensor equivalence
\[
 \alpha:\mathcal C\longrightarrow {}_A\mathcal C_A,\qquad V\longmapsto V\otimes A.
\]
No braiding on all of $\mathcal C$ is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The free right action and the left action obtained by moving $A$ past
$V$ give a strong tensor functor. A right-linear map $\alpha(V)\to
\alpha(W)$ is determined by $t_0:V\to W$ and $t_1:V\to W\otimes f$.
Left $f$-linearity imposes no extra condition on $t_0$. For $t_1$,
the two composites on $f\otimes V$ are, with associators suppressed
and $m:f^2\to1$,
\[
\begin{split}
(\mathrm{id}_W\otimes m)\circ(t_1\otimes\mathrm{id}_f)\circ c_{f,V},\\
(\mathrm{id}_W\otimes m)\circ(c_{f,W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_f)
                 \circ(\mathrm{id}_f\otimes t_1).
\end{split}
\]
Naturality and the half-braiding hexagon make the first the negative
of the second because $c_{f,f}=-\mathrm{id}_{f^2}$. Hence $t_1=0$.
The functor is fully faithful; equal FP dimensions give an equivalence.
This is the central version of the fermion Azumaya argument above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Morita rigidity of the based ring]\label{thm:r155-morita-ring-rigidity}
Every fusion category Morita equivalent to a pseudo-unitary
categorification of $\RR{4}{155}$ again has based ring $\RR{4}{155}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:r155-all-connected-algebras} reduces the proof to
the regular algebra, $1+h$, and a possible $A_1=1+X+Y$.
The first case is immediate and Lemma~\ref{lem:central-fermion-alpha}
handles the second.

For $A_1$, use module basis $A_1,hA_1,gA_1,hgA_1,M,N$, with
$F(M)=2X+Y,F(N)=X+2Y$ and dimension vector
$\mathbf r=(1,1,1,1,\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$. The $X$ action is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&2\\1&1&1&1&1&2\\1&1&1&1&2&1\\
1&1&1&1&2&1\\2&2&1&1&2&2\\1&1&2&2&2&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The pointed actions are regular on the first four entries; $h$ fixes
$M,N$ and $g$ exchanges them. The commutant equations show that every
nonnegative integral commuting matrix is uniquely
\[
 M(a,b,c,e,u,v)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b&c&e&u&v\\b&a&e&c&u&v\\c&e&a&b&v&u\\e&c&b&a&v&u\\
v&v&u&u&a+b&c+e\\u&u&v&v&c+e&a+b
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with six nonnegative integer parameters. For a dual simple put
$p=a+b+c+e,q=u+v$; its dimension is $\lambda=p+q\sqrt2$.
The regular action $\sum_j\lambda_j M_j=(D_0/8)\mathbf r\mathbf r^t$ gives
\[
 \sum p_j^2=36,\qquad \sum p_jq_j=24,\qquad\sum q_j^2=18.
\]
The four distinct central invertible lifts give four distinct pointed
permutations, hence four simples with $(p,q)=(1,0)$.
After removing these, Cauchy--Schwarz is equality:
$32\cdot18=24^2$. Every remaining pair is $t_j(4,3)$ with $t_j$
a positive integer, and $\sum t_j^2=2$ leaves precisely two simples
of dimension $d_0$.

Transpose exchanges $u,v$. Since $u+v=3$ is odd, the two simples are
a dual pair $Z,Z^*$. The regular identity gives $a=b=c=e=1$, and
their $(u,v)$ pairs sum to $(3,3)$. Their pointed stabilizer is
$\{1,h\}$, so reciprocity and $d_0^2=2+8d_0$ force
\[
 ZZ^*=1+h+4(Z+Z^*).
\]
The matrix identity gives $uv=2$. Together with $u+v=3$ this forces
$(u,v)=(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$, and then
\[
 Z^2=g+hg+4(Z+Z^*),
\]
the original ring. The six resulting matrices are linearly independent;
no faithfulness assumption on a general dual action is required.
\end{proof}

The theorem is rigidity of the based ring, not uniqueness of
categorification or algebra structures. It supplies neither associators
nor existence of $A_1$. The general-complex gate supplies a positive
representative if a complex categorification exists; it does not make
every original category pseudo-unitary.
