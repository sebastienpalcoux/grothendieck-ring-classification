\begin{abstract}
Ostrik classified the pivotal fusion categories of rank three. We show
that every complex fusion category of rank three admits a spherical
structure and recover the five Grothendieck rings. Both steps use the
mass at twist one in the Drinfeld center. For an irreducible cubic
codegree polynomial, Galois averaging in the sphericalization determines
the first six moments of the squared twist. For a spherical category, the
first two moments give a single inequality that excludes all the
remaining rings outside the list. The rank-specific descent theorem
we use from the literature is Ostrik's theorem for a quadratic factor.
\end{abstract}

\section{The classification and the proof}

A complex fusion category $\cC$ is a rigid, semisimple, $\C$-linear tensor
category with finite-dimensional morphism spaces, finitely many
isomorphism classes of simple objects and simple unit. Its rank is the
number of simple classes. Its Grothendieck ring $K_0(\cC)$ records their tensor
multiplicities and their distinguished basis. A pivotal structure is
a monoidal isomorphism from the identity to the double dual. It is
spherical when the two pivotal traces agree.

For nonnegative integers $k,l,m,n$ satisfying
$k^2+l^2=kn+lm+1$, denote by $K(k,l,m,n)$ the based ring on
$1,X,Y$ with
\[
 X^2=1+mX+kY,\qquad Y^2=1+lX+nY,\qquad XY=kX+lY.
\]
The elementary derivation of this presentation appears in
Section~\ref{sec:rings}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Up to based-ring isomorphism, the Grothendieck rings of complex fusion
categories of rank three are exactly
\[
 \Z[C_3],\qquad K(1,0,0,0),\qquad K(1,0,1,0),\qquad
 K(1,0,2,0),\qquad K(1,1,1,0).
\]
Every complex fusion category of rank three admits a spherical
structure. Each of the five rings has a unitary realization.
\end{theorem}

The five rows of Table~\ref{tab:models} specify unitary examples.
A row can have several inequivalent associators.

\paragraph{History.}
Two early sources of examples are Bion-Nadal's construction of an
$E_6$ subfactor \cite{BN} and the Tambara--Yamagami classification
\cite{TY}, which includes the Ising ring. Ostrik classified the
rank-three categories admitting a ribbon structure \cite{OstrikRibbon}.
Hagge and Hong classified the tensor structures on the $E_6$ ring
by solving the pentagon equations \cite{HH}. Thornton proved that
every near-group category, including the rank-three near-group
family, admits a spherical structure \cite[Theorem~1.3]{Thornton}.
Ostrik subsequently classified all pivotal rank-three categories,
up to tensor equivalence \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Ostrik}. His proof
also gave spherical descent in the quadratic case and obstructions for
individual cubic rings \cite[Section~5]{Ostrik}. We use his formal
codegrees and central-induction results \cite{OstrikCodegrees,Ostrik}
and the indicator formulas of Ng and Schauenburg \cite{NSind,NSexp}.

\paragraph{Outline.}
Sections~\ref{sec:rings} and \ref{sec:central} relate the three
formal codegrees to the mass at twist one in the center.
Section~\ref{sec:cubic} applies this relation to squared twists
in the sphericalization. Six moments force the cubic
ring and allow its spherical structure to descend. For a reducible
codegree polynomial we invoke Ostrik's quadratic descent or the
canonical spherical structure in the weakly integral case.
Section~\ref{sec:reducible-rings} then applies the same mass estimate
to ordinary twists. A degree-two polynomial recovers both Ostrik's
structural reduction and his bound for near-group multiplicities
\cite[Theorems~3.3 and~4.1]{Ostrik}. Each exclusion is independent
of any bound on the structure constants.

\section{The ring algebra and sphericalization}\label{sec:rings}


\subsection{The two codegree coefficients}

As in \cite[Proposition~3.1]{Ostrik}, a rank-three fusion ring is either $\Z[C_3]$, or all its basis elements are self-dual and it has the form $K(k,l,m,n)$, with basis $1,X,Y$ and multiplication
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring}
 X^2=1+mX+kY,\qquad Y^2=1+lX+nY,\qquad XY=kX+lY.
\end{equation}
Here $k,l,m,n$ are nonnegative integers satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assoc}
 k^2+l^2=kn+lm+1.
\end{equation}
Here is a quick derivation. If $X^*=Y\ne X$, the product $X^2$ has no
unit constituent; writing $d=\FPdim(X)=\FPdim(Y)$ gives
$d^2=(a+b)d$ for nonnegative integers $a,b$, so $d$ is an integer.
The product $XY$ has one unit constituent, hence
$d^2=1+(c+e)d$ for integers $c,e\geq0$; therefore $d\mid1$ and
$d=1$, giving $\Z[C_3]$. Otherwise all three simples are self-dual.
The involution on a based ring reverses products, so the ring is
commutative. Frobenius reciprocity forces the coefficients in $XY$
to be $k,l$ as displayed; the identity $(X^2)Y=X(XY)$ gives
\eqref{eq:assoc}, which also suffices for associativity.

The multiplication matrices in the ordered basis $1,X,Y$ are
\[
 N_X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&m&k\\0&k&l\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 N_Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&k&l\\1&l&n\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They are commuting real symmetric matrices, so their common characters
$\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_3$ are real. Their \emph{formal codegrees} are
\[
 f_i=1+\chi_i(X)^2+\chi_i(Y)^2.
\]
Equivalently, they are the eigenvalues of multiplication by the
Casimir element
\[
 \Omega=1+X^2+Y^2=3+(l+m)X+(k+n)Y.
\]
Let $\tau$ extract the coefficient of the unit. For the basis
$e_0=1,e_1=X,e_2=Y$ we have $\tau(e_i e_j)=\delta_{ij}$.
For multiplication by $a$, the $i$th diagonal entry is
$\tau(e_i a e_i)$. Thus $\operatorname{Tr}(a)=\tau(a\Omega)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir-trace}
 \operatorname{Tr}(a/\Omega)=\tau(a).
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{Tr}$ is the regular trace of the rational ring,
and $\Omega$ is invertible because its eigenvalues are positive.
We shall use the same calculation in the signed ring. Taking $a=1$
gives $\sum_i 1/f_i=1$. The codegrees are eigenvalues of an integral
matrix. Consequently their characteristic polynomial has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poly}
 p(t)=\prod_{i=1}^3(t-f_i)=t^3-bt^2+ct-c.
\end{equation}
We call the ring \emph{cubic} when $p$ is irreducible over $\Q$. We will use the following rank-three identities:
\begin{align}
 b&=9+(l+m)^2+(k+n)^2
   =7+3k^2+3l^2+m^2+n^2,\label{eq:b}\\
 c&=4b-9+(kl-mn)^2+\bigl(2(k^2-lm)-1\bigr)^2.\label{eq:c}
\end{align}
Both identities follow by expanding
$3I+(l+m)N_X+(k+n)N_Y$ and using \eqref{eq:assoc}.
The determinant identity is Ostrik's identity
\cite[equation~(12)]{Ostrik}, written as a sum of two squares. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 c\geq 4b-8,
\end{equation}
because the last square in \eqref{eq:c} is the square of an odd integer. Vieta's identities also give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-square}
 u:=\sum_{i=1}^3 f_i^{-2}=1-\frac{2b}{c}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The signed quotient}

Let $\cS$ be the sphericalization of a fusion category $\cC$ with self-dual rank-three ring. There is a tensor functor $\pi:\cS\to\cC$. Each simple object $V$ of $\cC$ has two simple lifts $V_+,V_-$
with nonzero opposite dimensions. When $V$ is self-dual, its lifts are
self-dual: the dual of $V_+$ is a lift of $V$, and sphericality gives
$\dim(V_+^*)=\dim(V_+)$, which rules out $V_-$.
This observation is due to Thornton \cite[proof of Lemma~2.3]{Thornton}, as noted in \cite[Section~5.1(a)]{Ostrik}. Put $\eps=\one_-$. Then
\[
 \eps^2\cong\one,\qquad \dim(\eps)=-1,\qquad
 \eps\otimes V_+\cong V_-.
\]
The construction and these properties are given in \cite[Remark~3.1 and Proposition~5.14]{ENO} and \cite[Section~5.1]{Ostrik}. A useful realization is the equivariantization for the canonical $C_2$-action \cite[Section~5.3]{Bartlett}.

All simples of $\cS$ are self-dual, so $K_0(\cS)$ is commutative. Its two quotients at $\eps=1$ and $\eps=-1$ are respectively
\[
 R=K_0(\cC),\qquad \widetilde R=K_0(\cS)/(\eps+1).
\]
Choose lifts of $X,Y$ and denote their images in $\widetilde R$ by $\widetilde X,\widetilde Y$. Then $\widetilde R=K(\widetilde k,\widetilde l,\widetilde m,\widetilde n)$, with the same multiplication formulas and associativity relation as above, but with integer, possibly negative, coefficients. For each corresponding pair of coefficients $a,\widetilde a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed}
 |\widetilde a|\leq a,\qquad \widetilde a\equiv a\pmod 2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $a$ is the sum and $\widetilde a$ the difference of the two lift multiplicities. Changing the chosen lift changes the sign of the corresponding basis element.

The coefficient-of-$1$ functional $\tau$ on $\widetilde R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthonormal}
 \tau(\widetilde X_i\widetilde X_j)=\delta_{ij},
 \qquad (\widetilde X_0,\widetilde X_1,\widetilde X_2)=(1,\widetilde X,\widetilde Y).
\end{equation}
For example, $X_+^2$ contains $\one$ once and does not contain $\eps$, since $X_+$ is self-dual and $\eps X_+\not\cong X_+$. Thus taking the signed quotient preserves this pairing.

\subsection{The general categorical input}

For a spherical fusion category $\cD$, write $F:Z(\cD)\to\cD$
for the forgetful functor from its Drinfeld center and $I$ for its right adjoint. If its
Grothendieck ring is commutative, central induction gives a bijection between its ring characters
$\chi$ and the simple summands $A_\chi$ of $I(\one)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:induction-codegrees}
 I(\one)=\bigoplus_\chi A_\chi,\qquad
 \dim(A_\chi)=\frac{\dim(\cD)}{f_\chi},\qquad
 \theta_{A_\chi}=1.
\end{equation}
These are the commutative-ring cases of Ostrik's central-induction
and trivial-twist theorems \cite[Theorems~2.5 and~2.13]{Ostrik}.
Two consequences will be used repeatedly. First,
$\dim(\cD)/f_\chi$ is an algebraic integer, because it is an object
dimension. Second, every $f_\chi$ belongs to the field of simple
object dimensions: the forgetful functor preserves spherical dimensions,
so both the numerator and denominator in
$f_\chi=\dim(\cD)/\dim(F(A_\chi))$ belong to that field.
These are \cite[Corollaries~2.14--2.15]{Ostrik}.

For a fusion category without a specified pivotal structure, every
formal codegree still divides its global dimension
\cite[Corollary~2.14]{Ostrik}.

\subsection{The dimension field in the cubic case}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:field}
Suppose that $R$ is cubic. The dimension field $L$ of $\cS$ is a totally real cyclic cubic field, and the dimension homomorphism identifies $\widetilde R\otimes\Q$ with $L$. Set
\[
 x=\dim(X_+),\qquad y=\dim(Y_+),\qquad
 \delta=1+x^2+y^2=\dim(\cC),
\]
so that $\dim(\cS)=2\delta$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-relations}
 \Tr_{L/\Q}(1/\delta)=1,\qquad
 \Tr_{L/\Q}(x/\delta)=\Tr_{L/\Q}(y/\delta)=0.
\end{equation}
The formal codegrees of $\widetilde R$ are the three conjugates $g_i$ of $\delta$, counted with multiplicity. Write
\[
 \prod_i(t-g_i)=t^3-\widetilde b t^2+\widetilde c t-\widetilde c.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 0<\widetilde b\leq b,\qquad
 \frac{\widetilde c}{c}\in\Z_{>0},\qquad
 0<\frac{\widetilde b}{\widetilde c}\leq\frac bc.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v}
 v:=\sum_i g_i^{-2}=1-\frac{2\widetilde b}{\widetilde c}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The dimension homomorphism factors through $\widetilde R\otimes\Q$, so its image is a field of degree at most three. The formal codegrees of $\cS$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six-codegrees}
 2f_1,2f_2,2f_3,2g_1,2g_2,2g_3:
\end{equation}
characters with $\eps=1$ come from $R$, and those with $\eps=-1$ come from $\widetilde R$; the two lifts double the sum defining each codegree. Every formal codegree of a spherical category lies in its dimension field \cite[Corollary~2.15]{Ostrik}. Therefore $L$ contains all three roots of the irreducible cubic $p$. It has degree exactly three and is its splitting field. Spherical dimensions are totally real \cite[Corollary~2.10]{ENO}, so $L$ is totally real. The map $\widetilde R\otimes\Q\to L$ is now an isomorphism.

Apply the regular-trace calculation \eqref{eq:casimir-trace} to the basis $1,x,y$, whose pairing is \eqref{eq:orthonormal} and whose Casimir element is $\delta$. This gives \eqref{eq:trace-relations}. The embeddings of $L$ are precisely the three characters of the signed algebra, and its Casimir element has image $\delta$; hence its codegrees are $g_i=\sigma_i(\delta)>0$. Their reciprocal sum is one, giving the displayed polynomial and \eqref{eq:v}.

Formula \eqref{eq:b}, valid also for the signed coefficients, gives
\[
 \widetilde b=7+3\widetilde k^2+3\widetilde l^2+
                    \widetilde m^2+\widetilde n^2\leq b.
\]
Finally, formal codegrees divide the global dimension even without a pivotal structure \cite[Corollary~2.14]{Ostrik}. Thus $\delta/f_1$ is an algebraic integer in $L$. Taking its field norm yields
\[
 N_{L/\Q}(\delta/f_1)=\frac{\widetilde c}{c}\in\Z_{>0}.
\]
The last inequality in \eqref{eq:comparison} follows.
\end{proof}


\section{Central induction and twist moments}\label{sec:central}


Let $F:Z(\cD)\to\cD$ be the forgetful functor for a spherical
fusion category $\cD$, and let $I:\cD\to Z(\cD)$ be its right adjoint. We use the induction formula \cite[Proposition~2.4]{Ostrik}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FI}
 FI(V)\cong\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr\cD}W\otimes V\otimes W^*.
\end{equation}
Together with adjunction, this identifies
$[I(V):A]=[F(A):V]$ and
$\dim_{\C}\operatorname{End}(I(V))=[FI(V):V]$.
Write $d=\dim(\cD)$, and for $A\in\Irr Z(\cD)$ put $a_A=\dim(A)$ and let $\theta_A$ be its twist. The numbers $a_A$ are real, and the twists are roots of unity. We use the identities
\begin{align}
 \sum_A a_A^2&=d^2,\label{eq:mass}\\
 \sum_A a_A^2\theta_A^n
   &=d\sum_{V\in\Irr\cD}\dim(V)\nu_n(V)\qquad(n\geq1),\label{eq:moments}
\end{align}
where $\nu_n$ is the $n$th Frobenius--Schur indicator for the specified pivotal structure. To see \eqref{eq:moments}, multiply the Ng--Schauenburg identity
\[
 \tr(\theta^n|_{I(V)})=d\nu_n(V)
\]
by $\dim(V)$ and sum over $V$. Adjunction and sphericality of the forgetful functor give
$a_A=\sum_V[I(V):A]\dim(V)$, yielding the formula. The indicator identity is \cite[Theorem~4.1]{NSexp}, and \eqref{eq:mass} is Müger's dimension formula for the center \cite{Muger}. Also,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclo}
 \nu_n(V)\in\Q(\zeta_n),
\end{equation}
because the cyclic operator defining the indicator has $n$th power equal to the identity \cite[Theorem~5.1]{NSind}. The trace identity uses sphericality. The cyclotomicity assertion holds in any pivotal category.

Both estimates below use the following observation. If $\mu$ is a
finite positive measure on the unit circle $\T$ and $q$ is a polynomial,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom}
  |q(1)|^2\mu(\{1\})\leq\int_{\T}|q(z)|^2\,d\mu(z).
\end{equation}
The right side depends only on the moments through degree $\deg q$.

\paragraph{The central sign.}
The equivariantization model of $\cS$ gives a central copy of
$\Rep(C_2)$ whose sign representation lifts $\eps$
\cite[Definition~5.13 and Corollary~5.15]{Bartlett}. Its underlying
fusion subcategory has the usual tensor product of representations,
with pivotal map on the sign equal to minus its usual pivotal map
because $\dim(\eps)=-1$. For even $n$, cyclic rotation on the line
$\Hom(\one,\eps^{\otimes n})$ is the ordinary rotation multiplied
by this inverse pivotal scalar. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps-ind}
  \nu_{2j}(\eps)=-1\qquad(j\geq1).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[A forced squared-twist sector]\label{lem:sector}
In the cubic situation of Lemma~\ref{lem:field}, the simple constituents of $I(\one)$ and $I(\eps)$ are twelve distinct simple objects of $Z(\cS)$. Their squared twists are all one, and the sum of their squared dimensions is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-mass}
 2\delta^2(u+v).
\end{equation}
The same statement holds after every embedding of $L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $K_0(\cS)$ is commutative, $I(\one)$ is multiplicity-free. The formal-codegree theorem \cite[Theorem~2.13]{Ostrik} and \eqref{eq:six-codegrees} give its six simple constituent dimensions as
\[
 \frac{\delta}{f_1},\frac{\delta}{f_2},\frac{\delta}{f_3},
 \frac{\delta}{g_1},\frac{\delta}{g_2},\frac{\delta}{g_3}.
\]
They are positive under every real embedding and their squared sum is $\delta^2(u+v)$. Their twists are one \cite[Theorem~2.5]{Ostrik}.

Write $\widehat\eps$ for the canonical central lift of $\eps$. The projection formula gives $I(\eps)\cong\widehat\eps\otimes I(\one)$. Thus its six constituents have the opposite, negative dimensions, and are disjoint from those of $I(\one)$. The indicator identity gives
$\tr(\theta^2|_{I(\eps)})=-2\delta=\dim I(\eps)$.
All six constituent dimensions are negative, so their absolute values
sum to $2\delta$. Equality in the triangle inequality forces each
squared twist to equal one. Their squared dimensions give the second
copy of $\delta^2(u+v)$ in \eqref{eq:known-mass}.
\end{proof}


\section{The cubic case}\label{sec:cubic}
The following estimate is the step that treats an arbitrary
categorification of a cubic ring. It will force $b\leq16$, so its final
arithmetic consequence can be read off without a search.



\begin{proposition}[Cubic codegree bound]\label{prop:bound}
If $K(k,l,m,n)$ is cubic and admits a complex fusion categorification, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
 c\leq\frac72 b.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the notation of Lemmas~\ref{lem:field}--\ref{lem:sector}, and put
\[
 M=\Q(\zeta_{120}).
\]
The fields $L$ and $M$ are linearly disjoint: $L/\Q$ is Galois of degree $3$, whereas $[M:\Q]=\varphi(120)=32$. Choose three automorphisms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$ of $\overline\Q$ restricting to the three embeddings of $L$ and fixing $M$ pointwise. Fix these choices for the rest of the proof. In particular, they fix every indicator of each of the orders
\[
 2,4,6,8,10,12,
\]
by \eqref{eq:cyclo}. The same choices work for all six orders, since each divides $120$. We apply these automorphisms only to algebraic scalar identities; no choice of a field of definition for the associators is needed.

On the unit circle $\T$, define the finite positive measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure}
 \mu=\sum_{i=1}^3\ \sum_{A\in\Irr Z(\cS)}
       \frac{\sigma_i(a_A)^2}{4\sigma_i(\delta)^2}
       \Dirac_{\sigma_i(\theta_A)^2}.
\end{equation}
Here $\Dirac_z$ is unit point mass at $z$. Every central dimension belongs to $L$, since the forgetful functor is spherical. Its conjugates are real, and $\sigma_i(\delta)>0$. Thus the coefficients in \eqref{eq:measure} are nonnegative. Twists and their conjugates are roots of unity, so the support is contained in $\T$. Equation~\eqref{eq:mass} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m0}
 \mu(\T)=3.
\end{equation}

For $1\leq j\leq6$, apply $\sigma_i$ to \eqref{eq:moments}, with $n=2j$, and divide by $4\sigma_i(\delta)^2$. Since the indicators are fixed, summing over $i$ gives
\begin{align}
 \int_{\T}z^j\,d\mu(z)
 &=\frac12\sum_{V\in\Irr\cS}\nu_{2j}(V)
       \Tr_{L/\Q}\left(\frac{\dim(V)}{\delta}\right)\notag\\
 &=\frac12\bigl(\nu_{2j}(\one)-\nu_{2j}(\eps)\bigr)=1.
 \label{eq:averaged}
\end{align}
For the second equality, the four non-unit lifts have dimensions $\pm x,\pm y$ and vanish under the weighted trace by \eqref{eq:trace-relations}; the traces for $\one$ and $\eps$ are $1$ and $-1$. The last equality uses \eqref{eq:eps-ind} and $\nu_n(\one)=1$. This step does not require knowing any indicator of $X_\pm$ or $Y_\pm$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:sector} forces the lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom-lower}
 \mu(\{1\})\geq\frac32(u+v).
\end{equation}
Indeed, in each of the three summands defining $\mu$, those twelve objects contribute $(u+v)/2$ at one. The sums $u,v$ are rational and hence independent of the embedding.

Apply \eqref{eq:atom} to the Fejér polynomial
\[
 P(z)=|1+z+\cdots+z^6|^2
     =7+\sum_{j=1}^6(7-j)(z^j+z^{-j})\qquad(z\in\T).
\]
Its value at one is $49$. Since the negative moments are the complex conjugates of the positive ones, \eqref{eq:m0} and \eqref{eq:averaged} imply
\[
 49\mu(\{1\})\leq\int_{\T}P(z)\,d\mu(z)
   =7\cdot3+2\sum_{j=1}^6(7-j)=63.
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:atom-lower} yields
\[
 u+v\leq\frac67.
\]
Substitute \eqref{eq:inverse-square} and \eqref{eq:v} to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-ratios}
 \frac bc+\frac{\widetilde b}{\widetilde c}\geq\frac47.
\end{equation}
But \eqref{eq:comparison} bounds the second ratio by the first. Therefore $b/c\geq2/7$, which is \eqref{eq:bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The role of the six indicators]\label{rem:six}
The moment argument uses only the fields containing the indicators, not their values, signs or sizes. Its atom bound is sharp for the stated moment information: the measure with mass $9/7$ at $1$ and mass $2/7$ at each nontrivial seventh root has mass $3$ and first six moments $1$. The choice of order twelve is also natural for field disjointness. At order fourteen, $\Q(\zeta_{14})$ already contains a cubic field, so the degree argument used above cannot be continued uniformly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The only cubic ring and its spherical structure}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cubic-classification}
A cubic rank-three fusion ring admits a complex fusion categorification only if it is isomorphic to $K(1,1,1,0)$. Every categorification of this ring admits a spherical structure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Combine \eqref{eq:lower} and Proposition~\ref{prop:bound}:
\[
 4b-8\leq c\leq\frac72b,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad b\leq16.
\]
If one of $k,l$ is zero, interchange $X,Y$ so that $l=0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:assoc} becomes $k(k-n)=1$, giving $k=1,n=0$. The character $X\mapsto0$, $Y\mapsto-1$ then has formal codegree $2$, contrary to irreducibility of $p$. Thus $k,l\geq1$.

By \eqref{eq:b},
\[
 3(k^2+l^2)+m^2+n^2\leq9.
\]
This forces $k=l=1$. Associativity now gives $m+n=1$. The two resulting choices are interchanged by exchanging $X$ and $Y$, so the ring is $K(1,1,1,0)$.

It remains to descend sphericality to the given categorification, rather than merely exhibit a different spherical categorification. For $K(1,1,1,0)$, \eqref{eq:signed} gives
\[
 \widetilde k,\widetilde l,\widetilde m\in\{1,-1\},
 \qquad\widetilde n=0.
\]
Signed associativity implies $\widetilde l\widetilde m=1$. Replacing $\widetilde X$ by $\widetilde l\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$ by $\widetilde k\widetilde Y$ makes all four signed parameters equal to the original ones. For these choices of lifts, each signed multiplicity equals the corresponding unsigned multiplicity. Since these are respectively the difference and sum of the two lift multiplicities, the unwanted lift has multiplicity zero in every product. The three chosen lifts therefore form a fusion subcategory of $\cS$.

The restriction of $\pi$ to this subcategory is a tensor equivalence with $\cC$: it is bijective on simple objects and fully faithful. Transporting its spherical structure gives a spherical structure on $\cC$. This is also the section criterion of \cite[Section~5.1(c)]{Ostrik}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Spherical descent in the remaining cases}\label{sec:quadratic}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:spherical}
Every complex fusion category of rank three admits a spherical structure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pointed case is weakly integral. For a self-dual ring, the
Frobenius--Perron dimension of the category is a formal codegree.
If $p$ splits over $\Q$, this dimension is an integer and again the
category is weakly integral. The canonical spherical structure exists
in both cases \cite[Propositions~8.23--8.24]{ENO}. If $p$ has an
irreducible quadratic factor, Ostrik's descent theorem
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{Ostrik} gives a spherical structure on the original
category, with no pivotality hypothesis. Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-classification}
handles the irreducible cubic.
\end{proof}

\section{A two-moment test for the five rings}\label{sec:reducible-rings}

Let $K(k,l,m,n)$ be the ring of a spherical rank-three category. Put
$x=\FPdim(X)$, $y=\FPdim(Y)$ and $D=1+x^2+y^2$.
We may use these positive dimensions in the twist identities, as in
\cite[Lemma~3.4]{Ostrik}. Indeed, if $D$ is an integer, use the
canonical positive spherical structure \cite[Propositions~8.23--8.24]{ENO}.
Otherwise the spherical dimension field contains the irrational
codegree $D$ by \cite[Corollary~2.15]{Ostrik}. The spherical and
Frobenius--Perron dimension characters therefore both have Galois
orbits of size at least two. Among three characters there is room for
only one such orbit. An automorphism carrying the first character to
the second carries every central dimension to the positive dimension
of its underlying object and every twist to another root of unity.
We use these conjugated scalar identities below.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:second-bound}
For $u=\sum_i f_i^{-2}$ as in \eqref{eq:inverse-square},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-bound}
  100u\leq 34+\frac{66+18(x+y)}{D}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Give $\theta_A$ mass $a_A^2/D^2$ for each central simple $A$.
By \eqref{eq:mass} this is a probability measure. The constituents
of $I(\one)$ supply mass $u$ at one by \eqref{eq:induction-codegrees}.
Its first moment is $1/D$ by \eqref{eq:moments}, since
$\nu_1(V)=[V:\one]$. Its second moment is real and at most
$(1+x+y)/D$: the second indicator of a self-dual simple is $1$ or
$-1$, the trace of an involution on $\Hom(\one,V^{\otimes 2})$.
Use $q(z)=3+4z+3z^2$ in \eqref{eq:atom}. On the unit circle,
\[
 |q(z)|^2=34+24(z+z^{-1})+9(z^2+z^{-2}).
\]
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:second-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Sphericality follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:spherical}. The non-self-dual
case gives $\Z[C_3]$. For a self-dual ring, we first show that
$b\geq22$ and $D\geq16$ cannot hold together. By \eqref{eq:lower}
and \eqref{eq:inverse-square},
\[
 u\geq\frac{b-4}{2b-4}\geq\frac9{20}\qquad(b\geq22).
\]
This gives $100u-34\geq11$. But $x+y\leq\sqrt{2(D-1)}$,
so for $D\geq16$ the right side of \eqref{eq:second-bound} minus
$34$ is smaller than $66/16+18/\sqrt8<11$.

If $k,l>0$ and $\max(k,l)\geq2$, \eqref{eq:b} gives $b\geq22$.
From $xy=kx+ly$ we obtain $x>l$, $y>k$ and $(x-l)(y-k)=kl$. Hence
\[
 x+y\geq(\sqrt k+\sqrt l)^2\geq3+2\sqrt2>11/2,
 \qquad D\geq1+\tfrac12(x+y)^2>16.
\]
This is impossible. The choice $k=l=1$ forces $m+n=1$ and gives
$K(1,1,1,0)$ after interchanging $X,Y$.

If $kl=0$, arrange $l=0$. Then \eqref{eq:assoc} forces $k=1,n=0$,
so $y=1$, $x=(m+\sqrt{m^2+8})/2$, $D=2+x^2$ and
\[
 b=10+m^2,\qquad c=4b-8,\qquad u=\frac{b-4}{2b-4}.
\]
As $m$ increases, $u$ increases and $(84+18x)/(2+x^2)$ decreases:
the latter has derivative of sign $36-168x-18x^2<0$ for $x\geq1$.
It therefore suffices to test $m=3$ to exclude all $m\geq3$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:second-bound} would give
\[
 \frac{15}{34}\leq \frac{11}{20}-\frac{9\sqrt{17}}{340},
 \qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad 9\sqrt{17}\leq37.
\]
This fails since $81\cdot17=1377>1369=37^2$. This endpoint explains
the coefficients in Proposition~\ref{prop:second-bound}: among
$1+az+z^2$ with $a\geq0$, the two-moment upper bound for the atom
there is minimized at $a=4/3$.

This leaves $m=0,1,2$. The unitary models in Table~\ref{tab:models}
realize each surviving ring. The three near-group rings have two
invertible basis elements and are distinguished by $m$. The pointed
ring has three invertibles and the even $SU(2)_5$ ring has one.
\end{proof}
