\begin{abstract}
Ostrik classified fusion categories over $\mathbb C$ with two simple
objects. We recover the two possible Grothendieck rings from
sphericalization and central induction. For a candidate rule
$X^2=1+nX$, the dimension divisibility of Etingof, Nikshych and Ostrik
forces one lift of $X$ to satisfy the same rule in the sphericalization.
The induction matrix then expresses $-n\dim(X)$ as the sum of two roots
of unity. Taking a conjugate gives $n\leq 1$. Both rings occur.
\end{abstract}

\section{The classification}

A complex fusion category is a rigid semisimple $\C$-linear tensor
category with simple unit $\one$, finite-dimensional morphism spaces and
finitely many simple objects up to isomorphism. Their number is the
\emph{rank}. The simple classes form the distinguished basis of the
Grothendieck ring. All ring isomorphisms below preserve the unit and
this basis. In rank one the ring is $\Z$. In rank two duality fixes
the only nontrivial simple $X$, so rigidity gives
$[X\otimes X:\one]=1$. Thus the possible based rings are
\[
 \Kn_n=\Z[X]/(X^2-1-nX),\qquad n\in\Z_{\geq0},
 \qquad\text{with basis }\{1,X\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Ostrik {\cite{OstrikRank2}}]\label{thm:main}
The Grothendieck rings of complex fusion categories of rank two are
precisely $\Kn_0$ and $\Kn_1$. Every such category admits a spherical
structure.
\end{theorem}

The two rings have different origins. The simple $X$ in $\Kn_0$ is
invertible, as in the category of $\Z/2\Z$-graded vector spaces.
In $\Kn_1$ it is the noninvertible simple of the Fibonacci category.
Its Galois conjugate, the Yang--Lee category, has the same fusion rule.
In their work on rational conformal field theory, Moore and Seiberg
studied the consistency equations underlying the surviving tensor
structures \cite{MooreSeiberg}. Ostrik credits them with the
classification for $n=0,1$ and proved the exclusion for all $n\geq2$.
His proof obtains a braiding on the category and, when $n>0$, uses its
modularity \cite[Sections~2.1--2.3]{OstrikRank2}.

Thornton subsequently proved
that a fusion category with exactly one noninvertible simple object
admits a spherical structure \cite[Theorem~1.3]{Thornton}.
Ostrik observed that his lower bound on global dimensions already
excludes $n\geq2$ once sphericality is given
\cite[Example~4.1.2]{OstrikDimensions}. Here a single rational
divisibility proves sphericality, and the two rows of the central
induction matrix give the exclusion directly.

\section{Choosing a spherical lift}\label{sec:spherical}

A pivotal structure identifies the identity and double-dual functors
by a monoidal natural isomorphism. It is \emph{spherical} if its left
and right traces coincide. The resulting dimension is additive and
multiplicative, has equal values on dual objects, and is nonzero on
simples. The Frobenius--Perron dimension $\FPdim$ is the unique positive
dimension character on the fusion ring.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:spherical}
If $K_0(\cC)=\Kn_n$, the sphericalization of $\cC$ contains a
spherical fusion subcategory mapped equivalently onto $\cC$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the sphericalization $\pi:\cS\to\cC$, each simple has two lifts,
interchanged by tensoring with a central invertible object
$\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon^2=\one$ and $\dim(\varepsilon)=-1$
\cite[Remark~3.1 and Proposition~5.14]{ENO}. Let $x$ be a lift of
$X$ and $d=\dim(x)$. The two lifts of $X$ have distinct dimensions
$d,-d$, so $x^*=x$. Consequently $x^2$ contains the unit once. Its
image under $\pi$ contains the unit only once, so $\varepsilon$ does
not occur. In $K_0(\cS)$ we have
\[
 x^2=1+a x+b\varepsilon x,\qquad a+b=n,\qquad
 d^2=1+md,\quad m=a-b .
\]
Write $u$ for the positive root of $u^2=1+nu$. Both lifts of $X$
have Frobenius--Perron dimension $u$, since $\FPdim(\varepsilon)=1$.
The global dimension of $\cS$ is the sum of the squared spherical
dimensions of its four simples. Dividing by the corresponding sum of
squared Frobenius--Perron dimensions gives the algebraic integer
\cite[Proposition~8.22]{ENO}
\[
  \alpha=\frac{\dim(\cS)}{\FPdim(\cS)}
         =\frac{1+d^2}{1+u^2}.
\]
The quadratics for $d$ and $u$ have constant term $-1$, so both roots
are algebraic units. Also $d-d^{-1}=m$ and $u-u^{-1}=n$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisibility}
 \left(\frac{u}{d}\alpha\right)^2
   =\left(\frac{d+d^{-1}}{u+u^{-1}}\right)^2
   =\frac{m^2+4}{n^2+4}.
\end{equation}
Since $|m|\leq n$, the right side is a rational algebraic integer in $(0,1]$, and thus
equals $1$. We obtain $|a-b|=a+b$, so $ab=0$. Interchanging $x$
and $\varepsilon x$ if necessary gives $x^2=1+nx$, including when
$n=0$. The full additive subcategory on $\one,x$ is closed under
products, summands and duals. The restriction of $\pi$ is bijective
on simple classes and induces isomorphisms between their morphism
spaces, hence is a tensor equivalence. It transports the spherical
structure to $\cC$.
\end{proof}

\section{Two roots of unity}\label{sec:center}

Fix the spherical structure obtained above and put $d=\dim(X)$, so
$d^2=1+nd$. The Drinfeld center $\cZ(\cC)$ consists of objects
equipped with a coherent half-braiding. Let $F:\cZ(\cC)\to\cC$ be
the forgetful functor and $I$ its right adjoint. The induction formula
of Etingof, Nikshych and Ostrik \cite[Proposition~5.4]{ENO} reads
$FI(V)\cong V\oplus(X\otimes V\otimes X)$ here. Set
$M_{V,B}=[F(B):V]$, with $V=\one,X$ and $B$ ranging over the
central simples. Adjunction identifies $M_{V,B}$ with $[I(V):B]$.
The matrix of $FI$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 M M^{\mathsf T}=
 \begin{pmatrix}2&n\\ n&n^2+2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
This is the induction-matrix method used more generally by Morrison
and Walker \cite{MW}. In rank two the matrix is forced. The column of
the central unit is $(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. The first diagonal entry of
\eqref{eq:gram} leaves one more column with first entry $1$, and the
off-diagonal entry forces its second entry to be $n$. The remaining
second entries have squares summing to $2$. Since $F$ sends each
simple to a nonzero object, there are exactly two more columns, each
$(0,1)^{\mathsf T}$. We recover Ostrik's four central simples and their images
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{OstrikRank2}, with inductions given by the rows of $M$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 B&\one&A&B_1&B_2\\ \hline
 F(B)&\one&\one\oplus nX&X&X
\end{array}
\qquad
 I(\one)=\one\oplus A,\quad I(X)=nA\oplus B_1\oplus B_2 .
\]

The twist acts trivially on $I(\one)$ and has categorical trace zero
on $I(X)$ \cite[Theorem~2.5]{OstrikRank3}. Write $\theta_i$ for the
twist of $B_i$. Both are roots of unity
\cite[Theorem~5.5]{NS}. The dimensions in the center agree with
those of the underlying objects, so $\dim(A)=1+nd=d^2$ and
$\dim(B_i)=d$. It follows that
\[
 0=\Tr(\theta_{I(X)})=nd^2+(\theta_1+\theta_2)d,\qquad
 \theta_1+\theta_2=-nd .
\]
Every algebraic conjugate of a sum of two roots of unity has
absolute value at most $2$. For $n>0$, $t^2-nt-1$ is irreducible
over $\Q$ since neither possible rational root $1,-1$ is a root.
Its positive root $u=(n+\sqrt{n^2+4})/2$ is therefore a conjugate of
$d$. We conclude $nu\leq2$. Since $u>n$, this gives $n\leq1$.

To finish Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the trivial associator on
$\Z/2\Z$-graded vector spaces realizes $\Kn_0$. In the integer-spin
subcategory of $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $3$, the two
simple labels are $0,2$, and $V_2\otimes V_2\cong V_0\oplus V_2$
realizes $\Kn_1$. For the tensor structures on both rings see
\cite[Sections~2.4--2.5]{OstrikRank2}.
