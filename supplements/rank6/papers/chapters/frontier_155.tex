\subsection{The generalized near-group ring $R^\prime(4)$}
\label{sec:frontier-155}
Write the basis of $\RR{4}{155}$ as $1,h,g,hg,X,Y$, where the
invertibles form $C_2^2$, $h$ fixes $X,Y$, and $g$ exchanges them.
The ring belongs to the family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonselfdual-neargroup}
 Y=X^*,\qquad XY=1+h+m(X+Y),\qquad X^2=g+hg+m(X+Y),
\end{equation}
with $m=4$. The following uniform argument eliminates signed-table
search for this family.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonselfdual-neargroup-spherical}
Every complex fusion category with rules
\eqref{eq:nonselfdual-neargroup}, for $m\ge0$, is spherical and has a
pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the sphericalization choose the dimension-one lifts $k_+$ of the
invertibles, and choose $X_+$ and $Y_+=g_+X_+$. Their common nonzero
dimension $\delta$ forces
$h_+X_+=X_+$, $h_+Y_+=Y_+$ and $X_+^*=Y_+$.
The invertible part of the self-dual product $X_+Y_+$ is $1_++h_+$,
by reciprocity; hence
\[
 X_+Y_+=1_++h_++s(X_++Y_+)+t(X_-+Y_-),\qquad s+t=m.
\]
Thus $\delta^2=2+r\delta$ with $r=2(s-t)$; changing both lifts if
necessary gives $0\le r\le2m$. The squared-norm global dimension and
the Frobenius--Perron dimension are
\[
 d_r=8+r^2\pm r\sqrt{r^2+8},\qquad
 D_m=8+4m^2+2m\sqrt{4m^2+8}.
\]
The ratio $d_r/D_m$ is an algebraic integer
\cite[Proposition~8.22]{ENO}. For nonzero even $r$, $r^2+8$ is not a
square: $(v-r)(v+r)=8$ would force $r=1$. The same holds for $2m$.
When $m>0$, the compositum admits an automorphism negating both
quadratic roots (or only the denominator root when $r=0$).
Multiplying the ratio by its image gives the positive rational
algebraic integer
\[
 \frac{r^2+8}{4m^2+8}\le1.
\]
It equals one, so $r=2m$ and $t=0$. For $m=0$ this holds directly.
The lift $g_+$ commutes with $X_+,Y_+$, since both orders have the same
underlying simple and nonzero dimension. Therefore
$X_+^2=Y_+^2=g_+X_+Y_+$, and all products of the chosen lifts close
among them. They form a full spherical fusion subcategory projecting
tensor-equivalently onto the original category. Its noninvertible
dimension is $m\pm\sqrt{m^2+2}$; conjugating to the positive root
gives pseudo-unitarity.
\end{proof}

For $m=4$ the dimensions are
\[
 d=4+3\sqrt2,\qquad D=72+48\sqrt2.
\]
There is no nontrivial faithful grading: the adjoint subring is the
whole ring. The pointed subrings are its only proper nontrivial fusion
subrings. Thus neither a grading nor rank-three subring descent resolves
this case.

The retained central-induction certificate gives a finite necessary
domain of six profiles, reduced below to the single center rank $52$.
For clarity, its categorical reductions are as follows. Pointed
subcategory projection for all three involutions leaves one low frame
and indicators $(\nu_2(X),\nu_2(Y),\nu_2(h),\nu_2(g),\nu_2(hg))
=(0,0,1,-1,-1)$. The $1+h$ module action and stabilizer-rotation
criterion constrain the third indicators. Exact Gram, Galois, word
and balancing equations then force central lifts of $h,g,hg$.
The lift of $h$ is fermionic: if it were bosonic, de-equivariantization
would split the fixed object $X$ into dimensions $d/2$, whose norm
is $-1/2$, contrary to integrality. A lift of $g$ generates a semion
subcategory of the center, which splits off from the center.

In its modular centralizer the restricted adjoint algebra is
$1+A_1+A_2$. For its restriction matrix $V$, adjunction gives
$V^{\mathsf T}V=I+N_{A_1}+N_{A_2}$. The two low simples have opposite
monodromy charges against the fermion. Consequently their multiplication
matrices are the same-charge and opposite-charge blocks of
$V^{\mathsf T}V-I$. Exhausting charge counts, duality, pointed actions,
restriction products and ribbon balancing leaves the six profiles,
each with a forced pair of low fusion matrices.

\begin{proposition}[Fermion traces and odd Gauss sums]
\label{prop:155-centralizer-cuts}
The centralizer profiles $275,277,279,298,306$ cannot occur.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work in the positive spherical representative and normalize the central
semion twist to $i$. Write
\[
 d=4+3\sqrt2,\quad D=\dim\mathcal C=72+48\sqrt2,\quad
 b=2+4d=D/4=3\sqrt2d,\quad u=\zeta_8^{-1}.
\]
Split the center as $Z(\mathcal C)\simeq\mathrm{Semion}\boxtimes\mathcal B$;
let $f\in\mathcal B$ be its fermion. The packet and charge enumeration,
using Proposition~\ref{prop:central-packets-adjunction} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:graded-adjunction-splitting}, gives the following data.
Exponents in the last column denote multiplicities; signs denote
monodromy with $f$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr|l}
 \text{profile}&r_+&r_-&r_+-r_-&\text{odd dimensions}\\\hline
 275&16&14&2 &d^4,(2d)^8,b^2\\
 277&16&11&5 &d^4,(2d)^4,4d,b^2\\
 279&16&8 &8 &d^4,(4d)^2,b^2\\
 296&16&10&6 &d^2,(2d)^4,(3d)^2,b^2\\
 298&16&7 &9 &d^2,(3d)^2,4d,b^2\\
 306&16&6 &10&(3d)^4,b^2
\end{array}
\]
All packet orientations and charge counts are reconstructed by
\filename{verify_center_cuts.py}. In every row the two $b$ objects
have restrictions $1+h+2X+2Y$ and $g+gh+2X+2Y$ and twists $1,-i$.
Each is $f$-fixed, being unique in its restriction type.

Verlinde diagonalization gives
$\#\operatorname{Fix}(f)=\Tr(N_f)=r_+-r_-$. Balancing implies that a
fixed simple is odd, immediately excluding $298$ and $306$.
Even $f$-pairs have opposite twists and cancel in the Gauss sum.
Odd pairs have equal twists, as do dual simples. Since
$\tau^+(\mathcal B)=D/(1+i)$ and the $b$ pair contributes $b^2(1-i)$,
the other odd contributions, divided by $d^2$, sum to $-24u$.

For $275$, the $b$ pair accounts for all fixed points. The four
$d$ objects restrict to $X,X,Y,Y$; the free $f$-pairs and duality force
one common twist $\alpha$. The eight $2d$ objects form four pairs of
twists $\beta_j$. Thus
$\alpha+2\sum_j\beta_j=-6u$, making the root of unity $\alpha$ divisible
by $2$ in the ring of algebraic integers, a contradiction.
For $279$, duality gives the four $d$ twists as
$\alpha,\alpha,\beta,\beta$. If $\gamma,\delta$ are the $4d$ twists,
\[
 \alpha+\beta+8(\gamma+\delta)=-12u.
\]
The algebraic integer $(\alpha+\beta)/4$ has all conjugates of absolute
value at most $1/2$, so it vanishes. Then
$\gamma+\delta=-3u/2$ is not integral, again a contradiction.

For $277$, the $b$ pair and the unique $4d$ simple supply three of five
fixed points. Fixed points among the restrictions $X,X,Y,Y$ occur in
multiples of four, since duality commutes with $f$. Hence these four
are free with common twist $\alpha$; the $2d$ objects have two fixed
twists $\beta,\gamma$ and one free-pair twist $\delta$. Write
$\epsilon$ for the $4d$ twist. The Gauss equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:155-277-gauss}
 \alpha+\beta+\gamma+2\delta+4\epsilon=-6u.
\end{equation}

Use Galois symmetry in the full center, whose anomaly is trivial:
$\theta_{\widehat\sigma Z}=\theta_Z^{a^2}$
\cite[Theorem~II]{DLN}. Rational pointed eigenvalues preserve both
monodromy charges, and the signed Galois permutation commutes with
$N_f$, preserving fixedness. Pure dimensions $kd$ are preserved: when
$\sigma(\sqrt2)=-\sqrt2$,
$D/\sigma(D)=1+4d$ and $(1+4d)\sigma(d)=-d$;
the Galois dimension sign gives positive dimension $kd$.
Consequently $\alpha,\delta,\epsilon$ are fixed by every square power
and have orders dividing $24$. The pair $\beta,\gamma$ has square-power
orbit of size at most two. Cauchy's prime support is $\{2,3\}$, so their
orders divide $48$: the square subgroup modulo $2^a3^c$ has order
$2^{\max(a-3,0)}3^{\max(c-1,0)}$. If a $16$-part occurs, the nontrivial
square sends the root to its negative (also at order $48$), so
$\gamma=-\beta$. Their first and third sums vanish, allowing replacement
by an opposite pair of $24$th roots for the following equations.

The spherical center formula \cite[Theorem~4.1]{NS}, grouping
$Z\in\mathcal B$ with $gZ$, gives
\[
 \nu_3(X)=1+\frac{u}{6}S_3,\qquad
 S_3=\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3+2\delta^3+4\epsilon^3.
\]
Indeed, their numerator is
$d_Z\theta_Z^3([F(Z):X]-i[F(Z):Y])$; even $f$-pairs cancel,
the $b$ terms contribute $4b=D$, and $2d/D=1/(6\sqrt2)$.
Thus $|\nu_3(X)-1|\le3/2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilizer-rotation}
gives the complete four-dimensional rotation domain
$\{-2,-2\zeta_3,-2\zeta_3^2,1,\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2\}$.
Every value except $1$ is at distance at least $\sqrt3$ from $1$;
hence $S_3=0$.

Finally, the same verifier tests the $24^3$ choices of
$\alpha,\delta,\epsilon$ in
$\ZZ[t]/(t^8-t^4+1)$, looking up all unordered $\beta,\gamma$ sums.
Equation~\eqref{eq:155-277-gauss} has $30$ solutions and none has
$S_3=0$. This finite integer calculation completes the exclusion.
\end{proof}

Only profile $296$ remains, of center rank $52$. This is a necessary
condition, not a categorical construction. The semion factorization
applies to the center.

\begin{proposition}
If profile $296$ is categorifiable, normalize the central semion to twist
$i$ and put $\mathcal B=\langle g\rangle'\subset Z(\mathcal C)$.
Set $d=4+3\sqrt2$, $b=2+4d$, $u=\zeta_8^{-1}$ and $w=\zeta_3$.
The twist multiset of $\mathcal B$ is forced as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
 h\text{-charge}&\text{dimension}&\text{twists}\\\hline
 +&1&1,-1\\
 +&d&1,1,-1,-1\\
 +&2d&w,-w,w^2,-w^2\\
 +&1+2d&i,i,-i,-i\\
 +&1+4d&1,-1\\
 -&d&u,u\\
 -&2d&uw,uw,uw^2,uw^2\\
 -&3d&-u,-u\\
 -&b&1,-i
\end{array}
\]
Consequently $Z(\mathcal C)$ has rank $52$ and
$\operatorname{FSexp}(\mathcal C)=24$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The fermion trace is $16-10=6$. The six odd simples with unique charged
restriction columns exhaust the fixed points. Gauss summation gives
$\alpha+9\beta+4(\gamma+\delta)=-12u$ for the remaining odd twists.
The algebraic norm bound gives $\beta=-\alpha$; then
$|2\sigma(\alpha/u)-3|\le2$ for every Galois conjugate forces
$\alpha=u$ and $\{\gamma,\delta\}=\{uw,uw^2\}$.
Low balancing leaves the even twists $a,-a,a,-a$ at dimension $d$ and
$c,-c,e,-e$ at dimension $2d$.
Write $t=a^2$, $p=c^2$, $q=e^2$, $F=8+2t+4p+4q$.
For the self-dual low objects $A,L$ of dimensions $1+4d,b$, the known
fusion matrices give exactly
\[
 \nu_2(A)=\frac{|F|^2+36}{72},\qquad
 \nu_2(L)=1-\frac{\operatorname{Im}F}{6}.
\]
Since $|\operatorname{Im}F|\le10$ and $\operatorname{Re}F\ge-2$,
these indicators, each equal to $\pm1$, force $F=6$.
Thus $t+2(p+q)=-1$; the norm bound on the algebraic integer $(t+1)/2$
gives either $t=1$, $\{p,q\}=\{w,w^2\}$, or $t=-1$, $q=-p$.
The fourth rotation has dimension $34$ and half-turn trace $-2$, so
$\nu_4(X)\in2\mathbb Z+2i\mathbb Z$. But
\[
 \nu_4(X)=4+\frac{1+t^2+2p^2+2q^2}{3\sqrt2}
\]
lies within $\sqrt2$ of $4$, and hence equals $4$. The second branch
would require $2+4p^2=0$, an impossibility. This proves the table.
The center spectrum is this spectrum together with its multiple by $i$.
The accompanying exact verifier checks the moment identities and full
low balancing; no root-order cutoff is assumed. This remains a necessary
condition, not a realization or exclusion of profile $296$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is determined]
The unique retained profile and twist multiset do not determine complete
modular data. The remaining entries of $S$, the remaining fusion
matrices, and categorical existence are unknown. In particular, matching
the displayed $T$-spectrum alone would not identify a category.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Duality and indecomposability]
\label{prop:155-prime-centralizer}
In profile $296$, the four simples of dimension $1+2d$ form two dual
pairs. Only four residual duality assignments remain, giving respectively
$10,12,$ or $14$ self-dual simples in $\mathcal B$.
The modular category $\mathcal B$ is prime, and its rank-$16$ fermion
centralizer is a nonsplit supermodular category.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two dimension-$1+2d$ constituents of $I(1)$ correspond to the
characters $\phi_\pm$ with
$\phi_\pm(X)=\pm i\sqrt2$ and $\phi_\pm(Y)=\mp i\sqrt2$.
For either constituent $M$ and every center simple $Z$, ribbon duality
and the low-character formula give
\[
 S_{M^*,Z}=S_{M,Z^*}
   =d_M\phi_M(F(Z)^*).
\]
Thus duality exchanges these two character rows and hence the two
constituents, since $S$ is invertible. Removing their common semion
factor gives the dual pair of twist $-i$ in $\mathcal B$; their fermion
translates give the pair of twist $i$. All other dualities are forced
except on the two odd $2d$ pairs, of twists $uw$ and $uw^2$.
On each pair duality is either the identity or the transposition. The
ten already self-dual simples therefore acquire zero, two, or four more.

A nontrivial modular factorization of rank $26$ would have a rank-two
factor. Its nonunit simple has dimension $x$ satisfying
$x^2=1+rx$, $r\in\ZZ_{\ge0}$. If $x$ is irrational, its
$\QQ(\sqrt2)$-norm is $-1$, whereas the available norms are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
 x&1&d&2d&3d&1+2d&1+4d&b\\\hline
 N(x)&1&-2&-8&-18&9&1&36.
\end{array}
\]
If $x$ is rational, the same equation forces $x=1,r=0$. But
$\mathcal B$ has only one nonidentity invertible, its fermion, so its
rank-two pointed subcategory is degenerate. This proves primeness.
Finally, a splitting $\langle f\rangle'\simeq
\mathrm{sVec}\boxtimes\mathcal A$ would give a modular rank-eight
subcategory $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal B$. M\"uger decomposition
\cite{DGNO} would then force $8\mid26$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Near-group dimension comparison]
\label{prop:155-neargroup-dimensions}
If the Frobenius--Perron dimension of the center of a near-group
categorification of group order $n$ and multiplicity $m$ is a positive
rational multiple of $D^2$, then
\[
 (n,m)=(8,16)\quad\text{or}\quad(32,32).
\]
This is a necessary dimension comparison, not a construction or a
Morita equivalence.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\rho^2=n+m\rho$ and $\Delta=n+\rho^2$. Rational dimension is
impossible here. For the quadratic field trace and norm,
\[
 \frac{\Tr(\Delta^2)}{\sqrt{N(\Delta^2)}}
   =\frac{m^2}{n}+2,
 \qquad
 \frac{\Tr(D^2)}{\sqrt{N(D^2)}}=34.
\]
These quotients are unchanged by positive rational scaling, so
$m^2=32n$, or $m=8k,n=2k^2$ for a positive integer $k$.
Pass to a pseudo-unitary Galois representative using the two-dimension
reduction in Proposition~\ref{prop:inputs}(iv).
The formal-codegree argument of \cite[Proposition~3.3]{SchTwo} gives
$n\mid m$: the relevant induced simple has dimension
$1+(m/n)\rho$, whose restriction forces $m/n$ to be an integer.
This use of a Galois representative does not assume unitarity or
pseudo-unitarity of the original category. Hence $k\mid4$.
The case $(n,m)=(2,8)$ is excluded by
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{SchTwo}, leaving the two displayed possibilities.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[No braided Morita representative]
\label{prop:155-no-braided-morita}
A categorification of $\RR{4}{155}$ is not Morita equivalent to any
braided fusion category.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work with a pseudo-unitary Galois representative. Since the adjoint
subcategory is the whole category, the forgetful map on central
invertibles is injective. The central-induction certificate gives all four
central lifts, with twists $1,-1,i,-i$, up to complex conjugation.
Thus the center has no nonidentity invertible of twist one.

If a braided category $\mathcal M$ were Morita equivalent, it would
also be pseudo-unitary. The equivalence of centers preserves their
canonical positive ribbon structures. Every central invertible
forgetting to the unit of $\mathcal M$ has twist one, so this
forgetful map is injective. The image $a$ of the central semion has
its canonical lift supplied by the braiding of $\mathcal M$; uniqueness
forces that lift to be the semion itself. Hence $\langle a\rangle$
is nondegenerate, and braided decomposition gives
$\mathcal M\simeq\mathrm{Semion}\boxtimes\mathcal M_0$
\cite{DGNO}. Its center then contains the nonidentity twist-one
invertible $(a,a)$ from
$Z(\mathrm{Semion})\simeq\mathrm{Semion}\boxtimes\mathrm{Semion}^{\rm rev}$,
a contradiction. Galois conjugation preserves braidings and Morita
contexts, so the conclusion applies to the original category.
\end{proof}
