\subsection{The Ising subcategory at multiplicity eight}
\label{subsec:frontier-1660}
The ring $\RR{8}{1660}$ is self-dual. In the basis
$1,g,X,gX,\sigma,\sigma X$ its products are determined by
\[
 g^2=1,\quad g\sigma=\sigma,\quad\sigma^2=1+g,\qquad
 X^2=1+\bigl(3(1+g)+4\sigma\bigr)X,
\]
with commuting generators. Put $d=3+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6$.
Then $\FPdim X=d$ and $\FPdim\mathcal C=4(1+d^2)$.
The retained unrestricted sphericalization certificate reduces every
categorification to its pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate, without
assuming a pivotal structure. The signed reduction and actual-root
certificate in \filename{research/frontier/R6_8_1660/} also force
second indicators $(1,1,1,-1,1)$ on the nonunit basis objects.
The Ising subcategory gives the following small structural problem.

\begin{lemma}
For a fusion subcategory $\mathcal D\subset\mathcal C$, the category
$Z(\mathcal D)$ has a central lift, with reverse braiding, to
$Z(Z_{\mathcal D}(\mathcal C))$. In particular its Grothendieck ring
is central in that of the relative center.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use strict notation and write a relative-central object as $(X,\gamma)$,
where $\gamma_V:VX\to XV$ for $V\in\mathcal D$. For
$b=(Y,\beta)\in Z(\mathcal D)$, define its commutation with $(X,\gamma)$
by $\gamma_Y:YX\to XY$. To check that this is a relative-central morphism,
we must verify
\[
 (\gamma_Y\otimes1_V)(1_Y\otimes\gamma_V)(\beta_V\otimes1_X)
 =(1_X\otimes\beta_V)(\gamma_V\otimes1_Y)(1_V\otimes\gamma_Y).
\]
The two half-braiding identities rewrite this as naturality of $\gamma$
at the morphism $\beta_V:VY\to YV$. Naturality in relative-central objects
and tensor compatibility follow from their defining identities. On
$Z(\mathcal D)$ the resulting braiding is the reverse of its usual braiding.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Relative-center reconstruction]\label{prop:1660-relative-center}
Any categorification $\mathcal C$ of $\RR{8}{1660}$ has a rank-eighteen
relative center $\mathcal E=Z_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal C)$, where
$\mathcal I=\langle g,\sigma\rangle$ is Ising. Write
$B=K_0(Z(\mathcal I))\cong K_0(\mathcal I)\otimes K_0(\mathcal I)$ and
$e=g\otimes g$. Its Grothendieck ring has basis $b,bt$ for the nine
basis elements $b$ of $B$, with
\[
 tb=bt,\qquad t^2=L+At,\qquad L\in\{1,e\},\qquad A\in B_{\ge0},
 \qquad A=LA.
\]
The forgetful map on $B$ is $F(a\otimes b)=ab$, and
\[
 F(t)=X,\qquad F(A)=3(1+g)+4\sigma,\qquad t^*=Lt.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The complement $\mathcal M=\langle X,gX,\sigma X\rangle$ is an
$\mathcal I$-bimodule category. The functor $Y\mapsto YX$ is a left
module equivalence from $\mathcal I$ onto $\mathcal M$: it takes the
three simples bijectively to the three simples, or equivalently
$\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal I}(X)=1$.
Transport the right action to this regular left module. It becomes a
tensor autoequivalence of $\mathcal I^{\rm rev}$, since it fixes all
simple classes. Every tensor autoequivalence of an Ising category is
isomorphic to the identity \cite[Remark B.6(i)]{DGNO}. Thus
$\mathcal M$ is the regular bimodule, with $1$ corresponding to $X$.

Passing to bimodule centers gives a regular $Z(\mathcal I)$-module
$Z_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M)$ \cite[Definition 2.1 and Remark 2.2]{GNNrel}.
Consequently its simples are $bt$, and $\mathcal E$ has eighteen
simples. The lemma gives commutativity. The part of $t^2$ supported on
$Z(\mathcal I)$ forgets to $1$, and hence is exactly $1$ or $e$.
Frobenius reciprocity gives $t^*=Lt$. All simples of $B$ are self-dual;
taking duals of $t^2=L+At$ therefore gives $A=LA$. The remaining
statements follow by applying the forgetful functor.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An arithmetic relative-center domain]\label{prop:1660-arithmetic-domain}
Using the previously proved reduction to a pseudo-unitary
categorification, the above relative-center rings reduce to $58$
labelled alternatives.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The relative center inherits positive spherical dimensions from
$\mathcal C$. Its dimension and dimension field are
\[
 D=16(1+d^2)=576+384\sqrt2+320\sqrt3+224\sqrt6,
 \qquad K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).
\]
Its dimension has minimal polynomial
\[
 z^4-2304z^3+184320z^2-4718592z+37748736,
\]
whose constant term is $2^{22}3^2$.

The Ising character rows are $(1,1,\sqrt2)$, $(1,1,-\sqrt2)$ and
$(1,-1,0)$, with formal codegrees $4,4,2$. Thus for a character $\chi$
of $B$, let $f_B$ be the product of these codegrees and write
$a=\chi(A)=u+v\sqrt2$. If $\chi(L)=-1$, the identity $A=LA$ forces
$a=0$; its two extensions have formal codegree $2f_B$. If
$\chi(L)=1$, their formal codegrees are
\[
 f_\pm=\frac{f_B}{2}\left(4+a^2\pm a\sqrt{a^2+4}\right).
\]
Every $D/f_\pm$ is an algebraic integer and every $f_\pm$ belongs to
$K$ \cite[Corollaries 2.14--2.15]{Ostrik3}.

First, $f_+f_-=f_B^2(a^2+4)$ implies that
\[
 n(u,v)=(u^2+2v^2+4)^2-8u^2v^2
\]
has no prime divisor other than $2$ and $3$. Second, the primitive
minimal polynomial of $D/f_\pm$ must be monic. Finally, if $a\ne0$,
field membership implies $\sqrt{a^2+4}\in K$. A square root in
$K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\oplus\sqrt3\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ of an element of
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ lies in one of those two summands. Taking its
quadratic norm shows that $n(u,v)$ must be a square in $\mathbb Q$,
hence an integer square.

The finite domain is particularly small. If $z$ is the coefficient of
$\sigma\otimes\sigma$ in $A$, then $0\le z\le3$. The coefficients
of $1\otimes1,g\otimes g$ sum to $3-z$, those of
$1\otimes g,g\otimes1$ also sum to $3-z$, and the coefficients of the
four $\sigma$-supported simples sum to four. This gives $1050$ possible
$A$ for $L=1$ and six satisfying $A=eA$ for $L=e$.
The exact checker produces
\[
1056\xrightarrow{\text{prime norms}}158
\xrightarrow{D/f\text{ integral}}130
\xrightarrow{\text{dimension field}}58.
\]
\end{proof}

The following categorical conditions reduce this arithmetic domain further.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform indicator and central-lift constraints]
\label{prop:1660-uniform-indicators}
Every pseudo-unitary categorification of $\RR{8}{1660}$ has
\[
 \nu_2(g)=\nu_2(X)=\nu_2(gX)=\nu_2(\sigma X)=1,
 \qquad\nu_2(\sigma)=-1,
\]
and its center contains a unique simple restricting to $g$ alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We describe the finite certificate and the twist bound making it complete.
For a central restriction column $v=(v_0,\ldots,v_5)$ put
\[
 a(v)=v_0+v_1+v_2+v_3+2v_4-2v_5,
 \qquad b(v)=v_0+v_1-v_2-v_3+v_4+v_5.
\]
The saturated dimension-field characters of conductors $24$ and $12$
give $a(v)\ne0\Rightarrow\theta_v^{24}=1$ and
$b(v)\ne0\Rightarrow\theta_v^{12}=1$, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:saturated-conductor-support}.
A high column containing $\sigma$ has primitive order-$16$ twist and
$v_1=0$, by the Ising projection. Both displayed forms then vanish,
forcing $v_2+v_3=4v_4$ and $v_5=3v_4$.
The individual root-mass capacity gives $v_4^2/2\le1$, hence $v_4=1$.
If both forms vanish and $v_4=0$, then $v_5=2v_1$ and
$v_2+v_3=3v_1$; a nonzero high column has $v_1>0$ and twist $-1$.
Thus every actual twist has order dividing $48$.

The first three indicators are already $+1$ by odd multiplicities in
squares. Exhaust all four choices for the other two signs, all low
frames and all character assignments. There are two low frames, each
with two assignments, hence $16$ global systems. The equations use all
$48$ actual roots, induction Gram identities, joint Galois balance,
Ising root masses, low-product restriction constraints and cyclic
rotation moments. Thirteen exact integer separating vectors exclude
thirteen systems. Every one of the other three has the displayed
indicator pattern. This conclusion precedes any specialization to
individual high profiles.

For the central lift, bounded affine enumeration of the Gram, character,
Galois and cubic-word equations gives $371$ profiles in each assignment
of the first frame and $2011$ in each assignment of the second.
Every profile contains exactly one copy of the high vector
$(0,1,0,0,0,0)$. All domains are reconstructed by the retained exact
profile checker; the uniform multiplicity is checked on all $4764$
raw profiles. A simple with this restriction has dimension one and is
the required central invertible. The sources, separating vectors and
independent replay are indexed in
\filename{research/frontier/R6_8_1660/}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Second indicator with a central factor]
\label{lem:central-second-indicator}
Let $\mathcal A$ be pivotal, let $b$ be self-dual in $Z(\mathcal A)$
with simple underlying object, and let $t$ be self-dual and simple in
$\mathcal A$. If $bt$ is simple, then
$\nu_2(bt)=\nu_2(b)\nu_2(t)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use half-braidings $c_{b,x}:bx\to xb$. Choose a central self-duality
$f_b:b\to b^*$ and a self-duality $f_t:t\to t^*$, and put
$f=c_{b^*,t^*}(f_b\otimes f_t):bt\to(bt)^*$.
Dual compatibility gives $(c_{b^*,t^*})^*=c_{b^{**},t^{**}}$.
Writing $j$ for the pivotal structure, naturality and centrality of
$f_b$ give
\begin{align*}
 f^*j_{bt}
 &=(f_t^*j_t\otimes f_b^*j_b)c_{b,t}\\
 &=\nu_2(b)\nu_2(t)(f_t\otimes f_b)c_{b,t}
 =\nu_2(b)\nu_2(t)f.
\end{align*}
Since $bt$ is simple, this transpose scalar is its second indicator.
No half-braiding for $t$ is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A single relative-center ring]
\label{thm:1660-single-relative-ring}
Up to exchanging the Ising factors, the relative center of a
categorification of $\RR{8}{1660}$ must have
\[
 L=e=(g,g),\qquad
 A=2(\sigma,1)+2(\sigma,g)+3(\sigma,\sigma).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forgetful functor $F:\mathcal E\to\mathcal C$ is faithful and
pivotal. It preserves second indicators for a self-dual simple whose
image is simple: it preserves a nonzero self-duality and its transpose
scalar, both computed in one-dimensional Hom spaces.
If $L=1$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:central-second-indicator} to
$b=(\sigma,1)$ and $t$. Their images, and that of $bt$, are
$\sigma,X,\sigma X$. Proposition~\ref{prop:1660-uniform-indicators}
gives their signs $-1,1,1$, contradicting the lemma. Thus $L=e$.
Of the $58$ arithmetic alternatives, only three have $L=e$: the
stated coefficient, its factor exchange, and the all-ones coefficient
$A=\sum_{b\in\Irr Z(\mathcal I)}b$.

To exclude the last choice, realize $\mathcal E$ as the dual of
$\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal I^{\rm op}$ acting on $\mathcal C$ by
left and right tensor multiplication. Compatibility of a module
endofunctor with the right action is exactly its relative half-braiding.
Morita invariance therefore gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Inv}Z(\mathcal E)|
       =4|\operatorname{Inv}Z(\mathcal C)|.
\]
All four invertibles of $\mathcal E$ belong to $B$ and have canonical
central lifts. The forgetful map on central invertibles is surjective;
its kernel is the group of tensor automorphisms of the identity.
For the all-ones coefficient, $t^2$ contains $t$ and every $bt$.
A tensor automorphism's scalars therefore satisfy
$\lambda_t^2=\lambda_t=1$ and $\lambda_{bt}=\lambda_b=1$.
The kernel is trivial, so $|\operatorname{Inv}Z(\mathcal E)|=4$.
This contradicts the nontrivial central invertible of
Proposition~\ref{prop:1660-uniform-indicators}.
\end{proof}

The last theorem is a necessary reduction, not a categorification of
the remaining ring. Its universal grading is $C_2$: the first Ising
spin and $t$ have odd degree. The neutral component has nine simples.
Writing $p=(g,1)$, $q=(1,g)$, $s=(1,\sigma)$ and $Y=(\sigma,1)t$,
its additional rules are
\[
 pY=Y,\qquad Y^*=qY,\qquad
 Y^2=q(1+p)+\bigl(4(1+q)+6s\bigr)Y,
\]
together with $p^2=q^2=1$, $qs=s$ and $s^2=1+q$.

\subsubsection{Two exact fermionic cuts on the remaining profiles}

Write $d=3+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6$ and $D=4(1+d^2)$.
The previous actual-twist certificate leaves $1764$ labelled restriction
profiles, in branches of sizes $168,798,798$.
The following reduction uses the already forced central fermion and second
indicators. It makes no unitarity assumption and does not resolve the ring.

\begin{proposition}[Fermion profile reduction]\label{prop:1660-fermion-profiles}
The necessary restriction-profile domain for $\RR{8}{1660}$ reduces from
$1764$ to $1727$. Exactly $25$ profiles fail the fermion orbit, dimension,
and rank conditions below, and $12$ further profiles fail an integral
second-indicator congruence. The two excluded sets are disjoint.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work in the retained pseudo-unitary Galois form.
Let $\mathcal M=Z(\mathcal C)$, and let $h$ be its unique nontrivial
invertible, with $F(h)=g$ and $\theta_h=-1$.
For a simple $Z$, write $\epsilon_Z\in\{1,-1\}$ for its monodromy with
$h$. Balancing gives
\[
 \theta_{hZ}=-\epsilon_Z\theta_Z.
\]
Thus the even sector consists of free $h$-pairs. Write its number of
pairs as $p$, and write the odd sector as $s$ free pairs and $t$ fixed
simples. The Verlinde diagonalization of $N_h$ gives
\[
 t=\operatorname{Tr}(N_h)=\sum_Z\epsilon_Z=2p-2s-t,
 \qquad p=s+t.
\]
The two sectors each have dimension $D^2/2$.

The six low simples are the summands of $I(1)$ indexed by the six ring
characters. Their monodromies are the characters' values on $g$.
The first four are even, and their restrictions, in order
$(1,g,X,gX,\sigma,\sigma X)$, are
\[
 (1,0,0,0,0,0),\quad(1,0,3,3,0,4),\quad
 (1,0,1,1,0,2),\quad(1,0,2,2,0,2).
\]
They give four forced even pairs, whose high partners have the
$g$-translated restrictions and twist $-1$. The two other low simples
are odd. In low frame zero their distinct restrictions force an
$h$-transposition. In low frame one the computation conservatively
allows either a transposition or two fixed simples.

Remove the four forced high partners. Tensoring with $h$ exchanges the
$X$ and $gX$ coordinates of each remaining restriction vector, so
asymmetric vector types must occur in pairs with equal multiplicities.
All remaining high vectors have zero $g$ coordinate.
Let $E,O,T$ count the remaining even pairs, odd pairs, and odd fixed
simples, and let $N$ be the number of remaining high simples. Then
\[
 (O,T)=
 \begin{cases}
 (N-3E-3,\,4E+6-N),&\text{odd low transposition},\\
 (N-3E-2,\,4E+4-N),&\text{two fixed odd low simples}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The even dimension condition becomes
\[
 \sum_{\text{remaining even pairs}}\frac{d_Z^2}{d^2}
       =180+120\sqrt2.
\]
Here high vectors omit the unit coordinate. For a vector $(0,b,c,0,a)$, the summand is
$(b+c+a\sqrt2)^2$. Every $\sigma$-containing type has
$b+c=4$, $\sigma$ coefficient $1$, and $\sigma X$ coefficient $3$;
its summand is $36+24\sqrt2$.

There is also a pivotal parity condition. A self-duality of $Z$ with
indicator $\eta$ induces a nondegenerate form with the same transpose
sign on $F(Z)$. An odd multiplicity of a base simple of indicator
$\delta$ forces $\eta=\delta$. Hence a type with odd $\sigma$ multiplicity
and an odd positive-indicator multiplicity cannot be self-dual.
Its total multiplicity is even. Its number of even $h$-pairs is also
even: duality preserves twists, and therefore must pair distinct even
$h$-orbits, whose two twists are opposite. This does not assert that
$h$-fixed objects are self-dual.

A bounded integer calculation of these dimension and rank equations,
with these orbit and parity conditions, excludes $3,11,11$ profiles in
the three branches. A separate recursion over the three orbit counts
independently verifies every rejected low-action branch.

For the second cut, put $\tau_n(\mathcal M)=\sum_Zd_Z^2\theta_Z^n$.
The modular indicator formula \cite[Theorem~7.5]{NS} implies
\[
 \sum_{Z\in\operatorname{Irr}\mathcal M}\nu_2(Z)d_Z
    =\frac{\tau_2(\mathcal M)\tau_{-2}(\mathcal M)}{\dim\mathcal M}.
\]
Indeed, multiply
$\nu_2(Z)=(\dim\mathcal M)^{-1}
\sum_{A,B}N_{AB}^{Z}d_A d_B(\theta_A/\theta_B)^2$
by $d_Z$ and sum. The center identity \cite[Theorem~4.1]{NS} gives
$\tau_2(Z(\mathcal C))=D\sum_Xd_X\nu_2(X)$. Dimensions and second
indicators are real, and twists are roots of unity, so
$\tau_{-2}=\overline{\tau_2}=\tau_2$. As $\dim\mathcal M=D^2$,
\[
 \sum_Z\nu_2(Z)d_Z
   =\bigl(2-\sqrt2+(2+\sqrt2)d\bigr)^2.
\]
The four even low restrictions are unique, so their simples are self-dual;
the odd unit multiplicity forces indicator $1$, as for their $h$-partners.
The two odd low objects also have indicator
$1$. This is immediate in frame zero. In frame one their alternative
would be a dual pair: after removing the four even pairs, the displayed identity would
then require an additional term $(8\sqrt3+4\sqrt6)d$. Every remaining
possible nonzero indicator contribution divided by $d$ belongs to
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$; the $\sigma$-containing types have indicator zero.
This rules out that alternative.

After removing all these forced contributions, the identity reads
\[
 \sum_{\text{remaining high }Z}\nu_2(Z)\frac{d_Z}{d}
      =36+24\sqrt2.
\]
For a restriction type of multiplicity $n_v$, its aggregate indicator
$s_v$ satisfies $s_v\equiv n_v\pmod2$, since nonself-dual objects occur in dual
pairs. Also $\nu_2(hZ)=\nu_2(Z)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:central-invertible-indicators}, applied to the braided
lift of $h$ in $Z(\mathcal M)$, whose twist is $-1$.
Thus asymmetric $h$-paired vector types have equal aggregate indicators.
For each type $(0,b,c,0,a)$, let $q_v=b+c$ if it is $h$-invariant,
and $q_v=2(b+c)$ for an asymmetric pair. The rational coefficient is
$\sum_v q_vs_v$.
For each low-one labelling, profiles $1032,\ldots,1037$ force the
integers $2q_v$ to be divisible by $8$ and
$\sum_vq_vn_v\equiv0\pmod8$. The rational coefficient is therefore
$0$ modulo $8$, whereas
$36$ is $4$ modulo $8$. These six profiles in each labelling are excluded.

The verifier \filename{verify\_fermion\_profiles.py} in
\filename{research/frontier/R6_8_1660/fermion_profiles/} reconstructs the entire old frontier
from its hash-bound source files, checks all field identities by integer
arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, and records both disjoint
excluded sets in \filename{fermion\_profiles\_certificate.json}.
It uses no optimizer and retains both low-one actions. The remaining count is
$165+781+781=1727$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1660-no-braiding}
No categorification of $\RR{8}{1660}$ admits a braiding.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Its Ising subcategory would be nondegenerate, so braided decomposition
would give $\mathcal C\simeq\mathcal I\boxtimes\mathcal D$ with
$\rk\mathcal D=2$ \cite[Corollary~B.12 and Theorem~3.13]{DGNO}.
Write $z$ for the nonunit simple of $\mathcal D$; then
$z^2=1+nz$. The dimension multiset forces $\FPdim z=d$, so
$X$ corresponds to $1\boxtimes z$ or $g\boxtimes z$.
Its square cannot contain $\sigma\boxtimes z$, contrary to
$N_{XX}^{\sigma X}=4$.
\end{proof}
