# Independent audit: the C4 pointed-associator constraint

The argument in `research/model_continuation/order4_pointed_associator.md`
is valid. It is a necessary condition on a hypothetical categorification,
not by itself a noncategorifiability result. This audit separates the
categorical argument from its independently checked finite arithmetic.

## General categorical reduction

The tensor has invertibles `1,t,t²,t³`, and two self-dual noninvertibles
`rho,sigma=t rho`, fixed by `t²`. Their common Frobenius–Perron dimension
is `d=4+3 sqrt(2)`, satisfying `d²=2+8d`.
Thus it has exactly two distinct Frobenius–Perron dimensions.
Schopieray, *Categorification of integral group rings extended by one
dimension*, J. London Math. Soc. 108 (2023), 1617–1641, Lemma 3.1 in the
**published** version states that every categorification of such a ring
is Galois conjugate to a pseudo-unitary category. The relevant source is
https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12789, checked on 2026-09-29. Its earlier
arXiv version states the stronger pseudo-unitarity conclusion; that
stronger assertion is not used here. Galois conjugation preserves the
fusion tensor. Consequently it suffices to exclude a pseudo-unitary
categorification.

## Excluding the trivial pointed associator

Suppose the pointed subcategory has trivial associator class. Its regular
algebra is `A=1+t+t²+t³`. The simple right A-modules are classified, within
each right G-orbit of simple objects, by the irreducible projective
representations of the stabilizer. The invertible orbit has trivial
stabilizer and contributes A. The noninvertible orbit has stabilizer C2;
since `H²(C2,C×)=0`, it contributes exactly two simple modules `m+,m−`,
each with underlying object `rho+sigma`. Their normalized positive
module dimensions are therefore `(1,d/2,d/2)`.

Left multiplication by invertibles fixes A and permutes the other two
modules. Write `chi:G→{±1}` for the permutation character on their
difference. In particular, `chi(t²)=1`; no swap assumption is required.
Because rho is self-dual, its module action matrix is symmetric.
Free/forgetful adjunction gives its first row and column `(0,1,1)`.
The dimension equations force its other row sums to be 8, so

```text
M_rho = [[0,1,1], [1,a,8-a], [1,8-a,a]],   0 <= a <= 8.
```

On `(0,1,-1)` it has the integer eigenvalue `lambda=2a-8`.
The relation `rho²=1+t²+4rho+4sigma`, with `sigma=t rho`, then gives

`lambda² = 2 + 4(1+chi(t))lambda`.

For `chi(t)=-1`, this says `lambda²=2`; for `chi(t)=1`, its
discriminant is 72. Neither equation has an integer solution. Thus the
pointed associator cannot be trivial.

The same proof works uniformly for the family
`rho²=1+t²+m(rho+sigma)`: the row sum is `2m`, and the two equations are
`lambda²=2` and `lambda²-2m lambda-2=0`. The latter would require
`m²+2` to be a square, which never occurs for an integer `m>=0`.

## Identifying the remaining class

The stabilizer `<t²>` of rho acts on the one-simple-object additive
subcategory generated by rho. Coherence of that module action supplies
a two-cochain trivializing the restriction of the pointed associator.
Hence the associator restricts trivially to C2.

Represent `H³(C4,C×)=Z/4` by

`omega_k(a,b,c)=exp(2 pi i k a floor((b+c)/4)/4)`.

On `<t²>`, `omega_k(2,2,2)=(-1)^k`; therefore the restriction homomorphism
is reduction modulo 2. Its kernel is `{0,2}`. Since 0 was excluded, the
class is exactly 2. For the canonical positive pivotal structure,
the fourth indicator of `t^x` is the product
`prod_{j=0}^3 omega_k(x,jx mod 4,x)`. For `x=1,3` this is `i^k`.
Consequently `nu_4(t)=nu_4(t³)=-1`. A convention replacing the cocycle
by its inverse gives the same conclusion at class 2. Also
`nu_2(t²)=1`, because its pointed subcategory has trivial associator
and the positive pivotal structure.

## Central twist consequence and its scope

Work now in a pseudo-unitary categorification with its canonical positive
spherical structure. The induction indicator formula gives

`D nu_4(t)=sum_Z [F(Z):t] FPdim(Z) theta_Z^4`,

while the sum of the positive weights on the right is
`FPdim(I(t))=D`. Dividing by D expresses -1 as a convex combination of
unit complex numbers. Equality in the triangle inequality forces every
one of those numbers to equal -1. Thus every simple central object
whose restriction contains t, or t³, has `theta_Z^4=-1`.
Likewise a central object whose restriction contains `t²` has
`theta_Z²=1`.

This is also the pseudo-unitary case of the invertible-induced twist
argument in Schopieray, Proposition 3.8. The proof here explicitly uses
positivity; it does **not** silently replace signed spherical dimensions
by Frobenius–Perron dimensions. In a relative-trace implementation,
`theta_Z^4=-1` implies that the fourth normalized relative trace is
exactly `(-1,0)`. Deleting other joint trace types for such a column is
therefore sound. The family theorem and the arithmetic check do not
assert that the remaining trace system is infeasible.

## Reproduction

```sh
python research/round3/models/c4_associator_audit.py
python -O research/round3/models/c4_associator_audit.py
```

The script checks the input tensor identities, both possible module
permutation characters, all 256 cocycle identities in each of four
classes, their restrictions, and the fourth-indicator products. It uses
explicit exceptions rather than assertions. The module-category
classification and the spherical induction formula remain categorical
theorems, not claims of formal verification by this small script.

## Review of the frozen exclusion implementation

The subsequent `software/round3_smallest.py` and
`research/round3/smallest/theorem.tex` were also inspected. The family
recognizer reconstructs all 216 tensor entries under the identified
ordering `(1,t,t²,t³,X,Y)`. The second-indicator array omits the unit, so
its involution index `h−1` is correct. The high-column labels similarly
omit the unit, giving indices `t−1,t³−1`; the fourth joint trace has
zero-based index 3. The added Clifford slack variables are retained by
the deletion filter. The verifier recomputes the family and module data,
every low frame, every allowed second-indicator assignment, each joint
trace domain, the deletion, and the rational systems before checking rays.

The frozen R6_4_6 certificate contains ten low frames and four indicator
assignments for each, hence forty separating vectors. Its complete module
domain has one representation and forces the two four-dimensional cubic
spaces, giving the interval `[-2,1]`. The prime support is `{2,3}`. This
static review found no indexing or missing-branch issue; the separately
recorded optimizer-disabled replay supplies the arithmetic execution check.
