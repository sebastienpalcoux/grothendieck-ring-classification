\subsection{A positive-semidefinite column refinement}
\label{sec:psd-gram}
The rank-one columns in Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-gram} satisfy an
additional exact constraint. This sharpens the finite Gram systems
without introducing categorical assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one positive-semidefinite constraint]
\label{prop:psd-column}
Let $Q$ be a nonzero rational symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix, let $I$
index a maximal independent set of columns, and put $P=Q_{I,I}$.
A column $v$ can occur in a decomposition $Q=\sum_jv_jv_j^{\mathsf t}$
only if
\[
 v=Q_{:,I}P^{-1}v_I,\qquad v_I^{\mathsf t}P^{-1}v_I\leq1.
 \tag{$\ddagger$}\label{eq:psd-column}
\]
These two conditions are equivalent to
$Q-vv^{\mathsf t}\succeq0$. A nonpositive-semidefinite $Q$ admits
no such column decomposition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every summand has range contained in that of $Q$, giving the first
identity. Since $Q$ is positive semidefinite and its selected columns
are independent, $P$ is positive definite. Set $B=Q_{:,I}P^{-1}$.
The rank identity $Q=BPB^{\mathsf t}$ and $v=Bv_I$ reduce the
condition to $P-v_Iv_I^{\mathsf t}\succeq0$. Conjugating by
$P^{-1/2}$ gives $I-ww^{\mathsf t}\succeq0$, where
$w=P^{-1/2}v_I$, exactly the second inequality. These are rational
identities and inequalities: a verifier can clear denominators and
use positive principal minors, without numerical eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:psd-gram}
The four rings $R7\_4\_776$, $R7\_4\_927$, $R7\_4\_2126$, and
$R7\_3\_909$ have no categorification over $\C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:anti-module} gives a finite exact anti-module
system for each source tensor. In the stated order, these systems have
$55$, $20$, $12$, and $27$ sign-gauge orbits. For each orbit, take a recorded simple module
$m$ and remove the unit column from its evaluation Gram matrix:
$Q=G_m-e_me_m^{\mathsf t}$. The nonnegative integral columns already
satisfy the dimension and coordinate bounds of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-gram}. Retain only those satisfying
\eqref{eq:psd-column}. The remaining Gram and dimension-class count
equations form a finite system $Ax=b$, $x\geq0$.

All $114$ systems have frozen integral separating vectors satisfying
$y^{\mathsf t}A\geq0$ and $y^{\mathsf t}b<0$. The public verifier
reopens the source census, regenerates every anti-module orbit,
reconstructs each full column domain, applies the rational range and
positive-semidefinite tests, and checks these inequalities. It uses
no optimizer and no numerical spectral comparison. Thus every
necessary module branch is impossible, proving the four general
exclusions.
\end{proof}
