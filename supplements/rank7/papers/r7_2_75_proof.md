# General exclusion of R7_2_75

**Conclusion.** R7_2_75 admits no fusion categorification over the complex field. The proof does not assume a braiding, pivotal structure, pseudo-unitarity, or unitarity.

The source fusion tensor has simple dimensions (1,1,1,1,4,4,4), total Frobenius–Perron dimension 52, invertible group V4, and trivial universal grading. Every invertible fixes each of the three dimension-four simples. The accompanying verifier checks these statements against all 343 tensor entries and checks associativity exactly.

## 1. The first forced equivariantization

Suppose C categorifies the ring. By Etingof–Nikshych–Ostrik, *Weakly group-theoretical and solvable fusion categories*, Theorem 1.6, dimension 52=4·13 implies that C is solvable. Proposition 4.4 realizes C as the last step of a chain of prime cyclic extensions or equivariantizations. The last step cannot be an extension because U(C)=1. A last Cp-equivariantization embeds Rep(Cp) into C, so its character group embeds into Inv(C)=V4. Thus p=2 and

    C = D^{C2},  FPdim(D)=26.

Let h be its nontrivial character. Each of the three possible h fixes every dimension-four simple. The four invertibles of C form two h-orbits of length two. The equivariant-simple orbit/stabilizer theorem therefore gives D exactly two invertibles and six dimension-two simples: each dimension-four simple comes from a free two-element orbit, while each h-pair of invertibles comes from one fixed invertible. In symbols,

    type(D) = (1²,2⁶),  rank(D)=8.

There is no projective-representation exception, since H²(C2,C×)=0.

## 2. The second forced equivariantization

The category D has trivial universal grading. In fact, all components of a faithful grading have the same FP dimension (ENO, *On fusion categories*, Proposition 8.20). The neutral component is integral, so the grading-group order divides 26. Orders 13 and 26 require at least thirteen simple objects, contrary to rank(D)=8. Order 2 would give a neutral component of prime dimension 13, which is pointed and has thirteen simples by the same paper's Corollary 8.30. This is equally impossible.

Apply solvability and the last-step argument to D. Its only nontrivial invertible has order two, so

    D = P^{C2},  FPdim(P)=13.

By ENO Corollary 8.30, P=Vec_{C13}^{ω}, for an unspecified associator ω. The C2 object action must be nontrivial; a trivial object action would give 26 invertible simples in D. The only nontrivial involution of C13 is inversion. This yields the familiar orbit type: the unit is fixed and the other twelve elements form six inversion pairs.

## 3. Combine the two equivariantizations

The canonical central Rep(C2) inside D is unique over its underlying two invertibles. Indeed, the kernel of Inv(Z(D))→Inv(D) consists of monoidal natural automorphisms of Id_D. Such an automorphism has scalar one on every summand of X⊗X*, and hence on the entire adjoint subcategory. Since D_ad=D, the kernel is trivial. Two central lifts of the same invertible differ by an element of this kernel.

Consequently, every tensor autoequivalence of D preserves this central Rep(C2). In particular, the outer C2 action defining C preserves it. Equivariantizing this preserved subcategory gives a central Tannakian subcategory Rep(G) of C, whose underlying functor into C is fully faithful. Its ordinary symmetric fibre functor is obtained by composing the equivariantization forgetful functor with Rep(C2)→Vec. The group G fits into an extension

    1 → C2 → G → C2 → 1.

Thus G is C4 or V4. Both are abelian, so all four simple objects of Rep(G) are invertible and their fusion group is the character group G-hat. This subgroup exhausts Inv(C)=V4, forcing G=V4.

De-equivariantizing in stages recovers P. Equivalently,

    C = (Vec_{C13}^{ω})^{V4},

where the normal C2 acts on C13 by inversion. This use of sequential equivariantization is the ordinary equivariantization/de-equivariantization equivalence for tensor categories over Rep(G): ENO §2.6 and Proposition 2.10; Drinfeld–Gelaki–Nikshych–Ostrik, *On braided fusion categories I*, Theorem 4.18(i) and Proposition 4.19. It concerns central Tannakian embeddings and does not assume that C is braided.

## 4. The object orbits give the contradiction

Every tensor autoequivalence of a pointed category induces a group automorphism of its simple-object group. Thus the V4 object action defines a homomorphism

    V4 → Aut(C13) = C12.

Its image has exponent at most two and contains inversion, so it is precisely the order-two subgroup generated by inversion. Each nonidentity object of C13 therefore has orbit size two and stabilizer C2. All irreducible projective representations of this stabilizer are one-dimensional, regardless of ω, tensorators, or action coherence. The simple-object formula for equivariantization consequently produces two dimension-two simples from each of the six nonidentity orbits. The unit orbit produces the four one-dimensional characters of V4.

Hence C would have dimensions (1⁴,2¹²), of rank sixteen. Its required dimensions are (1⁴,4³), of rank seven. This contradiction excludes R7_2_75 as a general complex fusion category.

No claim about triviality of ω, uniqueness of gauge tensorators, or classification of possible quasi-Hopf algebras is needed.
