# A unitary model for R7_4_2019

The category is the group-theoretical category

\[
\mathcal C(\Gamma,\Omega,S_3,1),\qquad \Gamma=Q_8\rtimes S_3,
\quad [\Omega]=c_2(V)-\operatorname{inf}c_2(R)
\in H^4(B\Gamma,\mathbb Z)=H^3(\Gamma,U(1)).
\]

Here the specified semidirect action and representations are explicit below.
This gives a unitary fusion category over the complex numbers, with census
tensor SHA256 `e53b4a423763328d160bf33d459e1e205a57e9d0af120da0b654177d5bc9f73f`.
It does not require a global table of 48^3 cocycle values.

## 1. A global cocycle exists with the required restriction

Write the imaginary quaternion axes as i,j,k. The action of p in S3 is

\[
e_a\longmapsto \operatorname{sgn}(p)e_{p(a)}.
\]

View the unit quaternions as SU(2), and distinguish the scalar complex
imaginary unit I from the quaternion i. The matrices/quaternions

\[
r=-\frac{1+i+j+k}{2},\qquad
s=I\frac{i-j}{\sqrt2}
\]

satisfy r^3=s^2=1 and srs=r^{-1}. Conjugation by r implements the cycle
(123), and conjugation by s implements the signed transposition (12).
Thus they give a unitary representation R:S3→U(2) implementing the entire
semidirect action. Its determinant is the sign character. The formula

\[
V(q,p)=qR(p)
\]

defines a genuine unitary representation of Γ. We use the ordinary second
Chern classes of these representations and the natural isomorphism
H^4(BΓ,Z)≅H^3(Γ,U(1)) for finite groups, coming from
0→Z→R→U(1)→0. The restriction of c2(V)−inf c2(R) to S3 is zero.

Put z=−1 in Q8 and K=〈z〉×S3. Let L be the sign line of 〈z〉, and put
x=c1(L), y=c1(det R). On K, V=L⊗R. By the splitting principle,

\[
c_2(L\otimes R)=x^2+xy+c_2(R),
\qquad [\Omega]|_K=x^2+xy.
\]

Writing elements of K as (a,p), with a in {0,1}, and writing ε(p) for the
parity of p, a representative for this restriction is

\[
\omega_K((a,p),(b,q),(c,r))
=(-1)^{abc+a\epsilon(q)\epsilon(r)}.\tag{1}
\]

For completeness, take the real logarithm
L3((a,p),(b,q),(c,r))=a(bc+ε(q)ε(r))/2. Its integer coboundary on four
arguments is

\[
ab\bigl(cd+\epsilon(r)\epsilon(s)\bigr),
\]

which represents x²+xy, since ab and ε(p)ε(q) represent the respective
first Chern classes. This also verifies the cocycle identity directly.
Choose any unitary global cocycle representing [Ω]. Its restriction to K
is cohomologous to (1). Extend the corresponding normalized 2-cochain
from K to Γ, and modify Ω by its coboundary. We can and do choose Ω|K
exactly equal to (1). In particular Ω|S3=1. These are compatible choices
of cocycle and subgroup trivialization.

Consequently A=C[S3] is the usual connected separable algebra object in
Vec_Γ^Ω, and its category of A-bimodules is the claimed category. The
cocycle is unitary; the finite subgroup algebra has its standard positive
Frobenius structure. Its bimodule category is therefore a unitary fusion
category. This establishes existence before any fusion-table matching.

The restriction of [Ω] to Q8 is c2 of its defining SU(2) representation,
which is a generator of H4(BQ8,Z)=C8. Thus this is also the odd-twist
quaternion construction suggested by the untwisted model. The generator
fact is not needed for the categorical existence or fusion calculation.

## 2. The four invertibles form C4

Double cosets of S3 in Γ are the S3-orbits in Q8. The two central orbits
are {1} and {z}; the six noncentral elements form one free orbit. At 1,
the three simple objects are 1, ε and v, the representations of S3 of
dimensions 1,1,2. At z, the projective stabilizer multiplier obtained
from (1) is

\[
\alpha_z(p,q)
=\frac{\omega_K(z,p,q)\omega_K(p,q,z)}{\omega_K(p,z,q)}
=(-1)^{\epsilon(p)\epsilon(q)}.
\]

The one-dimensional projective representation u(p)=I^{ε(p)} has this
multiplier. Write t for the corresponding object above z. The other two
objects in the central z sector are tε and tv. The tensorator at (z,z)
is trivial: all three cocycle factors
ωK(p,z,z), ωK(z,z,p), ωK(z,p,z) equal 1. Therefore

\[
t^2=\epsilon,
\]

because u(p)^2=(−1)^{ε(p)}. More generally every mixed tensorator on
central Q8 degrees is trivial; hence t commutes with Rep(S3), and the
six-object subring is generated by Rep(S3) and t subject to t²=ε.
In particular the invertible group is C4, not V4.

These cocycle formulas are the standard stabilizer and product formulas
for group-theoretical categories, or follow directly by imposing the
left/right A-module associativity on the central double cosets. Only
the restriction to K enters this computation.

## 3. The dimension-six object and the complete tensor

Let X be the simple associated to the free noncentral orbit. Its
stabilizer is trivial, so FPdim(X)=6, and there is exactly one such
simple. For Y among 1,ε,v,t,tε,tv,

\[
YX=XY=\operatorname{FPdim}(Y)X.
\]

One can see this directly from the free orbit, or from support and
dimension. In X², the ordered pairs of noncentral quaternions with
product 1 form a free transitive S3-set of size six. The same is true
with product z. Whatever unit-modulus associativity phases occur, a
projective monomial representation on a free transitive group set is
the corresponding projective regular representation. Its irreducible
multiplicities are their dimensions. Thus each central sector contributes
1,1,2. For each noncentral quaternion there are four ordered pairs of
noncentral quaternions with that product. It follows that

\[
X^2=1+\epsilon+2v+t+t\epsilon+2tv+4X.
\]

This determines every fusion coefficient. In the model basis

\[
[1,\epsilon,v,t,t\epsilon,tv,X],
\]

the map to the census basis is

\[
[0,6,5,1,2,3,4].
\]
