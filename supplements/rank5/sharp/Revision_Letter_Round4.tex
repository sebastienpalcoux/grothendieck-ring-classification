\section{Outcome and baseline}

This letter records the fourth review and every change relative to
\path{Rank5_General_Sharp_Revised_Round3.zip}. All seventeen sections,
the eighteen structural programs, the census and certificate code,
and the preceding repairs were reviewed afresh.

\textbf{No additional substantive mathematical or computational defect
was confirmed.} One duplicated phrase in a program description was
corrected. The citation for the two unitary multiplicity-four examples
was made precise. Three optional audit programs were added to preserve
new checks on positive models, an explicit Drinfeld center, and native
linear completion. Documentation, transcripts, the current letter,
the exact patch, the manuscript PDF, and the checksum manifest were
updated as specified below.

The mathematical statements, proof arguments, classification thresholds,
existing verification algorithms, separating forms, witness records,
and model tensors retain their third-revision values. In particular,
the bounds remain
\[
 \mu\le4,\qquad \operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C)\le48+24\sqrt3,
\]
and the census still has seventeen realized based rings. The absence
of another confirmed defect in this pass is an audit conclusion, not
a claim of formal certification or of immunity from further errors.

\section{New reproducible audit programs}

The following programs are new files under \path{audits_round4/}.
They are optional and are not called by the ordinary verifier.
They locate the package relative to their own file paths, require
assertions-enabled Python, and leave the mathematical data unchanged.
Their successful outputs are in
\path{additional_verification_round4.txt}.

\subsection{Positive models and their subrings}

\path{audits_round4/positive_models.py} checks all seventeen model
tensors against the formal-codegree $d$-number, central-dimension
norm and field, global-moment, and induction criteria. None produces
a false exclusion. The program also enumerates every proper
nontrivial duality-closed fusion subring of each model and checks
that its dimension quotient is an algebraic integer.

There are \textbf{27 such subring occurrences across the seventeen
models}, and all pass. This is not a count of distinct isomorphism
types of subrings. An explicit final coverage check requires both
the seventeen-model and twenty-seven-subring totals.

These are checks on known positive inputs. They use the existing
model registry and criterion implementations; they do not construct
associators or independently prove the unitary realization theorems.

\subsection{An explicit feasible higher-indicator system}

\path{audits_round4/dihedral_center.py} uses the explicit center of
$\operatorname{Rep}(D_{14})$, with $D_{14}$ of order fourteen, as a
feasible input to the higher-trace machinery. The conjugacy classes
are the identity, the reflections, and three pairs of rotations;
the associated centralizer representations supply the central simples.

The five unit-induction columns and all twenty-three remaining central
simples survive the column filters. The explicitly supplied
multiplicities satisfy every Gram and moment equality and all
\textbf{32 higher-indicator inequalities through order six}, exactly.
Thus this check tests both the column restrictions and the orientation
of the higher-indicator inequalities on a concrete categorified example.

The center data are supplied explicitly, while the filtered system is
built by the existing higher-trace implementation. This is one exact
feasible example, not an independent implementation of all four
negative higher-indicator certificates.

\subsection{Native arithmetic and linear completion}

\path{audits_round4/linear_completion.py} first compiles both optimized
generators with C++ undefined-behavior sanitization and runs the full
multiplicity-four census. They produce \textbf{269 rings} with no
sanitizer error. All compiled programs are created in temporary
directories. This optional probe requires a \texttt{g++} installation
with the undefined-behavior sanitizer in addition to the ordinary
compiler requirements.

It then compares the native augmented-matrix elimination with
independent exact rational row reduction in SymPy on
\textbf{659 input cases}: all 269 generated parameter records and
390 fixed-seed boundary/random inputs. The three duality types
contribute 350, 177, and 132 cases. In each case the original and
native-reduced matrices have the same rational reduced row-echelon
form. The random seed is fixed, and the total coverage is checked.

The 659 cases need not be distinct augmented matrices. Outside the
generated records, this is a sampled check, not exhaustive enumeration
of every rejected parameter choice. It also shares the native matrix
construction. Sanitization checks the executed multiplicity-four run;
it is not a proof of overflow safety at arbitrary larger bounds.
These limitations are stated in the documentation.

\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.2493v3}{arXiv:0806.2493v3}.

forms cover them. Its Galois averaging does not assume that the
indicators themselves are real. The subring checker still rejects
the malformed negative-index certificate, while both stored valid
subring certificates pass.

Further exact symbolic calculations checked the cubic fourth-moment
identity and resulting coefficients, the mixed-profile constant,
the quadratic kernel constants and final negative margin, the
biquadratic norm/twist margin, and the endpoint minimal polynomial.
The source also has no missing or duplicate labels, missing citation
keys, or uncited bibliography entries.

Primary-source checks confirmed the relevant ENO, Ostrik,
