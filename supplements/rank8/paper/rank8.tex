\subsection{A rank-eight noncommutative codegree lemma}
For any fusion ring, write
\[
\Omega=\sum_i b_i b_{i^*},\qquad
C_R=\sum_iN_iN_i^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For an irreducible representation $E$ of $R\otimes\C$ of degree $e_E$, the central element $\Omega$ acts as the scalar $e_E f_E$, where $f_E$ is its ordinary formal codegree. The regular representation contains $E$ with multiplicity $e_E$, so that scalar has multiplicity $e_E^2$ in the characteristic polynomial of $C_R$ \cite{OstrikCode,R6}.

\begin{proposition}[Noncommutative rank-eight codegrees]\label{prop:noncomm}
Let $R$ be a noncommutative fusion ring of rank eight. Its semisimple complex algebra has Wedderburn decomposition $\C^4\oplus M_2(\C)$. The formal codegree of the unique matrix-block representation is a rational integer. Every irreducible factor of the characteristic polynomial of $C_R$ of degree at least two consists entirely of formal codegrees of one-dimensional representations. Consequently, if any such factor fails Ostrik's $d$-number divisibility condition, $R$ has no complex fusion categorification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The FP dimension character is one-dimensional, so at least one scalar summand exists. Noncommutativity requires a matrix summand of dimension at least two. Since the sum of the squares of the degrees of simple modules is eight, there can be only one matrix summand and it must have degree two; the four remaining dimensions are one. The rational based algebra is split by some number field. Galois automorphisms permute its irreducible representations preserving their degrees, so the unique two-dimensional representation is Galois-fixed. Its formal codegree is an algebraic integer by \cite{OstrikCode} and rational by Galois invariance, hence belongs to $\Z$. Its Casimir eigenvalue is therefore rational. Every nonlinear irreducible factor of the integral Casimir polynomial comes from the four one-dimensional representations; on each, the eigenvalue is exactly the formal codegree. Finally, formal codegrees of complex fusion categories are $d$-numbers \cite{OstrikCode}. For an irreducible polynomial $q(t)=t^d+a_1t^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d$, this requires $|a_d|^j\mid a_j^d$ for every $j=1,\dots,d$. Failure of one exact integer divisibility gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

The commutative branch uses the ordinary $d$-number test on every Casimir factor. The script never treats raw Casimir eigenvalues as formal codegrees of noncommutative representations without Proposition~\ref{prop:noncomm}. We also ran the rational-indivisibility screen of \cite[Theorem~4.4]{SchNorm}, noncommuting field-automorphism witnesses from \cite{R6}, and finite-field cyclotomic witnesses from \cite{R4Finite}. These three screens add no exclusions in the present bound; their zero rows and exact code remain in the stage data. Schopieray's greedy formal-codegree obstruction $2,3,7$ \cite{SchGreedy} cannot occur before multiplicity five, since its forced rank-four subring has a coefficient five, so it is not an active step here.

\subsection{Grading obstructions with rank-one components}
The following short arguments give five further exclusions without any pivotality assumption. In a faithful grading $\mathcal C=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal C_g$, every component is an invertible bimodule category over $\mathcal D=\mathcal C_e$, and $g\mapsto[\mathcal C_g]$ is a homomorphism to $\operatorname{BrPic}(\mathcal D)$ \cite[Theorem~1.3 and Section~6]{ENOExt}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rankone}
If a pointed category $\Vec_A^\omega$ has a module category with one simple object, then $[\omega]=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Identify the module category with $\Vec$. Each invertible object acts by the identity functor, up to isomorphism. The scalar module constraints form a nonzero $2$-cochain whose coboundary is $\omega$, by the module pentagon. Hence the associator class is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:graded}
The rings $R8\_1\_26$, $R8\_2\_116$, and $R8\_2\_189$ have no complex fusion categorification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the original zero-based census labels, $R8\_1\_26$ has a faithful $C_4$ grading with components
\[
\{0,3,4\},\quad\{1\},\quad\{5,6,7\},\quad\{2\}.
\]
Its neutral group is $A=C_3$. Lemma~\ref{lem:rankone} identifies its neutral category with $\Vec_{C_3}$. By \cite[Corollary~1.2]{ENOExt}, its Brauer--Picard group is the orthogonal group of $A\oplus A^*$ with hyperbolic quadratic form. Over $\mathbb F_3$ this consists of $I,-I,J,-J$, where $J$ exchanges the two coordinates. Every square is the identity, so $[\mathcal C_2]=[\mathcal C_1]^2=1$.
On the other hand, the degree-two invertible object $s=5$ conjugates $a=3$ to $a^{-1}=4$. Its component is the regular left module with right action twisted by inversion. It cannot be equivalent to the regular bimodule: such an equivalence would send $s$ to some $b\in A$ and the relation $as=sa^{-1}$ would imply $ab=ba^{-1}$, which is false in $C_3$.

For $R8\_2\_116$ and $R8\_2\_189$, the universal grading has five components, of ranks $4,1,1,1,1$, so its group is $C_5$. Their neutral groups are respectively $C_4$ on $\{0,1,2,7\}$ and $C_2^2$ on $\{0,5,6,7\}$. Again their associator classes are trivial. In the first case the Brauer--Picard group embeds in $\operatorname{GL}_2(\Z/4)$, of order $96$; in the second it is $O_4^+(\mathbb F_2)$, of order $72$. Neither group has an element of order five. Hence the component homomorphism is trivial, forcing every component to be equivalent to the rank-four regular bimodule, contrary to the rank-one components. The verifier enumerates the small orthogonal groups and checks every grading support identity exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf6}
The rings $R8\_2\_78$ and $R8\_2\_168$ have no complex fusion categorification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each has a faithful grading with a rank-one component and neutral fusion rules $\Rep(S_3)$: $g^2=1$, $gx=xg=x$, and $x^2=1+g+x$. A rank-one module category gives a fiber functor on the neutral category $\mathcal D$, so $\mathcal D\simeq\Rep(H)$ for a semisimple Hopf algebra of dimension six. The dimension-six classification \cite{HopfPQ} and the rank-three fusion rules force $H\simeq\C[S_3]$. The dual category of its fiber-functor module is $\Rep(H^*)\simeq\Vec_{S_3}$, of rank six \cite[Example~7.12.26]{EGNO}. But invertibility of the homogeneous $\mathcal D$-bimodule would identify that dual with $\mathcal D$, of rank three, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
