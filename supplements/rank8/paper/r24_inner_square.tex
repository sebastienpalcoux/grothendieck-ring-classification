\section{An inner-square obstruction for \texorpdfstring{$R8\_2\_24$}{R8\_2\_24}}
\label{sec:r24-rigidity}
The obstruction in this section combines a local pivotal calculation with
rigidity of tensor functors. It applies to an arbitrary complex
categorification after a proved Galois reduction to pseudo-unitarity.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:r24-general-exclusion}
The fusion ring $R8\_2\_24$ has no complex fusion categorification.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The neutral indicators and the actual inner square}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:2d2-neutral-indicators}
Let $\mathcal B$ be pseudo-unitary with simple objects $1,g,r,s=gr$
and fusion rules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-neutral-rules}
 g^2=1,\qquad gr=rg=s,\qquad r^2=1+2r+2s.
\end{equation}
For its canonical spherical structure,
$\nu_2(g)=-1$ and $\nu_2(r)=\nu_2(s)=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $d=2+\sqrt5$ and $D=2+2d^2=20+8\sqrt5$, and let a prime
mean the nontrivial embedding of $\Q(\sqrt5)$. The four formal
codegrees are $D,D',4,4$. The corresponding constituents of $I(1)$
have dimensions $1,D/D',D/4,D/4$ and twist one.
Use the normalized anomaly-free center modular matrices $S,T$, with
$S_{1A}=\dim(A)/D$. In the span of these constituents let
$w$ be the unit vector proportional to the difference of the first two
basis vectors. The modular-character formula for central induction gives
\[
 \operatorname{proj}_{I(1)}Sw
   =\left(\frac1D-\frac1{D'}\right)w=-\frac{w}{\sqrt5}.
\]
The diagonal torus acts on $w$ by the quadratic character $\chi_5$.
Indeed its Galois action interchanges the first two constituents precisely
at the nonresidues modulo five; their signs are positive since $D,D'>0$.
The local compression bound \cite[Lemma~4.1]{SchDimensionBlock} is
therefore attained at conductor five.

We recall why equality supplies the needed level statement. The
congruence theorem \cite{NgCongruence} makes the modular representation a
representation of $\operatorname{SL}_2(\Z/N\Z)$. Decompose it into local
irreducible tensor factors. At the prime five, an irreducible factor
with an upper-unipotent-fixed vector embeds in the permutation
representation on primitive vectors modulo $5^a$. In the quadratic
torus eigenspace, the maximal singular subspace of compression is
spanned by
\[
 F(p,q)=\chi_5(q)[q\text{ is a unit}],\qquad
 G(p,q)=\chi_5(-p)[q=0\pmod5].
\]
These are the equality vectors in the proof of the cited local bound;
both are pullbacks from level five. Any irreducible constituent
containing an equality vector consequently factors through level five.
At every other prime the torus character is trivial. Equality in the
norm bound one makes the vector fixed by both upper and lower
unipotents, hence by the whole local group. The maximal singular
subspace of a tensor product of contractions is the tensor product of
their maximal singular subspaces, by diagonalizing $A^*A$ in each
factor. Thus the entire cyclic subrepresentation generated by $w$
factors through level five. The known twist-one projection already
attains the norm bound, so no additional twist-one coordinate occurs.
This is the same equality argument as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:q17-conductor-equality}.

For a central simple $A$ with dimension $z_A$, its coordinate in $Sw$
is proportional to
\[
 \frac{z_A}{D}-\left(\frac{z_A}{D}\right)'.
\]
Consequently $\theta_A^5=1$ whenever this difference is nonzero.
Let $\ell(z)$ be the rational coordinate of the nontrivial quadratic
part of $z/D$, scaled so that its values on $(1,g,r,s)$ are
$(2,2,-1,-1)$. Choose a cyclotomic automorphism $\sigma$ with unit
exponent $k$ modulo five, lifting that exponent by the Chinese remainder
theorem to be coprime to the full conductor. Write $\pi$ and
$\varepsilon_A$ for its modular permutation and signs. The canonical
anomaly-free center normalization gives
\begin{align*}
 z_{\pi A}&=\varepsilon_A\frac D{\sigma D}\sigma(z_A),&
 \ell(z_{\pi A})&=\varepsilon_A\chi_5(k)\ell(z_A),&
 \theta_{\pi A}&=\sigma^2(\theta_A).
\end{align*}
The first induction trace, weighted by $\ell$, is
\[
 \sum_A\ell(z_A)z_A\theta_A=D\ell(1).
\]
The two Galois signs cancel in
\[
 \sigma\bigl(\ell(z_A)z_A\bigr)
 =\chi_5(k)\frac{\sigma D}{D}\ell(z_{\pi A})z_{\pi A}.
\]
Apply $\sigma$ and reindex by $\pi$. Every nonzero term has twist
dividing five; the constituents of $I(1)$ have twist one. If $h$ is
an inverse of $k$ modulo five, then
$\sigma(\theta_A)=\theta_{\pi A}^{h}$. Cancelling $\sigma D$ and
using the induction indicator formula \cite[Theorem~4.1]{NgIndicators}
gives
\[
 \sum_X\ell(\dim X)\nu_h(X)=\chi_5(k)\ell(1).
\]
Inversion permutes the unit residues and preserves $\chi_5$, so this
holds with $h$ renamed $k$. At $k=2$ it becomes
\[
 2+2\nu_2(g)-\nu_2(r)-\nu_2(s)=-2.
\]
The three unknowns are signs, and the asserted values are the unique
solution.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Inner square supplied by the source tensor]
\label{prop:r24-reduction}
A complex categorification of $R8\_2\_24$ has a pseudo-unitary Galois
conjugate containing a neutral subcategory $\mathcal B$ with
\eqref{eq:r24-neutral-rules} and the indicators of
Lemma~\ref{lem:2d2-neutral-indicators}. It supplies a label-fixing tensor
autoequivalence $T$ of $\mathcal B$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-inner-square}
 T^2\simeq_{\otimes}\operatorname{Ad}(g).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The full source tensor has exactly two Frobenius--Perron dimensions,
$1$ and $2+\sqrt5$. The published Galois-conjugacy statement
\cite[Lemma~3.1]{SchNear} applies to the entire category. Its
pseudo-unitary conjugate has the same tensor and the faithful $C_2$
grading with neutral basis $(1,g,r,s)=(X_0,X_7,X_5,X_6)$.
The odd invertible $a=X_1$ has inverse $X_2$ and square $g=X_7$.
Commutativity of the source tensor makes the actual inner tensor functor
$T=\operatorname{Ad}(a)|_{\mathcal B}$ fix all four neutral labels.
Coherent composition of inner functors and the isomorphism $a^2\simeq g$
give \eqref{eq:r24-inner-square}; no trivialization of the pointed
associator is required. The neutral category inherits the canonical
pseudo-unitary spherical structure, so the preceding lemma applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The local pivotal defect}
\begin{theorem}[No label-fixing inner square root]
\label{thm:r24-no-square-root}
For a pseudo-unitary category $\mathcal B$ with
\eqref{eq:r24-neutral-rules} and
$\nu_2(g)=-1$, $\nu_2(r)=\nu_2(s)=1$, no label-fixing tensor
autoequivalence satisfies \eqref{eq:r24-inner-square}.
\end{theorem}
We prove the theorem in the next four steps. We use a strict monoidal
rigid realization while retaining its pivotal map $j$; we do not set
$j$ equal to the identity.

Let $Z$ and $gZ$ have indicator $+1$, and choose right dual objects
$g^*=g$, $Z^*=Z$ with identity self-duality charts. Thus
$j_g=-\operatorname{id}_g$ and $j_Z=\operatorname{id}_Z$.
Write $e=\operatorname{ev}_g:g^2\to1$. Pseudo-unitarity gives
$\dim(g)=1$, so the pivotal trace identity
$\dim(g)=-e\operatorname{coev}_g=1$ fixes the coevaluation sign.
The snake identities and dualization give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-g-signs}
 \operatorname{coev}_g=-e^{-1},\qquad
 e\otimes g=-g\otimes e,\qquad e^*=-e^{-1}.
\end{equation}
An object written as a tensor factor in a morphism denotes its identity.
Use $F(X)=gXg$ with tensorator and unit
\[
 F_2(X,Y)=-gX\otimes e\otimes Yg,
 \qquad F_0=e^{-1}.
\]
This represents ordinary inner conjugation: its usual tensorator and
unit in these dual conventions are $+gX\otimes e\otimes Yg$ and
$-e^{-1}$, and the natural isomorphism with every component
$-\operatorname{id}$ changes both signs. The monoidal comparison of
its square with the identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-square-comparison}
 \eta_X=-e\otimes X\otimes e:ggXgg\longrightarrow X.
\end{equation}
Indeed the adjacent contraction without the minus sign sends the
unit of $F^2$ to $-1$ and has tensor-product defect $-1$, by
\eqref{eq:r24-g-signs}. Its negative satisfies both monoidal equations.
The canonical dual comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-dual-comparison}
 \delta_Z=-\operatorname{id}_{gZg}:F(Z^*)\longrightarrow F(Z)^*.
\end{equation}
To check the sign, the transported evaluation is
\[
 F_0^{-1}F(\operatorname{ev}_Z)F_2(Z^*,Z)
 =-e(g\otimes\operatorname{ev}_Z\otimes g)
       (gZ\otimes e\otimes Zg),
\]
which is the negative of the ordinary nested evaluation of $gZg$.

Choose a nonzero $c:gZ\to Zg$. For arbitrary self-dualities
$\phi_g,\phi_Z$, the self-duality
$\psi=(\phi_Z\otimes\phi_g)c$ of $gZ$ satisfies
$\psi^*j_{gZ}=\nu_2(gZ)\psi$. Expanding this equation gives
\[
 (\phi_Z^{-1}\otimes\phi_g^{-1})c^*(\phi_g\otimes\phi_Z)
 =\frac{\nu_2(gZ)}{\nu_2(g)\nu_2(Z)}c=-c.
\]
Thus $c^*=\epsilon c$, with $\epsilon=-1$, in our identity charts.
Define
\[
 q=(Z\otimes e)(c\otimes g):gZg\to Z
\]
and the nonzero scalar $\kappa$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-double-crossing}
 (Z\otimes e)(c\otimes g)(g\otimes c)
       =\kappa(e\otimes Z):ggZ\to Z.
\end{equation}
Every map is invertible. Dualizing $q$ yields
\[
 q^*=-\epsilon(g\otimes c)(e^{-1}\otimes Z),\qquad
 qq^*=-\epsilon\kappa\operatorname{id}_Z.
\]
Define its form defect $\gamma$ and composition defect $\lambda$ by
\[
 q^*\phi_Zq=\gamma\delta_ZF(\phi_Z),\qquad
 qF(q)=\lambda\eta_Z.
\]
The first equation and \eqref{eq:r24-dual-comparison} give
$\gamma=\epsilon\kappa$. For the second, the following are typed
maps $ggZgg\to Z$, with tensor symbols suppressed:
\begin{align*}
 qF(q)&=(Z e)(c g)(g Z e g)(g c g g)\\
      &=(Z e)(Z g e g)(c g g g)(g c g g)\\
      &=-(Z e e)(c g g g)(g c g g)\\
      &=-\kappa(e Z e)=\kappa\eta_Z.
\end{align*}
The second line uses interchange, the third uses
$e(g e g)=-(e\otimes e)$, and the fourth uses
\eqref{eq:r24-double-crossing}. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-defect-ratio}
 \lambda=\kappa,\qquad \frac\lambda\gamma=-1.
\end{equation}
Rescaling $q$ multiplies both defects by the same square.

Apply this calculation to $Z=r$. On $H=\operatorname{Hom}(r^2,r)$,
use full categorical transpose from the symmetric self-duality of $r$
and set $\beta(f,h)=\operatorname{tr}_r(fh^{\mathsf t})$.
The self-duality on $r^2$ is the tensor product of those on its two
identical factors, with reversed dual order. Transpose is involutive;
transposing the scalar composite proves that $\beta$ is symmetric.
Semisimplicity makes the composition pairing nondegenerate, and hence
$\beta$ is nondegenerate. These are bilinear forms over $\C$.
The induced action
\[
 K(f)=qF(f)F_2(r,r)(q^{-1}\otimes q^{-1})
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-K-square-form}
 K^2=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{id}_H,\qquad
 \beta(Kf,Kh)=\gamma^{-1}\beta(f,h)
             =-\lambda^{-1}\beta(f,h).
\end{equation}
For the square, compose the monoidal comparison \eqref{eq:r24-square-comparison}
with the output chart and two input charts. For the form, the output
chart contributes $\gamma$ and the inputs contribute $\gamma^{-2}$.
A scalar $K$ would have equal square and form multipliers, contrary to
\eqref{eq:r24-K-square-form}. Since $\dim H=2$, it has two distinct
opposite eigenvalues. Each eigenline is isotropic, and nondegeneracy
pairs the two lines. Thus $[K]$ is a nontrivial projective involution.

\subsection{One simultaneous chart for the square root}
Suppose $T^2\simeq_\otimes F$. A tensor autoequivalence preserves the
canonical pseudo-unitary pivotal structure, by its uniqueness
\cite[Proposition~8.23]{ENO}. Fix the unit chart by $T_0^{-1}$ and
choose charts $q_g:T(g)\to g$, $q_r:T(r)\to r$. Extend them to strict
tensor words by
\[
 q_{uv}=(q_u\otimes q_v)T_2(u,v)^{-1},\qquad
 \widehat T(f)=q_vT(f)q_u^{-1}\quad(f:u\to v).
\]
Coherence makes this transported action strictly multiplicative on
words. Rescale the two simple charts independently to make
$\widehat T(e)=e$ and $\widehat T(\operatorname{ev}_r)=\operatorname{ev}_r$;
each condition requires only a nonzero complex square root. Use the
induced chart on $s=gr$. Distinct isomorphic words remain connected by
the displayed morphisms, rather than by further identifications.
We suppress the hat in the following calculations.

The one-dimensional crossing space has $T(c)=\alpha c$.
Applying $T$ to \eqref{eq:r24-double-crossing} gives
$\alpha^2\kappa=\kappa$, hence $\alpha=\pm1$.
Moreover $T(q)=\alpha q$, while $T$ fixes the tensorator of $F$ and
commutes with its word operation. The explicit formula for $K$ therefore
implies the exact covariance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-crossing-covariance}
 T_HKT_H^{-1}=\alpha^{-1}K.
\end{equation}
The exponent is one output chart minus two input charts.
A monoidal natural isomorphism $T^2\Rightarrow F$ acts by a scalar
on the simple $r$; its induced change on $H$ is therefore scalar.
Thus $[T_H^2]=[K]$. If $\alpha=-1$, equation
\eqref{eq:r24-crossing-covariance} makes $T_H$ exchange the two
$K$-eigenlines. Its square would be scalar, a contradiction. Hence
$\alpha=1$.

All composites of $e,c,c^{-1},\operatorname{ev}_r$ and their cups
are now fixed in this same chart. In particular the nonzero evaluation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-s-evaluation}
 \operatorname{ev}_s=(e\otimes\operatorname{ev}_r)
                  (g\otimes c^{-1}\otimes r):grgr\to1
\end{equation}
is fixed. Its associated self-duality is symmetric since
$\nu_2(s)=1$. For $X=r,s$ the full-transpose form
\[
 B_X(f,h)=\operatorname{tr}_X(fh^{\mathsf t}),
 \qquad f,h\in\operatorname{Hom}(r^2,X),
\]
is therefore nondegenerate, symmetric and preserved exactly by $T$.
Putting $J=\operatorname{Hom}(r^2,s)$, we obtain
$T_H,T_J\in O(2,\C)$ in their respective forms. A determinant-minus
orthogonal matrix in dimension two squares to the identity. Thus
$\det T_H=1$, and in a hyperbolic basis
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-u-fourth}
 T_H=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1}),\qquad u^4=-1.
\end{equation}
The latter equation expresses the eigenvalue ratio $-1$ of $[T_H^2]=[K]$.
For $J$, either $\det T_J=1$ and
$T_J=\operatorname{diag}(v,v^{-1})$ in a hyperbolic basis, or
$\det T_J=-1$ and $T_J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ in an eigenbasis.

\subsection{All fusion spaces and all associator weights}
For $a\in\mathbb F_2$ write $g_a=g^a$ and $r_a=g^ar$.
The complete fusion rules are
\[
 g_ag_b=g_{a+b},\qquad g_ar_b=r_bg_a=r_{a+b},\qquad
 r_ar_b=g_{a+b}+2r_{a+b}+2r_{a+b+1}.
\]
Every multiplicity-one vertex has a basis built from the fixed
structural maps. For the two-dimensional spaces choose
$A_{ab}:r_ar_b\to g_{a+b}r^2$ by
\[
 A_{00}=A_{10}=\operatorname{id},\quad
 A_{01}=c^{-1}\otimes r,\quad
 A_{11}=(e\otimes r^2)(g\otimes c^{-1}\otimes r).
\]
For $m=a+b$ let $B_{abc}:r_c\to g_mr_{m+c}$ be the identity if
$m=0$ or $c=1$, and $e^{-1}\otimes r$ if $m=1,c=0$.
For $f:r^2\to r_{a+b+c}$ transport the vertex by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-vertex-transport}
 f\longmapsto B_{abc}^{-1}(g_m\otimes f)A_{ab}
       \in\operatorname{Hom}(r_ar_b,r_c).
\end{equation}
Every structural transport map in this expression is fixed by $T$;
$f$ itself carries the $H$ or $J$ action. These vertices therefore
have exactly the $H$ action for $a+b+c=0$ and the $J$ action for
$a+b+c=1$, without additional scalar characters. The thin vertex
$r_ar_b\to g_m$ is $(g_m\otimes\operatorname{ev}_r)A_{ab}$.
The other thin vertices use $e,c$ and unit maps. Composition-dual
incoming bases have inverse eigenvalues. One chosen transport for
each space suffices; equality of alternative transport paths is not
required.

First suppose $\det T_J=1$. Give the two $H$ basis vectors weights
$\pm(1,0)$, the two $J$ vectors weights $\pm(0,1)$, and thin vertices
weight $(0,0)$. These define a diagonal gauge torus $U(z,w)$.
Every associator coefficient has the signed sum of its four vertex
weights, and every such weight belongs to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-weight-diamond}
 W=\{(p,q)\in(2\Z)^2:|p|+|q|\le4\}.
\end{equation}
To prove this uniformly, use the six-edge, four-vertex incidence
pattern of a full associator coefficient. A vertex has zero, two or
three noninvertible incident edges, and it is thick precisely in the
last case. Counting each marked edge twice makes the number of thick
vertices even. Assign bit $a$ to both $g_a$ and $r_a$. The incident
bit sum is zero at a thin or $H$ vertex, and one at a $J$ vertex.
Counting each edge twice makes the number of $J$ vertices even;
the number of $H$ vertices is then even as well. This proves evenness
of both coordinates, and four vertices give the norm bound.

Let $L\subset\Z^2$ be generated by the weights of actual nonzero
associator coefficients. Monoidality of $T$ implies
$u^pv^q=1$ for every $(p,q)\in L$. If $L$ has rank two, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r24-lattice-axes}
 (4,0),(0,4)\in L.
\end{equation}
Indeed choose two independent vectors from $W$ and divide them by
two. The possibilities are coordinate axes of length one or two and
diagonals $(\pm1,\pm1)$. Two independent axes, an axis and a diagonal,
or two independent diagonals each generate a lattice containing the
two doubled coordinate axes. Multiplying back by two proves
\eqref{eq:r24-lattice-axes}. It would force $u^4=1$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:r24-u-fourth}. Hence $L$ has rank below two, and its
annihilator in $(\C^\times)^2$ contains a positive-dimensional torus
fixing every associator coefficient.

If $\det T_J=-1$, the $J$ eigenvalues are $1,-1$. Track only the
$H$ exponent $p$, still even and with $|p|\le4$. At $p=\pm2$,
the $H$ factor is $\pm i$, which no $J$ sign can cancel. At
$p=\pm4$, all four vertices belong to $H$, so there is no $J$ factor
and the coefficient has multiplier $-1$. All these coefficients
vanish. Every nonzero coefficient therefore has $p=0$. The torus
acting by $\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})$ on all transported $H$
spaces and identically elsewhere fixes the full associator.

\subsection{Tensor-functor rigidity and the exclusion}
In either case a positive-dimensional diagonal gauge torus fixes all
associator coefficients and all unit channels. In a skeletal semisimple
realization, its matrices on the fusion spaces define tensorators for
the identity underlying linear functor: extend them naturally from
pairs of simple objects to direct sums. Equality of the two composites
of these tensorators on each triple is exactly invariance of the full
associator matrix. The fixed unit channels supply both unit equations.
Thus every element of this torus is an actual tensor autoequivalence.

These functors have infinitely many monoidal natural-isomorphism
classes. A natural isomorphism has scalar components on simple objects,
so its change on each of $H$ and $J$ is scalar. It cannot remove a
varying projective eigenvalue ratio. More explicitly, on the
two-dimensional torus the parameters with scalar action on both
spaces satisfy $z^2=w^2=1$ and form a finite subgroup; the one-dimensional
case has the same conclusion from $t^2=1$. A positive-dimensional
torus remains infinite after this quotient. This contradicts the
finiteness of tensor functors between fixed fusion categories
\cite[Theorem~2.31]{ENO}, and proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:r24-no-square-root}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:r24-general-exclusion}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:r24-reduction} would supply precisely the
pseudo-unitary neutral category and the tensor autoequivalence forbidden
by Theorem~\ref{thm:r24-no-square-root}. Therefore a complex
categorification cannot exist.
\end{proof}
