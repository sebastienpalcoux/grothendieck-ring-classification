\subsection{When a unitary obstruction is a general obstruction}
The negative primary-three form is ordinarily only an obstruction to
unitary categorification. We use it for a general exclusion only after
establishing that the hypothetical category is weakly group-theoretical.
This reduction uses exact integer dimension below $84$, solvability for
dimensions with at most two prime factors, or a faithful grading with
neutral category in that range; weak group-theoreticity is closed under
extensions \cite[Proposition~4.1 and the small-dimension theorems]{ENOSolvable}.
Every weakly group-theoretical category is completely unitary
\cite[Theorem~5.20]{GHR}. The primary-three contradiction then applies to
its unitary realization and excludes general existence.

The source-bound verifier checks the dimension hypotheses, every nonzero
structure constant of the grading, the neutral dimension and the grading
dimension ratio before accepting this implication. In particular it uses
no assertion that arbitrary weakly integral categories are unitarizable.
When the reduction fails, the same negative form retains its unitary-only
scope and does not change a general unresolved status.
