\section{Structural restrictions on the three open rings}
\label{sec:pairing-frontier-20261027}
The rings $\sha{R8_3_928}$, $\sha{R8_3_941}$ and $\sha{R8_3_1154}$
remain unresolved. The general-complex exclusion of $\sha{R8_3_1768}$
is Theorem~\ref{thm:r1768-general-exclusion}. The positive center and
indicator arguments below use the established sphericalization/Galois
existence reductions. The simple-center argument for R1154 uses only
Frobenius--Perron dimensions and applies to every complex categorification.

\subsection{Third rotations and $R8\_3\_1154$}
\begin{proposition}
All six noninvertible third indicators equal two in a positive
categorification of $R8\_3\_1154$. Each corresponding trivalent rotation
is identity. All three necessary center patterns still survive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The actual rank-ten equivariantization reduction supplies lifts $x,ux$
of $X_4$ and likewise $X_5$, with $u$ central of order two and $x^*=ux$.
For odd $n$ the two character subspaces of the cubic or higher vacuum
space give $\nu_n(X_4)=\nu_n(x)+\nu_n(ux)=2\nu_n(x)$, by spherical
duality. Each lift's cubic vacuum space has dimension one. Thus each of
$\nu_3(X_4),\nu_3(X_5)$ belongs to
$\{2,2\zeta_3,2\zeta_3^2\}$, rather than an arbitrary sum of cube roots.
Imposing these discrete possibilities on all three complete retained
center systems forces both to equal two. Duality and the inner
autoequivalence, followed by a second discrete check, force the other
four noninvertible third indicators to equal two. The $33$ excluded
branches have exact integer separating certificates; all six retained
branch records have exact rational feasibility witnesses. An independent
matrix reconstruction checks every certificate. An order-three rotation
on a two-dimensional space with trace two is identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Simple center for R1154]
Every complex fusion categorification of $\sha{R8_3_1154}$ has simple
Drinfeld center. No category in its Morita class is braided or a
nontrivial Deligne tensor product.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An integral central simple forgets to $a1+bg$, because all six
noninvertible FP dimensions have strictly positive $\sqrt5$ coefficients.
Its class is central in the source ring, whereas $g$ is not, so $b=0$.
The source adjoint subcategory is the whole category. A half-braiding on
$a1$ therefore gives a representation of its trivial universal grading
group, and simplicity forces the central object to be the unit.
Thus the integral part of the center is Vec, without a sphericality
assumption.

Put $D=\FPdim(\mathcal C)$. Every noninvertible source simple generates
$\mathcal C$, and the only proper nontrivial source subcategory is
pointed. Every nontrivial fusion subcategory $\mathcal H$ of the center
contains a nonintegral simple. Its forgetful image is therefore dominant,
so $\FPdim(\mathcal H)\ge D$. Equality would make this dominant tensor
functor an equivalence and transport a braiding to the noncommutative
source ring. Hence $\FPdim(\mathcal H)>D$. If $\mathcal H$ were proper,
its nontrivial centralizer would satisfy the same strict bound, contrary
to the centralizer identity
\[
 \FPdim(\mathcal H)\FPdim(\mathcal H')=D^2;
\]
see \cite[Theorem~3.14]{DGNO}. This proves simplicity. A braided Morita
representative would embed as a proper nontrivial fusion subcategory of
the center; a nontrivial Deligne product would give a nontrivial product
of centers. Both contradict simplicity.
\end{proof}

More generally every nontrivial central tensor functor from a braided
category $\mathcal P$ to any Morita representative requires
$\FPdim(\mathcal P)\ge D^2$, since its braided lift dominates this simple
center. If $\mathcal P$ is nondegenerate and $A$ is connected etale with
$\mathcal P_A$ in the Morita class, then
\cite[Corollary~3.30]{DMNO} gives
\[
 Z(\mathcal P_A)\simeq\mathcal P\boxtimes(\mathcal P_A^0)^{\rm rev}.
\]
Simplicity forces $\mathcal P_A^0=\Vec$, $\mathcal P\simeq Z(\mathcal C)$,
and $A$ Lagrangian of dimension $D$. This does not exclude extracting a
proper fusion subcategory after condensation or using noncentral
constructions. The three inherited positive center ranks remain
$21,23,29$; simple modular categories can have Lagrangian algebras,
so simplicity itself is not a contradiction.

The auxiliary rank-ten category has integral center double Semion,
whose nontrivial Tannakian descent returns to the source. It also passes
Lemma~\ref{lem:pivotal-conjugation-parity}: its selfdual parity components
are $\{1,u,vy\}$ with positive sign and $\{v,uv,y\}$ with negative
sign. Its quaternionic simple therefore supplies no further exclusion.

\subsection{Simple centralizers for $R8\_3\_928$ and $R8\_3\_941$}
Write $D=144+96\sqrt2+80\sqrt3+56\sqrt6$.
For either source, the only proper nontrivial subcategory is pointed of
rank two. Any central fusion subcategory with a nonintegral object
therefore forgets dominantly to the full source and has dimension at
least $D$. Equality for a braided subcategory would make the noncommutative
source braided, which is impossible.

For R928 its center is $S\boxtimes\mathcal B$, with $S$ Semion and
$\dim\mathcal B=D^2/2$. The only integral simple in $\mathcal B$ is its
unit. A proper nontrivial subcategory and its centralizer would both have
dimension at least $D$, contradicting their product $D^2/2$.
Thus $\mathcal B$ is simple; its possible ranks are $17,20,23$.
The regular-module rigidity theorem and the correspondence between module
categories and Lagrangian algebras imply that $I(1)$ is the unique
Lagrangian algebra. Every connected etale algebra extends to one, by
Witt triviality and lifting local-module algebras. The reverse subcategory
correspondence then gives precisely three etale algebra classes in the
whole center, in a chain $1\subset A\subset I(1)$, with $\dim A=D/2$.
The restriction from $\mathcal B$ is dominant, so its right adjoint gives
that same unique nontrivial $A$ in $\mathcal B$, and
$\mathcal B_A\simeq\mathcal C$, $\mathcal B_A^0\simeq S^{\rm rev}$.
These use DMNO, Lemma 3.5, Proposition 3.16, Corollary 3.30,
Proposition 4.8, Theorem 4.10 and Propositions 5.4--5.8.

For R941 the fermion centralizer $\mathcal E$ has rank $22$, dimension
$D^2/2$, and integral part sVec. If
$\mathcal E=\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}$ with $\mathcal M$
nondegenerate, modular decomposition would give
$Z(\mathcal C)=\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathcal K$ with
$\FPdim(\mathcal K)=4$. The category $\mathcal K$ contains the fermion
and has no other integral simple. Its remaining dimension two must
therefore consist of one simple of dimension $\sqrt2$: two noninvertibles
would each have squared dimension strictly greater than one. But no
source simple has dimension $\sqrt2$, so no central simple can have
that dimension. Thus the splitting is impossible.

A proper nonpointed subcategory of $\mathcal E$ containing sVec and its
centralizer would both dominate the source, with dimensions strictly
greater than $D$. Their product is $D^2$, a contradiction. A nonpointed
subcategory not containing the fermion generates $\mathcal E$ with
sVec; its symmetric center is integral and hence trivial, so it would
give the excluded splitting. Thus the only subcategories of
$\mathcal E$ are Vec, sVec and $\mathcal E$.

The conditional $1+X$ construction in the rank-six manuscript produces
precisely R941. Equal global FP dimension does not supply that algebra
or a Morita equivalence. If the equivalence exists, Morita invariance of
odd dimension-weighted indicators forces four R941 cubic rotations to
be identities; the remaining pair has two possible combined spectra.
Neither source category nor its required nonpointed algebra is
constructed.

\subsection{Forced fusion matrices in the R928 modular factor}
The simple factor $\mathcal B$ has four retained patterns of ranks
$23,20,20,17$, indexed $40,49,186,191$. Put $L=D/\sqrt2$ and $w=D/4$.
For the four field automorphisms $\sigma$ let
$r_\sigma=D/\sigma(D)$. Their normalized Galois-unit columns are
\[
 u_\sigma(i)=\frac{r_\sigma\sigma(d_i)}{L},
 \qquad d_i=\FPdim(B_i).
\]
The columns are real and orthonormal by the standard modular Galois
identities \cite{DLNGalois}. There is one selfdual object $W$ of
dimension $w$, with restriction
\[
 F(W)=2g+2X_1+2X_2+2X_3+2X_4+3X_5+3X_6.
\]
Its normalized column, in the order $(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3,W,\ldots)$, is
\[
 u_W=(1,1,1,1,-2,0,\ldots,0)/(2\sqrt2).
\]
Indeed the source degree-two character is $2$ at $1$, $-2$ at $g$, and
zero elsewhere. The distinguished center-row formula
\cite{DeshpandeCenter}, followed by semion splitting, gives precisely
this row; no high $S$-entry is guessed.

\begin{proposition}[Spectral reconstruction]
In every retained pattern the fusion matrix of $W$ is forced:
\[
 N_W=\sum_\sigma\frac{w}{r_\sigma}u_\sigma u_\sigma^{\mathsf T}
                  -2u_Wu_W^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It has rank five and trace $142$. The fusion matrix of $B_3$ is also
forced in patterns $49$ and $191$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The known $W$ row gives fusion eigenvalues $w/r_\sigma$ on the four
Galois-unit columns, $-2$ at $W$, and zero elsewhere. Verlinde gives the
first formula. Its trace is
$\sum_\sigma\sigma(D)/4-2=142$.

For $B_3$, write
$\lambda(i)=r_3\sigma_3(d_i)/d_i$. Away from the four Galois-unit columns,
these eigenvalues are $\pm1$. Thus
\[
 N_{B_3}=M+Q,\qquad
 M=-I+\sum_\sigma(\lambda(B_\sigma)+1)
                       u_\sigma u_\sigma^{\mathsf T},
\]
where $Q$ is twice a positive-semidefinite projection. In patterns $49$
and $191$, $\operatorname{tr}(Q)=4$. The four known $S$ rows determine
the leading $4\times4$ block of $N_{B_3}$, and
$N_{B_3}(i,W)=N_W(i,B_3)$ supplies its $W$ entries. Subtracting $M$
gives the leading block
\[
 Q_{\le5}=\frac14
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&-1&-1&3&-2\\-1&3&3&-1&-2\\-1&3&3&-1&-2\\
 3&-1&-1&3&-2\\-2&-2&-2&-2&4
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
It already has trace four. All other diagonal entries of $Q$ vanish;
positivity then makes their whole rows and columns zero. Hence the full
matrix is determined. In patterns $40,186$, the residual projection has
rank three, so this argument does not fix the whole second matrix.
\end{proof}

All four $N_W$ and both forced $N_{B_3}$ are nonnegative integral,
have the required unit rows and dimension eigenvectors, and pass the
exact commutation and square checks. Their common leading $N_W$ block
has $N_{WW}^{W}=17$. The braided parity theorem of
Fuchs--Runkel--Schweigert \cite{FRSEven} therefore forces
$\nu_2(W)=1$. The corresponding semion-twisted canonical summand has
indicator $-1$ and multiplicity two, consistently with its symmetric
Frobenius pairing. These matrices and indicators are necessary data,
not modular completions or categorical constructions. None of the four
patterns is eliminated.

\subsection{Whole-restriction Galois orbits for R941}
For R941 the complete source character table lies in
$K=\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$: four characters are the FP conjugates and the
remaining degree-two character is $(2,0,0,-2,0,0,0,0)$. Their values on
the center basis
\[
 1,\ g,\ X_1+X_2,\ X_4+X_5,\ X_6+X_7
\]
have determinant $3072$ and therefore separate central elements.
For a central simple $Z$, the distinguished center entries are
\[
 s(A_E,Z)=\frac{\chi_E(FZ)}{(\dim E)f_E};
\]
see \cite{DeshpandeCenter}. A modular Galois automorphism fixing $K$
fixes all these entries. Its column sign is positive because the unit
row lies in $K$ and is positive. Hence all characters agree on
$FZ$ and its Galois translate; centrality of these classes gives equality
of their full restriction vectors.

If a twist order is divisible by nine, choose the automorphism with
exponent $25$ on the $2$- and $3$-primary cyclotomic parts and a unit on
every other prime part. It fixes $K$ and acts on ninth-root exponents by
its square, namely multiplication by four. Thus it forces at least
three distinct central simples with the same restriction vector.
For the residual high-column Gram matrix $Q_0$, every such vector
therefore satisfies
\[
 3vv^{\mathsf T}\le Q_0,\qquad
 3v^{\mathsf T}Q_0^{-1}v\le1.
\]
Exact arithmetic excludes twists of order divisible by nine on $43$ of
the $65$ column types. All fourteen retained allocations still have
eligible fibers of multiplicity at least three. No allocation is
eliminated, no exponent bound $48$ follows, and R941 remains unresolved.
