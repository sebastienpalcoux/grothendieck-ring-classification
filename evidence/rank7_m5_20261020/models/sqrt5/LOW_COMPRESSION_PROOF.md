# Full low-block compression and its equality refinement

This note concerns a hypothetical **pseudo-unitary** categorification of
R7_5_3895 or R7_5_5039. The separate general-to-positive reduction is not part
of this certificate. The conclusions here prune one low frame and constrain
the twists in every remaining frame; they are not a complete exclusion by
themselves.

The primary representation-theoretic input is A. Schopieray, *The dimension
block of a spherical fusion category*, arXiv:2609.35287v1, Lemma 4.1 and the
proof of Theorem 5.1. Its local lemma applies to **all** torus characters,
including the odd quadratic character of conductor 8. Let `s` be the normalized
center S-matrix, `U` the unipotent subgroup generated by the twist, and `P` the
projection onto all twist-fixed vectors. On the torus-character subspace
`V^U_chi`, the operator `P s P` has norm at most `cond(chi)^(-1/2)`.

## The complete I(1) block

For a commutative source ring its seven characters index the seven distinct
simple summands `A_E` of `I(1)`. If `v_F` is the multiplicity vector of
`F(A_F)`, the normalized block is

\[
s_{E,F}=\frac{\chi_E(v_F)}{f_E}.
\]

The equality follows from the center-character formula and
`dim(A_E)=D/f_E`. Its Galois action permutes these rows without signs:
applying a field automorphism gives `chi_sigma(E)/f_sigma(E)`. Hence the
torus-isotypic subspaces below really lie in `V^U_chi`. Restricting the target
of `P s P` to this seven-dimensional space preserves the upper norm bound.

Each ring has two pairs of characters exchanged by `sqrt(5) -> -sqrt(5)`:
the FP pair and the pair of formal codegree 5. In the orthonormal basis
consisting of the two differences divided by `sqrt(2)`, the complete
conductor-five block in **every** character-compatible low frame is

\[
B_5=\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt5/10&0\\0&\delta\sqrt5/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\tfrac15 I-B_5^*B_5=
\begin{pmatrix}3/20&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \delta\in\{-1,1\}.
\]

Thus the codegree-five difference saturates the conductor-five bound. The
value of `delta` depends on the labelling of the two codegree-five summands;
the certificate records it for each exact assignment. The FP difference
alone does not saturate the bound.

R5039 also has the two characters

\[
\chi_\pm=(1,-1\pm\sqrt{-2},-1\mp\sqrt{-2},0,0,0,1),\qquad f_\pm=8.
\]

Their difference is a twist-fixed vector of odd conductor-eight character.
If `v` is the restriction row for `A_+`, the compression on the normalized
difference is

\[
B_{-8}=\frac{\sqrt{-2}}4(v_1-v_2).
\]

Therefore `|v_1-v_2|<=1`. The frame containing the two rows
`(1,0,2,2,2,2,3)` and `(1,2,0,2,2,2,3)` violates this bound, since
`|B_{-8}|^2=1/2>1/8`. The frame with three identical codegree-eight rows
`(1,1,1,2,2,2,3)` has zero compression and survives this particular test.
The imaginary character field implies that 8 divides the FS exponent, but
that fact alone is compatible with the Cauchy prime support `{2,5}`.

## Equality forces level five

Here is the equality refinement of the cited local computation. In the
universal module `Ind_U^{SL_2(Z/5^a)} 1`, use the primitive-vector model from
Lemma 4.1. On the quadratic conductor-five torus subspace, its compression
has a one-dimensional big-cell part and a small-cell part. Write

\[
F(p,q)=\chi_5(q)[q\text{ is a unit}],\qquad
G(p,q)=\chi_5(-p)[q\in5\mathbb Z/5^a].
\]

Then `P s F=G`, `P s G=F/5`, and the remaining small-cell kernel has
compression zero. Consequently the two extremal eigenlines are
`F+delta sqrt(5) G`. Both functions depend only on `(p,q) mod 5`; their
generated representations factor through `SL_2(F_5)`. This description
transfers to every unitary representation by the Frobenius-reciprocity
embedding used in Lemma 4.1.

At any other prime the character is trivial and the local bound is 1.
Equality there means that both `w` and `s w` are fixed by the unipotent,
so `w` is fixed by the unipotent and its Weyl conjugate, which generate the
local group. These local factors act trivially. Tensor decomposition over
the prime powers then shows that an extremal conductor-five vector generates
a representation of level five.

The arithmetic in this application has
`D=80(9+4 sqrt(5))`, with `9+4 sqrt(5)` an algebraic unit. The Cauchy theorem
of Bruillard--Ng--Rowell--Wang, *Rank-finiteness for modular categories*,
arXiv:1310.7050v3, Theorem 3.9, restricts the exponent primes to 2 and 5;
the argument above also disposes of any other trivial-character local factors.

## The sign of the fifth-root phase

The certificate independently checks the equality representation on the
24 nonzero vectors in `F_5^2`. For `w_delta=F+delta sqrt(5) G`, the span
generated by `s w_delta` has dimension 3, and the twist has polynomial

\[
(x-1)\left(x^2+\frac{1-\delta\sqrt5}{2}x+1\right).
\]

The nontrivial twists are precisely the primitive fifth roots with
Legendre symbol `delta`. Their relative first and second traces over
`Q(sqrt(5))` are

\[
\left(\frac{-1+\delta\sqrt5}{4},
      \frac{-1-\delta\sqrt5}{4}\right).
\]

One may alternatively obtain the sign using the twist equation and norms.
For the unnormalized difference `w` of the two codegree-five summands,
`||w||^2=2`, `P s w=(delta/sqrt(5))w`; hence the twist-one squared weight
is `2/5`. The remaining squared weight is `8/5`. The identity
`<w,s T s w>=<w,s w>=2 delta/sqrt(5)` forces all remaining weight into
the primitive fifth-root class `delta`.

Finally, the already known `I(1)` projection attains the full compression
norm. There is no additional twist-one component of `s w`. Its coordinate
at a center simple object with restriction vector `v` is a nonzero constant
times `v_4-v_5` in R3895, or `v_3-v_4` in R5039. Every **additional** center
row with unequal entries at those two indices therefore has primitive
fifth-root twist of class `delta`; twist one is excluded for such a row.
In the trace engine's scale-eight convention its only permitted phase is
`(-2,2*delta,-2,-2*delta)`.

## Reproduction

`verify_low_compression.py` first replays the pinned transport certificate,
then reconstructs the complete low-frame inventory, exact character table,
and every character-reciprocity assignment. It verifies all matrices above,
including the level-five finite representation and the conductor-eight cut.
Run it normally and with `python -O`. Its receipt is
`low_compression_certificate.json`. The surviving character-labelled frame
indices are 6 and 8 for R3895, and 1 and 3 for R5039; conductor eight removes
the latter frame 1. Frame indices refer to the current exact enumeration
including duality closure, and the receipt also stores the actual rows.
