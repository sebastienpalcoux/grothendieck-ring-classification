# A necessary rank-ten Morita reduction for R7_5_443

This is an exact finite reduction of a hypothetical general complex fusion
categorification. It supplies neither a categorical construction nor an
exclusion of R7_5_443. Four labelled based-ring completions remain after
the checks recorded here. The separate center-trace and sphericalization
investigations determine whether further exclusions can be made.

## Algebra and module category

Write `s=sqrt(15)`, `b=4+s`, `c=5+s`, `d=3+s`. The census dimensions
are `(1,1,b,c,c,b,2d)`. Its involution `g=X1` fixes `Z=X6`; this rank-one
pointed module trivializes the associator on `<g>`. Thus `A=1+g` is a
connected separable algebra in every hypothetical categorification.

The complete anti-invariant module enumeration has exactly one solution,
with scalar values `(1,1,-1,0,0,-1,1)` on the seven source simples.
The right-A-module category has five simple objects of relative dimensions
`(1,b,c,d,d)`, labelled `A,B,C,P,Q`. The source involution acts as the
identity permutation on these five module objects. All action matrices
are regenerated by the verifier.

The category D of A-bimodules has ten simple objects, two over each of
`A,B,C,P,Q`, and dimensions `1²,b²,c²,d⁴`. Indeed evaluation at A has
Gram matrix `I+M_g=2I`, whose nonnegative integral Gram decomposition
has exactly two singleton columns at each coordinate.

## The simultaneous four-index identity

For left and right action matrices, use row conventions

`M_i[p,q]=[X_i tensor m_p:m_q]` and
`N_Y[p,a]=[m_p tensor_A Y:m_a]`.

Morita tensor-Hom adjunction gives

`sum_i M_i[p,q] M_i[a,b] = sum_Y N_Y[p,a] N_Y[q,b]`.

This is the four-index form of the internal-end evaluation Gram identity.
It follows by decomposing the two internal Homs and using the adjunction
between `M tensor_D M*` and `M* tensor_C M`. Setting `p=q` gives the
individual internal-end Gram constraints used in the earlier manuscript.
Every N_Y also commutes with every M_i, is nonnegative integral, and has
one of the five singleton first rows, each occurring twice.

The joint Gram diagonal bounds every matrix entry. Solving the rational
commutant equations leaves two free entries in each first-row type.
Exhaustive integer enumeration gives respectively `(2,4,5,6,6)` matrix
domains. Their unordered pairs reduce, using the joint Gram bounds, to
`(1,2,3,3,3)` possibilities. Exactly one of the 54 combinations satisfies
the full four-index identity. It is recorded in
`r443_morita_reduction.json`.

The resulting dual action is noncommutative. Consequently this particular
Morita dual is not the braided rank-ten super-modular category from the
C6 near-group center. Equal dimension and rank do not identify those
categories.

## Complete based-ring completion

Number the dual simples by first-row type, two in each of the five types.
The nontrivial invertible has left and right permutations

`L=(1,0,3,2,5,4,7,6,9,8)` and
`R=(1,0,3,2,5,4,8,9,6,7)`.

The two b-objects and the two c-objects are exchanged on each side. For
these free source orbits this follows from twisting the character of the
diagonal C2 stabilizer. The four d-objects form the displayed left and
right action square. Matrix transposition determines all duals except
whether the c-objects are self-dual or exchanged.

Impose unit, duality, Frobenius reciprocity, the left/right invertible
actions, and the exact equations

`N_i N_j = sum_k n_ij^k N_k`.

The exchanged-c alternative forces a fusion coefficient `3/2` and is
impossible. In the self-dual-c alternative the linear equations leave
four actual fusion coefficients as parameters. Their complete
nonnegative integer domain has 72 points. Checking all associativity
equations leaves exactly ten labelled based rings.

Four fail the formal-codegree d-number criterion: their regular Casimir
polynomials contain `t²-34t+136` or `t²-82t+1640`. This criterion applies
also to noncommutative rings: a regular Casimir eigenvalue is an integer
multiple of a formal codegree, and the d-number property is invariant
under nonzero rational scaling. Two further completions contain a closed
rank-five, multiplicity-three subring excluded by the existing rank-five
classification. Their exact subring certificates are included.

The four remaining labelled rings pass these two checks. Two have
regular Casimir polynomial

`(t-20)(t-12)(t-10)^4(t-6)^2(t²-480t+3600)`;

the other two have

`(t-70)(t-28)(t-10)^4(t-6)(t-4)(t²-480t+3600)`.

No associator is constructed for any of these rings. They are necessary
Morita alternatives, not categorical models.

## Reproduction and status

Run `python evidence/rank7_m5_20261020/models/verify_r443_morita_reduction.py`
from the repository. The verifier pins the source tensor, regenerates all
finite domains, and uses exact integer/rational arithmetic. It does not
call an optimizer or Z3. The older `*_exploration.json` files and
`r443_ring_search.py` record discovery only; the authoritative receipt
for this reduction is `r443_morita_reduction.json`.

The scope is general complex fusion categories. This reduction does not
require pivotality, sphericality, pseudo-unitarity, or unitarity. Applying
further dimension or center arguments to the four surviving alternatives
would require checking the scope of those additional arguments separately.
