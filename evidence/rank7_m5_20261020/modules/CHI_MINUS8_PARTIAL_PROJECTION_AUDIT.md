# Independent audit: a conductor-eight partial-projection ladder

Let `chi=chi_{-8}` be the primitive odd quadratic character modulo eight:
its values at `1,3,5,7` are `1,1,-1,-1`. In a unitary congruence
representation of `SL_2(Z)`, let `V^U_chi` denote the twist-fixed vectors
with this diagonal-torus character. For `r=0,1,2`, set

`R_r=P_{<T^{2^r}>} S`.

Then, uniformly over the congruence level,

`||R_r w||^2 <= 2^{r-3} ||w||^2` for `w in V^U_chi`.

This note independently verifies the model agent's conductor-eight
calculation, including all higher powers of two. The proof below also
identifies the equality space. No bounded-level numerical extrapolation
is used.

## Local universal representation

Put `M=2^a`, `a>=3`, and realize `Ind_U^{SL_2(Z/M)} 1` on primitive
vectors `(p,q)` modulo `M`. Its torus-character and unipotent-fixed
subspace is an orthogonal sum of the one-dimensional big cell

`F(p,q)=chi(q)[q odd]`

and a small cell supported on `q` even and `p` odd. Every small-cell
function has the form

`f(p,q)=chi(p) g(pq)` for `q` even and `p` odd,

where `g(z)=f(1,z)` is defined on the even residues. Invariance under the
upper unipotent gives

`g(z)=chi(1+u z) g(z+u z^2)`.

The map `z -> z+u z^2` is a permutation of the even residues and of each
residue class used below: its difference at `z,z'` is
`(z-z')(1+u(z+z'))`, and the second factor is odd. Taking `u=2` on
`z=2 mod4`, and `u=1` on `z=4 mod8`, gives respectively

`sum_{z=2 mod4} g(z)=0`, and `sum_{z=4 mod8} g(z)=0`,

because the character multiplier is minus one in each case.

Use the Weyl convention `(S f)(p,q)=f(-q,p)`. An overall Weyl sign does
not affect the resulting norm bounds. Averaging the translates by
`T^{2^r}` now gives, for all three values of `r`,

`R_r F(p,q)=chi(p)[q=0 mod 2^{3-r}]`,

and

`R_r f(p,q)=chi(-q)*(2^r/M)*sum_{z=0 mod8}g(z)`

on `q` odd and `p=0 mod2^r`, and zero elsewhere. For the second formula,
the averaging variable traverses the congruence class of `-pq` modulo
`2^r`. The two cancellation identities remove all even residues outside
`0 mod8`; odd residues contribute zero by small-cell support.

The first output has squared norm `2^{r-3}||F||^2`. For the second,
there are `M^2/2^{r+1}` nonzero positions, so

`||R_r f||^2 = 2^{r-1}|sum_{z=0 mod8}g(z)|^2`

`<= M*2^{r-4} sum_{z=0 mod8}|g(z)|^2`

`<= M*2^{r-4} sum_{z even}|g(z)|^2`

`= 2^{r-3} ||f||^2`.

The big-cell and small-cell output supports have opposite parity of `q`,
so there are no cross terms. This proves the local operator bound.

Equality on the small cell requires `g` to be constant on `0 mod8` and
zero elsewhere. Consequently the complete largest-singular-value space
is

`span(F, chi(p)[q=0 mod8])`.

Both functions are pulled back from primitive vectors modulo eight.

## Transfer and other prime factors

Every irreducible representation with nonzero unipotent-fixed vectors
embeds in the universal representation by Frobenius reciprocity; the
embedding can be rescaled to an isometry. Thus the local norm bound
transfers to arbitrary unitary representations by orthogonal
decomposition. Chinese remaindering gives tensor products of local
representations. At odd primes, the subgroup generated by `T^{2^r}` is
the full upper unipotent, and its compression has norm at most one.
Hence the same bound holds globally.

If equality holds globally, the other prime factors act trivially on
the relevant cyclic representation: norm-one compression means both
the vector and its Weyl transform are upper-unipotent-fixed, hence the
vector is fixed by upper and lower unipotents. At the two-primary factor
the exact equality space above lies in the modulo-eight pullback
subrepresentation. Thus equality forces the cyclic representation to
factor through level eight.

## Exact ratios and projection only on the two-primary factor

The explicit formulas prove more than the bound: on the entire local
domain,

`R_1^* R_1 = 2 R_0^* R_0`, and `R_2^* R_2 = 4 R_0^* R_0`.

Indeed each big-cell and small-cell squared norm scales by `2^r`, and
their images remain orthogonal. These identities also follow for mixed
vectors by polarization.

Let `Q_r` now project only on the two-primary unipotent subgroup generated
by `T^{2^r}`, tensor the identity at all odd prime factors. This is a
different projector from the global `P_{<T^{2^r}>}` above: it retains
every odd component of the twist. The odd Weyl operators are unitary,
so the exact norm ratios and the bound remain valid for `Q_r S`.
Consequently, if `b=||Q_0 S w||^2`, the squared masses of `S w` with
two-primary twist order exactly `1`, `2`, and `4` are

`b`, `b`, and `2b`, respectively, with `b<=||w||^2/8`.

Equality for this projector forces only the two-primary cyclic factor
to descend to level eight; it imposes no triviality assertion on the
odd factors.

## Exact extension to the eighth-power projector

The ratio extends once more, to `r=3`. The first cancellation above is
stronger than initially used: `z -> z+2z^2` preserves each of the classes
`z=2 mod8` and `z=6 mod8` individually, and its character multiplier is
minus one on each. Their two sums therefore vanish separately. The
`z=4 mod8` sum also vanishes as before. Consequently

`R_3 f(p,q)=chi(-q)*(8/M)*sum_{z=0 mod8}g(z)`

on `q` odd and `p=0 mod8`, and zero elsewhere. Its squared norm is
`4|sum g|^2`, exactly eight times the `r=0` squared norm. The big-cell
output is `R_3 F(p,q)=chi(p)[p odd]`, on all primitive vectors; its
squared norm equals `||F||^2`, also eight times its `r=0` value. The
two outputs are disjoint by parity of `p`. It follows that

`R_3^*R_3=8 R_0^*R_0`, and `||Q_3 S w||^2=8b`.

Thus the exact two-primary order-eight squared mass is `4b`, in
addition to the previously established masses `b,b,2b` at orders
one, two, and four. This calculation uses no normalized row transport
or assumption about its signs. The finite checker verifies all four
operator identities at moduli eight and sixteen.

## Application to R7_5_5039

For the difference `w_8` of the two induced-unit constituents of formal
codegree eight, `||w_8||^2=2`, and the character table gives

`(S w_8)_Y = (sqrt(-2)/4)(v_1-v_2)`.

Therefore all center rows, counted with their actual integral
multiplicities, satisfy

`sum_Y (v_1-v_2)^2 = 16`,

while the rows whose twist order divides `1`, `2`, or `4` contribute at
most `2`, `4`, or `8`, respectively. In the remaining low frame all
known induced-unit rows have `v_1=v_2`, so these budgets concern only
the additional center rows. These are necessary inequalities, not a
categorification or a standalone exclusion.

Using the exact ratios and the known exponent prime support `{2,5}` gives
the stronger separate bounds. In squared-difference units, the combined
nonzero phase groups of root orders `{1,5}`, `{2,10}`, and `{4,20}` have
budgets at most `2`, `2`, and `4`, respectively. Roots with an odd
component divisible by `25` have zero first and second relative traces
and can hide additional mass in any of these two-primary classes;
therefore the phase-group statements are inequalities, not equalities.

## Rejected unsigned-transport strengthening

An attempted strengthening of the phase-group budgets was rejected during
independent review. It assumed that every active row had an orbit of
squared-difference cost at least four. Actual normalized row transport
can be the negative of the rational linear map, so that assertion is
false. For example, in R7_5_5039 the row
`v=(0,1,0,1,1,1,1)` satisfies `T(v)=-dual(v)`; its actual row orbit can
have two members of difference one, with total cost two.

Consequently this note does **not** establish a pointwise product-zero
condition, an exclusion of all higher five-primary twist orders, or a
zero-phase requirement for every conductor-eight-active row. Any earlier
claims to those effects are retracted. The uniform projection ladder,
exact norm ratios, and phase-group budgets proved above do not use the
incorrect unsigned-transport assumption and remain valid.
