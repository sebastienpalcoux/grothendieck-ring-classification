# The Haagerup extension obstruction for R8_3_455

**Conclusion:** `R8_3_455` has no unitary categorification. The general
complex categorification problem remains open under this argument.

## Reusable criterion

Let `C` be any complex fusion category with a faithful finite-group
grading and neutral component `D`. If `BrPic(D)` is trivial, every
homogeneous component has rank `rank(D)`: each component is an
invertible `D-D` bimodule, its class is therefore the regular class,
and a bimodule equivalence is in particular an equivalence of
semisimple categories. This criterion does not require unitarity.
Its application here uses a unitary classification to identify `D`.

Separately, whenever a pointed subcategory `Vec(G,omega)` fixes a
simple object `Y`, the span of `Y` is a rank-one module category.
The scalar module associator is a 2-cochain whose coboundary is
`omega`, so the pointed associator class vanishes. This second
criterion also holds without unitarity. It makes it possible to
apply a classification theorem that requires an untwisted pointed
subcategory; it does not assume that requirement from the fusion
rules alone.

## The pointed twist must vanish

Suppose a categorification exists, and let `D` be its neutral category.
Its pointed subcategory is `Vec(C3,omega)` for some associator
3-cocycle. The additive category generated by `Y` is a rank-one left
module over that pointed subcategory, because every invertible fixes
`Y`. Choose isomorphisms `u_g:g tensor Y -> Y`. Their composition
scalars give a 2-cochain `mu(g,h)`. The module pentagon says that its
coboundary is `omega` (or its inverse under the opposite convention).
Consequently `[omega]=0`.

This step is essential. Izumi explicitly records twisted categories
with the same `C3` Haagerup fusion rules after Definition 2.7 in
*The classification of 3^n subfactors and related fusion categories*,
[arXiv:1609.07604v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07604v2), printed p.12.
The fusion rules of the neutral category alone do not identify it with
a known Haagerup category.

In Izumi's notation, we have proved `c^(0,3)(D)=0`. His second
obstruction belongs to `H^1(C2,H^2(C3,C^*))` (Section 2.1, pp.10--11).
Since the Schur multiplier `H^2(C3,C^*)` vanishes, this obstruction
vanishes too. The latter standard fact also follows by rescaling a
projective lift of the cyclic generator so that its cube is the
identity. Thus `D` satisfies the untwisted hypotheses of Definition 2.7.
Neither untwisting step requires unitarity.

## The unitary rank contradiction

Now assume the full category is unitary, so `D` is unitary. Izumi's
Theorem 5.9 and Theorem 9.1 (pp.36 and 57) classify untwisted
C*-Haagerup categories for `C3` by exactly two solutions. Section 9.2
identifies the solution without the indicated Q-system with the
Grossman--Snyder category; the two possibilities are the known `H2`
and `H3`.

Grossman--Snyder, *Quantum subgroups of the Haagerup fusion categories*,
[arXiv:1102.2631v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2631v2), Theorem 1.5
(printed p.5), says that between any two of `H1,H2,H3`, including an
equal pair, there is exactly one Morita equivalence up to equivalence
of bimodule categories. In particular `BrPic(H2)=BrPic(H3)=1`.
This is the full Brauer--Picard conclusion, not merely the assertion
that their outer tensor automorphism groups vanish.

By Etingof--Nikshych--Ostrik, *Fusion categories and homotopy theory*,
[arXiv:0909.3140v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.3140v2), Theorem 6.1(i),
the odd component of a faithful graded extension is an invertible
`D-D` bimodule category. Its class must therefore be the regular
bimodule class. Equivalence preserves the number of simple objects,
so its rank must equal `rank(D)=6`. Its source rank is two, a
contradiction.

The same argument rules out the proposed extension construction from
either known Haagerup category without separately assuming that the
extension is unitary. It does not classify hypothetical nonunitary
untwisted `C3` Haagerup categories, and is not used to exclude a
general complex categorification of the source ring.
