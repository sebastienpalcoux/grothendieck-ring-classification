# R8_3_436: withdrawal of the TPE argument and an independent general exclusion

Research correction, 24 October 2026. Online publication is deferred.

## What was wrong

The earlier proof `evidence/rank8_frontier_20261021/structural_models/R436_GENERAL_TPE.md`
claimed that every complex near-group category of type `(C3,3)` is Galois
conjugate to a unitary category. Its crucial normalization assertion is not
established by the cited source. In He–Liu–Lu–Palcoux–Ren,
arXiv:2610.11652v1, Corollary 4.7 uses unitary representations and an
orthonormal basis, and the normalization following it uses unitary duality.
The separate algebraic reconstruction theorem constructs a category from
specified scalar data. It does not identify an arbitrary original spherical
category with that constructed category. The old exact scalar computation
establishes neither the missing parameterization nor the needed equivalence.

**Withdraw that general-unitarization proposition and its use in the old
proof.** The present argument uses no near-group associator classification,
unitarization theorem, or TPE parameterization. It replaces the invalid proof;
it does not validate the invalid premise retrospectively.

## Theorem

No complex fusion category has fusion tensor `R8_3_436`.

### 1. Source structure

The tensor SHA256 is
`0f6d68de67a7287db7dd32a786237f7af9f35ec7c26b3ada2ba50012ff5455c7`.
It has a faithful `C2` grading whose neutral basis is `[0,1,2,5]` and whose
odd basis is `[3,4,6,7]`. In the neutral component write `g=1`, `X=5`:

\[
g^3=1,\quad gX=Xg=X,\quad X^2=1+g+g^2+3X.
\]

The odd invertible `t=3` obeys `t²=1`, `tgt^{-1}=g^{-1}`, and
`tXt^{-1}=X`. These claims are exact statements about all relevant source
coefficients; the accompanying verifier checks them directly.

Suppose a fusion category `C` realizes the tensor, and let `D=C_e`.

### 2. Removing a possible associator anomaly

Do not assume that the pointed category generated by `t` is untwisted.
Choose skeletal identifications `t⊗t ≅ 1`; their associativity discrepancy
is a normalized scalar 3-cocycle `ω` on `C2`. Twist the associator of the
whole graded category `C` by the inflated cocycle `ω^{-1}`:

\[
\alpha'_{U,V,W}=\omega(|U|,|V|,|W|)^{-1}\alpha_{U,V,W}
\]

for homogeneous objects. The cocycle identity is precisely the additional
pentagon condition. This gives a fusion category with the same fusion ring,
with unchanged neutral category `D`, and with a monoidal section of `C2`
provided by `1,t`: its pointed associator is now trivial.

Conjugation by that monoidal section gives a **coherent categorical action**
of `C2` on `D`. In a strict realization of the section, its functors are
`F_h(U)=t_h⊗U⊗t_h^{-1}`; the tensor structures cancel adjacent
`t_h^{-1}⊗t_h`, and the action composition maps use
`t_h⊗t_k ≅ t_{hk}`. Their coherence is the associativity of the section.
The nontrivial functor interchanges `g,g²` and fixes `X`.
This cocycle cancellation avoids making any claim that an arbitrary bare
order-two autoequivalence automatically admits an action structure.

### 3. Spherical equivariantization

By Thornton, *On braided near-group categories*, Theorem 1.3,
`D` has a spherical structure. That theorem does not assume braiding.
Its spherical dimensions are `dim(g)=1` and

\[
d=\dim(X),\qquad d^2=3+3d,
\]

so its dimension field is `Q(√21)`; either root is allowed.

The pivotal structure of `D` is unique. Indeed, a tensor automorphism of
its identity functor acts by nonzero scalars `λ_g,λ_X` on simple objects.
The summand relation `gX=X` gives `λ_g=1`. The occurrence of `X` in `X²`
gives `λ_X²=λ_X`, hence `λ_X=1`. Any two pivotal structures differ by
such an automorphism. Consequently every tensor autoequivalence preserves
the spherical pivotal structure. The above coherent action therefore
preserves sphericality, and its equivariantization `E=D^{C2}` is spherical.
The pivotal maps of equivariant objects are those of their underlying
objects; equivariance follows from preservation of the pivotal structure.
The forgetful functor is thus spherical and preserves dimensions.

There are exactly five simples of `E`, which we denote
`1, ε, Y, A, B`. They arise from the two fixed neutral simples `1,X`
and the free orbit `{g,g²}`. A fixed simple gives two equivariant lifts,
since a projective representation of `C2` over `C` can be linearized:
rescale its generator to make its square 1. Thus

\[
F(1)=F(\varepsilon)=1,\quad F(Y)=g\oplus g^2,
\quad F(A)=F(B)=X,
\]
\[
\dim(1,\varepsilon,Y,A,B)=(1,1,2,d,d).
\]

The sign representation `ε` is central, satisfies `ε²=1`, fixes `Y`,
and exchanges `A,B`.

### 4. All possible equivariantized fusion rules

The fusion rules are forced up to nonnegative integers `a,b` with `a+b=3`.
First `Y` is self-dual, and forgetful images plus Frobenius reciprocity give

\[
Y^2=1+\varepsilon+Y.
\]

Both `YA` and `AY` have underlying object `2X`. Since `εY=Y` and `ε`
is central, they are invariant under interchanging `A,B`. Hence

\[
YA=AY=A+B,
\]

and the same holds with `B`. Moreover `B=εA`, so `A,B` commute with one
another. These relations prove that the entire based ring is commutative;
commutativity is not inferred merely from commutativity of `K_0(D)`.

The duality must either fix both `A,B` or interchange them. In the latter
case Frobenius reciprocity and the forgetful images force

\[
A^2=\varepsilon+Y+aA+bB,\qquad B^2=A^2.
\]

Dualizing this equality forces `a=b`, contradicting `a+b=3`. Thus both
`A` and `B` are self-dual, and the remaining products are

\[
A^2=B^2=1+Y+aA+bB,
\qquad AB=\varepsilon+Y+bA+aB,
\qquad a+b=3.
\]

All four ordered pairs `(a,b)` are retained. Interchanging `A,B` groups
them into two isomorphism classes, but that identification is unnecessary.

### 5. Formal codegrees contradict the dimension field

Set `s=a-b`; then `s∈{-3,-1,1,3}`. The preceding fusion rules have the
one-dimensional character

\[
\chi(1)=1,\quad\chi(\varepsilon)=-1,\quad\chi(Y)=0,
\quad\chi(A)=x,\quad\chi(B)=-x,
\quad x^2=1+s x.
\]

As all basis elements are self-dual, its formal codegree is

\[
f=\sum_Z\chi(Z)^2=2+2x^2
 =s^2+4\;\pm\;s\sqrt{s^2+4}.
\]

It lies in `Q(√5)\Q` if `|s|=1`, and in `Q(√13)\Q` if `|s|=3`.
Neither field equals `Q(√21)`.

Ostrik, *Pivotal fusion categories of rank 3*, Corollary 2.15, says that
all formal codegrees of a spherical fusion category lie in its dimension
field. For completeness, Theorem 2.13 supplies a central simple of dimension
`dim(E)/f`; forgetting to `E` expresses this as an integral combination of
simple dimensions. Hence both `dim(E)/f` and `dim(E)` lie in the dimension
field, as does `f`.

Our spherical category `E` has dimension field exactly `Q(√21)` by
Section 3. This contradiction proves the theorem. No sign of `d`,
pseudo-unitarity, unitarity, or associator parameterization was assumed.

An independent arithmetic check is also available. The global dimension
`dim(E)=6+2d²=21±3√21` has quadratic norm 252. The two possible formal
codegree norms are 20 and 52. Thus the norm of `dim(E)/f` in the relevant
biquadratic field is respectively `3969/25` or `3969/169`, not an integer.
Such a number cannot be the dimension of a central simple.

## General reusable criterion

The same argument applies to a coherent inversion action on any complex
near-group category `NG(C3,m)` with odd `m>0`. There must exist an odd
integer `s` with `|s|≤m` such that

\[
\mathbb Q\bigl(\sqrt{s^2+4}\bigr)
\subseteq\mathbb Q\bigl(\sqrt{m^2+12}\bigr).
\]

It is a necessary condition, not an existence assertion. At `m=3` it fails
for all possible `s`. The parity assumption is essential: for even `m`,
`s=0` and non-self-dual branches can occur, and the present contradiction
does not apply. In particular it does not exclude the known `C3+6`
equivariantization.

## Scope and publication instructions

* Withdraw the old claimed theorem that every complex `NG(C3,3)` category
  is Galois-unitary. No replacement proof of that stronger assertion is given.
* Replace the old general exclusion with the theorem above after independent
  review. The *conclusion* remains general noncategorifiability, now with a
  different proof and explicit treatment of the pointed associator anomaly.
* The previous unitary inversion proof remains valid independently. Braided
  existence is also impossible from noncommutativity of the source ring.
* The source tensor has coefficient 3. It cannot occur as a fusion subring
  of a tensor of maximum coefficient 1 or 2. Thus this proof correction has
  no negative-subring transfer consequences for the rank9 M2 classification.
* No online files were changed by this research task.
