# A braided obstruction for three rank-nine rings

**Result.** None of `R9_2_137`, `R9_2_1607`, `R9_2_1976` is the Grothendieck ring of a braided complex fusion category. This does **not** decide ordinary, spherical, pseudo-unitary, or unitary categorifiability. No pivotality or unitarity hypothesis is used in the proof.

## A general criterion

Let R be a fusion ring with trivial universal grading group. Suppose it has a basis element g of order two and a simple basis element X satisfying gX=X, such that FPdim(X)/2 is not an algebraic integer. Then R has no braided categorification.

**Proof.** Suppose C were a braided categorification. Monodromy with an invertible object gives a tensor automorphism of the identity functor (DGNO, Lemma 3.31(i)). Since the universal grading is trivial, every such tensor automorphism is the identity (DGNO, Proposition 2.3(iii)). Hence g belongs to the symmetric center C'. The symmetric rank-two subcategory generated by g is either Rep(C2) or sVec. The latter is impossible: a transparent fermion acts without fixed simple objects, by DGNO, Lemma 3.28, which is stated and proved without any pivotality hypothesis.

Thus <g> is Tannakian Rep(C2). De-equivariantization gives a fusion category E with a C2-action and an equivalence C=E^{C2}; see DGNO, Theorem 4.18 and Proposition 4.19. Under this equivalence g is the nontrivial character of C2.

For completeness, the relevant simple-object consequence can be seen directly. A simple equivariant object comes from a C2-orbit of simples in E and an irreducible projective representation of its stabilizer. If the orbit has size one, its stabilizer is C2 and its projective irreducible representations are one-dimensional (H^2(C2,C^*)=0). Tensoring by the nontrivial character exchanges its two equivariant structures, so it cannot fix the simple object. Therefore gX=X forces the underlying orbit to have size two, with trivial stabilizer. The underlying object of X is Y plus t(Y), with Y and t(Y) distinct simple objects of E of the same FP dimension. Tensor functors preserve FP dimensions, hence FPdim(X)=2 FPdim(Y). But FPdim(Y) is an algebraic integer, contrary to the hypothesis. QED.

## Exact witnesses

All three rings have FP dimensions

    1,1,1,1,2,d,d,d,d,   d=4+3 sqrt(2),

in the source-specific order recorded by `verify_braided_obstruction.py`.
The adjoint based subring equals the whole ring: starting with supports of all b_i b_i^*, its closure under multiplication contains all nine basis elements. This is a purely exact tensor check and is precisely the condition that the universal grading is trivial.

One can use the following source-basis witnesses (indices are zero-based):

| Ring | g | X |
|---|---:|---:|
| R9_2_137 | 7 | 3 |
| R9_2_1607 | 4 | 1 |
| R9_2_1976 | 6 | 1 |

Each satisfies g^2=1 and gX=X. The number d/2 has minimal polynomial

    x^2 - 4x - 1/2,

whose constant term is not integral. Equivalently, its field norm is -1/2. It is therefore not an algebraic integer, so the criterion applies.

## What this does and does not provide

This eliminates all braided constructions for the three rings at once. In particular, a future ordinary categorification, if one exists, must genuinely lack a braiding. It does not show that an invertible in an arbitrary nonbraided categorification has a half-braiding. Confusing centrality of a based-ring element with a central object would invalidate that stronger conclusion.
