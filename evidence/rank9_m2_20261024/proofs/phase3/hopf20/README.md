# The dimension-40 rank-one extension family

This argument excludes `R9_2_1225` and `R9_2_1673` over **all complex fusion
categories**, and constructs `R9_2_965`. All indices and basis maps below are
zero-based. The construction also recovers the already realized `R9_2_21`.

## The rank-one module criterion

Let a fusion ring have a fusion subring D of global FP dimension 20 and a
single complementary simple object m, with

`X m = m X = FPdim(X) m`, and `m m = sum_X FPdim(X) X`.

In any categorification, the full additive subcategory generated by m is a
rank-one left D-module category, equivalent to Vec. Its action gives a tensor
functor D -> End(Vec)=Vec. This is a fiber functor: it is exact, and sends every
simple X to a nonzero vector space of dimension FPdim(X), hence is faithful.
Tannaka reconstruction therefore gives D=Rep(H) for a semisimple complex Hopf
algebra H of dimension 20. No unitarity, pseudo-unitarity, braiding or pivotal
assumption enters this argument. In particular, the rank-one module condition
alone suffices; self-duality of H is not needed for this exclusion.

The complete dimension-4p Hopf classification says that the semisimple Hopf
algebras are group algebras, their duals, and the two nontrivial self-dual
Gelaki algebras A0,A1. Cheng--Ng, Introduction, explicitly states this with
reference to Natale's classification. Function Hopf algebras have pointed
representation categories, and group algebras have commutative character
rings. The character rings of A0 and A1 are also commutative, as follows
directly from the explicit construction below.

Both rings1225 and1673 have odd basis index5 and neutral basis
`[0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8]`, of dimensions `1^4,2^4`. Their neutral rings are
noncommutative: for1225 the products1*2 and2*1 differ; for1673 the products1*3
and3*1 differ. Therefore neither can have a complex categorification.

## Explicit Hopf algebras and their characters

For any odd prime p and t=0,1, use the Andruskiewitsch--Natale construction
(Proposition2.2.4), with N=Cp, G=Gamma=C2, both acting on N by inversion,
and cocycle sigma(g,g)(u)=(-1)^t. Write basis elements

`b(a,e,b)=x^a delta_e g^b`, a in Z/p, e,b in Z/2.

The structure maps are

```
b(a,e,b) b(c,f,d)
  = delta(e,f) (-1)^(t e b d) b(a+(-1)^b c,e,b+d),
Delta b(a,e,b)
  = sum_(s=0,1) b(a,s,b) tensor b((-1)^s a,e-s,b),
epsilon b(a,e,b)=delta(e,0).
```

The four one-dimensional simples L(e,s), e,s in Z/2, have x=1 and
g=(-1)^s i^(te), supported on delta_e. The remaining p-1 simples V(e,j),
1<=j<=(p-1)/2, have dimension2, x-eigenvalues zeta_p^(+/-j), and g interchanges
these eigenspaces. These exhaust the simple modules, since their squared
dimensions sum to4p. Their characters are invariant under a -> -a. Thus the
inversion occurring in the second leg of Delta is invisible in every
character product, so their character ring is commutative for both t.

The exact fusion rules are

```
L(e,s)L(f,r)=L(e+f,s+r+t e f),
L(e,s)V(f,j)=V(f,j)L(e,s)=V(e+f,j),
V(e,j)V(f,k)=V(e+f,j+k)+V(e+f,j-k),
```

where V(e,-j)=V(e,j) and V(e,0)=L(e,0)+L(e,1). The script `hopf20.py`
constructs these tensors independently from the census.

## Symmetric self-duality and the extension

Set zeta=zeta_p. The nondegenerate symmetric Hopf pairing furnished by
Andruskiewitsch--Natale(3.2.5),(4.2.1) is

`P(b(a,e,b),b(c,f,d))=delta(e,d) delta(f,b) zeta^(a c) i^(t b d)`.

Its nonzero blocks are Fourier matrices times phases, so it is invertible.
Symmetry is literal. The script checks the Hopf pairing multiplication
identities on all(4p)^3 basis triples. Symmetry supplies the identity on the
other side as well. Let Gamma0:H*->H be the inverse pairing, using the
ordinary dual Hopf algebra. Its corresponding symmetric copairing is

`gamma0=(1/p) sum_(a,c,e,b) zeta^(-ac) i^(-t e b)
       b(a,e,b) tensor b(c,b,e)`.

**Dual-Hopf convention:** Davydov--Runkel use H^vee=(H*)^(op,cop), as
specified in their equation2.3 and footnote2. Thus their theorem must be
applied with `Gamma=S Gamma0`. Consequently

`(id tensor Gamma)coev=(S tensor id)gamma0
      =(1/p) sum_(a,c,e,b) zeta^((-1)^(e+b) a c) i^(t e b)
        b(a,e,b) tensor b(c,b,e)`.

The antipode gives the required Hopf isomorphism from the opposite product
and coproduct. This expression is symmetric, either from its displayed
formula or from P(Sx,y)=P(x,Sy). Equation2.28 of Davydov--Runkel inserts an
additional inverse antipode after Gamma. Thus their actual associator
copairing is `gamma=gamma0`. Equivalently, one applies their Corollary3.19
directly to gamma0, with its ordinary Hopf-copairing identities and symmetry.
The cointegral normalization in that corollary includes `(id tensor S)`,
which has no effect because hS=h.

The normalized Haar cointegral is
`h(b(a,e,b))=delta(a,0)delta(b,0)/2`.
Set lambda=sqrt(4p)h. Then the distinguished grouplike is1, the antipode
squares to1, and `(lambda tensor lambda)(gamma)=1` (already
`(h tensor h)(gamma)=1/(4p)`). Therefore every condition in
Davydov--Runkel Theorem2.5 is satisfied, including its crucial symmetry
condition(c). Their rigidity result in Section3.6 gives a fusion category

`C_t(p)=Rep(A_t) + Vec`,

whose odd simple m has dimension sqrt(4p), with the fusion rules stated in
the first section. This is an actual coherent extension; Hopf self-duality
alone was not used as an existence assertion.

## Unitarity

The Hopf algebras above have their evident compact involution: x and g are
unitary and delta_e are self-adjoint projections. Explicitly,

`b(a,e,b)*=(-1)^(t e b)b(-(-1)^b a,e,b)`.

This is a Hopf involution, and h is positive: the displayed basis is
orthogonal with h(B*B)=1/2. Give H its Haar inner product. The copairing gamma
is unitary in H tensor H: in each central block delta_e tensor delta_b it
is a phase times the unitary g^b tensor g^e and a Fourier bicharacter
element `(1/p)sum_(a,c)zeta^(+/-ac)x^a tensor x^c`. The same argument applies
to (S tensor id)gamma. The Fourier operator phi in Davydov--Runkel's
associator, `phi=(id tensor lambda)(S tensor multiplication)(gamma tensor id)`,
is explicitly

`phi(b(a,e,b))=i^(-t e b)/sqrt(p)
              sum_u zeta^(-(-1)^e u a) b(u,b,e)`.

It is unitary, being a direct sum of normalized Fourier matrices times
phases, with a permutation of the four blocks. The remaining Hopf
transport maps in their associators are unitary for the Haar inner product:
for example `h tensor v -> h_(1) tensor h_(2)v` is an isometry by the
cointegral identity, and its antipode formula gives its inverse. In the
odd-even-odd case, the regular leg acts by right multiplication composed
with S, which is a star representation; thus the unitary gamma0 again acts
unitarily. Equivalently, that regular leg uses the opposite algebra. This
accounts explicitly for the order reversal from the antipode. All eight
associator cases in Section2.2 are compositions of these unitary transports,
representations of gamma or (S tensor id)gamma, the unitary phi, and tensor
permutations. Thus the construction is a unitary fusion category.
