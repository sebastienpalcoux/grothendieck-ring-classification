# Independent Hopf-algebra audit for R9_2_1225 and R9_2_1673

Both proposed exclusions are approved in general complex scope. No
pseudo-unitary assumption, sphericalization gate, or Hopf self-duality
assumption is needed for this argument.

## A rank-one component forces a genuine fiber functor

In either source tensor, the eight labels other than 5 are a dual-closed
fusion subring D with dimensions 1^4,2^4 and FP dimension 20. The remaining
simple X=5 satisfies YX=XY=FPdim(Y) X for every simple Y in D, and
X²=sum_Y FPdim(Y)Y. Thus a hypothetical categorification has a rank-one
D-module category, the additive subcategory generated by X.

Identifying that semisimple linear category with Vec, the left action gives
a strong tensor functor D->End(Vec)=Vec. It is exact and faithful, as any
nonzero tensor functor between fusion categories is, and hence is a fiber
functor. Ordinary Tannaka reconstruction yields D=Rep(H) for a semisimple
Hopf algebra H over C, of dimension 20. The grading also makes this module
invertible, but that stronger fact is not required.

## The dimension-twenty list is exhaustive

Cheng-Ng, On Hopf algebras of dimension 4p, arXiv:1005.1267v2, introduction,
explicitly states that the semisimple case at dimension 20 is classified:
besides group and dual group algebras there are exactly two nontrivial
semisimple Hopf algebras A0,A1. The Frobenius-type hypothesis in the older
classification is supplied there by the cited ENO result. It is therefore
not an extra assumption on the hypothetical H.

Natale, On the semisolvability of semisimple Hopf algebras of low dimension,
arXiv:math/0305038v2, Section 5.2, identifies A0,A1 as self-dual, with
G(A0)=V4 and G(A1)=C4. Proposition 5.2.1 identifies A0 as a Drinfeld twist
of a group algebra. These facts alone exclude R9_2_1225: its neutral
Grothendieck ring is noncommutative with V4 invertibles. Group representation
rings and K0(Rep(A0)) are commutative, a dual group algebra has a pointed
rank20 category, and Rep(A1) has C4 invertibles.

## A direct character argument also excludes R9_2_1673

To avoid relying on grouplikes to distinguish the two residual cases, one can
compute both A0 and A1 character rings directly from their primary
construction. Andruskiewitsch-Natale, Examples of Self-Dual Hopf Algebras,
J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 6 (1999), Proposition 2.2.4 and Section 2.4,
gives a basis e(i,j,l)=x^i#delta_j#g^l, where i is modulo 5 and j,l modulo 2.
For kappa=0,1 the multiplication has g²=(-1)^(kappa*j) in sector j, and

  Delta(e(i,j,l)) = sum_(s+t=j) e(i,s,l) tensor e((-1)^s i,t,l).

The irreducibles are the following eight characters. In each sector j there
are two one-dimensional characters with x=1 and g=+i^(kappa*j) or
-i^(kappa*j). There are also two two-dimensional characters indexed by
{a,-a} in F5 nonzero, a=1,2; their value at e(i,r,l) is zero unless r=j and
l=0, in which case it is zeta5^(ai)+zeta5^(-ai).

These characters are exhaustive since their squared dimensions sum to 20.
Every character is invariant under replacing i by -i. The inversion in the
displayed coproduct is therefore invisible in the convolution of any two
characters. Interchanging s and t proves that every such convolution
commutes. Hence K0(Rep(A1)) is commutative as well as K0(Rep(A0)). This
argument does not infer commutativity from self-duality.

The source neutral rings are explicitly noncommutative: in R1225 labels 1
and 2 have different products in the two orders; in R1673 labels 1 and 3 do.
They therefore match none of the possible semisimple Hopf algebras. This is
the required contradiction for both rings.

## Exact independent replay

`verify.py` rebuilds the eight characters of both A0,A1 over the exact
cyclotomic field Q(zeta20), checks 2×64×20 convolution values, reconstructs
the nonnegative integral fusion tensors, and checks their fusion axioms.
It also re-reads the original rank-nine tensors and checks the neutral fusion
subring, rank-one action, dimensions, grading and noncommuting witnesses.
No numerical approximation or earlier audit result is used.
