# Cyclic isotypic-summand integrality

**Proposition.** Let C be a complex fusion category, and let g be an
invertible simple of order n. Suppose gX is isomorphic to X, Yg to Y,
and both gZ and Zg to Z, where X,Y,Z are simple. Set m=N_XY^Z and
d=FPdim(Z). Then m d/n is an algebraic integer. Equivalently,
gcd(n,m) d/n is an algebraic integer.

**Proof.** The left action of H=<g> on the one-simple additive category
generated by X is a rank-one module category. Hence the associator class
of the pointed subcategory Vec_H^omega is trivial. The regular algebra
A=direct-sum_{h in H} h therefore exists as a connected separable algebra.
The remaining obstruction to a left A-module structure on X lies in
H^2(C_n,C^times)=0. Choose such a structure; the same argument gives a
right A-module structure on Y. The two outside actions make X tensor Y
an A-bimodule.

The Z-isotypic summand of X tensor Y is stable under both actions:
left or right tensoring by h preserves the isomorphism class of Z, and
cannot carry a different simple into that class. Consequently the
underlying object Z^{direct-sum m} is an A-bimodule M. The category of
A-bimodules is a fusion category, and its Frobenius--Perron dimension
normalization is FPdim(M)=FPdim_C(M)/FPdim_C(A)=m d/n. This is an
algebraic integer. Since d is itself an algebraic integer, Bezout's
identity converts m d/n to gcd(n,m) d/n. The converse follows by
multiplication by m/gcd(n,m). This proves the proposition.

The argument is categorical and does not assume pivotality, sphericality,
pseudo-unitarity, or unitarity. It extends the existing prime-order
cyclic-module divisibility proposition in the rank-seven manuscript and
also gives a shorter proof of that proposition. It does not require the
classification of projective representations of H times H.

## New order-four applications

The source labels below are zero-based. In all five examples the subgroup
is generated by g=1, has order four, and fixes X,Y,Z on both sides.
The exact witnesses are in `cyclic_isotypic_results.json`. All have odd
m, so the necessary quantity is d/4.

| Ring | Minimal polynomial of d | Minimal polynomial of d/4 |
|---|---|---|
| R7_5_4702 | t^2-6t-4 | t^2-(3/2)t-1/4 |
| R7_5_4704 | t^2-8t-4 | t^2-2t-1/4 |
| R7_5_4706 | t^2-10t-4 | t^2-(5/2)t-1/4 |
| R7_5_4757 | t-10 | t-5/2 |
| R7_5_5676 | t^2-10t-4 | t^2-(5/2)t-1/4 |

Each scaled minimal polynomial has a nonintegral coefficient; therefore
none of these five tensors can be categorified. These are five new
applications beyond the prime-order scan, with possible overlap with
other independently certified exclusions.

## Verification scope

`cyclic_isotypic.py` searches all nontrivial cyclic subgroups, all fixed
triples, and exact minimal polynomials. For a nonnegative fusion matrix
N_Z, FPdim(Z) is its largest real eigenvalue. The checker factors the
integral characteristic polynomial and uses rational root-isolating
intervals to establish which irreducible factor contains that eigenvalue.
It then recomputes the monic polynomial of d/(n/gcd(n,m)) and checks that
some coefficient is nonintegral. No floating-point decision is accepted.
