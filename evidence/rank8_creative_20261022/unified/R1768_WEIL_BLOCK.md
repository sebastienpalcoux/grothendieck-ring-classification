# The forced nineteen-dimensional block in the eighteen-object R1768 branch

This is a necessary modular-data theorem, not an exclusion. Its forced
fusion coefficients are nonnegative integers, so the branch survives
these additional Verlinde checks.

Use the reduction in `../r1768`: D=444+72sqrt(37), the other dimension-orbit
simple is alpha of dimension a=73+12sqrt(37), and put q=D/12=37+6sqrt(37).
The eighteen-object branch has one irrational-ratio simple Z_k of
dimension d=D/sqrt(37)=72+12sqrt(37) at each primitive twist zeta^k for
k a quadratic nonresidue modulo 37. Other simples Y have dimension tq
for a positive integer t. Such rational-ratio objects may have primitive
37th-root twists too: they are not excluded by this statement.

Here irrational/rational refers to the ratio `dim(Z)/D`. The conclusion
concerns the eighteen forced irrational-ratio objects specifically.

## Explicit normalized s block

Let p=37, zeta=exp(2pi i/p), and

    e_* = (e_1-e_alpha)/sqrt(2).

The invariant nineteen-dimensional subspace has orthonormal basis e_*
and the eighteen coordinate vectors e_Zk. Its normalized s matrix is

    s_** = -1/sqrt(p),
    s_*k = s_k* = sqrt(2/p),
    s_kl = -(zeta^(2r)+zeta^(-2r))/sqrt(p),   r^2=kl mod p.       (1)

The sign of the square root r is immaterial. Every s entry from a Z_k to
a rational-ratio object outside 1,alpha vanishes. In the full s matrix,

    s_{1,Zk}=1/sqrt(p),   s_{alpha,Zk}=-1/sqrt(p).                (2)

In particular these Z_k are self-dual, since the real symmetric block
(1) squares to the identity and has no entries to its complement.

## Derivation from the saturated primitive-column model

The local model used in `SIGNED_PRIME_SUPPORT.md` consists of functions
on nonzero columns (x,y) over F_p. Let chi be the quadratic character,
extended by zero at zero, and set

    F(x,y)=chi(y),  H(x,y)=chi(x)[y=0],  v=F-sqrt(p)H.

Its squared norm is `2p(p-1)`. Write `s f(x,y)=f(-y,x)` and
`t f(x,y)=f(x-y,y)`, a convention consistent with the primitive twists
in (1). Normalizing v to v_0, its nontrivial twist spectral projections
are supported exactly at the nonsquares. Choose their unit vectors v_k
so that `(s v_0,v_k)=sqrt(2/p)`. Explicitly, for y nonzero,

    v_k(x,y) = -chi(y) zeta^(-kx/y)/sqrt(p(p-1)),

and v_k is zero for y=0. Direct finite summation gives

    (v_k,s v_l) = (1/p) sum_{u!=0} chi(u) zeta^(ku+l/u).

The quadratic Salie sum is

    sum_{u!=0} chi(u) zeta^(ku+l/u)
       = -sqrt(p) (zeta^(2r)+zeta^(-2r)),  r^2=kl,

for nonsquare k,l. The exact verifier checks this identity at p=37 in
`Q[zeta]`, using the quadratic Gauss sum itself for sqrt(p).

The saturated compression eigenspace at conductor 37 is one-dimensional
in the universal induced representation. Every irreducible component
supporting the saturated vector embeds equivariantly and isometrically
into that representation. Thus its cyclic representation is the same
one generated by v_0, with the same normalized matrix coefficients;
orthogonal multiplicities do not change those coefficients. The known
spectral fibre vectors generate this nineteen-dimensional subspace: the
displayed matrix closes it under s and t.

In the eighteen-object branch each nonzero fibre of `s(e_1-e_alpha)` has
one nonzero coordinate, of positive value `2/sqrt(p)`; rational-ratio
objects in that same twist fibre have coordinate zero. Consequently its
unit vector is exactly e_Zk. This establishes (1) in the actual simple
coordinate basis and proves the vanishing to the orthogonal complement.

In the seventy-two-object branch, the same calculation gives (1) on
the normalized equal sums of the four irrational-ratio objects at each
nonsquare twist. It does not determine their individual s entries.

## Exact forced Verlinde coefficients in the eighteen-object branch

Parameterize the nonsquares as `k=2b^2 mod37`, with b taken modulo sign;
2 is a quadratic nonresidue modulo37. For k=2b^2, l=2c^2, m=2e^2 let
`epsilon(k,l,m)` be the number of zero residues among

    b+c+e,  b+c-e,  b-c+e,  -b+c+e.

It is independent of the signs of b,c,e, and is either zero or one.
The full product of two distinguished objects is forced:

    Z_k Z_l = [k=l] 1 + (4-[k=l]) alpha
              + sum_Y 2t_Y Y
              + sum_m (4-epsilon(k,l,m)) Z_m.                (3)

The sum over Y runs over all other rational-ratio simple objects, with
`dim Y=t_Y q`, each simple counted once. Equation (3) is conditional on
the complete list of simple dimensions in this branch; it does not
claim that the rational sector's remaining products exist.

To verify it, use `s_{Y,1}=s_{Y,alpha}=dim(Y)/D`, the dimension-orbit
Galois identity, and the vanishing established above. The Y coefficient
is

    (dim(Y)/p)(1+1/a)=2t_Y,

since `q(1+1/a)=74`. For alpha the two orbit columns contribute
`(a+1/a)/p`; orthogonality of the primitive rows supplies the rest, giving
`(a+1/a+2)/p-[k=l]=4-[k=l]`. The unit coefficient is [k=l].

For a distinguished target, the two orbit columns contribute 144/37.
Put `B_kl=sqrt(p) s_kl`. Then

    sum_m B_km B_lm B_nm = 4-37 epsilon(k,l,n),

because the four cosine product frequencies sum to -1/2 when nonzero
and to18 when zero. Division by37 gives `4-epsilon`. Thus every forced
coefficient in (3) is integral and nonnegative. A hoped-for irrational
Verlinde obstruction at this step does not occur.

`r1768_weil_verification.json`.
