# Saturation forces a conductor bound on the modular subrepresentation

This note strengthens the twist-one consequence recorded in the preceding
research stage. It applies to arbitrary twist orders; it is not a cutoff search.
The categorical input is the normalized, **linear** modular representation of
the Drinfeld center of a spherical fusion category. No projective anomaly is
introduced: its two Gauss sums equal the global dimension of the source.

Primary source: Andrew Schopieray, *The dimension block of a spherical fusion
category*, arXiv:2609.35287v1, Sections 2--5, especially Lemmas 3.1 and 4.1.
The source is available at https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35287 . The additional
argument below analyzes the equality space in the proof of Lemma 4.1.

## 1. A finite-group equality lemma

Let `M=p^a`, `G=SL_2(Z/M)`, `U=<t>`, and let `h_b=diag(b,b^-1)`.
Let `(pi,V)` be a finite-dimensional unitary representation, `P` the orthogonal
projection onto `V^U`, and `V^U_alpha` the alpha-eigenspace for the torus.

**Lemma.** Suppose `v` is in `V^U_alpha` and

    ||P pi(s) v|| = cond(alpha)^(-1/2) ||v||.

If alpha is nontrivial, of conductor `p^c`, then the G-subrepresentation
generated by v factors through `SL_2(Z/p^c)`. If alpha is trivial, that
subrepresentation is trivial.

**Proof for nontrivial alpha.** Decompose V orthogonally into irreducibles.
Both P and the group operators preserve each summand. The local norm bound
in Schopieray's Lemma 4.1 applies to each summand, so equality for v implies
equality for every nonzero irreducible component of v. An irreducible with
a nonzero U-fixed vector embeds G-equivariantly and isometrically up to a
scalar into

    H = C[Omega] = Ind_U^G(1),

where Omega is the set of primitive columns `(x,y)` modulo M. The proof
of that lemma decomposes `H^U_alpha = C F_alpha + Sigma_alpha`, with

    F_alpha(x,y) = alpha(y) [y is a unit],

and Sigma_alpha consisting of small-cell functions supported where y is
not a unit. The compression A=P pi(s)P interchanges the big-cell line and
the small-cell space. Its small-to-big map has rank one, norm `p^(-c/2)`,
and adjoint range spanned by

    H_alpha(x,y) = conjugate(alpha)(x) [y = 0 modulo p^c].

(A harmless nonzero scalar from alpha(-1) changes neither this span nor
the equality space.) The big-to-small map has the same norm. The two
images are orthogonal. Consequently the **full** equality space for the
norm bound on `H^U_alpha` is exactly

    span(F_alpha, H_alpha).

Both displayed functions factor through reduction of primitive columns
modulo `p^c`, since alpha itself has conductor `p^c`. The subspace of all
such functions is G-invariant and is the inflation of
`C[primitive columns modulo p^c]`. Thus every nonzero equality vector in
an irreducible summand lies in this invariant subspace. Irreducibility
then puts its whole summand there. Every summand supporting v therefore
factors through `SL_2(Z/p^c)`, proving the assertion.

**Proof for trivial alpha.** Equality means `pi(s)v` is U-fixed. Since v
is U-fixed too, it is fixed by U and by `s^-1 U s`. These two elementary
unipotent subgroups generate `SL_2(Z/p^a)`. Hence v is G-fixed. QED.

## 2. Global conductor support

Let `rho` be a unitary representation of `SL_2(Z/N)`. Suppose `w` is
t-fixed, is a torus eigenvector of character chi, and achieves the bound

    ||P rho(s)w|| = f(chi)^(-1/2) ||w||.

By the Chinese remainder decomposition, the group is a product of the
groups `SL_2(Z/p^a)`. Its irreducibles are tensor products of local
irreducibles. The U-fixed chi-eigenspace is the tensor product of the
local U-fixed eigenspaces, and the compression is the tensor product of
their compressions. Each local norm is bounded by its local conductor
factor. Equality in the product bound puts every nonzero component of w
in the tensor product of the maximal singular subspaces. The local lemma
therefore gives the following theorem.

**Theorem.** The subrepresentation generated by w factors through
`SL_2(Z/f(chi))`. In particular,

    rho(t)^f(chi) rho(s) w = rho(s) w.

The assertion includes primes dividing N but not the conductor: their
local action on this subrepresentation is trivial. It also removes excess
prime powers at primes dividing the conductor.

## 3. Application to a saturated dimension-block character

Let C be spherical, `K=Q(dim(X): X simple)`, `G_K=Gal(K/Q)`, and
`D=dim(C)`. Write `A_sigma` for the distinguished center simple indexed
by `sigma(dim)`, and

    w_chi = sum_sigma chi(sigma) e_(A_sigma),
    h_chi = sum_sigma chi(sigma)/sigma(D).

Assume the **individual character bound is saturated**:

    f(chi) |h_chi|^2 = 1.

This is not an assertion about equality in an unrelated aggregate bound.
Schopieray's Lemma 3.1 gives `||w_chi||^2=|G_K|` and
`<w_conjugate(chi), S_normalized w_chi>=|G_K| h_chi`.
Cauchy--Schwarz together with the compression bound then forces equality
of the operator norm on w_chi. The theorem applies.

The dimension-row formula gives, for every center simple Z,

    (S_normalized w_chi)_Z = sum_sigma chi(sigma) sigma(dim(Z)/D).

Thus the new necessary condition is

    theta_Z^f(chi) != 1
       ==> sum_sigma chi(sigma) sigma(dim(Z)/D) = 0.             (* )

Equivalently, whenever the Fourier coefficient on the right is nonzero,
the order of theta_Z divides f(chi). Unlike the earlier equality deduction,
this restricts **all** twist fibres, not only the twist-one fibre.

## 4. Exact applications at rank eight

The accepted arithmetic in
`../../rank8_research_20261022/pointed_followup/dimension_block/verify.py`
establishes the following saturated characters and dimension fields.
The accepted general-to-spherical/Galois-pseudo-unitary gates let us apply
these conditions to a pseudo-unitary Galois form of any categorification.

* For R8_3_1768, `D=444+72 sqrt(37)`. The nontrivial quadratic character
  has conductor 37 and `h=-1/sqrt(37)`. Consequently every center simple
  with `dim(Z)/D` irrational has twist order dividing 37. If
  `dim(Z)=a+b sqrt(37)`, every other twist order requires `6a=37b`.
  The preceding pointed-induction theorem forces the nontrivial C2
  associator and hence twists `+i` or `-i` on **every** constituent of
  I(g). All such constituents must therefore satisfy `6a=37b`.

* For R8_3_928, R8_3_941 and R8_3_982, use the basis
  `(1,sqrt(2),sqrt(3),sqrt(6))` for `K=Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3))`.
  The two saturated characters select the sqrt(3) and sqrt(6) coefficients
  of `dim(Z)/D` and have conductors 12 and 24, respectively. A nonzero
  sqrt(3) coefficient forces twist order dividing 12. A nonzero sqrt(6)
  coefficient forces twist order dividing 24. If both are nonzero the
  order divides 12. Types with both coefficients zero remain unrestricted
  by this theorem.

These deductions alone do not assert a new exclusion. They provide exact
additional restrictions to be combined with the complete previously
enumerated central induction/fusion/phase domains. Separate case files
must record the outcome of that combination.
