# The sign of saturation selects a quadratic-residue twist class

This refinement was suggested by the coordinating researcher and is proved
here using the same finite-group equality space as `SATURATED_CONDUCTOR.md`.

Assume the dimension-block character is the real quadratic character modulo
a prime `p=1 mod 4`, and

    h_chi = epsilon/sqrt(p),     epsilon in {+1,-1}.

Then every center simple Z for which

    c_Z = sum_sigma chi(sigma) sigma(dim(Z)/D)

is nonzero has twist either 1, or `exp(2 pi i k/p)` with

    Legendre(k,p) = epsilon.                                    (1)

For `R8_3_1768`, epsilon is -1 and p is 37: only 1 and the eighteen
nonresidue primitive 37th roots can occur when `dim(Z)/D` is irrational.

## Proof

The conductor theorem reduces the cyclic representation generated by
`w=w_chi` to `SL_2(F_p)`. Let P project onto its t-fixed vectors and put
`A=P rho(s)P`. Since chi is real, the equality of the matrix coefficient
and the sharp operator norm gives the vector identity

    A w = epsilon/sqrt(p) w.                                    (2)

This follows directly from equality in Cauchy--Schwarz, with its sign fixed
by `<w,A w>=h_chi ||w||^2`.

Each irreducible component of w embeds into the permutation representation
on nonzero columns `(x,y)` of `F_p^2`. In the t-fixed chi-eigenspace put

    F(x,y)=chi(y) [y!=0],       H(x,y)=chi(x) [y=0].

With `s^-1(x,y)=(y,-x)` and `chi(-1)=1`, direct averaging gives

    A F=H,              A H=F/p.

Hence the entire eigenspace of A with eigenvalue epsilon/sqrt(p) is the
line spanned by

    v_epsilon = F + epsilon sqrt(p) H.                          (3)

On the big cell write `y=q!=0`, `x=tq`. Then

    (rho(s) v_epsilon)(tq,q)
       = chi(q) (chi(t)+epsilon sqrt(p) [t=0]).                 (4)

On the small cell y=0 the function is constant along each unipotent orbit,
so it has t-eigenvalue 1. On each big-cell orbit the unipotent generator
acts on functions by `f(t) -> f(t-1)`. The Fourier coefficient at frequency
k!=0 of the parenthesis in (4) is

    sum_t chi(t) exp(-2 pi i kt/p) + epsilon sqrt(p)
       = sqrt(p) (chi(k)+epsilon),                              (5)

using the classical quadratic Gauss sum
`sum_t chi(t) exp(2 pi i t/p)=sqrt(p)` for p=1 mod 4.
Thus only frequencies with chi(k)=epsilon survive. Their t-eigenvalues
are `exp(-2 pi i k/p)`, whose exponents have the same Legendre symbol
because chi(-1)=1. The zero frequency also has eigenvalue 1.

Every irreducible component of w maps to a scalar multiple of (3), so
every component of `rho(s)w` has exactly this allowed spectral support
(some allowed eigenspaces may have zero projection). In the canonical
center basis rho(t) is diagonal with entries theta_Z and
`(rho(s)w)_Z=c_Z`. Nonzero c_Z therefore implies (1). QED.

This argument does not require p to be the full Frobenius--Schur exponent.
All other prime factors and excess prime powers act trivially on the
relevant cyclic representation by the preceding conductor theorem.

## Exact spectral weights

The same model determines more than support. Let E_k project onto the
`exp(2 pi i k/p)` eigenspace of rho(t). In the above permutation model,

    ||v_epsilon||^2 = 2p(p-1).

For an allowed nonzero frequency k, the Fourier coefficient in (4), with
the usual factor 1/p, is `2 epsilon/sqrt(p)`. There are p-1 big-cell orbits,
each of p points. Therefore

    ||E_k rho(s)v_epsilon||^2 = 4(p-1).

The zero-frequency coefficient on each big-cell orbit has absolute value
`1/sqrt(p)`, contributing p-1 to its squared norm. The small cell contributes
another p-1, giving

    ||E_0 rho(s)v_epsilon||^2 = 2(p-1).

For any irreducible component of w, its equivariant embedding sends that
component to a scalar multiple of v_epsilon, by the one-dimensional
compression eigenspace (3). Spectral projectors commute with equivariant
embeddings, and the inner products agree up to a scalar. Thus these ratios
hold in every component. Orthogonality lets us add them, proving

    sum_(theta_Z=1) |c_Z|^2 = ||w||^2/p,
    sum_(theta_Z=exp(2 pi i k/p)) |c_Z|^2 = 2||w||^2/p
        for each k!=0 with chi(k)=epsilon.                    (6)

All other twist fibres have zero c-mass. In the quadratic-field case
`||w||^2=2`, so the two right-hand sides are 2/p and 4/p.

For R1768, write `dim(Z)=a_Z+b_Z sqrt(37)`. Its normalized Fourier
coefficient is

    c_Z = sqrt(37)(37 b_Z-6 a_Z)/222.

The source FP dimensions lie in `Z[sqrt(37)]`, and every center dimension
is the dimension of its forgotten object, so both coefficients are integers.
Putting `delta_Z=37 b_Z-6 a_Z`, formula (6) becomes the integral capacities

    sum_(theta_Z=1) delta_Z^2 = 72,
    sum_(theta_Z=exp(2 pi i k/37)) delta_Z^2 = 144
        for each of the eighteen quadratic nonresidues k.      (7)

Every other twist fibre has delta_Z=0 object by object. The two dimension
orbit objects have dimensions 1 and `73+12 sqrt(37)`, hence delta=-6 and +6;
they already exhaust the twist-one capacity. Every other irrational-ratio
center simple therefore has primitive nonresidue 37th twist and
`1 <= |delta_Z| <= 12`. The sums count distinct simple objects, not their
multiplicities in an induction object.

## Scope

For R1768 the already established twist-one equality condition excludes
additional twist-one objects with irrational `dim(Z)/D` outside the
two-element dimension orbit. Combining that condition with (1), such
additional irrational-ratio objects have primitive nonresidue 37th twists.
Objects with rational `dim(Z)/D` have c_Z=0 and remain unrestricted by this
signed-support theorem. Their separate induction, indicator and fusion
constraints must still be imposed.
