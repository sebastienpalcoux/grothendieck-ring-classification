# A Morita obstruction for the two noncommutative dimension-40 rings

The result applies to **R10_1_72 and R10_1_127 over C, without any pivotal,
braided, or unitary assumption**. The proof is structural; the supplied verifier
only checks its finite fusion-table hypotheses.

## Proposition

Let C be a fusion category of type (1^4,2^4,sqrt(10)^2). Suppose its invertible
group is C2 x C2 and it has an involution h with the following properties:

1. For every dimension-two simple X, the left and right invertible stabilizer
   of X is exactly {1,h}.
2. Left and right tensoring with h exchange the two sqrt(10)-dimensional
   simples.
3. Some dimension-two simple is not self-dual.

Then C does not exist.

### Proof

Let D be the integral fusion subcategory, of dimension 20. It is the neutral
component of the dimension Z2-grading. The subcategory generated by h has
trivial associator class: the full semisimple category generated by any fixed
simple X is a rank-one module category over <h>, and a pointed category
Vec(C2,omega) admits such a module category only when omega is a coboundary.
Thus A=1+h is a connected separable algebra in D.

The simple right A-modules in D are as follows. The four invertibles form two
free right-h orbits, each yielding one simple module of underlying dimension
2. Each of the four fixed dimension-two simples supports exactly two simple
right module structures, because projective representations of C2 over C
have two one-dimensional irreducibles. Thus D_A has ten simple objects,
all of underlying dimension 2.

For each such module M, its internal endomorphism object M tensor_A M* in D
has FP dimension 2^2/FPdim(A)=2, and contains 1 once. Consequently it is
1+g for an invertible g different from 1. Frobenius reciprocity gives
g tensor M isomorphic to M as right A-modules. The stabilizer of the underlying
object of M among the four invertibles is exactly {1,h}, both for a free-orbit
module and for a module supported on a dimension-two simple. Hence g=h.

Therefore h fixes every simple of D_A. Equipping a fixed module with a left
A-action gives two simple bimodules (again the projective C2 stabilizer has
two one-dimensional representations). Each has bimodule FP dimension
2/FPdim(A)=1. The dual fusion category B = _A D_A is consequently pointed,
of dimension 20; write B=Vec(G,eta), where |G|=20.

The Morita module category D_A has rank 10. Under the classification of
indecomposable module categories over a pointed category, it corresponds to
a subgroup K of G with [G:K]=10, so |K|=2. Hence D is a group-theoretical
category C(G,eta,K,psi). No choice of eta or psi is omitted here.

We determine G using only its order and the non-self-dual dimension-two
simple of D. Sylow theory and Schur-Zassenhaus show that every group of order
20 is C5 semidirect Q, where Q is C4 or C2 x C2. The possibilities are the
two abelian groups, D20, Dic5, and the faithful semidirect product
F20=C5 semidirect C4, where a generator of C4 acts on C5 by a -> a^2.
For abelian G every subgroup K is normal. In Dic5 the only subgroup of order
two is central. If K is normal of order two, every simple of
C(G,eta,K,psi) is invertible: every stabilizer is K and every irreducible
projective K-representation is one-dimensional. These possibilities cannot
give D.

For G=D20, a nonnormal subgroup K of order two is generated by a reflection.
Every double coset K a^j K is equal to its inverse. A noninvertible simple
has trivial double-coset stabilizer, hence is the unique simple supported on
that double coset. Its dual is therefore itself, independently of eta and
psi. This contradicts hypothesis 3. We conclude G=F20.

Now form the full Morita dual E = _A C_A, still of dimension 40. Its even
component is B=Vec(F20,eta). The two odd simples of C form one free right-h
orbit, hence give one simple right A-module of underlying dimension
2 sqrt(10). Left tensoring with h fixes this sole odd module, so it supports
two simple A-bimodule structures. Each has dimension sqrt(10). Thus E is
a generalized Tambara-Yamagami category with 20 invertibles and two
noninvertibles.

The invertible group acts transitively on these two simples. Their stabilizer
has order 10 and is abelian by Liptrap, Corollary 4.10. But F20 has a unique
index-two subgroup, namely C5 semidirect C2 with inversion action, which is
the nonabelian dihedral group of order 10. This contradiction proves the
proposition.

## References and scope

- Etingof--Gelaki--Nikshych--Ostrik, *Tensor Categories*, AMS 2015,
  chapters 7 and 8: separable algebras, relative tensor products, dual
  categories, and group-theoretical categories.
- V. Ostrik, *Module categories over the Drinfeld double of a finite group*,
  IMRN 2003, 1507--1520: module categories over pointed categories.
- J. Liptrap, *Generalized Tambara--Yamagami categories*,
  https://arxiv.org/html/1002.3166v2 , Lemma 2.3 and Corollary 4.10:
  the adjoint stabilizer of a noninvertible is abelian.

The proof does not assume that an arbitrary categorification of the integral
subring is equivalent to its known untwisted model C(F20,1,C2,1). Instead,
the algebra A forces a pointed Morita dual and then identifies its group
while allowing arbitrary associators.
