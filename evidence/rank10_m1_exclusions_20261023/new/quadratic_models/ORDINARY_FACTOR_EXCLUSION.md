# Exclusion of R10_1_114 and R10_1_115 by ordinary rank-four factors

Neither R10_1_114 nor R10_1_115 admits a complex fusion categorification.
The proof combines the established pivotality and Morita reductions with
relative factorization and central-algebra descent. An independent review
of the descent is recorded in
[ORDINARY_DESCENT_AUDIT.md](../rank16_dual/ORDINARY_DESCENT_AUDIT.md).

## Input from the Morita reconstruction

The [involution-module reconstruction](../quadratic_obstructions/MORITA_REDUCTION.md)
supplies, from a hypothetical
categorification C of either ring, a Morita dual D with a faithful C2
grading, neutral component B of rank eight, and a distinguished invertible
tau in the nontrivial component. The canonical inverse Morita algebra is
1+tau. The category D is therefore a crossed product B semidirect C2,
and C is equivalent to the equivariantization B^tau.

Convention: the stored multiplication uses End_C(C_A), whereas some
references identify the Morita dual with the opposite of this category,
written as literal A-bimodules. Accordingly the reconstruction may read
C^op equivalent to B^tau if that latter convention is used. This has no
effect here: the target Grothendieck rings are commutative, and taking
opposites preserves their based multiplication, invertible group, and
duality permutation. The same convention must be used on both Morita
steps.

The rank-eight neutral ring is generated by two rank-four subrings P1,P2
with the ordinary PSU(2)_6 rules. Their intersection is {1,f}; conjugation
by tau exchanges them. The two marked dual based rings for 114 and 115
are isomorphic to that for the established model 21. This is only a based
ring identification and is not used as an identification of associators.

## Classification of the rank-four factors

The existing Galois-to-pseudo-unitarity reduction allows us to replace the
hypothetical original category C by a pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate,
without changing its based ring. Pseudo-unitarity passes to its Morita
dual D: global dimension is Morita invariant by Etingof--Nikshych--Ostrik,
*On fusion categories*, Theorem 2.15, and Frobenius--Perron dimension is
Morita invariant by their Corollary 8.14. Consequently

    dim(D)=dim(C)=FPdim(C)=FPdim(D).

Their Proposition 8.23 therefore supplies D with its canonical positive
spherical structure. Restricting that structure to B and to P1,P2 gives
positive spherical structures there as well, so in particular the
rank-four factors in the Morita dual are pivotal. This step uses
pseudo-unitarity, rather than assuming that an arbitrary pivotal
structure transfers under Morita equivalence.

Source: Etingof--Nikshych--Ostrik, *On fusion categories*, Annals of
Mathematics 162 (2005), 581--642, Theorem 2.15, Corollary 8.14, and
Proposition 8.23:
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v162-n2-p01.pdf .

Etingof--Ostrik, *On semisimplification of tensor
categories*, arXiv:1801.04409, Theorem A.3, applies without any unitarity
assumption: every pivotal categorification of K_l, l>2, is Rep(SO(3)_q)
with q a primitive 4(l+1)-st root of unity. Here l=3. Thus each factor is
an ordinary quantum PSU(2)_6 category at a primitive sixteenth root.
Conjugation by tau identifies the two factors monoidally.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04409 . The theorem appears on pp.32--33
of the downloaded source `semisimplification.pdf`.

This theorem already handles the possible nontrivial rotational eigenvalue
and negligible morphisms in the trivalent presentation. No unsupported
assumption of symmetric self-duality or generation by a trivalent vertex
is needed here.

## The relative factorization ingredient

Apply the independently proved
[relative-factorization lemma](../rank16_dual/RELATIVE_FACTORIZATION.md).
In this particular ring, for a small simple Y of P2,
the complementary P1-bimodule M=P1 Y is regular on the left and has
underlying form P1(phi) for a soft tensor autoequivalence phi. Multiplication
M tensor_{P1} M -> M corresponds to a twisted-central object with underlying
1+f. Because f acts freely on every P1 simple, its half-braiding is block
diagonal and the unit summand proves phi is monoidally trivial. Uniqueness
of the resulting relative half-braidings, using U(P1)=1, makes the lifts
of P2 tensor closed. Consequently multiplication gives a dominant tensor
functor

    F: P1 boxtimes P2 -> B.

The right adjoint sends 1 to A=1+(f,f), as follows directly from the
intersection P1 cap P2={1,f}. Bruguières--Natale, *Exact sequences of
tensor categories*, arXiv:1006.0569, Corollary 5.6, identifies B with
modules over the induced central commutative algebra A. Thus B is the
ordinary central product (P boxtimes P)_A. The central lift of (f,f) is
unique, because U(P boxtimes P)=1, and is bosonic since the unique central
f of each quantum factor is fermionic.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0569 .

## The two possible factor-exchange actions

Let T be a coherent order-two tensor action on B which exchanges P1,P2.
Identify P2 with P1 using T restricted to P1. The lift to P boxtimes P is
then the ordinary factor interchange L. The relative half-braiding used
to define F is unique on each simple (U(P)=1), so applying T sends it to
the inverse relative half-braiding. Therefore the restrictions of T to
the two factors extend to a tensor natural isomorphism T F ~= F L.

More explicitly, if c_{Y,X}:Y X -> X Y is the relative half-braiding,
then T(c_{Y,X}) and c^{-1}_{T(X),T(Y)} are relative half-braidings of the
same simple with the same factor. They agree after the fixed restriction
isomorphisms, by uniqueness. The universal property of the Deligne
product now gives the asserted comparison of tensor functors.

Passing to right adjoints shows that this isomorphism supplies a central
algebra isomorphism L(A) -> A. Conversely such an isomorphism defines the
descended tensor functor on A-modules. Since A=1+h with h=(f,f), its
unital algebra automorphisms are exactly h -> +h and h -> -h. There are
no additional identifications or action coherences from tensor natural
automorphisms of the identity, because U(P boxtimes P)=U(B)=1.

This is also explicit from the module description: a lift L with an
algebra isomorphism lambda:L(A)->A sends an A-module M to L(M), with
right action transported by lambda^{-1}. Every T with the comparison
T F ~= F L is recovered in this way, since each module is a direct
summand of a free module and the comparison transports its action and
idempotent. Thus there is no unclassified tensorator left in the descent.
For either sign, lambda L(lambda)=Id_A. Their squares consequently
descend to Id_B. Any further coherence is unique because
Aut_tensor(Id_B)=1.

Hence there are at most two factor-exchange actions on B, represented by
the two equivariant structures on A. Both have an order-two coherence;
equivariantization and central condensation commute, yielding

    B^T ~= ((P boxtimes P)^swap)_(1+(f,f)_epsilon), epsilon=+,-.

The same description works for every Galois form of P, since it uses
only the central fermion, the standard factor interchange, and algebra
descent. These constructions are Galois conjugates of their usual forms.

## Neither outcome is either target ring

For epsilon=+, let b be the simple induced image of d_x^+ in
W=(P boxtimes P)^swap. In W one has

    (d_x^+)^2=d_1^+ + d_x^+ + d_y^+ + p_1x + p_1y + p_xy.

Induction identifies d_x^+ with d_y^+, so b^2 contains 2b. This outcome
is not multiplicity free.

For epsilon=-, it suffices to determine the invertibles directly. Write
s=F(d_1^-) and let g,sg be the two summands of F(p_1f). The pointed
subcategory of P boxtimes P has trivial associator and can be made
strict. Put e_1=(1,f), e_2=(f,1). The equivariant structure exchanges
these two components, and an equivariant right h=(f,f)_- action is

    r(e_1)=i e_2,     r(e_2)=-i e_1.

The other simple module has action -r. The half-braiding of h with
p_1f is -1 on both components, so the left action in the balanced
tensor product is -r. Accordingly g tensor_A g is the quotient by the
image of r tensor 1 + 1 tensor r. This operator is diagonalizable over
the algebraic numbers, and its zero eigenspace gives the quotient. That
eigenspace has homogeneous lines

    u=e_1e_1+e_2e_2,     v=e_1e_2-e_2e_1.

Their equivariance signs are respectively + and -, so they underlie
1+h. The remaining right action sends u to i v and v to -i u. Rescaling
v by i identifies this right module with the regular algebra A. Hence
g^2=1. Also s^2=1, and s exchanges the two module structures. The four
distinct invertibles 1,s,g,sg therefore form C2 x C2. This contradicts
the C4 group in each target ring, without requiring a calculation of
any nonpointed product in the negative-sign category.

The established full balanced-module computation additionally identifies
this outcome with R10_1_21, whose simples are all self-dual.
The exact existing certificate is
`research/prior/rank9_rank10_m1_20261023/work/resolve_rank10_quadratic/model21.tex`,
independently checked by
`research/prior/rank9_rank10_m1_20261023/work/audit_new_models/verify_model21.py`
and documented in the adjacent `MODEL21_AUDIT.md`.
For exclusion here the checked invertible-group invariant alone is
sufficient: its four invertibles form C2 x C2, whereas both targets have
C4. No full table needs to be repeated in the argument.
Every primitive sixteenth root is a Galois conjugate of the standard
one. The quantum associators, the central algebra, the standard
factor-swap action, and the balanced-module construction are defined
over algebraic number fields and transport under that conjugation.
Galois conjugation preserves nonzero isomorphisms, all fusion
coefficients, and the duality permutation. It therefore leaves the
stated based-ring properties unchanged. Both
target rings 114 and 115 have invertible group C4 and nonselfdual simples.

The two possible outcomes are therefore incompatible with either target:
one has a fusion multiplicity of at least two, and the other has
invertible group C2 x C2. This proves the claimed exclusions. The proof
does not use the non-exhaustive 2026 F-symbol inventory as a
classification theorem.
