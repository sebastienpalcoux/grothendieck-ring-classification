# The pointed associator of a hypothetical categorification of R10_1_189 is trivial

This is a structural theorem, not a categorifiability exclusion.

**Theorem.** In any complex fusion-category categorification of the based ring
`g^5=1`, `gx=xg`, `x*=x`, `x^2=1+(g+g^2+g^3+g^4)x`, the pointed fusion subcategory is tensor equivalent to the untwisted `Vec_C5`.

Schopieray's pseudo-unitary Galois reduction permits the proof to be made in the canonical positive spherical structure. Vanishing of a finite-group associator cohomology class is preserved by Galois conjugation, so the conclusion also holds before that reduction.

Use the normal form and exact Galois transform in STRUCTURAL_PROGRESS.md. Let a hypothetical pseudo-unitary categorification be C, and identify its pointed subcategory with Vec_{C5}^omega. Write the cohomology class of omega as k in Z/5. Suppose k is nonzero.

For a generator g of C5, the fifth Frobenius–Schur indicator in Vec_{C5}^omega is a primitive fifth root of unity. This follows directly from the standard representative

    omega(a,b,c)=zeta5^(k*a*floor((b+c)/5))

and the product formula for the cyclic indicator (Mignard–Schauenburg, §3.3, Corollary3.3): their convention gives `nu5(g)=zeta5^(-k)`. Replacing the convention by its inverse does not affect primitivity. Indicators restrict to fusion subcategories. Therefore, in the canonical positive spherical structure, all simple constituents Z of I(g) have theta_Z^5=nu5(g), by equality in the triangle inequality for Tr(theta^5 I(g))=D nu5(g). Every such theta_Z is a primitive 25th root.

Choose an automorphism sigma of the full cyclotomic field of the center whose exponent is 2 modulo25, using an odd exponent on any2-primary factor. Its restriction to Q(zeta5) is zeta5 -> zeta5². The exact normalized-S restriction transform is T from structure_receipt.json. Directly T^4=identity. Since every constituent of I(g) has invertible restriction multiplicity1, its central Galois sign is positive, and sigma^4 preserves its complete restriction vector.

For the Drinfeld center the multiplicative central charge is1, so its canonical normalized T-matrix is the ribbon twist itself. Modular Galois symmetry gives

    theta_(sigma_hat Z) = theta_Z^(a²)

for a the cyclotomic exponent. Consequently sigma_hat^4 takes a primitive25th-root twist to its power a^8, which is its sixth power modulo25. Iterating this operation gives five distinct twists, since 6=1+5 has order5 modulo25. Hence every distinct restriction vector occurring in I(g) occurs for a multiple of five distinct central simples.

There are exactly ten simple constituents of I(g), and each of the four allowed noninvertible coordinates occurs five times in their restrictions. Consequently the ten restrictions consist of five copies of g+sum_(i in S) X_i and five copies of g+sum_(i in S^c) X_i, where S and S^c partition the four allowed coordinates. The other three nontrivial pointed induction blocks are forced by applying T.

For completeness, the ten I(1) restrictions used next are determined without any earlier reciprocity search. Its simple dimensions `D/f_chi`, calculated exactly from the ten scalar characters, have b-coefficients `0,4,1,1,1,1,3,3,3,3` in `1+b*d`. All restrictions are binary, omit X_0, and contain the unit once. Let c_i count singleton restrictions at i and e_i count triple restrictions missing i, for i=1,2,3,4. The i-coordinate of FI(1) is5, giving `1+c_i+(4-e_i)=5`, hence c_i=e_i. The Galois transform fixes the I(1) block and maps a singleton at i to a triple missing2i, so `e_(2i)=c_i`. Since multiplication by2 is a four-cycle on the nonzero residues, all c_i=e_i=1. Thus I(1) contributes the noninvertible Gram matrix G0 whose row and column0 vanish and whose other diagonal and off-diagonal entries are5 and3 respectively.

The empty/full pair would produce five distinct central invertible objects restricting to the same g. Two half-braidings on a fixed invertible object differ by a monoidal automorphism of the identity of C. Such automorphisms are trivial here: writing their scalars on g,x as u,v, the fusion rules force v²=1 and v²=u^j v for j=1,2,3,4. The equations for j=1,2 imply u=1, and then v=1. Thus there is at most one central lift of any invertible object, excluding the last pair.
