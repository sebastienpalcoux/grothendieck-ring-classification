# R8_3_928 and R8_3_941: simple centralizers and condensation rigidity

Research date: 27 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai).

**Outcome: both individual categorification problems remain unresolved.**
This pass proves new conditional restrictions on their centers and on possible
condensation constructions. It neither constructs associators nor gives a
contradiction to their existence. No online classifications were changed.

## 1. Inputs and a useful dominant-image observation

Write

\[
a=2+\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt6,\quad
b=2+2\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt6,\quad
c=3+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6,
\]
\[
D=2(1+a^2+b^2+c^2)=144+96\sqrt2+80\sqrt3+56\sqrt6.
\]

Both source rings are noncommutative, and their only proper nontrivial fusion
subring is the pointed subring \(\{1,g\}\). The preceding integral-center
lemma proves that the only integral simples in their centers are \(1,G\),
where \(F(G)=g\). In R928, G is a semion; in R941, G is a fermion. These
categorical statements retain their explicit inherited general-complex gates.

**Observation.** If a fusion subcategory \(\mathcal H\subset Z(\mathcal C)\)
contains a nonintegral object, then the restriction of the forgetful functor
to H is dominant onto C. Indeed its fusion image is a fusion subcategory of
C. A proper image would be pointed, making every object of H integral,
because tensor functors preserve FP dimensions. Consequently
\(\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal H)\geq D\). If equality holds and H is
braided, the dominant functor is an equivalence; C would then be braided,
contradicting the noncommutativity of its given ring.

The observation applies to subcategories of the center, not to arbitrary
based subrings guessed from dimension coincidences.

## 2. R928: the nonpointed central factor is simple

Let S be the semion subcategory generated by G and let
\(\mathcal B=S'\subset Z(\mathcal C)\). Nondegeneracy gives

\[
Z(\mathcal C)\simeq S\boxtimes\mathcal B,
\qquad \operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal B)=D^2/2.
\]

The only integral simple in B is its unit: G does not centralize itself.

**Theorem.** B is a simple nondegenerate braided fusion category: it has no
proper nontrivial fusion subcategory.

**Proof.** Suppose \(\mathrm{Vec}\subsetneq\mathcal H\subsetneq\mathcal B\).
Its centralizer \(\mathcal H'\) inside B is nontrivial. Both H and H' contain
nonintegral objects, because B has no other integral simples. The observation
gives FPdim(H), FPdim(H') at least D. The centralizer dimension identity instead
gives their product \(D^2/2\), a contradiction. QED.

This is stronger than proving that B has no Deligne factorization. The four
retained center patterns give the following specific possibilities:

| Pattern | Rank of the center | Rank of B |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | 46 | 23 |
| 49 | 40 | 20 |
| 186 | 40 | 20 |
| 191 | 34 | 17 |

The short verifier recomputes these ranks from full column multiplicities.

## 3. R928: only three connected etale algebras in its whole center

The earlier all-ranks module theorem proves that every indecomposable C-module
category is regular. We combine that result with the precise DMNO algebra
correspondences to obtain a stronger constraint on model-building.

**Theorem.** Up to algebra isomorphism, the connected etale algebras in Z(C)
are exactly three, represented by a chain

\[
1\subset A\subset I(1),\qquad
\operatorname{FPdim}(A)=D/2.
\]

**Proof.** The module theorem and DMNO Proposition 4.8 imply that I(1) is the
only Lagrangian algebra up to isomorphism. Every connected etale algebra Q in
Z(C) extends to a Lagrangian algebra: its local module category is Witt-trivial
by Proposition 5.4, hence is a center by Proposition 5.8, and has a Lagrangian
algebra. Lifting that algebra through Proposition 3.16 gives a Lagrangian
algebra in Z(C) containing Q. Thus Q embeds into an algebra isomorphic to I(1).
Theorem 4.10 identifies the etale subalgebras of I(1), in reverse order, with
the fusion subcategories of C. There are exactly three of the latter,
\(\mathrm{Vec},\langle g\rangle,\mathcal C\), with dimensions 1,2,D.
The corresponding subalgebra dimensions are D,D/2,1. QED.

Moreover A belongs to B and is its **unique nontrivial connected etale
algebra**, with

\[
\mathcal B_A\simeq\mathcal C,\qquad
\mathcal B_A^0\simeq S^{\mathrm{rev}}.
\]

Here is a way to identify the underlying object that does not assume a
neutral graded subalgebra is automatically separable. Restrict the actual
central functor F to B. It is dominant by Section 1. DMNO Lemma 3.5 and its
central-functor reconstruction identify the right adjoint applied to the unit
with a connected etale algebra in B, whose dimension is
\(\operatorname{FPdim}(B)/D=D/2\). It must be A. Adjunction identifies it as
the B-part of I(1).

Let \(B_0=1,B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4\) be the five distinguished central simples in
the fixed low frame. Their G-monodromies are \(+,+,+,+,-\), so

\[
A=1+B_1+B_2+B_3,
\quad
\dim B_1=a^2,\quad \dim B_2=b^2,\quad \dim B_3=c^2.
\]

The canonical centralizer statement in DMNO Corollary 3.30 identifies
\((B_A^0)^{\rm rev}\) with S, proving the local-module assertion.

This leaves a sharply specified construction problem: a *simple* modular
category B of one of the three ranks above, having exactly this sole
nontrivial connected etale algebra and this opposite-semion local category.
It does not follow that such a modular category is impossible. Nor is its
existence supplied by the presently feasible numerical center patterns.

## 4. R941: the fermion centralizer is nonsplit and s-simple

Let \(\mathcal E=\langle G\rangle'\subset Z(\mathcal C)\). Its dimension is
\(D^2/2\), its Müger center is sVec, and its integral part is exactly sVec.
Every retained allocation has rank(E)=22. Their total center ranks are
34,37,40,43.

**Lemma.** E cannot split as \(\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}\).

**Proof.** Such an M would be nondegenerate and have rank 11 and dimension
\(D^2/4\). Its embedding in Z(C) would split that center as
\(\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathcal K\), where FPdim(K)=4. The rank of K is 3 or 4:
it is at most 4 since simple FP dimensions are at least 1; rank 1 is
impossible; rank 2 would have one nonunit dimension d, with \(1+d^2=4\) and
\(d^2=1+nd\), forcing \(d=\sqrt3\) and \(n=2/\sqrt3\), not an integer.
The center would therefore have rank 33 or 44, neither of which occurs in
the complete remaining allocation list. QED.

**Theorem.** The only fusion subcategories of E are Vec, sVec, and E itself.

**Proof.** First suppose a proper nonpointed subcategory H contains sVec.
Its centralizer H' in E contains sVec. If H'=sVec, the centralizer dimension
formula forces H=E, a contradiction. Otherwise H and H' both contain a
nonintegral object and both dominate C. The same formula gives

\[
\operatorname{FPdim}(H)\operatorname{FPdim}(H')
=\operatorname{FPdim}(E)\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathrm{sVec})=D^2.
\]

Each factor is at least D, hence both equal D. A braided category H would
then be tensor equivalent to the noncommutative C, which is impossible.

Now suppose H is nontrivial but does not contain G. It is not pointed,
since E has only two invertibles. Its join with sVec must be E by the preceding
paragraph. Its Müger center is symmetric and therefore integral, hence lies
in the integral part sVec of E. Its intersection with sVec is Vec, so H is
nondegenerate. The product functor then gives
\(E\simeq H\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}\), contrary to the lemma. QED.

Thus a R941 construction requires a nonsplit, s-simple, rank-22 slightly
degenerate category of dimension D²/2 with no additional integral simples.
In particular it cannot be assembled as a relative tensor product having
proper nonpointed factors. This is an obstruction to those construction
routes, not an obstruction to an unknown simple category of that size.

## 5. Additional individual attempts and their limits

* The new Galois-unit Frobenius pairing argument was applied to the low
  frame. For R928 all six nonpointed source simples are nonselfdual, and g
  is absent from every distinguished restriction, so no forbidden odd
  alternating block occurs. For R941 the first nontrivial Galois-unit
  restriction contains each of the four selfdual nonpointed simples once;
  it forces their second indicators to be +1. This agrees with the retained
  positive branch and produces no contradiction.
* The complete necessary dimension multiset for each of R928's four patterns
  was tested against each of the three nontrivial Galois automorphisms of
  Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3)). Each multiset is invariant, with multiplicity, under
  \(d\mapsto |\sigma(d)|D/\sigma(D)\). Thus dimension-Galois closure cannot
  discard these patterns. This check does not assume that fixing dimensions
  fixes a central restriction column or a whole modular simple.
* R941's four-object fermionic quotient has unavoidable even and odd fusion
  channels. It is not an ordinary rank-four category. No ordinary rank-four
  dimension bound, or imaginary 'dimension character' at g=-1, was used.
  Current rank-four superfusion literature supplies no exhaustive theorem
  for this nonpointed quotient. A super-pentagon determinant obstruction was
  considered, but no complete, checked obstruction identity was obtained;
  it is not part of the theorem above.
* The newly available *Anyon Atlas*, arXiv:2610.25439v1 (18 October 2026),
  was checked as a possible source of models for the simple rank-17/20
  modular factor or rank-22 supermodular centralizer. Sections 1.1 and 4.1
  expressly say that its numerical search is not certified exhaustive.
  The rank-23 R928 factor is outside its modular coverage in any event.
  Its public dynamic catalog did not expose a matching record in the
  retrieved material. No claim of absence from its underlying database is
  made, and absence would not be a categorification obstruction.
