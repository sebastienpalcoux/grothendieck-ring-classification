# R1154 has no nontrivial integral center, throughout its Morita class

This is a direct, general-complex structural theorem. It does **not**
exclude the source fusion ring. Unlike the third-indicator theorem, it
does not depend on the sphericalization gate or the three-pattern center
enumeration.

Let C be any complex categorification of R8_3_1154. Its FP dimensions are
(1,a+1,a+1,1,a,a,a+1,a+1), where a=6+3 sqrt(5), and g=X3 is its sole
nontrivial invertible. A sum of these dimensions can be rational only if
all its summands are invertible, because every other summand has strictly
positive sqrt(5) coefficient.

If W is an integral-dimensional simple of Z(C), the forgetful functor
preserves FP dimension and hence F(W)=m·1+n·g. Its class is central in the
source ring. But g is not central: gX1=X7 and X1g=X6. Therefore n=0.

The based adjoint subring is the whole source ring, as follows directly
from its eight-by-eight multiplication table. Thus U(C)=1. More explicitly,
a half-braiding on V tensor 1 is given on each simple X by a matrix eta_X
on V. The hexagon implies eta_Z=eta_X eta_Y whenever N_XY^Z is nonzero.
These matrices form a representation of the universal grading group;
because that group is trivial, every eta_X is identity. Such a central
object is just a direct sum of central units. Simplicity gives W=1.

Consequently

    Z(C)_int = Vec.

Every Tannakian category Rep(G) is integral. There is no nontrivial one
inside Z(C), and braided equivalence of centers preserves this fact.
By ENO, *Weakly group-theoretical and solvable fusion categories*,
arXiv:0809.3031, Theorem 1.3, no category Morita equivalent to C is a
nontrivial finite-group extension. An equivariantization by a nontrivial
finite group has the corresponding central Rep(G), so it is ruled out
throughout the same Morita class as well.

This rules out whole construction routes, including extension or
equivariantization of an arbitrary nonintegral input category; it is not
just the already immediate observation that FPdim(C) is nonintegral.
It does not rule out arbitrary nonpointed separable-algebra constructions,
or neutral-subcategory constructions inside larger categories.

For the necessary rank-ten category B of the preceding reduction,

    Z(B) = Z(C) tensor Z(Semion),

so its integral center is precisely Z(Semion), the pointed double-semion
category with twists (1,1,i,-i). It has exactly one nontrivial Tannakian
subcategory, the canonical Rep(C2) generated by the boson u. Thus a group
descent anywhere in that Morita class can only have group C2. Its neutral
Morita class is that of C: de-equivariantizing the centralizer of u gives
Z(C). This explains why adding more group-descent attempts cannot bypass
the remaining categorical problem in the original source.
