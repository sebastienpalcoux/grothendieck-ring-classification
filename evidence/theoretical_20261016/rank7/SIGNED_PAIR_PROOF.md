# Complete general exclusions of R7_4_1212 and R7_4_1213

**Proposition.** Neither census ring R7_4_1212 nor R7_4_1213 is the
Grothendieck ring of a complex fusion category. No pivotality assumption is
made on a hypothetical categorification.

The proof combines the existing quadratic-field reduction with a complete,
small finite enumeration of a **single** signed multiplication matrix. It is
not an enumeration of associators, modular data, or complete signed tables.
The attached checker uses integer, finite-field, and rational arithmetic only.

## 1. Necessary categorical data

Suppose that one of the rings is categorified. Pass to the canonical
sphericalization and choose the positive-second-indicator lift of each
self-dual simple. Quotient its Grothendieck algebra by the central sign object
being -1. The resulting signed algebra has structure constants

    |T_ij^k| <= N_ij^k,    T_ij^k = N_ij^k (mod 2),
    T_ii^j = N_ii^j (mod 4).

These are the already established sphericalization and pivotal-parity
conditions. All simples here are self-dual. Cyclic symmetry and dual
reflection of the trace tensor consequently give full symmetry of T_ij^k;
in particular the signed algebra is commutative, with real symmetric
multiplication matrices. The element g=X_1 has order two and acts as +1 on
all five noninvertibles, as follows from the square-channel congruence and
Frobenius reciprocity.

Let d=(1,1,d_2,...,d_6) be the spherical dimension character, and
let delta=sum d_i^2. The previous exact reduction, in
`evidence/upper_residual_20261002c/scoped_research/QUADRATIC_REMAINDER.md`,
proves that the field **generated by the individual d_i**, not merely by
delta, is K=Q(sqrt(5)), and that

    delta in {180, 360, 270+90sqrt(5), 270-90sqrt(5),
                         450+90sqrt(5), 450-90sqrt(5)}.

Every d_i is nonzero. Every signed formal codegree h belongs to K, and
delta/h is an algebraic integer, by applying Ostrik's formal-codegree
results to the full sphericalization (its codegrees and global dimension
are twice the signed codegrees and delta, respectively). Separately, h is
an algebraic integer because it is an eigenvalue of the integer signed
Casimir matrix sum_i M_i^2. Integrality of 2h alone would not suffice.

The dimension character and its quadratic conjugate are **distinct**, even
when delta is rational. Their formal codegrees are delta and delta', with
repetitions allowed. The remaining character (1,-1,0,0,0,0,0) has codegree 2.

## 2. One multiplication matrix and its spectral weights

It suffices to examine multiplication by X_2. Restrict it to the g=+1 sector,
using the basis (1+g,X_2,...,X_6). Its matrix is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&0&0&0\\
2&0&u&v&w&z\\
0&u&a&b&c&d\\
0&v&b&e&f&h\\
0&w&c&f&j&k\\
0&z&d&h&k&l
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here u=1. All other entries range independently over their bounded parity
and, for repeated triples, modulo-four domains. Using a superset of actual
signed tables is intentional: associativity constraints on the other
multiplication matrices are unnecessary. The exact source tensors give
97,200 possible matrices for R7_4_1212 and 259,200 for R7_4_1213.

Put p(t)=det(tI-M), and let q(t) be the characteristic polynomial of the
lower-right 5-by-5 principal block. M is self-adjoint for the metric
G=diag(2,1,1,1,1,1). Write w_lambda for the first diagonal entry of the
G-orthogonal projection to its lambda-eigenspace. The spectral decomposition
of the resolvent gives

    q(t)/p(t) = sum_lambda w_lambda/(t-lambda).

A common character of the signed algebra, with codegree h, contributes 2/h
to this weight. Indeed its vector is proportional to
(1,chi(X_2),...,chi(X_6)) in the present coordinates and its squared G-norm
is h. Equivalently, in the orthonormal basis
((1+g)/sqrt(2),X_2,...,X_6), its first coordinate is sqrt(2).

In particular, if lambda=d_2 is simple, then

    2 p'(lambda) - delta q(lambda) = 0.

Thus p must have a common root with at least one of the following four
integer polynomials:

    2p' - 180q,
    2p' - 360q,
    (2p' - 270q)^2 - 40500q^2,
    (2p' - 450q)^2 - 40500q^2.

The exact checker first tests these gcd conditions modulo 1009 and 1013,
then computes the integer-polynomial gcds for every survivor. Because p is
monic, a nonconstant characteristic-zero common factor remains nonconstant
modulo either prime. The modular rejection cannot discard a solution.

The enumeration results are:

| Ring | All matrices | Pass modulo 1009 | Pass both primes | Surviving simple roots |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| R7_4_1212 | 97,200 | 996 | 372 | 0 |
| R7_4_1213 | 259,200 | 1,888 | 288 | 0 |

The last column is checked over Q, not numerically: every root in each of
the four gcds belongs to a repeated factor of p. Thus d_2 must be a repeated
eigenvalue. Every repeated factor in the retained matrices is linear, with
multiplicity two or three.

## 3. Repeated eigenvalues

Since d_2 is rational, the two distinct conjugate dimension characters
belong to the same eigenspace. Their total weight is

    b = 2/delta + 2/delta'
      = 1/45   if delta=180,
        1/30   if delta=270 +/- 90sqrt(5),
        1/90   if delta=360 or 450 +/- 90sqrt(5).

For multiplicity two, the exact weight w must equal b. For multiplicity
three, there is exactly one further character in that eigenspace, whose
codegree must be

    h = 2/(w-b).

Because w and b are rational, h must be a positive integer. Every
multiplicity-three case fails: if w>b, the displayed rational number is
nonintegral. Every multiplicity-two case fails w=b except for four
matrices belonging to R7_4_1213; these have delta=180 and d_2=-3.
The checker computes projections exactly as

    P = V (V^t G V)^(-1) V^t G,

where the columns of V are a rational basis of ker(M-lambda I).
It records every repeated eigenvalue, multiplicity and weight in the
certificate. There are no approximate comparisons.

## 4. The four final matrices

For each of these four matrices,

    p(t)=(t-2)(t+3)^2(t^3-3t^2-5t+3),
    q(t)=(t+3)(t^4-2t^3-12t^2+12t+13).

The simple eigenvalue 2 therefore has codegree

    2p'(2)/q(2) = 10.

The cubic factor is irreducible over Q. Each cubic eigenvalue has a
one-dimensional eigenspace, so the corresponding character coordinates
and its codegree lie in its cubic field. Since every signed codegree also
lies in K=Q(sqrt(5)), that codegree is rational. The three characters are
Galois conjugate and consequently have the same positive integer codegree
h.

The reciprocal-codegree identity now gives

    1/2 + 2/180 + 1/10 + 3/h = 1,

hence h=54/7, contradicting algebraic integrality. This eliminates the
four final matrices, and proves both general exclusions.

## Reproduction and scope

Run

    python3 verify_signed_pair.py --repo /path/to/grothendieck-ring-classification --output certificate.json

The verifier reads the complete rank-seven census, constructs the matrix
domains itself, compiles and reruns the complete C++ enumeration, checks the
exact gcds and projections in SymPy, and derives the final nonintegral
codegree. A timeout or nonzero child exit is a failure, not an exclusion.
It runs equally with `python3 -O` and does not rely on Python assertions.

The C++ arithmetic is modular throughout. The primes are at most 1013;
the largest direct product in the polynomial test has magnitude below
40500*1012^2, and each addition is immediately reduced. All arithmetic is
therefore safely within signed 64-bit range.

This is a complete finite proof after the stated, existing quadratic
reduction; it does not assert that the enumeration itself is a purely
conceptual theorem. It upgrades exactly these two formerly pivotal-only
exclusions to general exclusions.

The public verifier also replays the finite parts of the prerequisite
quadratic-field reduction directly from the census. The manuscript
`quadratic_signed_reduction.tex` contains that reduction in full; the
original dated statement is retained as `PRIOR_QUADRATIC_REDUCTION.md`.
