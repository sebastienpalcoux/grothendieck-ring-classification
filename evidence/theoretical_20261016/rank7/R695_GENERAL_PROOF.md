# A general exclusion of R7_3_695 at the compression boundary

**Theorem.** R7_3_695 is not the Grothendieck ring of a complex fusion category, without any pivotality assumption.

We use the full-I(1) compression proposition and its boundary corollary derived from Schopieray's local modular-representation estimate (arXiv:2609.35287v1, Lemma4.1 and proof of Theorem5.1). The precise proof is in `FULL_I1_COMPRESSION.tex`; it does not use any dimensional-grading assertion for an arbitrary spherical category.

The source ring is commutative and every simple is self-dual. Its Casimir polynomial is

    P(x)=(x-7)^3(x-5)^2(x²-840x+4900).

The two irrational codegrees are D=420+70sqrt35 and its conjugate. Their field has discriminant140, and the full-I(1) obstruction quantity is

    140(840²−4·4900)/4900²=4.

Suppose C categorifies the ring. Its canonical sphericalization has dimension field K containing Q(sqrt35). The ε=+1 sector consists of the original based algebra, with doubled formal codegrees. Its contribution already saturates the compression bound. The boundary corollary therefore gives K=Q(sqrt35) and rational formal codegrees throughout the ε=−1 sector.

Both sphericalization lifts of every original simple are self-dual: duality preserves the nonzero spherical dimension, whereas the opposite lift has its negative. Therefore the sphericalization's fusion ring is commutative, and so is its signed quotient T at ε=−1. The seven signed formal codegrees h are eigenvalues of its integer Casimir matrix B_T, so they are algebraic integers; being rational, they are positive integers.

Let d=(d_i) be the spherical dimension character in the signed quotient and δ=Σ_i d_i². The field generated by its coordinates is K=Q(sqrt35), so d and its Galois conjugate are distinct. Their signed codegrees are both the rational integer δ. The induction integrality theorem gives δ/D algebraically integral. Since

    δ/D=(δ/70)(6−sqrt35),

and the integer ring of Q(sqrt35) is Z[sqrt35], it follows that70 dividesδ. In particular δ is even.

The signed coefficients agree with the source coefficients modulo2, so B_T≡B_N modulo2 and

    det(xI−B_T)≡P(x)≡x²(x+1)^5 mod2.

Because all seven signed codegrees are integers, exactly two are even. These must be the two occurrences ofδ just found. Denote the other five, all odd, by h1,...,h5. The induction theorem gives δ/hi∈Z. The reciprocal-codegree identity is

    2/δ + Σ_i 1/hi =1,

and consequently

    Σ_i δ/hi =δ−2.

But δ is even and hi is odd, hence δ/hi≡δ mod4. The left side is therefore5δ≡δ mod4, while the right side isδ−2. This contradiction proves the theorem.

No inference from rational global dimension to a nontrivial grading is made. In particular the invalid general-spherical dimensional-grading shortcut is unnecessary.
