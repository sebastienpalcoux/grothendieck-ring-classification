# A Morita-dual obstruction for R8_2_745

**Status:** general exclusion, independently audited. This proof assumes no pivotal, spherical, pseudo-unitary, or unitary structure on the ambient category.

Let d=1+sqrt(2), so d²=1+2d. The source tensor R8_2_745 has the positive dimension character

    (1,1,d,d,2,2,2d,2d).

Write g=b1. The subsets D={0,1,2,3} and E={0,1,4,5} are fusion subrings; their categories, if a categorification C exists, have simple dimensions (1,1,d,d) and (1,1,2,2). The latter has FP dimension10. The element g swaps b2,b3 and fixes b4,b5,b6,b7 on both sides.

## 1. The algebra and the dimension10 dual

Since g fixes b4, the pointed subcategory generated by g has trivial associator: the rank-one orbit of b4 is a module category over it. Thus A=1+g has its connected separable algebra structure. The dual B=A-bimod(C) is a fusion category, with full fusion subcategories D'=A-bimod(D) and E'=A-bimod(E).

Etingof–Gelaki–Ostrik, *Classification of fusion categories of dimension pq*, arXiv:math/0304194v2, Lemma6.2 and proof of Theorem6.3, show that E' is pointed. More concretely, each two-dimensional simple fixed by g has four A-bimodule structures, each of FP dimension1; the orbit {1,g} contributes two further invertibles. Their theorem identifies the pointed group as the nonabelian semidirect product C5⋊C2=D10. One can also rule out C10 directly from their group-theoretical description: the dual of Vec_{C10}^ω along a subgroup of order2 is pointed (all stabilizer groups are cyclic and all double cosets have size2), whereas E is nonpointed.

For clarity, the identification of E' here is the particular dual for A=1+g, not merely some pointed Morita partner: this is the algebra explicitly used in Lemma6.2 and Theorem6.3.

## 2. All simple dimensions in B

A-bimodules are described by projective representations of stabilizers for the left-right C2×C2 action on the simple labels. The orbit {1,g}, and likewise {b2,b3}, has stabilizer C2, whose Schur multiplier vanishes. These two orbits therefore contribute respectively two simples of dimension1 and two of dimensiond. In particular D' has dimension profile (1,1,d,d).

Each fixed label b6 or b7 has stabilizer C2×C2. Its twisted group algebra is either C^4 or M2(C). Consequently the corresponding block contributes either four simples of dimensiond, or one simple of dimension2d. (The FP dimension of an A-bimodule is its underlying FP dimension divided by FPdim(A)=2.) The already analyzed E' contributes all ten invertibles. Therefore B has only dimensions1,d,2d, and the number of its d-dimensional simples is 2,6,or10.

For a d-dimensional simple X, write FPdim(XX*)=a+bd with nonnegative integers a,b. Here a is exactly the number of invertibles fixing X; objects of dimension2d contribute even summands to b. Since d²=1+2d and d is irrational, a=1. The D10-action on the d-dimensional simples is thus free. Their number must be divisible by10, so it is10. Both fixed blocks split. Hence B has exactly ten invertibles and ten other simples, all of dimensiond; the invertibles act transitively and freely on the latter.

## 3. The adjoint subcategory is impossible

B is a generalized near-group category with a nonzero noninvertible term in XX*. Dong's *On generalized near-group fusion categories*, arXiv:1903.08791v1, Proposition3.6(1)(1), imply that B_ad is nonpointed and is contained in every nonpointed fusion subcategory, hence in D'.

Any nonpointed fusion subcategory F of D' must equal D'. Indeed, if F has only one noninvertible Y, then the invertibles in YY* are exactly1, by d²=1+2d, and Y²=1+2Y. This is the forbidden rank-two ring (if F also contains the other invertible, it would fix Y, contradicting the preceding stabilizer calculation). If F contains both noninvertibles, their product XY* contains an invertible transporting Y to X, so it contains the nontrivial invertible and equals D'. Thus D' has no proper nonpointed fusion subcategory and B_ad=D'.

The invertible group of the adjoint category is normal in the invertible group of the ambient category: conjugation preserves all products XX*. Therefore the order-two group G(D') is normal in D10. But D10 has no normal subgroup of order two. Contradiction.
