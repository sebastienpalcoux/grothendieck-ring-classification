# Independent categorical audit of the three-fixed-simple module obstruction

Date: 2026-10-16. The construction and the seven short contradictions below
pass independent mathematical audit. They apply to arbitrary complex
fusion categories; pivotality and sphericality are not assumptions.

The categorical construction is valid for any positive number `f` of
fixed simples. Its specialization to `f=3` is used in the seven rank-seven
arguments below. In particular, `f=4` rank-eight applications require no
additional hypothesis: replace three by `f`, and use the same equal-norm
difference basis and coefficient identities.

## The necessary signed module

Let `g` be an invertible simple of order two, and suppose right tensoring
by `g` fixes exactly three simple objects `x_1,x_2,x_3`.

The additive category generated by a fixed simple is a rank-one module
category over the pointed category generated by `g`. A rank-one module
category over `Vec(C2,omega)` makes the associator cocycle a coboundary.
Consequently the algebra `A=1+g` exists and is connected and separable.
This argument does not require a central lift of `g`.

In the category of right `A`-modules, each fixed simple has two simple
module structures, denoted `m_a^+` and `m_a^-`, each with underlying object
`x_a`. A free orbit of two original simples gives one simple module with
underlying object their sum. These statements also follow from the
endomorphism algebra of the induced module: its dimension is two in the
fixed case and one in the free case. In the fixed case the twisted group
algebra of `C2` splits over the complex numbers.

The algebra automorphism of `A` that is the identity on `1` and minus the
identity on `g` gives an involution of the module category by twisting the
right action. It commutes with left tensoring by every object of the
fusion category. It exchanges `m_a^+` with `m_a^-` and fixes the simple
modules associated to free orbits.

Thus the anti-invariant part of the complexified module Grothendieck
group has dimension three, with integral basis

    v_a = [m_a^+] - [m_a^-].

Let `T_i` describe the action of a simple `X_i` on this basis. If the two
lift multiplicities are `u` and `v`, their sum is the original fusion
multiplicity and their difference is the corresponding entry of `T_i`.
Hence, for the restricted original matrix `B_i`,

    |(T_i)_ab| <= (B_i)_ab,
    (T_i)_ab = (B_i)_ab (mod 2).

Rigidity of the module action gives `T_i^T=T_{i*}`. One can use the
orthogonal basis `v_a/sqrt(2)` to see this without a hidden weighting:
all three difference vectors have the same squared norm two. The `T_i`
satisfy all original fusion relations. If the original ring is
commutative, `T_i` commutes with `T_{i*}` and is normal. A self-dual
generator gives a real symmetric matrix. Every source polynomial
identity for a generator also holds on this module.

These are necessary categorical conditions. Finding no such matrices
therefore excludes categorification, without asserting that a matrix
solution would suffice to construct a category.

## Seven short theoretical contradictions

For `R7_2_116` and `R7_2_261`, the specified generator has restricted bound

    [[0,1,1],[1,0,1],[1,1,2]].

Its source polynomial has roots among `0,1,-1,5` in the first case and
`0,1,-1,i,-i,5` in the second. The absolute row-sum bound is four, so five
cannot be an eigenvalue. Normality then implies operator norm at most
one. The first row has two entries of absolute value one, so its Euclidean
norm is `sqrt(2)`, forcing operator norm at least `sqrt(2)`. This is a
contradiction.

For `R7_3_364` and `R7_3_938`, the specified self-dual generator has bound

    [[3,1,2],[1,1,2],[2,2,2]]

and satisfies `x(x^2-8x-8)(x^2-2x-2)=0`. A rational three-dimensional
characteristic polynomial supported on these roots has spectrum either
three zeros, or zero together with one of the two quadratic conjugate
pairs. Three zeros are impossible for a nonzero symmetric matrix; the
odd entries force nonzero entries. The larger pair gives
`trace(T^2)=80`, exceeding the bound `32` obtained by summing the squares
of all entries of the displayed matrix.

For the smaller pair, `trace(T^2)=8`. Write

    T = [[a,b,c],[b,d,e],[c,e,f]].

The bounds and parities force `a,d,b` to be `+/-1`, `c=e=0`, and
`f=+/-2`. Since the determinant is zero, `ad=1`, so `a=d`. Its trace is
therefore one of `-4,0,4`, whereas the smaller quadratic pair has trace
two. This is the final contradiction.

For `R7_3_970`, the normal matrix `T_1` has bound
`[[0,0,0],[0,1,2],[0,2,1]]` and source roots among `0,9,1,-1,i,-i`.
The row-sum bound three excludes nine. Normality gives norm at most one,
and parity then forces `T_1=diag(0,e,f)` with `e,f in {1,-1}`.
Duality gives `T_2=T_1`. The restricted bounds on `T_5` and `T_6` have
support respectively in the upper-left and lower-right two-coordinate
blocks, with their only possible off-diagonal entries connecting the
zero eigenspace of `T_1` to its nonzero eigenspaces. Commutation therefore
kills those entries. The remaining diagonals have disjoint support, so
`T_5 T_6=0`. The source relation `X_5 X_6=2X_1+2X_2` instead gives the
nonzero matrix `4T_1`.

For `R7_4_2644`, `T_1` has bound
`[[1,2,2],[2,1,2],[2,2,3]]`, is normal, and has roots among
`1,-1,i,-i,7+4sqrt(3),7-4sqrt(3)`. The row-sum bound seven excludes the
larger quadratic root; integrality of the characteristic polynomial
excludes its conjugate too. Norm at most one and odd diagonal parity
then force `T_1` to be diagonal with entries `+/-1`; `T_2=T_1`.
The first column of `T_5` is zero. The relation
`X_4 X_5=2X_1+2X_2` gives a zero `(0,0)` entry on the left and `+/-4`
on the right.

For `R7_4_2683`, the same bound and roots for `T_3` force a diagonal
matrix with entries `+/-1`, and `T_4=T_3`. The first row of `T_1` is
zero. The relation `X_1^2=2X_3+2X_4` again gives a zero `(0,0)` entry
equal to `+/-4`.

The tensor labels and restricted matrices are finite source inputs;
their independent source audit is being recorded by the rank-eight
agent. These seven matrix arguments and their unrestricted categorical
scope are independent of the exploratory enumeration script.

## Dimension-independent audit of the dual-module Gram identities

The formulas in `supplements/rank7/software/module_dual_gram.py` are also
valid with three fixed simples. The restriction to at most two fixed
simples in that file was a restriction of its enumeration routine, not
of the categorical identities below. This audit does not, by itself,
certify completeness of any replacement enumerator or its Farkas vectors.

Let `M=C_A` and `D=_A C_A`, with `u=A` the distinguished simple module.
The functor `F_u:D -> M` forgets the left `A`-action. Its right adjoint
induces the left action, and their composite is left tensoring by `A`.
If `B` is its nonnegative integral restriction matrix, adjunction gives

    B B^T = I + M_g.

Because `M_g` is an involutive permutation, this Gram matrix has a unique
decomposition into nonzero nonnegative integral columns up to permutation:
two unit columns for each fixed point, and one column supported with ones
on each transposition. Zero columns do not arise, since a nonzero simple
bimodule remains nonzero after forgetting its left action.

Normalize module Frobenius--Perron dimensions by
`d_M(m)=FPdim_C(m)/2`, so `d_M(u)=1`. For a simple bimodule `Z`,
`d_D(Z)=FPdim_C(Z)/2`. Thus each restriction column has weighted dimension
exactly `d_D(Z)`. The unit of `D` is the known column at `u`; the dimensions
of all other simples of `D` follow, with multiplicity, from this unique
Gram decomposition.

For an arbitrary simple module `m`, use the functor
`F_m(Z)=m tensor_A Z` and its right adjoint `I_m`. The composite
`F_m I_m` is the action of the internal end object
`m tensor_A m*` in `C`. Its multiplicity of `X_i` is `M_i[m,m]`
(duality gives the same diagonal if the inverse convention is used).
Consequently its restriction Gram matrix is

    G_m = sum_i M_i[m,m] M_i.

The unit bimodule maps to `m`, so subtracting `e_m e_m^T` removes its
known column. Every remaining column is nonnegative integral and has
weighted dimension `d_D(Z) d_M(m)`. The dimension classes and their counts
are the ones already found using `u`.

All these statements use Frobenius--Perron dimensions and rigidity only.
No positivity of categorical dimensions and no pivotal structure enter.
It is therefore legitimate to exclude a candidate module action by an
exact Farkas certificate for the resulting finite column system, provided
the column domain and all candidate module actions are exhaustively
reconstructed and the rational inequalities are checked.
