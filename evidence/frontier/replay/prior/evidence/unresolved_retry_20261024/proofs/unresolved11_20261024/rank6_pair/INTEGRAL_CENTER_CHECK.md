# Integral-center criterion: why it adds no rank-six cut

Work in the positive spherical representative of R6_8_1660, with source basis 1,g,X,gX,sigma,T and

    d(X)=d(gX)=3+2sqrt(2)+2sqrt(3)+sqrt(6),
    d(T)=4+3sqrt(2)+2sqrt(3)+2sqrt(6),
    d(sigma)=sqrt(2).

For a nonnegative restriction vector v=(a,b,c,e,f,t), its dimension has sqrt(3) coefficient 2(c+e+t). If this dimension is an integer, c=e=t=0, and its sqrt(2) coefficient then forces f=0. Thus every integral central simple restricts to a*1+b*g.

The existing complete low-induction frame lists all six simples occurring in I(1). Of the first four, the restriction vectors are

    (1,0,0,0,0,0), (1,0,3,3,0,4),
    (1,0,1,1,0,2), (1,0,2,2,0,2).

The last two also contain noninvertible base simples. Consequently the only integral central simple with a>0 is the tensor unit, and its restriction is exactly1. Tensoring with the unique central lift h of g exchanges the first two coordinates; hence the only integral simple with b>0 is h. Since a=b=0 would give zero restriction, there are exactly two integral center simples, 1 and h. Both are invertible, and h is fermionic.

This argument uses the already-established low-induction frame, not an enumeration over the 640 remaining profiles. It shows that the newly proposed no-boson/two-prime integral-center obstruction is already satisfied throughout the rank-six domain.

The universal grading is trivial directly: X² contains1,X,gX,T, forcing all these degrees to be the identity; gX and sigmaX=T then force the degrees of g and sigma to be the identity. Thus no nontrivial central invertible can forget to the unit.

For R6_4_155, the same observation is immediate from d=4+3sqrt(2): an integral central object can restrict only to pointed base objects. The forced center has exactly the four pointed lifts; its reconstructed rank-26 factor has only1 and its fermion as integral simples. It too already satisfies the proposed obstruction.
