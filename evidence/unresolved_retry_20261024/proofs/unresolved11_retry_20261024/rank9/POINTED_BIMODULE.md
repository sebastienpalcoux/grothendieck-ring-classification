# A forced anticommutator and a new Morita third-indicator constraint

## Result and scope

For each of R9_2_137, R9_2_1607, R9_2_1975 and R9_2_1976, the previously established necessary rank-twelve Morita reduction has eight invertibles forming D8 and four other simples of dimension `d=4+3sqrt(2)`. On every remaining uniform identity-quotient-action branch, the left and right actions of the central involution on each noninvertible **anticommute**. This is forced for every compatible pointed-bimodule associator, not just for the previously exhibited cochain. Consequently the four-object pointed bimodule is invertible.

For any spherical realization, each of the four noninvertibles in the Morita dual has third indicator in

`{-1, -zeta_3, -zeta_3^2}`.

For a pseudo-unitary source and its canonical pseudo-unitary Morita dual, write `S` for the sum of the normalized traces to `Q(sqrt(2))` of the four nonintegral source objects' third indicators. Then

`S in {-4, -5/2, -1, 1/2, 2}`,

in particular `S <= 2`. This is a new necessary constraint; it does not by itself exclude or construct any of the four source rings. Their global dimension has norm `2304=2^8*3^2`, so one must **not** discard the nonreal cube-root cases by a coprime-exponent argument.

The pairing statement is general complex. The indicator statement is spherical, and the positive center application uses the existing audited source-to-pseudo-unitary Galois reduction. No new unitarity assumption is introduced.

## 1. Precisely which Morita candidates are covered

Let `G=D8`, `H=Z(G)=C2`, and `Q=G/H=V4`. The necessary uniform rings have

`g X_q = X_q g = X_(q+gH)`,

`X_p X_q = sum_(gH=p+q+alpha) g + 2 sum_(t in Q) X_t`,

`X_q^* = X_(q+alpha)`.

There are **three types in the union**, not two common types. For each of the three commutative source cases, alpha is zero or a reflection coset. For R9_2_1975 the surviving types have alpha a reflection or a rotation coset. Only the reflection type overlaps. This proof covers all three because neither the stabilizer nor the cubic multiplicity depends on alpha:

`dim Hom(1,X_q^3)=N_(X_q,X_q)^(X_q^*)=2`.

The earlier nonidentity quotient action was already excluded using the pointed associator; the earlier R1975 extreme branch was already excluded using the R6_4_155 tensor-auto-equivalence obstruction. Neither exclusion is counted as new here.

## 2. The cyclic data determine the entire pointed associator

Use coordinates `G={(x,y,n):x,y,n in F2}` with multiplication

`(x,y,n)(u,v,m)=(swap^m(x,y)+(u,v),n+m)`.

Its center is generated by `z=(1,1,0)`. The even subgroup `E={n=0}` is V4. The elements `(1,0,1)` and `(0,1,1)` are the rotations of order four; the two remaining odd elements are reflections.

The previous TY-origin theorem gives: the pointed associator restricts to class 2 on the rotation C4, to zero on E, and to sign `epsilon=sgn(tau)` on either odd reflection C2. These restrictions determine its class in `H^3(D8,C*)` uniquely.

Here is the cohomological justification, which was not required in the earlier obstruction. Goff--Mason--Ng, Lemmas 4.4 and 4.5, identify `H^3(D8,C*)` as a direct product of an order-four factor and the four-element inflation image from `Q`. Restriction to the rotation C4 detects the order-four factor; the inflation image restricts trivially there. Every nonzero member of that inflation image restricts nontrivially to an involution subgroup, as shown in the proof of their Lemma 4.4. Thus a class trivial on all cyclic subgroups is zero. The previously forced cyclic restrictions therefore specify the class completely.

An explicit representative is `omega_epsilon=i^w_epsilon`. Put `t=0` when epsilon is +1 and `t=1` otherwise. For `a=(x,y,n)`, `b=(u,v,m)`, `c=(a0,b0,ell)`, set

`w_epsilon(a,b,c) = ell*(3*m*(x xor y)+x'*u+y'*v+2*y'*u) + 2*t*n*m*ell (mod 4)`,

where `(x',y')=swap^m(x,y)`. The verifier checks the 4096 cocycle equations for each sign and all cyclic restrictions. Therefore this is a representative of the actual forced pointed category for either sign.

## 3. A cochain-independent stabilizer pairing

Identify the acting group with `G x G` by letting `(g,k)` act as `g X k^(-1)`. The stabilizer of any one of the four large simples is

`L=G x_Q G`.

Write it as `G x C2`, using `(g,e)` for `(g,g*z^e)`. In coordinates `A=(x,y,n,e)`, the two projections are

`p1(A)=(x,y,n)`, `p2(A)=(x+e,y+e,n)`.

The pointed-bimodule pentagon says that its scalar stabilizer cochain beta has coboundary `p1^*omega / p2^*omega`. This is the standard pointed-bimodule stabilizer description; the change from an opposite-group convention contributes only the standard monoidal identification and does not alter the mixed central-stabilizer commutator.

For `B=(u,v,m,f)`, one exact solution is `beta(A,B)=i^b(A,B)`, where

```
b(A,B) = 2*n*f + 2*n*m + 2*y*f + 3*y*m + 2*y*m*f
       + 2*y*u + y*e*m + x*m + 2*x*m*f + 2*x*u
       + 3*x*e*m + 2*x*y*m                         (mod 4).
```

The same cochain works for both signs, because `p1` and `p2` preserve n. The verifier checks all 4096 coboundary equations for each sign.

The pure left and pure right central stabilizers are

`h_L=(1,1,0,1)`, `h_R=(0,0,0,1)`.

Their mixed pairing is

`beta(h_L,h_R)/beta(h_R,h_L)=i^2=-1`.

Now let beta' be **any** other solution for the same pointed cocycle. Their quotient gamma is an ordinary 2-cocycle on L. Since h_R is central, the commutator

`c_gamma(a,h_R)=gamma(a,h_R)/gamma(h_R,a)`

is a character of L in the variable a: this follows directly by taking commutators with a lift of h_R in the central extension represented by gamma. It is therefore trivial on `[L,L]`. But

`h_L*h_R=(z,0) in [G,G] x {0}=[L,L]`,

and `c_gamma(h_R,h_R)=1`. Hence `c_gamma(h_L,h_R)=1`. Twisting the solution by any 2-cocycle cannot change the pairing −1. Coboundary changes in the pointed associator act separately on the two projections and have trivial mixed pairing at this pair, so the conclusion is gauge independent.

This proves the forced anticommutation, without enumerating a preferred H² basis or imposing a fourth-root restriction on arbitrary categorical cochains.

The left and right stabilizers are both C2, and their nontrivial pairing is nondegenerate. The bitransitive pointed-bimodule invertibility criterion (Vainerman--Vallin, Proposition 2.7) now shows that the four-object bimodule is invertible. This conclusion supplies neither a central half-braiding on z nor a faithful group grading on the full fusion category.

## 4. Cubic rotation and transfer back to rank nine

The restriction of omega to H is trivial, so `1+z` has its associative subgroup-algebra structure. On every large simple X the two normalized left/right z actions anticommute by Section 3. The project’s already proved stabilizer-rotation proposition applies: adjacent pair insertions on `Hom(1,X^3)` generate a complex Clifford algebra, and third rotation has trace `-(a+b*zeta_3+c*zeta_3^2)` with `a+b+c=m/2`. Here m=2. Thus the third indicator has exactly the three necessary possibilities stated above, and its normalized trace to `Q(sqrt(2))` lies in `{-1,1/2}`.

The transfer uses the **weighted total indicator**, not individual Morita invariance. For a pseudo-unitary fusion category B,

`v_3(B)=sum_T FPdim(T)*nu_3(T)=1/FPdim(B) * sum_(Z in Irr Z(B)) FPdim(Z)^2*theta_Z^3`.

This follows by summing the Ng--Schauenburg induction formula. A Morita equivalence identifies the centers with their canonical positive spherical structures, so v3 agrees for the source C and the dual C'.

Every nonidentity invertible in D8 has order 2 or 4, hence third indicator zero. In the rank-nine source the three nonidentity V4 invertibles also have third indicator zero, and the TY simple Y has odd indicator zero. Both categories have exactly four large simples, all of dimension d. Therefore

`1+d*sum_(large T in C) nu_3(T) = 1+d*sum_(large X in C') nu_3(X)`.

The unweighted sums consequently agree. Taking normalized traces to `Q(sqrt(2))` gives a sum of four elements of `{-1,1/2}`, which is precisely

`{-4,-5/2,-1,1/2,2}`.

For a central-induction necessary system with columns of dimensions z and projected twist traces t3, the inequality can be inserted without selecting a Morita tensor:

`sum_Z z^2*t3 = D*(1+d*S)`, where `D=144+96sqrt(2)` and `D*d=1152+816sqrt(2)`.

In particular the sqrt(2) coefficient is at most `96+816*2=1728`. `screen_morita_third.py` adds exactly that row, with a nonnegative slack variable, to the existing integral-center/TY-spectrum systems. It does not call LP feasibility a categorical model.
