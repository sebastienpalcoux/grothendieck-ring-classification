# A conductor-seventeen indicator balance excludes five rank-eight rings

**Theorem.** The five rings R8_2_33, R8_2_36, R8_2_76, R8_2_412, and R8_2_446 have no complex fusion categorification.

This is a single theoretical proof. Its verifier checks only the original multiplication tensors and the displayed exact identities; it performs no low-frame enumeration, linear optimization, or associator search. Noncommutativity in R36 and R412 causes no exception.

## 1. Common categorical setting and source facts

All five source tensors have four invertibles, forming a group `G` of order four, and four noninvertibles, forming a set `O`. Their FP dimensions are

`1` and `d=4+sqrt(17)`,  with `d²=1+8d`,

and their global dimension is

`D=4+4d²=8+32d=136+32sqrt(17)`.

Schopieray, *Categorification of integral group rings extended by one dimension*, J. London Math. Soc. 108 (2023), Lemma 3.1, proves that every fusion categorification with exactly two FP dimensions is Galois conjugate to a pseudo-unitary category. This statement does not require commutativity. Thus a hypothetical complex categorification can be replaced by its pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate, with the same based ring. We use its canonical positive spherical structure.

Write `Omega=sum_X X X*`. Source multiplication gives

`Omega=8*1+8*sum_(rho in O) rho`,

and left multiplication by `Omega` has characteristic polynomial

`(x-8)^6 (x²-272x+1088)`.

This is a Casimir matrix. In a noncommutative ring it is not being identified with the full induction Gram matrix. The single identity `F(I(1))=Omega` suffices here.

For an irreducible ring representation `E` of dimension `e_E`, the Casimir eigenvalue is `e_E f_E`, with multiplicity `e_E²`, where `f_E` is its ordinary formal codegree (Ostrik, Remark 2.11). Hence the two simple eigenvalues `D` and `D'=136-32sqrt(17)` come from one-dimensional representations; every other formal codegree is `8/e_E`, and is rational. This observation handles the noncommutative cases without a character-table classification.

Ostrik's Theorem 2.13 supplies the simple constituents `A_E` of `I(1)`, with multiplicity `e_E` and dimension `D/f_E`. Let `J` be the constituent belonging to `f_E=D'`. Its multiplicity is one and

`dim(J)=D/D'=d²=33+8sqrt(17)`.

All constituents of `I(1)` have twist one. The source expression for `Omega` also shows that none of their restrictions contains any nonunit invertible.

## 2. Saturation of the quadratic dimension block

Normalize the center matrix by `S_00=1/D`. The multiplicity vector `m` of `I(1)` satisfies `Sm=m` (Ostrik, Proposition 2.8). Its support is therefore Galois-stable and all Galois signs on that support are positive.

For a cyclotomic automorphism `sigma`, the dimension of its image of the central unit is `D/sigma(D)`. If `sigma` fixes `K=Q(sqrt(17))`, that image is the unique constituent of `I(1)` of dimension one, namely the central unit. If it flips `K`, the image is `J`, the unique constituent with dimension `D/D'`. The Galois orbit of the central unit therefore consists of these two objects, independently of how `sigma` acts on other character fields.

Let `w=e_1-e_J` in the center's modular representation. The diagonal torus acts on it by the primitive quadratic character `chi_17`. On the span of the known twist-one constituents of `I(1)`, the compression of `S` sends

`w -> (1/D-1/D')w = -w/sqrt(17)`.

Indeed the `1,J` block has diagonal `1/D` and off-diagonal `1/D'`. For every other constituent `A_E`, the entry in the unit column is `1/f_E`, which is rational; the entry in the `J` column is its Galois conjugate and hence agrees. Multiplicities `e_E>1` in `I(1)` do not alter these entries or the calculation.

Schopieray's local compression bound, arXiv:2609.35287v1, Lemma 4.1, bounds the squared norm by `1/17`. It is already attained on this known block. The equality refinement proved in `compression/CONDUCTOR_EQUALITY_LEMMA.md` therefore gives

`P S w = -w/sqrt(17)`

for the full projection `P` onto twist-one vectors, and the cyclic representation generated by `w` factors through level 17. For a center simple `A` of dimension `z`, its coordinate in `Sw` is

`z/D - sigma(z/D)`.

Thus if `z/D` is nonrational, its twist has order dividing 17. If `A` is outside `I(1)`, its twist cannot be one: the preceding exact formula for `PSw` has no coordinate there. Such an `A` has a primitive seventeenth-root twist. The quadratic-residue refinement additionally places it in the negative residue class, although that stronger conclusion is unnecessary below.

Squaring a primitive seventeenth root preserves its relative trace to `K`, because `2` is a quadratic residue modulo 17. Equivalently, the field automorphism `zeta_17 -> zeta_17²` fixes `K`. Therefore, for every such high nonrational row,

`t_K(theta_A²-theta_A)=0`,

where `t_K` is normalized relative trace from a common cyclotomic field to `K`.

## 3. Invertible constituents have small-power twists

Let `g` be an invertible of order `n=2` or `4`. The order-`n` pivotal rotation on the one-dimensional space `Hom(1,g^n)` has order dividing `n`; its trace is `nu_n(g)`, an `n`-th root of unity. Ng–Schauenburg's center trace formula gives

`Tr(theta^n on I(g))=D nu_n(g)`,  while `dim I(g)=D`.

The left side is a positive weighted sum of unit-modulus numbers. Equality in the triangle inequality forces every simple constituent `A` of `I(g)` to satisfy

`theta_A^n=nu_n(g)`, hence `theta_A^(n²)=1`.

This uses only positivity of categorical dimensions, not a unitary associator realization. In particular these twists have order dividing 16.

For a center simple outside `I(1)`, let

`a=sum_(g in G\{1}) [F(A):g]`,  `b=sum_(rho in O) [F(A):rho]`.

Its dimension is `z=a+bd`. If `a>0`, it occurs in some `I(g)` and hence has a twist of 2-power order. Section 2 then rules out a nonrational ratio `z/D`: that would force a primitive seventeenth root. Thus `z/D` is rational. Comparing coefficients in

`a+bd = q(8+32d)`

forces **`b=4a`**.

If `a=0`, then `z=bd` and `z/D=b/(8sqrt(17))` is nonrational. Its first and second relative twist traces agree by Section 2.

## 4. The common indicator identity

Let `Q=sum_(rho in O) rho`. For every center simple outside `I(1)`, the preceding dichotomy gives

`(b-4a) t_K(theta_A²-theta_A)=0`.

Every constituent inside `I(1)` has twist one, so the same trace-difference expression vanishes there regardless of its multiplicities. Multiply by its dimension and sum over the center. Frobenius reciprocity identifies `b` as the multiplicity in `I(Q)` and `a` as the total multiplicity in `sum_(g!=1) I(g)`. Thus

`t_K Tr(theta²-theta on I(Q)) = 4 sum_(g!=1) t_K Tr(theta²-theta on I(g))`.

The induction identities `Tr(theta on I(X))=0` for nonunit simples and `Tr(theta² on I(X))=D nu_2(X)` now give the purely categorical balance

**`sum_(rho in O) nu_2(rho) = 4 sum_(g in G\{1}) nu_2(g)`.**

No enumeration of possible center rows has been used.

## 5. One parity contradiction for all five rings

For a non-self-dual simple the second indicator is zero; for a self-dual simple it is `+1` or `-1`. A group of order four has an odd number of nonunit involutions: one for `C4`, three for `V4`. The sum on the right is therefore four times a nonzero odd integer and has absolute value at least four.

The left sum has absolute value at most the number of self-dual noninvertibles. The source counts are:

| Ring | Pointed group | Self-dual noninvertible source labels | Maximum absolute left side |
|---|---|---|---:|
| R8_2_33 | C4 | 5,6 | 2 |
| R8_2_36 | C4 | none | 0 |
| R8_2_76 | C4 | none | 0 |
| R8_2_412 | V4 | 6,7 | 2 |
| R8_2_446 | V4 | none | 0 |

Every row contradicts the common indicator balance. Hence all five hypothetical pseudo-unitary categories are impossible. The two-dimension Galois reduction proves the theorem for arbitrary complex fusion categories.
