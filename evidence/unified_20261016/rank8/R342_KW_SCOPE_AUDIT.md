# Independent audit: the Kazhdan–Wenzl step for the PSU(3)_5 subcategory

This audit concerns the tensor-category classification step in the proposed R8_2_342 inverse-Morita obstruction. It does not by itself verify the inverse-Morita tensor reconstruction, the autoequivalence group, or the final obstruction.

## General complex scope

The primary research paper by Z. Feng, S. Ming, and E. C. Rowell, *Reconstructing braided subcategories of SU(N)_k*, arXiv:2202.00121v2 (26 April 2022), states the Kazhdan–Wenzl classification as Theorem 5.1, on printed page 10. Its hypothesis is a **rigid monoidal semisimple complex category** with the fusion ring of SU(N)_k. Its conclusion is a monoidal equivalence to

\[
\mathcal C(\mathfrak{sl}_N,k+N,q)^\omega,
\]

where \(q\) is a primitive root of order \(2(k+N)\), and \(\omega\) is a grading 3-cocycle on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). Braiding, pivotality, pseudo-unitarity, and unitarity are not hypotheses of this theorem. Thus a hypothetical complex fusion category can be used as input directly.

Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00121 . The published paper is *Journal of Algebra* 635 (2023), 436–458. This audit checked the explicit theorem in the primary paper; it did not independently retrieve the 1993 original Kazhdan–Wenzl article.

## Restriction to the adjoint factor

Let \(\mathcal B\) be any complex fusion category whose based ring is the adjoint SU(3)_5 ring, conventionally denoted PSU(3)_5. Its based ring is its own adjoint ring. Since 3 and 5 are coprime, multiplication by the invertible SU(3)_5 objects identifies its full based ring with

\[
K_0(\mathrm{PSU}(3)_5)\otimes \mathbb Z[C_3].
\]

Consequently the ordinary Deligne product \(\mathcal B\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}_{C_3}\), with the trivial pointed associator, has the SU(3)_5 based ring. No braiding on \(\mathcal B\) or special choice of pointed associator is needed for this step.

Apply Theorem 5.1 to that product. A tensor equivalence preserves the adjoint subcategory, because the latter is generated by the simple summands of \(X\otimes X^*\). On the left the adjoint subcategory is \(\mathcal B\boxtimes\operatorname{Vec}\). On the right it is the adjoint quantum-group subcategory. A normalized grading 3-cocycle restricts to 1 on the neutral component, so the cocycle twist does not affect this restriction. Therefore

\[
\mathcal B\simeq_\otimes
\mathcal C(\mathfrak{sl}_3,8,q)_{\mathrm{ad}},
\qquad q\text{ primitive of order }16.
\]

These are the Galois variants of the standard PSU(3)_5 tensor category. This conclusion holds for arbitrary complex fusion categories with the indicated based ring.

## Scope and remaining dependency

The use of a braided category in the application in Bonderson–Rowell–Wang, arXiv:1910.07061, does not restrict the scope above: the needed general monoidal theorem is explicitly available, and restriction to the adjoint subcategory is intrinsic to a tensor category. Theorem 5.4 of Feng–Ming–Rowell is a separate theorem about **braided** subcategories and is not needed for this argument.

To finish the proposed R342 exclusion one must still check the exact reconstructed dual tensor and the claimed obstruction to an extension by the required autoequivalence for every Galois variant. Those are separate inputs, not consequences asserted by this audit.
