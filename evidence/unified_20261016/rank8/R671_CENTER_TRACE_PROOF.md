# A conductor-forty center trace obstruction for R8_2_671

**Theorem.** R8_2_671 has no complex fusion categorification.

This proof uses a theoretical quadratic compression, actual character-labelled central objects, and two explicit integer inequalities. All finite domains are reconstructed from the original multiplication tensor by the accompanying standard-library verifier. No optimizer or numerical premise enters the verification.

## 1. General categorical scope and source data

The separately verified `HI6_PAIR_GALOIS_PU.md` proves that every complex categorification of R630 or R671 has a Galois conjugate which is pseudo-unitary. Its proof reconstructs the positive-indicator spherical cover and checks that its only signed associative quotient is the original based tensor. Every other ordinary character has a zero basis value, so a spherical dimension character must be one of the two FP/Galois characters. We therefore use the canonical positive spherical structure of that conjugate. A unitary associator realization is not assumed.

For R671 put q=sqrt(10). In source order the simple dimensions are

    d=(1,1,3+q,4+q,4+q,3+q,2+q,2+q),  D=120+36q.

All eight objects are self-dual. The ordinary characters, other than d and its Galois conjugate, are

    (1,-1, 1, 0, 0,-1,0,0),                  f=4;
    (1,-1,-1, 1,-1, 1,0,0),                  f=6;
    (1, 1, 1,-1,-1, 1,0,0),                  f=6;
    (1,-1,-1,-2, 2, 1,0,0),                  f=12;
    (1, 1,-1, 0, 0,-1,1+sqrt(3),1-sqrt(3)),  f=12;
    (1, 1,-1, 0, 0,-1,1-sqrt(3),1+sqrt(3)),  f=12.

Together with the two dimension characters these are eight distinct characters, hence the complete character table. The verifier checks all product equations and formal codegrees. It also checks

    F I(1)=(8,2,4,6,8,6,6,6),
    D D'=1440,  D/D'=19+6q,
    1/D-1/D'=-q/20=-1/sqrt(40).

The pivotal odd-isotypic lemma, applied to all words X Y X* and X^3, forces every second indicator to be +1. The exact source parity graph is connected to the unit. The invertible g is source label1 and has order2.

## 2. Galois row transport and saturation

For a central simple Z let v_i=[F(Z):X_i], and write its dimension as z=A+Bq, where

    A=v0+v1+3v2+4v3+4v4+3v5+2v6+2v7,
    B=v2+v3+v4+v5+v6+v7.

Ostrik's canonical correspondence labels the eight constituents Z_chi of I(1) by the actual ordinary characters:

    S_(Z,Z_chi)/S_(1,Z_chi)=chi(FZ),  S_(1,Z_chi)=1/f_chi.

This is the definition before Theorem2.13, using Example2.9, of arXiv:1309.4822. Repeated codegrees do not permit arbitrary permutations of character labels.

Choose a cyclotomic automorphism sigma which flips q and fixes sqrt(3). Such an automorphism exists since the quadratic character fields are disjoint. Modular Galois symmetry and character orthogonality give the row transport

    T(v)=v+b*(A-10B/3),  b=(0,0,1,1,1,1,1,1).

Every actual row therefore has partner T(v) or -T(v), whichever is nonnegative. Its entries must be integral; in particular B is divisible by3. The other six character evaluations are unchanged because each character annihilates b, while the dimension evaluation is multiplied by D/D' after conjugation. These statements verify the formula and T^2=Id directly. Distinct partner row types occur with equal multiplicities. The central unit is transported to the forced row

    (1,0,1,1,1,1,1,1).

The multiplicity vector of I(1) is fixed by S, so its support is Galois-stable and the Galois signs there are positive. The Galois orbit of the central unit consists precisely of itself and J=Z_(d'). Thus w=e_1-e_J has quadratic torus character chi40. The known twist-one block gives S-compression -w/sqrt(40), because all other formal codegrees are rational.

The conductor equality lemma in `compression/CONDUCTOR_EQUALITY_LEMMA.md` now applies: the complete twist-one projection satisfies

    P S w=-w/sqrt(40),

and the cyclic modular representation generated by w has level40. The quadratic Gauss-sum refinement in `compression/QUADRATIC_RESIDUE_SATURATION.md` places its primitive40-th twist modes in the negative chi40 class. The coordinate of Sw at Z is z/D-sigma(z/D).

Consequently every high central simple (outside I(1)) whose ratio z/D is nonrational has twist order dividing40 and **cannot have twist1**. The latter exclusion follows from the exact full projection identity: the right side has no high coordinate. Smaller twist orders 2,4,5,8,10,20 remain allowed. High rows with rational z/D retain unrestricted twists, including1.

For pairing moments one can choose sigma with exponent59 modulo120: it flips sqrt(10), fixes sqrt(3), and its square is1 modulo40. The nonrational partner rows consequently have equal twists. Alternatively, equal relative moments follow in general from modular Galois transport theta_(pi_sigma Z)=sigma^2(theta_Z) and sigma^2 fixing K. Rational-ratio rows are fixed by T and do not require pairing.

## 3. A root-order independent two-moment domain

Write t for normalized relative trace from a common cyclotomic field to K=Q(q). Every arbitrary-order root theta satisfies

    (t(theta),t(theta^2)) in convex hull of
    (1,1), (-1,1), (1/2,-1/2), (-1/2,-1/2), (0,-1),
    (q/8,0), (-q/8,0), (0,q/8), (0,-q/8).

Here convex weights are nonnegative real numbers. This imposes no global twist-order bound. To prove it, use Ramanujan sums for rational traces. Odd squarefree orders give (c,c), twice odd squarefree orders give (-c,c), with c=mu(n)/phi(n) between -1/2 and1; multiples of4 with rational traces have first coordinate0 and second in [-1,1]. These are in the first five vertices. A nonzero irrational first trace occurs only at order40m, with m squarefree and coprime to10, and is a signed q/(8phi(m)); the second trace is0 because the squared root has order20m. A nonzero irrational second trace occurs at order80m, with first trace0. Both are covered by the four displayed irrational vertices. Higher shared prime powers give zero. These assertions are the induced primitive quadratic Gauss-sum formula for chi40, with mu(40)=0 and positive Gauss sum sqrt(40).

For a high nonrational-ratio row, the exact allowed finite orders and residue sign instead give the seven pairs

    (-1,1), (0,-1), (-1/4,-1/4), (0,0),
    (1/4,-1/4), (0,1/4), (-q/8,0),

coming respectively from orders2,4,5,8,10,20,40. In particular the primitive40 relative trace is -sqrt(40)/phi(40)=-q/8, while its square has trace0.

If v1>0, Z occurs in I(g). The second indicator formula and nu2(g)=1 give Tr(theta^2 I(g))=D=dim I(g). Positivity and equality in the triangle inequality force every such twist to satisfy theta^2=1. Such a row therefore uses only (1,1),(-1,1); if its ratio is nonrational it uses only (-1,1).

## 4. Exhaustive restrictions and two integer inequalities

The source induction Gram matrix is

    C_ij=sum_(k,l) N_(ki)^l N_(kj)^l=sum_Z v_i(Z)v_j(Z).

Every row of I(1) has v0=1, known dimension D/f_chi, and their sum is the displayed FI(1). The unit and Galois-unit rows are forced. For the six remaining **labelled** rows, impose their dimensions, source bounds, character reciprocity

    chi_i(v_j)/f_i=chi_j(v_i)/f_j,

and positive semidefiniteness and entrywise nonnegativity of each partial residual Gram matrix. All these are necessary for an actual center. The exact exhaustive domains have sizes

    86,74,74,22,22,22.

Complete backtracking has node counts 1,5,8,8,8,6,6 and leaves exactly six low frames. The verifier reconstructs all of this using rational Schur-complement PSD tests; it trusts no saved frame list.

For each residual matrix R every high row has v0=0, nonnegative integer entries bounded by floor(sqrt(R_ii)). Its dimension must satisfy D/z algebraic integral: the center formal-codegree identity gives (D/z)^2 integral, hence D/z itself is integral by transitivity. Since O_K=Z[q], this is precisely

    A^2-10B^2 divides 120A-360B and 36A-120B.

Impose integral nonnegative Galois partners and the PSD bound R-sum_(partner orbit) vv^t >=0, together with the entrywise bound. This retains every actual partner orbit and leaves respectively

    73,85,73,28,36,28

orbits. For each orbit use the finite trace vertices from Section3. The resulting numbers of moment columns are

    360,398,360,138,168,138.

A column has 56 integer entries: the28 upper-triangular Gram contributions, followed by the14 coefficient entries of 8 sum v_i z t(theta), then the14 entries of 8 sum v_i z t(theta^2), with i=1,...,7. Coefficients are in the basis1,q; all eight-times traces are integral in that basis.

For each i>0 the targets are the residual Gram entries and

    Tr(theta I(X_i))=0,
    Tr(theta^2 I(X_i))=D nu2(X_i)=D,

after subtracting the known twist-one low contributions. Thus an actual center supplies a nonnegative real combination of the moment columns equal to the target.

The certificate `r671_center_trace_certificate.json` contains just TWO integer covectors y of length56. The verifier checks y.c>=0 for every reconstructed column and y.target<0. The first covector separates frames0,1,2 with target -225800939520; the second separates frames3,4,5 with target -960. Each minimum column pairing is0. A nonnegative combination cannot have a negative pairing, proving the contradiction for all six frames.

The general-to-pseudo-unitary reduction in Section1 proves the theorem for arbitrary complex fusion categories.
