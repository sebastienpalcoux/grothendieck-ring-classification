# Independent audit of the conductor-equality lemma

Reviewed the proof in `CONDUCTOR_EQUALITY_LEMMA.md` on 16 October 2026.
The conclusion concerns a finite unitary representation of
SL2(Z/NZ), and in categorical applications imposes a necessary twist
restriction. It alone supplies neither existence nor nonexistence.

For a prime-power modulus, the primitive-vector permutation representation
is induced from the upper unipotent subgroup: its stabilizer at (1,0) is
exactly that subgroup. Thus every irreducible representation having a
unipotent-fixed vector embeds equivariantly and, after rescaling,
isometrically into this permutation representation.

For a nontrivial torus character alpha, unipotent-invariant functions
supported on q a unit form the single direction F_alpha. The complementary
support has q a nonunit and p a unit. After applying the Weyl element and
averaging over the upper unipotents, this complementary support maps to
the single F_conjugate(alpha) direction. Its adjoint image is the
G_conjugate(alpha) direction. The two blocks have disjoint source and target
supports, and their common maximal singular value is l^(−c/2), where l^c
is the character conductor. Hence the maximal-singular subspace is exactly
the stated span of F_alpha and G_conjugate(alpha). Both depend only on
the primitive-vector coordinates modulo l^c. Their group translates
therefore remain in the subrepresentation inflated from conductor level.

For a trivial local character, equality in an orthogonal projection's
norm means that S w is upper-unipotent-fixed. Together with upper-
unipotent invariance of w, this gives invariance under both upper and lower
unipotents, which generate the entire local SL2 group.

The global step correctly uses the Chinese remainder factorization and
orthogonal irreducible decomposition. No cancellation between summands can
hide a strict norm deficit. The top eigenspace of a tensor product of
positive contractions is the tensor product of their top eigenspaces.
Thus equality forces conductor-level inflation at every prime, including
triviality at primes absent from the conductor. The cyclic subrepresentation
generated by w factors through the conductor, and T^f S w=S w follows.

The corollary using a known twist-one block is valid because its squared
norm already equals the global upper bound. Unknown twist-one coordinates
must vanish; all nonzero coordinates of S w then have f-th-root twists.

**Verdict: no gap found in the stated theoretical lemma and corollary.**
Any application must separately verify the torus character, its conductor,
and the exact saturation hypothesis. The earlier incorrect inference
S w=w is not used here.

## Conductor-five residue refinement

The later conductor-five polynomial refinement was independently checked.
For the quadratic character of F5, P S F=G and P S G=F/5, so the
eigenvector of compressed eigenvalue delta/sqrt5 is F+delta*sqrt5*G.
On a nonzero-q orbit, its Weyl image is proportional to
chi(x)+delta*sqrt5*1_(x=0). Its Fourier coefficient at nonzero k is
sqrt5*(chi(k)+delta), by the quadratic Gauss sum. Thus only frequencies
with chi(k)=delta occur, plus the zero frequency. Since chi(−1)=1,
switching the convention for translation or Fourier transform does not
change this residue class. The surviving primitive eigenvalues have sum
(−1+delta*sqrt5)/2, giving exactly

    (T−1)(T²−((−1+delta*sqrt5)/2)T+1) S w=0.

For a coordinate outside the saturated known twist-one block, the
eigenvalue one is absent. Averaging the two conjugate primitive roots
over Q(sqrt5) gives the stated normalized trace
(−1+delta*sqrt5)/4, and replacing the twist by its k-th power multiplies
delta by chi(k) when 5 does not divide k. No gap found in this refinement.
