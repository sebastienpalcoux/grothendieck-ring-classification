# Odd cyclic Clifford constraints for the R155 frontier

Research note, 17 October 2026. AI-generated; not independently human-verified or Lean-formalized. This note establishes additional necessary conditions and an exact surviving spectrum calculation. It does **not** construct or exclude a fusion categorification of R6_4_155.

## 1. Categorical input and scope

Let C be a spherical fusion category, h an invertible object of order two with an associative algebra structure on 1+h, and Y a simple with hY≅Y≅Yh. Choose normalized left and right actions l,r satisfying

    r(l⊗id_h) = −l(id_h⊗r).

This is the anticommuting-stabilizer hypothesis in the existing third-rotation proposition. It holds in the R155 candidate for h and each of X,Y. Define the pair-insertion endomorphism

    a = (r⊗l)(id_Y⊗q^−1⊗id_Y) ∈ End(Y²),

where q:h²→1 is the associative multiplication. Normalization gives a²=1. Insertions at adjacent positions anticommute; insertions on disjoint positions commute. These are identities in End(Y^n), with no positivity, braiding on C, or self-duality of Y assumed.

Let Z be a simple object of the Drinfeld center and V=Hom_C(FZ,Y^n). The generalized pivotal rotation E acts on V and satisfies E^n=θ_Z^−1 under the convention used in the public generalized-indicator checker. The foundational definitions and rotation identities are in Ng–Schauenburg, *Congruence subgroups and generalized Frobenius–Schur indicators*, arXiv:0806.2493, Section2; the modular trace formula used by the checker is Corollary5.6. Ordinary pivotal rotation and naturality are in Ng–Schauenburg, *Higher Frobenius–Schur indicators for pivotal categories*, arXiv:math/0503167, Definition=3.1, Lemma3.4, Theorem5.1.

Naturality in the tensor product of the n−1 unmoved factors gives

    E(a⊗id^(n−2))E^−1 = id⊗a⊗id^(n−3).

Repeated conjugation yields all ordinary adjacent insertions except the one crossing the cyclic boundary. Define that final insertion by conjugation. Since E^n is scalar, conjugation has exact order n. For n≥3 the resulting A_j, indexed by j modn, obey

    A_j²=1;
    A_j A_k=−A_k A_j if j−k=±1 modn;
    A_j A_k= A_k A_j otherwise.

For n=3 the first rule makes every distinct pair anticommute. For n≥4 any pair can be rotated away from the cyclic boundary, reducing the assertion to the local/disjoint identities above.

## 2. Odd cyclic algebra and its rotation

Take n odd and r=2^((n−1)/2). The abstract complex algebra generated by the A_j above has vector-space basis A_0^e0⋯A_(n−1)^e(n−1), e_j∈{0,1}. Its commutator form over F2 is the adjacency matrix of an n-cycle. Its radical is precisely the line of the all-ones vector: the equations are e_(j−1)=e_(j+1), and odd ness forces all coordinates equal. Consequently the algebra is M_r(C)⊕M_r(C).

A concrete model of either simple block uses Clifford generators γ_j with γ_j²=1 and γ_jγ_k=−γ_kγ_j for j≠k, setting

    A_j = i γ_j γ_(j+1).

The product of all the A_j is i^n. Replacing every A_j by −A_j gives the other central character because n is odd. The cyclic permutation γ_j↦γ_(j+1) is an element of SO(n); its spin lift implements A_j↦A_(j+1) in either block. Choose its unique sign for which its nth power is1. Call the resulting implementer U.

The eigenvalues of the orthogonal n-cycle are1 and exp(±2πij/n), 1≤j≤(n−1)/2. The standard exterior-algebra model of the spin representation therefore gives the following exact U-spectrum, with one eigenvalue per sign vector:

    ζ_n^[ (ε_1·1 + ε_2·2 + ⋯ + ε_((n−1)/2)·(n−1)/2)/2 ],
    ε_j∈{−1,+1}, division by2 taken modulo n.

This formula includes the sign that makes U^n=1. The two blocks have the same spectrum; any different choice of implementer with nth power1 merely shifts it cyclically.

Normalize E by any nth root of θ_Z so that E_0^n=1. On each block E_0 and U induce the same algebra automorphism. Thus S=U^−1 E_0 commutes with the algebra, and S^n=1. It follows that the C_n-character of E_0 factors as the spin character times a genuine C_n-character on a multiplicity space. This is stronger than requiring E_0 itself to have nonnegative integral eigenvalue multiplicities.

Explicitly, let c_j be the number of sign vectors giving spin weight j, and let m_j be the E_0 eigenvalue multiplicities. There must exist a_j∈Z_≥0 such that

    m_j = Σ_k c_(j−k) a_k,
    Σ_k a_k = dim(V)/r.

Changes of twist-root convention or reversal of rotation only cyclically permute or reverse these sequences and do not change feasibility. At n=3, c=(0,1,1), so a_j=dim(V)/2−m_j. This recovers the third-rotation result and applies it to every central target.

The cyclic convolution matrix is invertible for odd n. Indeed its eigenvalues are spin traces of powers of the n-cycle; each such power is a permutation all of whose cycles have odd length, so the spin-character product has no zero cosine factor. Thus the nonnegative integral a_j, if present, are uniquely determined.

## 4. All-order multiplicity divisibility adds no new exclusion to R′(m)

The path algebra generated by the n−1 ordinary adjacent pair insertions has simple-module dimension

    r_n = 2^floor((n−1)/2).

Hence every multiplicity of a simple summand in Y^n must be divisible by r_n. This statement requires no cyclic rotation at all.

For the generalized near-group based ring R′(m), these infinitely many divisibility conditions are already equivalent to m even. Let x=[X], y=[Y], and h,g be as in the checkpoint. The cubic product is

    x³ = m(1+h+g+hg) + 2m² x + (2+2m²)y.

Thus n=3 forces m even. Conversely, assume m even. The coefficients of x³ and x⁴ are even. The ring relations give

    x⁴ = 2m x³ + 4m x + 2(1+h),
    x⁵ = 2m x⁴ + 4m x² + 4x,

and therefore, for all n≥5,

    x^n = 2m x^(n−1) + 4m x^(n−3) + 4x^(n−4).

If each earlier power has coefficients divisible by r_k, the three terms have respectively at least

    2+floor((n−2)/2),
    3+floor((n−4)/2),
    2+floor((n−5)/2)

powers of2 in every coefficient. All are at least floor((n−1)/2). Induction proves the full family. The same argument applies to y by duality. Thus the all-order path-Clifford divisibility condition gives no further restriction once the established even-m condition is imposed.
