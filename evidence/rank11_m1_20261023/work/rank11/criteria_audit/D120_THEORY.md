# A central dihedral and Brauer–Picard obstruction in dimension 120

## Statement and scope

Neither R11_1_40 nor R11_1_82 admits a fusion-category realization over C.
The argument is uniform, uses only exact finite ring data listed below, and does
not assume braiding or unitarity of a hypothetical realization. Integral fusion
categories are pseudo-unitary, so their canonical positive spherical structures
are available. No bounded search, numerical infeasibility, or completeness claim
about a list of models enters this proof.

The new obstruction combines a forced central Rep(D10), a faithful C5 grading
after de-equivariantization, and the absence of order-five elements in the
Brauer–Picard group of every categorification of the Rep(A4) fusion ring. Here
D10 denotes the dihedral group of order ten.

## Exact input facts

Both rings are commutative, have rank eleven, and have simple dimensions

    1, 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4.

Write g for the unique nonidentity invertible. It has order two. Multiplication
by g exchanges 1 with g, exchanges the two dimension-three simples, and fixes
the dimension-two simple and all six dimension-four simples.

The basis elements of dimensions at most three form a fusion subring of rank
five and dimension twenty-four, with dimensions 1²,2,3². Its closure,
the displayed dimensions, and the action of g are all that is needed below.
The R11_1_40 subring is Rep(S4); the R11_1_82 subring has the dimension-three
simples dual to one another and must not be identified with Rep(S4). No
uniqueness or specific model of either subring is assumed.

For each of the eleven scalar characters χ of the complexified ring, let
fχ=Σ_i χ(b_i)χ(b_i*) be its formal codegree, and βχ=120/fχ. The multiset is

    {1, 2, 2, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 15, 30, 30}.

The two characters with βχ=2 both take the value one on g. A complete exact
character table is sufficient to verify these statements: eleven distinct
unital homomorphisms of this commutative semisimple algebra exhaust its
characters. The table must be checked against every multiplication identity,
and the tensor hash must match the source input.

For the forgetful functor F:Z(C)→C and its right adjoint I, the ring determines
B_ij=dim Hom(I(b_i),I(b_j)). In particular B_gg=11. This also follows directly
from the induction formula F(I(X))=⊕_a a⊗X⊗a*: since g is invertible and the
ring is commutative, each summand contributes one copy of g.

In the stored ordering, g has index 1 for R11_1_40 and index 9 for R11_1_82.
The permutations induced by g are respectively

    [1,0,3,2,4,5,6,7,8,9,10]
    [9,2,1,3,4,5,6,7,8,0,10].

## 1. Two dimension-two central simples force Rep(D10)

Assume that C realizes either ring, equipped with its positive spherical
structure. Ostrik's Theorem 2.13 identifies the simple constituents Aχ of I(1)
with the scalar characters. Each occurs once, has dimension βχ, and satisfies
[F(Aχ):1]=1. Theorem 2.5 gives twist θ_Aχ=1. The character identification preceding
Theorem 2.13 gives the unnormalized S-matrix relation

    S_(Aχ,Y) = βχ χ(F(Y)),  Y simple in Z(C).

Let A and B correspond to the two β=2 characters. Positivity and the unit
multiplicity force F(A)=F(B)=1⊕g. Therefore

    S_AA = S_AB = S_BB = 4.

In a positive spherical ribbon category this implies that A and B centralize
each other and themselves. Indeed the balancing equation expresses the
normalized trace of monodromy as a positive weighted average of roots of unity;
equality with its positive maximal value forces every eigenvalue to be one.
The tensor subcategory T generated by A and B is consequently symmetric, and
its twist is trivial. The positive symmetric category T is Tannakian.

Every object in T forgets to a direct sum of 1 and g. We now list the possible
simple central objects with this property, without enumerating low columns.

If [F(Y):1]>0, then Y is a constituent of I(1), and that multiplicity is exactly
one. Write F(Y)=1⊕m g. The Gram equality gives m²≤B_gg=11, so m≤3 and dim(Y)≤4.
The β multiset then shows that Y is exactly one of 1,A,B. In particular the only
simple central object whose forgetful image is supported on 1 alone is 1.

If [F(Y):1]=0, then F(Y)=m g. The image of Y*⊗Y is m² copies of 1, so all its
central simple constituents must be 1. Since Y is simple,
[Y*⊗Y:1]=1, giving m²=1. Thus Y is invertible and F(Y)=g. There is at most one
such object: the product of any two such lifts, one dualized, forgets to 1 and
must equal the central unit. Denote the possible lift by z.

This z must exist inside T. The object A*⊗A has dimension four and contains
1 exactly once. If the only simple objects available were 1,A,B, its remaining
dimension three could not be filled by dimension-two objects. Hence z occurs.
Thus T has precisely the four simple objects 1,z,A,B and dimension ten.
A Tannakian category with irreducible dimensions 1,1,2,2 is Rep(D10), since the
only groups of order ten are C10 and D10 and C10 has only one-dimensional
irreducibles. Its unique nontrivial invertible z has order two and twist one,
and F(z)=g.

## 2. De-equivariantization gives rank sixteen and a faithful C5 grading

The central subcategory E=⟨z⟩≅Rep(C2) embeds into C under F, with image ⟨g⟩.
ENO Proposition 2.10 therefore gives a de-equivariantization D=C_C2, of dimension
sixty, with C≅D^(C2). Its simple objects can be read directly from the action of
tensoring by g. A free pair {X,gX} produces one simple of dimension dim(X).
A g-fixed simple X splits into two simples of dimension dim(X)/2. The latter
statement uses H²(C2,C*)=0, or equivalently the two primitive idempotents of its
twisted stabilizer algebra; the algebra automorphism of 1⊕g interchanges them.
Consequently D has dimensions

    1³, 2¹², 3,

and rank sixteen.

Because T is symmetric, T⊂E'. The central de-equivariantization equivalence
(E')_C2≅Z(D) carries T_C2 to Rep(C5): de-equivariantizing Rep(D10) by its quotient
Rep(C2) leaves Rep(C5). Under the forgetful functor to D, every object of this
Rep(C5) becomes a multiple of the unit. Explicitly, the two dimension-two
D10 representations become pairs of nontrivial C5 characters, and their
forgetful images 1⊕g become two copies of the unit after de-equivariantization;
z and 1 also become the unit. Positivity then gives the claim for each simple
summand. ENO Proposition 2.9(i) now supplies a faithful C5 grading of D. Each
homogeneous component has dimension twelve.

The rank-five dimension-twenty-four subcategory of C determined by the small
basis elements contains ⟨g⟩. Its image K inside D has dimension twelve and simple
dimensions 1,1,1,3. The invertibles form C3, the unique dimension-three simple X
is fixed by them and self-dual, and dimension plus reciprocity gives

    X² = 1+a+a²+2X,  a³=1.

Thus K has the Rep(A4) fusion ring. Since X² contains every simple, K is its own
adjoint subcategory and has trivial universal grading. Any grading of D is
therefore trivial on K. The C5-neutral component D_e contains K, and equality
of dimensions gives D_e=K.

## 3. Every such K is Morita equivalent to a twisted pointed A4 category

This step concerns every complex categorification of the rank-four ring
C3+2, not merely the familiar category Rep(A4).

By ENO Theorem 1.6, dimension twelve=2²·3 makes K solvable. Definition 1.2(ii)
and Proposition 4.4 express a solvable category as a sequence of extensions and
equivariantizations by cyclic groups of prime order. Since K has trivial
universal grading, the last step cannot be a nontrivial extension. Hence
K=L^(Cp) for a prime p. The canonical Rep(Cp) subcategory gives p invertible
simples in K, so p divides three and p=3. Consequently dim(L)=4.

Simple objects in a C3-equivariantization have dimensions
[C3:H]·dim(π)·dim(Y), where H is a stabilizer and π an irreducible projective
representation of H. Here H is cyclic of order one or three, so dim(π)=1.
Every Y occurs below some simple of K. The available dimensions 1 and 3 imply:
a fixed Y must have dimension 1 or 3, the latter impossible in a dimension-four
category; a nonfixed Y lies in an orbit of length three and must have dimension
one. Thus L is pointed, say Vec_H^η with |H|=4.

The C3 action on H cannot be trivial, because otherwise all four pointed simples
are fixed and each gives three one-dimensional equivariant lifts. The group H
must therefore be V4, whose three nonidentity elements are permuted cyclically;
Aut(C4) has no element of order three.

The crossed product L⋊C3 is pointed of dimension twelve, and is categorically
Morita equivalent to L^(C3)=K (ENO Proposition 2.2). Its group of invertibles
is an extension of V4 by C3 with this nontrivial conjugation action. The
coprime-order extension splits and is V4⋊C3=A4. Therefore

    K is Morita equivalent to Vec_A4^Ω

for some three-cocycle Ω. No assumption that Ω is trivial has been made.

## 4. The twisted A4 Brauer–Picard group has no element of order five

Put P=Vec_A4^Ω. By Naidu–Nikshych Theorem 1.2 (equivalently Theorem 4.12), the
Lagrangian subcategories of Z(P) are parametrized by normal abelian subgroups
H of A4 together with invariant alternating Ω-bicharacters on H. The only
possible H are 1 and V4. For H=1 there is one choice. For H=V4, the ratio of any
two admissible Ω-bicharacters is an ordinary alternating bicharacter. V4 has
exactly two alternating bicharacters, so there are at most two choices. Thus
Z(P) has at most three Lagrangian subcategories.

Consider the stabilizer S of the canonical Rep(A4) inside Aut_br(Z(P)). It
preserves the regular connected étale algebra Fun(A4). Indeed a connected
étale algebra in Rep(A4) is the function algebra of a transitive A4-set, and
dimension twelve forces the free transitive set; hence that algebra is unique
up to algebra isomorphism. Conversely this algebra generates Rep(A4), so the
algebra stabilizer and subcategory stabilizer coincide.

Marshall–Nikshych Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4 identify this stabilizer with the
image of induction Aut_⊗(P)→Aut_br(Z(P)). A sufficient bound is already
available: Aut_⊗(P) has kernel H²(A4,C*)≅C2 over its image in Aut(A4)≅S4,
so its order divides 48. Therefore |S| has no factor five.

One may sharpen this using Marshall–Nikshych Proposition 4.5, which explicitly
allows arbitrary Ω. Its exact sequence is

    Z(A4) → H²(A4,C*) → S → OutStab(Ω) → 1.

As Z(A4)=1 and Out(A4)≅C2, |S| is two or four. This strengthened statement
is not obtained by applying an untwisted formula to a twisted category.
The group-cohomology and automorphism facts for A4 are also recorded in
Nikshych–Riepel §8.3.

The orbit of canonical Rep(A4) has size one, two, or three. Orbit–stabilizer,
together with BrPic(P)≅Aut_br(Z(P)), proves that |BrPic(P)| has no factor five.
Morita invariance gives the same conclusion for BrPic(K).

## 5. Contradiction

A faithful C5 extension D=⊕_(h∈C5) D_h of K defines a group homomorphism

    C5 → BrPic(K),  h ↦ [D_h].

Since BrPic(K) has no element of order five, this homomorphism is trivial.
Every D_h is consequently equivalent to the regular K-bimodule category and,
in particular, has exactly rank(K)=4 simple objects. Hence rank(D)=5·4=20.
This contradicts the independently forced rank sixteen. Both input rings are
therefore excluded for general complex fusion categories.
