# A cyclic stabilizer lemma for a pointed Morita dual

**Lemma.** Let E be a fusion category over C. Suppose there is an integer n>1 such that every noninvertible simple object has FP dimension n. Suppose that every such object has the same left and right stabilizer H in the group G of invertible objects, where H is cyclic of order n. Then the group algebra A=direct_sum(h in H) h exists as a connected separable algebra in E, and the category E_(A|A) of A-bimodules is pointed. This statement does not require a braiding, pivotal structure or unitarity.

**Proof.** A noninvertible simple X is fixed by every h in H, so the pointed subcategory on H has a rank-one module category. Its associator class therefore vanishes, and its regular algebra A exists. H is normal in G: conjugating a stabilizer by any invertible object produces the stabilizer of another noninvertible simple, which is again H.

As H is cyclic, its projective characters can be linearized. Thus X admits n pairwise nonisomorphic simple right A-module structures, each with underlying object X. Fix one of them, M. Its internal endomorphism object in E has FP dimension

    FPdim(internalEnd(M)) = FPdim(X)^2 / FPdim(A) = n.

The unit occurs with multiplicity one. Every noninvertible simple of E already has dimension n, so every constituent of this internal endomorphism object is invertible. An invertible g in it satisfies g tensor M isomorphic to M as right A-modules, hence g tensor X isomorphic to X in E. Therefore g belongs to H. Its multiplicity is at most one, since g tensor M and M are simple right A-modules. The internal endomorphism object has dimension n=|H|, so it is exactly the direct sum of H.

In particular the generator of H acts on M by a right A-linear isomorphism. Its cyclic coherence can be normalized because H^2(H,C^*)=0. This gives a commuting left A-action, with n choices distinguished by the ordinary characters of H. Varying the n right-module structures gives n^2 simple A-bimodules with underlying object X. These exhaust the X-isotypic bimodules, since A tensor X tensor A is n^2 copies of X. Each has bimodule FP dimension FPdim(X)/FPdim(A)=1.

For an invertible source object g, its H-double orbit is Hg, of size n because H is normal. The stabilizer for the left/right H action is a cyclic diagonal copy of H. Every irreducible projective representation of that cyclic stabilizer is one-dimensional. Thus all simple bimodules supported on this orbit have underlying dimension n and bimodule dimension one. All simple bimodules are therefore invertible. QED.

The internal-endomorphism dimension formula and the cyclic-action argument are the mechanism in Etingof--Gelaki--Ostrik, *Classification of fusion categories of dimension pq*, arXiv:math/0304194v2, Lemmas 6.1--6.2. The lemma above allows additional invertible objects outside H; its proof controls them using the exact common stabilizer. The categorical result is proved here; it is not an assumption that an arbitrary category equals a chosen lower-rank model.

## Application to the nine dimension-forty rings

All nine input rings have a unique nontrivial invertible h fixing all four dimension-two objects. Their common left and right stabilizer is exactly {1,h}. The full integral subring E has dimensions 1^4,2^4. Consequently E_(1+h|1+h) is pointed of dimension twenty for every categorification.

The entire ring has a faithful C2 grading into the integral E component and four sqrt(5) simples. The algebra 1+h lies in E. In all nine rings h commutes with every basis element and acts freely on the four sqrt(5) objects. Hence the full Morita dual has twenty invertibles and four sqrt(5) simples: the latter come from two double orbits of size two, each with two projective characters of a cyclic stabilizer. It is therefore a genuine feudal category.

Conversely, take the simple right (1+h)-module (1+h) itself in the Morita module category. Its internal endomorphism algebra for the dual action has dimension two, so it is 1+c for a nontrivial invertible c of order two. This is an actual algebra produced by Morita theory. Taking its dual recovers the original category. The involution c is noncentral in the twenty-element invertible group S: if it were central, every H-double orbit in S would have size two with cyclic stabilizer, and the recovered integral category would have twenty invertibles instead of four.

The only groups of order twenty with a noncentral involution are F20=C5 semidirect C4 with faithful action and D10 times C2. The cyclic and abelian groups have only central involutions; the remaining dicyclic group has a unique involution, which is central.

For the eight noncommutative dimension-forty inputs, their integral subring E is itself noncommutative. This rules out S=D10 times C2 independently of a chosen associator. Indeed, for H generated by a noncentral involution in this group, all four nonnormal double cosets are inverse-stable and have trivial stabilizer, so the four dimension-two objects of the recovered category are self-dual. Its invertibles form an abelian group of order four. Their left/right actions on the four dimension-two objects agree, since N_S(H)/H is represented by the extra central C2 factor; the kernel characters act trivially. Inverse invertibles also have the same action, because their squares belong to that kernel. Duality and Frobenius reciprocity now make every product commute. This contradicts the recorded noncommutative E tensor.

Thus all eight noncommutative inputs are recovered by the specific C2 algebra in an F20 feudal category. A complete classification of those feudal associators and their C2 duals could then exclude unsupported fusion tensors. **The lemma alone does not exclude any ring:** the remaining finite extension/cohomology classification must also be proved and checked.

## Cohomology coverage detail

For an F20 precursor the universal grading is D8, and its homomorphism to BrPic(Vec(C5))=O(C5 plus C5^*,q)=D8 is an isomorphism. Coprime-order cohomology gives H^2(D8,C5^2)=0. Once the graded product and one unobstructed associator are fixed, all associators form the usual H^3(D8,C^*) torsor. A claim of exhaustion must cover this whole torsor, subject to the actual C2 algebra's restriction condition; a failed finite root-of-unity phase search would not suffice.

The D10 times C2 precursor has two possible odd-reflection types in its Brauer--Picard homomorphism, corresponding to square/nonsquare stabilizer bicharacters. For positive exploratory constructions this distinction need not alter the recovered fusion tensor: multiply all fifth-root chi and upsilon phases by two, and use the cyclotomic automorphism zeta_40 -> zeta_40^17 to preserve fourth-root grading cocycles while changing zeta_5 to zeta_5^2. All monomial intertwiner dimensions are invariant. Nevertheless, a classification argument should explicitly account for this distinction rather than silently assume one Brauer--Picard homomorphism covers both.
