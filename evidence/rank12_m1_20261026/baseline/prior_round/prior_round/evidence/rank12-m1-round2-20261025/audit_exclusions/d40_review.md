## Categorical review

1. The source conditions are direct based-ring statements, independently checked for all nine inputs in `audit_d40_sources.py`. The integral subring is closed and dual closed; its common central involution fixes every two-dimensional simple and acts freely on the four square-root-five simples. The alternative internal-End proof of pointedness in Section 1 is especially convenient: each simple right A-module has underlying dimension two and normalized module dimension square-root-two, so its internal End has dimension two and is 1+g. This proves g fixes the right module itself, not only its underlying object. H2(C2,C*)=0 supplies the two compatible left actions, all normalized bimodule dimensions one. Hence the actual Morita precursor is pointed of dimension twenty, with no categorification-classification premise.
2. The inverse Morita algebra is in that integral pointed subcategory and has dimension two, hence 1+c. The invertible count in the original integral subring forces the normalizer of c to have order four. The five order-twenty groups are exhausted by C5 semidirect a Sylow-two complement. Only F20 and D10 x C2 have appropriate noncentral involutions.

The dihedral alternative has a commutative Morita ring for every allowable associator: outside the normalizer all double cosets have trivial stabilizer, are inverse invariant, and thus support unique self-dual simples. Invertible actions agree on the left and right and are involutions on this set. Dual reversal makes noninvertible structure coefficients symmetric, while the involutive invertible action makes the invertible coefficients symmetric as well. Thus the noncommutativity of each of the eight relevant integral subrings rules this group out. The supplementary six selected pointed-cocycle models are not needed for logical exhaustion.
4. In the F20 case, the faithful order-four conjugation on C5 makes the Brauer-Picard image of the rotation have order four. An odd component is a rank-one invertible C5-bimodule and maps to a reflection. Thus the universal group must be D8. Equivalently, the Liptrap biderivation and quasisymmetry equations give t_(r^-1 m)=2*t_m and t_(m*)=t_m, forcing sigma=-id and p=0; this derivation leaves tau and all other complex parameters unrestricted. It is not a failed phase search.
5. The neutral category is the untwisted Vec_C5 because a rank-one module fixes every C5 invertible. Its Brauer-Picard group is O(C5+C5*)=D8. The induced homomorphism is an isomorphism; all choices differ by a D8 grading relabeling that preserves the central square c=r*r. The lower product-system torsor is H2(D8,C5^2)=0 by coprime averaging. ENO's extension theorem therefore says that, once an actual precursor exists, every original complex precursor is a three-cocycle grading twist of it. The explicit Liptrap certificate provides the required initial category. No assertion that every category with a given ring is a known unitary model is inserted here.
6. The cocycle phase range is complete. Restrict to an index-two V4 in D8; its H3 with C* coefficients has exponent two by the ordinary Kunneth formula. Corestriction composed with restriction equals multiplication by two, so H3(D8,C*) has exponent dividing four. Thus all classes lift to fourth-root-valued cocycles. On the inverse-algebra subgroup C2 the degree-three coefficient map is an isomorphism, since H2(C2,C*)=0, so the normalized relative condition omega(c,c,c)=1 loses no allowable class. Twisted inverse-algebra multiplication has no additional parameter, again because H2(C2,C*)=0.

These facts invoke ENO, *Fusion categories and homotopy theory*, Theorems 1.1--1.3 and 6.1, and Liptrap, *Generalized Tambara--Yamagami categories*, Theorem 4 and Lemma 5.4. They apply to general complex categories. The precursor and its unit-modulus grading twists are unitary constructions for the affirmative cases.
