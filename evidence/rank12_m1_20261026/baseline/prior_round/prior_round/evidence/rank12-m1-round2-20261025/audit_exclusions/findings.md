## Categorical scope

- **Casimir / d-number.** Ostrik's rank-three paper, Proposition 2.2, explicitly gives the d-number property for all eigenvalues of multiplication by sum(b b*), with no rank-three or pivotal hypothesis in that proposition. Equivalently its eigenvalues are e_E*f_E. The audit never misidentifies this as the unweighted list of formal codegrees on a noncommutative ring.
- **Near-group restrictions.** The small-multiplicity argument is used with its commutativity hypothesis; the actual relevant source rings satisfy it. The irrational two-orbit criterion uses exactly the published divisibility restriction s|r for d*d=r*d+s.
- **Central fields and orders.** The hypotheses needed to replace categorical dimensions by FP dimensions are weak integrality or Galois pseudo-unitarity for two-dimension fusion rings. The latter is the statement of the **published** Schopieray Lemma 3.1. Its arXiv v1 instead says pseudo-unitarity of every original category, which the Yang--Lee example disproves. The first-round implementation and report already use the corrected Galois version, so this literature-version trap causes no status change. Galois conjugating the category leaves its based ring unchanged. Once sphericality is justified, Ostrik Theorem 2.13 supplies central simples of dimension D/f_E and unit multiplicity e_E. All noncommutative regular multiplicities are partitioned as sums of allowed e_E squared.
- **Cyclic isotypic criterion.** For a cyclic subgroup, the fixed-object module associators trivialize the H3 class, and H2(C_n,C*)=0 identifies the two relevant module-cochain classes. Consequently the chosen left and right actions use the same separable subgroup algebra A. The Z-isotypic block is stable under both actions, and its dimension in Bimod(A) is m*FPdim(Z)/n. The first-round criterion has the needed cyclic hypothesis. An additional squared internal-End argument, developed below, now removes the cochain-matching requirement for a stronger arbitrary-H version.
- **Primary criterion.** The six rings have FP dimension 216=2^3*3^3. ENO Theorem 1.6 gives solvability; GHR Theorem 5.20 gives unitarizability. This is a proved bridge from a hypothetical arbitrary complex category to the setting of the unitary primary criterion. Neither weak integrality in general nor Galois pseudo-unitarity is asserted to give unitarity.
- **Liptrap and transfer.** Liptrap's Theorem 4 classifies fusion systems for the checked feudal rules over C without assuming unitarity. All possible stabilizer bicharacters are covered. Every subring transfer checks a unit-preserving based isomorphism, dual closure and every product coefficient. No rank-eight theorem is silently extended to rank twelve.

### Rank-independent positive-FS normalization

In a pivotal category, M=Y tensor Y* carries the symmetric self-duality J=j_Y tensor id_(Y*): M -> M*=Y** tensor Y*. The pivotal identity j_(Y*)=(j_Y*)^(-1) gives J* composed with j_M = J. On a self-dual simple S-isotypic summand, express this nondegenerate symmetric self-duality as phi_S tensor B on the multiplicity space. Transposition gives B^T=nu_2(S)*B. For nu_2(S)=-1, B is alternating and its dimension is even.

In the sphericalization, the central sign object epsilon has dimension and second indicator -1; tensoring by it exchanges the indicators of the two self-dual lifts. A self-dual original simple has self-dual lifts, because otherwise duality would exchange opposite nonzero spherical dimensions. Select its positive-indicator lift. If N is the original multiplicity and T the difference between the two lift multiplicities, the negative-indicator multiplicity is (N-T)/2 and is even in every Y tensor Y*. Therefore N-T is divisible by four. These necessary restrictions are valid at any rank and require no unitarity. After a basis-sign gauge makes T=N, the selected lifts form a full spherical fusion subcategory whose forgetting functor is a tensor equivalence to the original category. This supplies the actual categorical descent, beyond merely solving signed associativity.
