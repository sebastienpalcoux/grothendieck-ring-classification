# Dimension 144: a two-step Morita reduction

## Status

R12_1_180, R12_1_181, R12_1_320 and R12_1_321 remain unresolved. No new exclusion or model is asserted. The four exact ring-twist identities were rechecked independently from the categorical existence question. The continuation establishes the necessary reduction below for every hypothetical complex categorification, without assuming a braiding.

## A cyclic fixed-object obstruction

Let H be a cyclic subgroup of invertible objects of order h, and let X be a simple object fixed on the right by H. The rank-one module category generated by X trivializes the associator on H. Since H^2(H,C*)=0, X admits a right module structure over the connected separable regular algebra A_H. The relative product X tensor_(A_H) X* is an actual object of the original category, of FP dimension FPdim(X)^2/h. Consequently this quotient must be an algebraic integer.

In particular, an invertible object of prime order p cannot fix a weakly integral simple of squared dimension not divisible by p. Such simples occur in orbits of length p, so their number at each such dimension is divisible by p.

This is an algebra-object form of the familiar stabilizer obstruction. The relative tensor-product dimension formula is the same mechanism used by Etingof--Gelaki--Ostrik, *Classification of fusion categories of dimension pq*, arXiv:math/0304194, Lemmas 6.1--6.2. It applies to arbitrary complex fusion categories.

## First Morita dual

Write the common source-basis dimensions as

    1,z,E,W0,W1,W2,P,Q,s0+,s0-,s1+,s1-
    1,1,2,4,4,4,3,3,3sqrt(2),3sqrt(2),3sqrt(2),3sqrt(2).

In all four target tensors, z fixes E and the W_i, exchanges P,Q, and exchanges the two members of each odd charge pair. The rank-three subring {1,z,E} has Rep(S3) fusion rules. The algebra A=1+z exists because z fixes E. The cyclic-stabilizer Morita lemma proves that the rank-three subcategory has pointed dual of dimension six. The dual group is S3: its actual reverse algebra has dimension two, and a central involution in a pointed group of order six would recover a pointed category, contrary to the original dimension-two simple E.

Each free z-double orbit {P,Q} yields two simple bimodules of dimension three. Each odd charge pair yields two simple bimodules of dimension 3sqrt(2). Each z-fixed W_i has a C2 x C2 projective stabilizer. It therefore yields either four dimension-two bimodules (trivial alternating class) or one dimension-four bimodule (nontrivial class). Initially the integral Morita dual could therefore have type

    1^6, 3^2, 2^(4t), 4^(3-t),    0 <= t <= 3,

and the full dual has four additional simples of dimension 3sqrt(2).

## New restriction: t is zero or three

Let H=C3 be the normal subgroup of the pointed S3 part. It cannot fix a dimension-two or dimension-four simple: the fixed-object obstruction would require 4/3 or 16/3 to be algebraic integers. Hence its action on either set has only three-element orbits. Thus

    3 divides 4t, and 3 divides 3-t,

which forces t=0 or t=3. Mixed patterns in which just one or two W_i split are impossible in every categorification.

The only two possible integral types of this first dual are therefore

    1^6,3^2,4^3,             or             1^6,2^12,3^2.

This reduces the full dual rank from the previous four possibilities 15,18,21,24 to 15 or24.

## A second actual algebra

The C3 subgroup fixes the two dimension-three simples, because it acts on a set of cardinality two. Its associator is therefore trivial. The regular C3 algebra exists, and a second genuine Morita condensation is available.

It also fixes each of the four odd simples. To see this without supposing commutativity of the target ring, note that the six invertible bimodules in the first dual have underlying support in {1,z,E}. The exact identity E*s= s+z*s implies that their action preserves each of the two-element bimodule blocks supported on an original odd z-pair. An order-three group acts trivially on each such block.

The remaining issue is now visible in the C3 x C3 projective stabilizers. A fixed dimension-three object yields nine invertibles if the alternating class is zero, but a single dimension-three object if the class is nonzero. A fixed dimension-3sqrt(2) object likewise yields nine dimension-sqrt(2) objects or one dimension-3sqrt(2) object. These possibilities cannot be discarded merely because the known braided triality model chooses one of them. Their compatibility with associativity and the remaining bimodules is still to be established.

Thus the second condensation is proved to exist; identifying its entire category with the known S3-crossed triality precursor is NOT proved.

## Model routes revisited

The ordinary triality gaugings (two retained fusion tensors) and their actual bosonic central zests remain valid controls. Their canonical current action flips both odd charge pairs. Twisting by that action reproduces the already modeled central zests, and produces none of the four targets.

The exact target identities instead require either a flip of only one odd pair, or an interchange of P,Q together with odd species. Exhaustive based-ring automorphism checks confirm the required involutions, but such a check does not construct a monoidal lift or a coherent C2 action. The known braided or physical conjugation symmetries cannot simply be substituted: a tensor action is complex-linear and needs its tensorators and coherence. No bounded search over these evident constructions has been converted into a noncategorifiability assertion.

A useful next theoretical problem is to classify the two-step Morita possibilities above, including all C3 x C3 projective stabilizer classes, and track the actual reverse algebras. This would supply a categorical converse if successful. The present notes do not claim that converse.

## Stronger result: the t=0 branch is impossible

The following argument was independently checked by the parallel audit. It excludes a Morita branch, not any of the four target rings: the t=3 branch remains open.

### The second dual if t=0

In the first dual of type 1^6,3^2,4^3, a right C3-module on either fixed dimension-three simple has internal endomorphism algebra of dimension three. Since this algebra contains the unit and every noninvertible has dimension at least three, all its summands are invertible. Its support is necessarily the unique C3 in S3. The algebra is the regular C3 algebra, up to isomorphism, because H^2(C3,C*)=0. Consequently a commuting left regular-C3 action exists. Each of the two dimension-three objects therefore gives nine invertibles in the second dual, rather than one dimension-three object. The three dimension-four objects form a free C3 orbit on both sides and give three dimension-four objects. The integral second dual has type 1^24,4^3.

Let u of the four odd objects have trivial C3 x C3 projective class. The odd type is then (sqrt(2))^(9u),(3sqrt(2))^(4-u). A dimension-sqrt(2) simple has exactly two invertibles in XX*, so its orbit under the 24 invertibles has size twelve. Thus 12 divides 9u: u=0 or4. If u=4, the 24 invertibles and 36 dimension-sqrt(2) simples form a fusion subcategory: the product of two such noninvertibles has dimension two and can only contain invertibles. Its dimension would be 96, but 144/96=3/2 is not an algebraic integer. Hence u=0. The full second dual has type

    1^24,4^3,(3sqrt(2))^4.

Write D for its integral subcategory (dimension72), G for its group of 24 invertibles, and M for its odd component.

### A near-group subcategory of dimension24

Left and right multiplication by G are transitive on the three dimension-four objects. Indeed, a fixed object would have all 24 invertibles in XX*, exceeding dimension16; this also rules out a 2+1 orbit partition. The commuting transitive left and right permutation actions must both be the regular C3 action, since the centralizer of the transitive S3 action on three letters is trivial. Their kernels coincide by duality and give a normal subgroup H of order eight. Choose a self-dual dimension-four object X (duality permutes an odd set), and let g represent a generator of G/H.

Write Xg=g^a X, a=1 or-1, and

    X^2 = sum_(h in H) h + k0 X + k1 gX + k2 g^2X,
    k0+k1+k2=2.

Associativity gives X^2 g=g X^2. If a=-1, comparison of the noninvertible coefficients forces k0=k1=k2, contradicting their sum two. Thus a=1. Duality gives k1=k2. There are precisely two possible coefficient vectors: (2,0,0) and (0,1,1).

The second vector cannot occur in a fusion category. Map H to1, g to a primitive cube root of unity, and X to a root x of x^2+x-8=0. This gives a one-dimensional character, whose formal codegree is f=24+3x^2=48-3x. Its irreducible minimal polynomial is

    f^2-99f+2376.

The quadratic d-number condition requires 2376 to divide 99^2, which it does not. Thus only (2,0,0) remains, and H together with X generates an actual near-group fusion subcategory B of type NG(H,2), dimension24. D is faithfully C3-graded with neutral component B and an invertible object in each component.

### The odd module identifies B and its dual

The G-action on the four simples of M is transitive. For an odd simple Y, XX* has dimension18; a possible orbit of size1,2,3 would give stabilizer size24,12,8, respectively, and none allows 18 minus that stabilizer size to be a nonnegative multiple of four. The only possibility is orbit size4 and stabilizer size6. Since H is normal of index three, its orbits have equal sizes, and their number divides three. Four objects therefore form a single H-orbit. Hence M restricts to an indecomposable B-module category of rank four, with equal normalized simple dimensions sqrt(6).

The category B is unitarizable: its dimension is24=2^3*3, so the established solvability and unitarizability theorems for such fusion categories apply. Thus the unitary near-group classification may legitimately be used here. The abelian case NG(H,2), |H|=8 is impossible by the abelian integral near-group classification. The nonabelian classification and algebraic realization identify B with

    C(S4, omega^ell, C4, 1),   ell=0,...,5,

where omega is inflated from a generator of H^3(S3,C*). The invertible group is D8 for even ell and Q8 for odd ell.

By the classification of module categories over pointed categories, the B-module M corresponds, under Morita equivalence, to a subgroup K of S4 and a cochain psi with dpsi=omega^ell|K. Its simple dimensions, normalized to squared sum24, are

    sqrt(4 |K|) dim(pi) / s,
    s=|C4 intersection gKg^-1|.

The intersection is cyclic, so every irreducible projective pi has dimension one. Equality with sqrt(6) leaves only (|K|,s)=(6,2) or(24,4). The order-six subgroup is a point-stabilizer S3, which contains no double transposition; its intersection with any C4 is trivial, so the first possibility is impossible. Therefore K=S4 and omega^ell must be cohomologically trivial: ell=0. In particular H=D8. The cochain psi may represent either class in H^2(S4,C*); it must not be silently set to zero. In either case the dual of this B-module category is tensor-equivalent to Rep(S4) (a cocycle twist changes its chosen fiber functor, not its tensor-equivalence class).

### The equivariantization contradiction

Apply Galindo's *Clifford theory for graded fusion categories*, arXiv:1010.5283v3, Corollary6.4, directly. The category D is C3-graded with neutral B, and its module category M restricts indecomposably to B, as proved above. The corollary gives an actual coherent C3 action on the restricted module dual and an equivalence (up to harmless tensor reversal)

    End_D(M) = End_B(M)^(C3) = Rep(S4)^(C3).

No lift of a based-ring automorphism is assumed here; the action is supplied by the graded module-category theorem.

Every C3 tensor action on Rep(S4) fixes all simple isomorphism classes, because the dimensions 1,1,2,3,3 each have multiplicity less than three. All stabilizers are cyclic and their projective representations are linear. The equivariantization therefore has type 1^6,2^3,3^6.

On the other hand, M is the odd component of a faithful C2 extension of D, so it is an invertible D-bimodule category. Its dual must have the same fusion type as D, namely 1^24,4^3. This contradicts the previous type. Therefore t=0 is impossible.

**Necessary first-dual type, now unique:** 1^6,2^12,3^2,(3sqrt(2))^4, total rank24. The classification of its second Morita dual and reverse algebra is still missing; none of the four rings is declared excluded.

## Another categorical premise now justified

The pointed S3 subcategory of the first dual has trivial associator on C3, since C3 fixes a dimension-three object. Its associator is also trivial on C2, because the actual reverse algebra for the first condensation has dimension two and support C2. Restriction followed by transfer multiplies its cohomology class by the subgroup index. The two vanishing restrictions therefore give 2[omega]=3[omega]=0, hence [omega]=0. The original rank-three category is C(S3,1,C2,1), which is equivalent to (Vec_C3)^(C2). Fourier equivalence Vec_C3=Rep(C3) intertwines inversion, giving ordinary Rep(S3). Thus its regular function algebra of dimension six exists. This does not prove that its embedding in the full category lifts to the Drinfeld center; a central embedding remains an additional categorical requirement.
