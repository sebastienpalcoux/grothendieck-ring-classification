# A group-theoretical converse for the two dimension-24 quotient types

## Statement

Let C be a complex C3 extension of Vec_G^omega, where G is V8 or C4 times
C2. Suppose the two nontrivial homogeneous components each have two
simple objects of dimension2, and the left and right stabilizer subgroups
of a nontrivial component are distinct subgroups H,H' of order4 generating
G. Then C is group-theoretical.

The hypothesis is categorical: the component is an invertible bimodule
category. The tensor data of the two proposed quotient rings establish
these subgroup hypotheses; their exact input binding is a separate check.

## Common reductions

Every h in H fixes the chosen simple object. Its rank-one module structure
trivializes omega|H, with an arbitrary matching 2-cochain retained. The
same argument applies to H'. Thus both restrictions of the associator
class vanish, without assuming the neutral associator is trivial.

A graded extension of a pointed category is group-theoretical exactly
when its grading group action on the center fixes a Lagrangian fusion
subcategory. We use the stable-Lagrangian criterion of
Gelaki--Naidu--Nikshych, *Centers of graded fusion categories*,
Corollary3.10, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.3117 .

## The V8 case with a nontrivial neutral associator

A stronger reduction, independently supplied by the D120 auditor, in
fact eliminates this entire case. The common two-object bimodule set has
joint stabilizer

    S={(g,h):p_L(g)=p_R(h)} in G times G,

where p_L and p_R are its distinct quotient characters. Its actual
bimodule cochain trivializes omega times omega^-1 on S. Restricting to
each order-two pair (g,h) gives q(g)=q(h), where q(g) records the
restriction of omega to <g>. Thus q is simultaneously a scalar multiple
of p_L and p_R. Since these characters differ, q vanishes. The seven
Boolean monomials below show that cyclic restriction is injective on
H3(V8,C*), so omega itself is trivial. The following direct twisted-center
analysis is retained as an independent weaker route and does not enter
the shortest proof.

Choose V8 coordinates (x,y,z) so that H={x=0}, H'={y=0}. The usual seven
generators of H3(V8,C*) have cyclic-restriction functions

    x, y, z, xy, xz, yz, xyz.

These seven functions on the seven nonzero vectors are linearly
independent. Restriction to H and H' therefore leaves precisely the
two-dimensional kernel generated by xy and xyz. Representatives are

    omega_II(a,b,c)=(-1)^(a_x b_y c_y),
    omega_III(a,b,c)=(-1)^(a_x b_y c_z).

If the type-III coefficient is nonzero, the commutator of the slant
2-cocycle in a nonzero degree g is (-1)^det(g,u,v). It is nontrivial,
so that degree has no one-dimensional projective representation. Hence
the center has exactly eight invertibles, all in its canonical Rep(V8).
That is its unique pointed Lagrangian and every tensor action fixes it.

If the class is the nonzero type-II class, the center is pointed with
metric group

    B = C4^2 times C2^2,
    q(r,s,u,v) = i^(r s) (-1)^(u v).

One can obtain this formula directly: projective characters in degree g
are i^(g_x t_y) times ordinary characters; the lifts of the x and y
generators have order4, square to the opposite coordinate character, and
have mutual quadratic value i. The remaining coordinate gives the
ordinary hyperbolic C2 pair.

The characteristic subgroup 2B has order4. The quadratic form on B[2]
has radical 2B and its quotient is a hyperbolic plane over F2. Every
elementary-abelian Lagrangian of order8 contains 2B: its image in this
quotient has dimension at most1. There are exactly two such Lagrangians,
corresponding to the two singular lines. An order-three action fixes
both. This again proves the required group-theoretical conclusion.

## The V8 case with trivial neutral associator

Now the center is the six-dimensional hyperbolic quadratic space
B=G plus G*. Let c be its induced isometry of order dividing3. The
identity case is immediate. Otherwise write B=F orthogonal-sum W, its
fixed space and moving space. Since 3 is odd, this is an orthogonal
nondegenerate decomposition. The moving dimension is 2,4, or6 in
principle; dimension6 is impossible in a plus-type six-dimensional
space: each irreducible two-dimensional order-three block is anisotropic,
so three such blocks have minus Arf invariant. Thus only2 or4 occur.

For clarity, the block assertion is elementary. In a nonzero moving
space choose v with q(v)=1. Then v,cv span a nondegenerate anisotropic
plane because v+cv+c2v=0 and the polar value b(v,cv)=1. Its orthogonal
complement is c-stable; continue inductively.

If dim(W)=4, it is a sum of two anisotropic planes. An isometry between
these planes intertwining their order-three actions has an invariant
totally singular graph of dimension2. The fixed space F is a hyperbolic
plane and supplies a fixed singular line. Their sum is an invariant
Lagrangian. Thus this case is group-theoretical.

If dim(W)=2, W is anisotropic and F is a four-dimensional minus space of
Witt index1. Let L=G* be the canonical Lagrangian. Then E=L intersect F
has dimension1: projection to W gives the lower bound and the Witt
index gives the upper bound. Moreover

    L intersect cL = L intersect c2L = E.

Indeed, for v in L intersect cL, both v and c^-1v lie in L, and their
difference lies in W. Since W has no nonzero singular vector, this
difference is zero. The same proof handles c2.

For an invertible Vec_G bimodule, the intersections L intersect cL and
L intersect c^-1L are precisely the character annihilators of its two
stabilizer subgroups. This also follows directly from the subgroup
description of a pointed bimodule: a pair of unit-supported central
characters acts trivially on its stabilizer relation only when each
character annihilates the corresponding projection kernel. Thus here
both stabilizers are Ann(E), the same subgroup of order4. This contradicts
the hypothesis H != H'. The only potentially non-group-theoretical
orthogonal conjugacy class is therefore impossible.

## The C4 times C2 case

Write G=<a> times <b>, with orders4 and2. Its order4 subgroups are
<a>, <ab>, and <a2,b>. Distinct stabilizers are either the two cyclic
subgroups, or one cyclic subgroup and the unique V4; changing generators
puts every pair in one of these forms.

Use H3(G,C*)=H4(BG,Z), with generators x2 of order4 and xy,y2 of
order2. Restriction to <a> sends x to the generator u of H2(BC4,Z) and
y to0. Restriction to <ab> sends x to u and y to2u. Restriction to the
V4 sends x,y to its two degree-two generators.

Consequently simultaneous triviality on a cyclic subgroup and V4 forces
the whole associator class to vanish. Simultaneous triviality on both
cyclic subgroups leaves only0 and y2, the inflation of the nontrivial
C2 associator.

Their centers are respectively the orthogonal sums of the hyperbolic C4
metric group with the hyperbolic C2 metric group or with the double-semion
metric group. In both cases the underlying group is C4^2 times C2^2.
The characteristic subgroup 2B is again the radical in B[2]. In the first
case there are exactly two elementary-abelian Lagrangians of order8. In
the double-semion case there is exactly one, because the double-semion
metric group has just one nonzero boson. Every order-three action fixes
one of these Lagrangians. This completes the proof.

## Scope

No unitarity, sphericality, or classification of unitary lifts is used.
The argument includes arbitrary matching stabilizer cochains. It proves
that every categorification with the stated neutral group and distinct
stabilizer hypotheses is group-theoretical, not merely that a known
group-theoretical model exists.

## The possible pointed Morita source groups

The invariant Lagrangian proof has a graded Morita realization. Choose
its module-category algebra B inside the original neutral category.
The full dual C_BB is C3-graded with pointed neutral component Vec_A^alpha,
where |A|=8. Stability of the module category says that each graded
component is equivalent to the regular module over this pointed neutral
component; consequently all its simples are invertible. Thus the full
dual is Vec_Gamma^Omega with A normal and Gamma/A=C3.

Schur--Zassenhaus gives Gamma=A semidirect C3. Among groups of order8,
only V8 and Q8 have automorphisms of order3. Their nontrivial actions are
unique up to conjugacy and give A4 times C2 and SL(2,3), respectively.

The remaining trivial-action possibility A times C3 is impossible.
Coprime Kunneth gives H3(A times C3,C*)=H3(A,C*) plus H3(C3,C*), so its
pointed category is a Deligne product. The inverse Morita algebra lies
in the neutral Vec_A factor. Taking its dual therefore gives the
original pointed neutral category times Vec_C3^beta, which would make
the entire original category pointed. This contradicts its nontrivial
two-dimensional simples.

## Integral involutions on these pointed sources

For an indecomposable module category M over an integral fusion category
D, normalize its dimensions by sum d(m)^2=FPdim(D). Its dimension vector
is rational up to scale, and d(m)^2 is the integer dimension of the
internal endomorphism object. Consequently d(m)=k_m sqrt(s), with
integers k_m and one common squarefree positive integer s.

An integral component X of an actual extension transports M to a module
M'. The normalized dimension of X tensor m is FPdim(X)d(m). All simple
objects of M' have one squareclass s'. Comparing any nonzero such
product forces s'=s. Thus integral Morita actions preserve the class of
module categories having integer normalized dimensions.

For Vec_Gamma^Omega, a Lagrangian corresponds to a normal abelian
subgroup K and an admissible invariant alternating omega-bicharacter.
The associated module dimensions are sqrt(|K|), so the integral
Lagrangians have square-order K. In A4 times C2 the only such subgroups
are1 and its normal V4. The latter contributes either no bicharacters
or a torsor for its two ordinary invariant alternating forms. Hence
there are exactly1 or3 integral Lagrangians. In SL(2,3) only K=1 has
square order, giving exactly1.

Every actual integral C2 extension therefore fixes an integral
Lagrangian of either pointed source. By the same stable-Lagrangian
criterion, it is group-theoretical. This is the additional converse
needed when these dimension-24 categories occur as neutral components
inside the original integral dimension-48 problem.
