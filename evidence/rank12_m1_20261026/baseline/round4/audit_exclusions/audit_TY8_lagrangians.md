I read `../c6/verify_TY8_lagrangians.py`, replayed it with Python `-O`, and

## Complete categorical scope

The Tambara--Yamagami classification used in GNN Section 4.1 is a
classification over C, not merely a unitary construction list. At group
order four the possible groups are V4 and C4. V4 has two isometry classes
of nondegenerate symmetric bicharacters, alternating and nonalternating;
C4 has the two bicharacters determined by chi(1,1)=i or -i. Each allows
tau=1/2 or -1/2. These are exactly the eight cases in the replay.

GNN Proposition 4.1 lists eight center invertibles X, six dimension-two
objects Y, and eight dimension-two objects Z. The script enumerates all
four quadratic refinements rho, both allowed epsilon roots for every X,
and both allowed Delta roots for every rho. It checks the defining rho
equation and square-root conditions exactly. Its six S-entry formulas and
three twist formulas agree with the primary source, including the
chi(a,a)^2 factor in the Z,Z sum. The unit row and full exact S unitarity
are additional consistency checks; they are not substituted for matching
the published formulas.

## Completeness and closure of the candidate enumeration

The center is pseudo-unitary, with its canonical positive spherical
structure. For simple objects X,Y, the condition S_XY=d_X*d_Y implies
trivial monodromy: the balancing trace is a positive weighted sum of roots
of unity, and equality in its maximal real value forces every eigenvalue
to be one. Thus the exact S test really tests centralization.

Any Lagrangian here has dimension eight. Since every center simple has
dimension one or two and the unit must be present, its simple dimensions
are necessarily either eight invertibles or four invertibles and one
dimension-two object. The script enumerates every set of these forms,
requires the unit, twist one for every member, and centralization for
every pair, including equal pairs.

It is not necessary to assume these sets are fusion closed. The fusion
subcategory generated by such a set centralizes itself, because centralizers
are fusion subcategories and the generating objects centralize pairwise.
The balancing axiom propagates twist one to all of this symmetric
subcategory, so it is Tannakian. Its dimension is at most sqrt(64)=8,
by the centralizer dimension formula. The original set already has total
squared dimension eight. Hence the generated fusion subcategory has no
additional simple objects, and the original set is exactly a Lagrangian.
This proves both soundness and completeness of the finite enumeration.

## Results and extension consequence

The exact counts, ordered by tau=+1/2 then -1/2, are:

| Bicharacter type | Positive tau | Negative tau |
|---|---:|---:|
| V4 alternating | 7 | 5 |
| V4 nonalternating | 2 | 2 |
| C4, chi(1,1)=i | 1 | 1 |
| C4, chi(1,1)=-i | 1 | 1 |

Every count is nonzero modulo three. A C3 action on the center therefore
fixes a Lagrangian, independently of action coherence or the particular
associator choice. GNN Corollary 3.10 proves that every faithful C3
extension of any TY category of dimension eight is group-theoretical.

For the rank-nine dimension-twenty-four quotient in the parent argument,
the graded pointed Morita source has group Gamma with normal subgroup of
order eight and quotient C3. The same Schur--Zassenhaus and coprime
Kunneth argument in `audit_integral_lagrangians.md` applies. If the group
action were trivial, reversing the neutral algebra would force the
original category to be TY8 tensor Vec_C3, of type 1^12,2^3. This differs
from the actual rank-nine type 1^4,2^5. Thus the pointed source is again
of group A4 x C2 or SL(2,3), and the separately audited integral-
Lagrangian lemma handles its integral C2 extension.
