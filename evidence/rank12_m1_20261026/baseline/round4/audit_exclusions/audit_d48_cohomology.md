# Independent audit of D48 cohomological coverage

The proposed associator families cover every admissible cohomology class
for the ten specified group/subgroup pairs. This conclusion is conditional
on the candidate pair list and does not by itself prove that every D48
categorification is group-theoretical. The actual fusion calculations for
the admissible pairs are separately checked by the D48 workstream.

`audit_d48_cohomology.py` passes under `python -O`: it verifies all defining
cocycle identities, independently recomputes the cyclic and commuting-triple
fingerprints, checks the quotient maps and all ten subgroup Schur-class
lists. `audit_d48_groups.py` additionally constructs exact 2x2 matrices over
F3 and unit quaternions over Q(sqrt(2)), and checks complete table
isomorphisms for GL2(3), SL2(3), the binary octahedral and binary tetrahedral
groups. Hence the periodic-cover arguments below bind to the actual tables,
not merely to a SmallGroup name.

## 1. Small cohomological upper bounds

The two-primary groups H^j(A4,U1), for j=1,2,3, are respectively 0,C2,C2.
Indeed restriction to V4 identifies them with the invariants under the
odd-order quotient C3. The first group is the invariant part of the
nontrivial two-dimensional F2 representation, hence zero. The second is
the invariant alternating form, of order two. For the third, use
H^4(V4,Z)=F2{x²,xy,y²}; the C3-invariant line is generated by
x²+xy+y². This proves the claims without a cohomology-table assumption.

Apply LHS to A4 normal in S4, with quotient C2. The only potentially
nonzero two-primary terms of total degree three have indices (3,0),
(1,2),(0,3), each of order two. Thus |H³(S4,U1)_2| is at most eight.
The actual eight fourth-root cocycles have distinct cyclic evaluations,
so the bound is attained and this family is complete. Distinctness is
all that cyclic detection is used for; no claim of a general detection
theorem is needed.

For A4 normal in A4 semidirect C4, the analogous terms have orders at most
4,2,2, giving a bound of sixteen. The supplied family has sixteen distinct
fingerprints among its 32 parameter choices and hence covers all classes.
The explicit quotient has kernel A4 and an order-four section, freshly
checked from the group table. Coincident fingerprints are not individually
declared cohomologous; reaching the upper bound makes that unnecessary.

For S4 times C2, the integral homology Künneth sequence gives two-primary
order 8*2*2*2=64 in degree three. The two extra cross factors come from
H2(S4,Z) tensor H1(C2,Z) and Tor(H1(S4,Z),H1(C2,Z)); both have order two.
Here H1(S4,Z)=C2 and H2(S4,Z)=C2; the latter also follows from the same
A4 LHS bound together with the explicitly nontrivial Schur cocycle.
The 64 actual family fingerprints are distinct, so the family is complete.
The map (projection to S4, extra character) is checked to be a bijective
group homomorphism, binding the product assumption to the frozen table.

Both fingerprint types are evaluations on integral bar cycles. For a
cyclic generator a the cycle is sum_i [a|a^i|a]. For a commuting triple
the cycle is the signed sum over its six permutations. Coboundaries have
trivial evaluations on these cycles even when their trivializing cochains
are arbitrary U1-valued, so the distinctness is in H³(-,U1), not merely
cohomology with finite coefficients. The independent replay checks every
saved value. All family generators are normalized genuine cocycles; sums
and pullbacks preserve this property.

## 2. Periodic-cover and Pontryagin arguments

The periodic generator assertion has a short direct proof. Let K be any
finite subgroup of SU2. The unit sphere bundle of its defining complex
two-dimensional representation over BK has total space homotopy equivalent
to S³/K. This is a closed oriented three-manifold. The Gysin sequence has
H³(S³/K,Z)→H⁰(BK,Z) given by multiplication by |K|, since pullback to the
sphere has that degree. It follows that H⁴(BK,Z)=Z/|K| is generated by the
Euler class c2 and that H³(BK,Z)=0. Applying the same argument to each
subgroup proves that restriction takes this c2 to a generator of its
degree-four cohomology. Thus no cyclic-detection assumption is needed.

For N=SL2(3), the exact quaternion identification supplies this SU2
representation. Hence H³(N,U1)_2=Z8 and H²(N,U1)=0. Its abelianization is
C3, so H¹(N,U1)_2=0. In GL2(3), the relevant D8 intersects N in a cyclic
C4, checked directly. Consequently an H-admissible class restricts to
N in {0,4} inside Z8. LHS for N normal in GL2(3), quotient C2, bounds
the kernel of restriction by two; therefore the entire inverse image of
{0,4} has at most four elements.

The S4 pullback family already contains four such elements. One is the
nontrivial sign-cube class, detected by an actual determinant C2 section.
Another is the two-primary part of p1 of the rotational S4 representation.
On N its pullback is −4c2, the nonzero order-two element of Z8. These two
classes are independent, one vanishing on N and the other not, so they
fill the four-element upper bound. They need not all be admissible on
the whole D8: the exact cochain test performs that final restriction.
But every admissible class is within the tested S4 pullback family.

For the binary octahedral group, H⁴=Z48, with two-primary part Z16. Its
quaternion H=Q8 has H⁴=Z8, and the generator restricts to a generator.
Thus the two-primary admissible kernel has two elements. The rotational
S4 representation pulls back to the adjoint representation of SU2. On
a maximal torus the fundamental weights are x,−x and the adjoint complex
weights are 2x,0,−2x. Therefore c2(adjoint complex)=4c2(fundamental),
and p1(adjoint real)=−4c2. Its two-primary component has order four in
Z16, so its cyclic subgroup contains the two-element admissible kernel.
Again the S4 pullbacks cover every admissible class. Changing a periodic
generator by a unit does not affect either of these subgroup statements.

## 3. Trivializing cochains and every Schur choice

Every associator in the tested families is fourth-root-valued, even though
stored modulo eight. Suppose its restriction W to H is U1-trivial, so
delta(psi)=W (or W inverse, according to algebra convention). Then psi^4
is a 2-cocycle. For every H in these ten pairs, H²(H,U1) has exponent at
most two, so psi^8=delta(lambda). Choose a pointwise eighth root eta of
lambda and replace psi by psi/delta(eta). The new trivializer has values
in mu8. Thus failure of the complete modulo-eight linear system is a
genuine restriction obstruction, rather than failure of a phase ansatz.

Given one trivializer, all algebra structures modulo coboundary are its
products with H²(H,U1). The independent replay verifies the actual
mu2 Schur cocycles and distinguishes them by commutator evaluations on
commuting pairs. Their cardinalities are complete: one for C4 and Q8;
two for V4, D8 and C4 times C2; eight for C2³. The abelian cases follow
from the exterior square. For D8, LHS for C4 normal with quotient C2
bounds H² by the single C2 term H¹(C2,C4 with inversion action); the
exhibited two classes meet the bound. For Q8, H²(U1)=H³(Z)=0 follows
from the preceding sphere-bundle argument.

Three-primary associator classes cannot change the fusion coefficients
for these pairs because H is a two-group, by the previously audited
coprime-primary invariance lemma. Its separable stabilizer and balanced
tensor argument applies without requiring 3 to be coprime to |G|.

These facts complete the cohomological coverage claim for all ten pairs.
They do not promote an incomplete pair list or a failed bimodule ansatz
to a negative categorification criterion. Those separate finite-group
and full-fusion checks must remain explicit in the final D48 argument.
