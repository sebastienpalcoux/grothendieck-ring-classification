The primary source https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.4822 supports the crucial hypotheses explicitly:

- Section5.1(a): both sphericalization lifts of an original self-dual simple are self-dual.
- Section5.1(b): the signed quotient coefficient satisfies the absolute-value bound by the original coefficient.

- Corollary2.15: formal codegrees lie in the spherical dimension field.

Applying these statements to the **full sphericalization** yields dimensions delta/L and delta/f that are algebraic integers in K, the field generated by the chosen spherical dimensions. It forces Q(f) inside K without assuming a pivotal structure on the original category.

Because every sphericalization simple is self-dual, the signed quotient has symmetric multiplication matrices. Its Casimir is sum_i N_i^2, positive definite since N_0 is the identity. Each field embedding of K gives a distinct dimension character (K is generated by its dimension coordinates). Distinct characters give distinct joint eigenspaces, even if their Casimir eigenvalues delta coincide. Thus every [K:Q] conjugate of delta is counted, and Tr_K(delta) is at most the full Casimir trace. No equality K=Q(delta) is assumed.

All signed character values and every field conjugate are real, by joint diagonalization of real symmetric multiplication matrices. Hence each conjugate of delta=sum_i d_i^2 is strictly positive, with unit contribution1. The relative trace t=Tr_K/F(delta) is nonzero and totally positive. Trace preserves algebraic integrality, so t/L and t/f belong to O_F. The ideal-intersection bound applies exactly as stated.
