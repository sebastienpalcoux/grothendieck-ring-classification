# A pointed integral-center filter

Let C be pseudo-unitary. Suppose that its simple dimensions have the form
`a_i+b_i sqrt(q)`, with every `b_i>=0`, and that the simples with `b_i=0`
are precisely the invertible source objects. Suppose also that the tensor
unit is the only integral-dimensional distinguished simple in `I(1)`.
For a commutative source ring, the latter condition is checked on the
complete list `FPdim(C)/f_E`.

Every integral-dimensional central simple restricts to the pointed source
subcategory: positivity of all irrational coefficients rules out any other
source summand. If an integral central simple Z contains the source unit
in F(Z), adjunction places it in I(1), so Z must be the tensor unit.

Now F(ZZ*) lies in the pointed source subcategory. Every central constituent
of ZZ* therefore has integral dimension. Only its unit constituent can
contribute a source unit after forgetting. Since Z is simple,

    1 = [1 : ZZ*] = [1 : F(ZZ*)] = sum_g [g : F(Z)]^2.

Thus F(Z) consists of one source invertible, and Z itself has dimension
one. If two central invertibles have the same restriction, their quotient
forgets to the unit and hence is the unit. Consequently the integral
central subcategory is pointed, and forgetting injects its invertible
objects into those of C.

The finite adapter checks the complete quadratic dimension and distinguished
dimension hypotheses, then deletes every high restriction supported on
the source invertibles unless it is exactly one basis invertible. Entire
duality orbits are removed, preserving the necessary orbit-count convention.
This is a pseudo-unitary necessary condition; general scope still needs a
separate dimension-character and signed-sphericalization argument.

The mathematical argument was independently challenged by the frontier
reviewer, including tensor closure and source-unit multiplicity. The exact
target receipts record all tested dimensions and every removed restriction.
