# A complete C3 split-module test for 439 and 567

This extends the fixed-point module method to an odd cyclic subgroup and
to noncommutative rings. It gives completed necessary systems with exact
feasible witnesses for `R8_3_439` and `R8_3_567`; it does not exclude or
categorify either ring.

In both rings the pointed subgroup generated by index `g=1` is `C3` and
fixes both noninvertible simples on the right and left. Their fixed
indices are `[4,5]` in 439 and `[3,4]` in 567. A fixed simple trivializes
the pointed associator on this subgroup, so `A=1+g+g²` is a separable
algebra. Each free three-element orbit induces one simple right module;
each fixed simple induces three modules, each with that simple as
underlying object. One can see the latter using the twisted group
algebra of its stabilizer: `H²(C3,C*)=0`, and the three irreducible
representations are one-dimensional.

Twisting right module structures by the three characters of `C3` gives
a module autoequivalence of order three commuting with every left
fusion action. It cyclically permutes the three modules over each
fixed simple. On one nontrivial Fourier eigenspace (dimension two),
write the action as `S_X`, with entries in `Z[ζ]`, `ζ²+ζ+1=0`.
If the fixed-block coefficient is `a`, the corresponding entry belongs
to the complete finite set

`E_a = {u0+u1 ζ+u2 ζ² : u0,u1,u2≥0, u0+u1+u2=a}`.

The matrices satisfy every fusion relation and `S_(X*)=S_X*` (Hermitian
transpose). This uses module rigidity and does not require a unitary
category; the Fourier change of an orthonormal integral basis is an
ordinary linear-algebra operation.

An odd involution `h` swaps the two fixed simples. Its matrix has zero
diagonal and entries `ζ^r,ζ^-r`. Changing the cyclic labels on the fixed
module triples normalizes it to `H=[[0,1],[1,0]]`, without losing a
representation. The relation `hgh=g^-1` forces
`S_g=diag(ζ^a,ζ^-a)`, `a=0,1,2`.

Both noninvertible fixed blocks have every entry equal to 3, so each
entry of their Fourier matrices lies in the ten-element set `E_3`.
Enumerating all **3×10⁴=30,000** choices per ring, reconstructing the
other noninvertible matrix as `H S_X`, and checking all 64 multiplication
relations, dualities and entry domains leaves exactly two conjugate
solutions:

* `S_g=diag(ζ,ζ²)`, `S_h=H`, `S_X=S_Y=0`;
* the complex conjugate of this system.

There is also a short completeness explanation. If `a` is nonzero,
`S_g S_X=S_X` forces `S_X=0`, and similarly for `Y`. If `a=0`, the
source relations make `S_X` commute with `H`, with `Y=hX`. On the
one-dimensional `+1` eigenspace of `H`, the scalar for `X` is real by
duality, hence rational in `Q(ζ)`, and is an algebraic integer. But the
source product gives `λ²−6λ−3=0`, which has no rational root. Thus
`a=0` is impossible. The zero entry is permitted by `E_3`, via
`1+ζ+ζ²=0`, so the two remaining systems are genuinely feasible at the
necessary matrix level.

As a positive control the known model
`R8_3_331=NG(C3,3) tensor Vec(C2)` is checked by the same implementation.
Its generator is index 3; odd conjugation is trivial rather than
inversion, and the two surviving Fourier systems have
`S_g=ζ I` or `ζ² I`, respectively. All source relations and entry
domains pass. The code discovers the involution, fixed labels and
conjugation directly from each tensor, and uses exact integer-pair
arithmetic in the Eisenstein integers.
