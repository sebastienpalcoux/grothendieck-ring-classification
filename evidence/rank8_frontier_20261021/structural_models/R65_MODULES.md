# R65: complete rank-two modules and the pointed-coherence branch

We do not assume a braiding or triviality of the pointed associator.
The source tensor has invertibles C4, generated by index1, and a self-dual
noninvertible `rho=5` of FP dimension `d=(11+5 sqrt(5))/2`. Its invertible
orbit is free, and all noninvertibles have this dimension. The source rule
gives `d²=1+11d`.

Consider any connected rank-two based module that could underlie a module
category. The invertible generator acts by a permutation P of two vertices.
If P is the transposition, its commuting self-dual rho matrix is
`[[a,b],[b,a]]`; its Perron eigenvalue a+b is integral, contradicting d.
Thus all four invertibles act trivially, and the four noninvertibles act by
the same symmetric nonnegative integer matrix N. The source relations force
`N²=I+11N`. Its characteristic polynomial is the irreducible polynomial
`x²-11x-1`, so `N=[[a,b],[b,11-a]]`, with `0<=a<=11` and
`b²=a(11-a)+1`. Testing this complete twelve-element diagonal domain gives,
up to exchanging module vertices, exactly

`[[0,1],[1,11]]` and `[[3,5],[5,8]]`.

The exact checker verifies the positive FP dimension vector and all fusion
and duality relations for both complete actions, against the full source
tensor. Internal-end candidate vectors are also recorded. These are
**based modules**, not categorified module categories.

Every actual module category with either based module would trivialize the
pointed H³(C4,C*) class: each simple is fixed by all four invertibles, and
its scalar module associator supplies a two-cochain whose coboundary is the
pointed associator. Consequently a nontrivial pointed class rules out all
rank-two module constructions, but not the original fusion category.

Conversely, if the pointed class vanishes, the subgroup algebra A=sum(C4)
exists. Its right modules have two simples, from the free pointed and free
noninvertible orbits. Free module adjunction gives the first displayed
matrix, so that based module is realized conditionally on this cocycle
vanishing. The dual A-bimodule category then has eight simples: the left
C4 action fixes the two right-module simples and H²(C4,C*)=0, so each has
four one-dimensional projective equivariant structures. The four pointed
ones have dimension one and form the character group C4; the other four
have dimension d. This determines the dual rank and dimension profile but
does not determine its complete fusion tensor or an equivalence with the
original category. Its action on a rank-two module does not faithfully
represent its eight-dimensional based algebra, so a commutant calculation
alone cannot supply the missing tensor.

We have not forced the pointed class to vanish, and have not solved the
module associator for the second matrix. The rank-eight hypothesis also
does not put this ring within the recent classifications of rank-six
quadratic categories. The general unbraided categorification remains open.

Primary categorical input: EGNO, *Tensor Categories*, Chapter7 (module
categories over subgroup algebras and their duals); Burciu--Natale,
[Fusion rules of equivariantizations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.6625),
Corollary2.13 for the orbit and projective-stabilizer description.

## All nontrivial finite-group equivariantizations are impossible

This is a construction-family exclusion, independent of the unresolved
pointed-cocycle question above. Suppose C is a nontrivial finite-group
equivariantization B^G. The canonical Rep(G) embeds into C and lifts to its
center. Its simple dimensions are integers, whereas the four noninvertible
source dimensions are irrational. Thus Rep(G) is pointed, and its character
group embeds into C4. Necessarily G is C2 or C4. In either case Rep(G)
contains a central Rep(C2), whose nontrivial character is source index7.
Equivariantization/de-equivariantization for this central Rep(C2) presents
C as an equivariantization of a fusion category of dimension D/2.

Index7 acts freely on all eight source simples. In the orbit-stabilizer
description of a C2-equivariantization, a free orbit of base simples gives
one induced simple fixed by the nontrivial character. That is impossible
here. Therefore every base simple is fixed, and each has two simple
equivariant lifts (H²(C2,C*)=0). The base has rank four. Its positive FP
dimensions are 1,1,d,d, and its global dimension is

`D/2 = 125+55 sqrt(5) > 125 > 45 > 24+12 sqrt(3)`.

The last strict inequality follows from 21²>12²·3. This contradicts the
repository's unrestricted rank-four theorem, Corollary
`finite:cor:sharp-bounds` in
`manuscripts/rank4/papers/companion_sections/14_finite_certification.tex`.
The verifier pins the exact theorem source by SHA256; it does not purport
to re-prove that theorem. This rules out every nontrivial finite-group
equivariantization, and every central Tannakian condensation route, for
any complex categorification of R65. It does not rule out noncentral
subgroup-algebra module constructions, a nontrivial pointed associator,
or categorifications without this construction structure.
