# Cyclic internal-end algebras and multiplicity


Date: 24 October 2026. Scope: arbitrary fusion categories over C. No
pivotal, spherical, pseudo-unitary, unitary, or braided hypothesis occurs.

## Theorem

Let D be a fusion category, M a finite semisimple indecomposable left
D-module category, and x a simple object of M. Suppose its internal end
algebra has underlying object

    A = [x,x]\_D = direct\_sum\_{g in H} g,

where H is a finite cyclic group of invertible objects, each with
multiplicity one. Then y tensor x is multiplicity-free for every simple
object y of D.

### Proof

For each g in H, the nonzero morphism g tensor x -> x is an isomorphism.
Consequently every component g tensor h -> gh of the multiplication of A
is nonzero: it is induced by composition of these isomorphisms. This is
not an assumption about an arbitrarily chosen algebra structure.

The internal-Hom equivalence of Ostrik, Theorem 1, identifies M with the
category of right A-modules in D, sends x to A, and sends y tensor x to
the free module y tensor A. Therefore their endomorphism algebras agree.
By free-module adjunction,

    End\_A(y tensor A) = Hom\_D(y, y tensor A)
                      = direct\_sum\_{g in K} Hom\_D(y,y tensor g),

as vector spaces, where K = {g in H : y tensor g is isomorphic to y}.
K is a subgroup of H, hence cyclic. Every displayed summand is
one-dimensional. Choose nonzero isomorphisms u\_g:y -> y tensor g.
The associated free-module endomorphism is the composite

    y tensor A --u\_g tensor id--> (y tensor g) tensor A
               --associator--> y tensor (g tensor A)
               --id tensor multiplication--> y tensor A.

Call this endomorphism e\_g. The multiplication of A and the u\_g are
isomorphisms on the relevant simple summands, so composition satisfies
e\_g e\_h = c(g,h) e\_{gh} (or the reversed product according to convention),
with c(g,h) nonzero. In particular each e\_g is invertible. If t generates
K of order n, the powers of e\_t span all these homogeneous summands and
e\_t\^n = c id for c nonzero. Thus

    End\_A(y tensor A) is isomorphic to C[T]/(T\^n-c).

This algebra is commutative. In a semisimple C-linear category the
endomorphism algebra of direct\_sum\_i m\_i z\_i, for distinct simples z\_i,
is the product of Mat\_{m\_i}(C); it is commutative precisely when every
m\_i is at most one. This proves the theorem. The case K={1} is included.

The proof does not invoke H\^2(H,C\*)=0, a fiber functor on D, a
classification of Tambara-Yamagami categories, or a normalization of
their associators. The cyclic hypothesis is essential: for V4 a twisted
group algebra can be Mat\_2(C).

# A nonsolvable dimension-sixty neutral component


Date: 24 October 2026. General-complex scope, with no braiding or
unitarity assumptions on the hypothetical categorification.

## Theorem

Let D be a fusion category with simple FP dimensions 1,1,2,2,5,5.
Assume:

1. D equals its adjoint subcategory (its universal grading is trivial).
2. The nontrivial invertible g fixes the two dimension-two simples and
   exchanges the two dimension-five simples.

Then every faithful finite-group extension of D is group-theoretical,
and in particular is integral.

### Step 1: D cannot be solvable

Suppose D were solvable. By ENO Proposition 4.4 it admits a construction
from Vec by successive extensions and equivariantizations by cyclic
groups of prime order. The last step cannot be an extension, since D
has no nontrivial faithful grading. Thus D = E\^{C\_p} for a fusion
category E, and the canonical central Rep(C\_p) embeds into D under
forgetting its central structure. D has exactly two invertibles, so
p=2, and the nontrivial character has underlying object g.

De-equivariantize by this central Rep(C2), or equivalently take modules
over its regular central algebra A=1+g. This is a fusion category of
dimension 30. Its simple dimensions can be determined without knowing
any associator of D. The free orbit {1,g} gives one invertible; the free
orbit consisting of the two dimension-five objects gives one simple
of dimension five. Each fixed dimension-two object gives two distinct
simple A-modules of dimension one. Indeed, the endomorphism algebra
of its free module has dimension two and is semisimple, hence is C+C;
the two simple summands each have underlying object equal to the
original dimension-two object. FP dimension after passage to A-modules
is the underlying FP dimension divided by FPdim(A)=2. These six objects
exhaust the simples since every simple A-module is a summand of a free
module. Therefore the de-equivariantization has dimensions 1\^5,5.

Its invertibles form the cyclic group C5. Writing z for its unique
noninvertible simple, rigidity and dimensions force

    hz=z=zh,    z\^2 = sum\_{h in C5} h + 4z.

But Etingof–Gelaki–Ostrik, Corollary 7.4, applies to arbitrary complex
fusion categories with n cyclic invertibles and one noninvertible of
dimension n. It forces n+1 to be a prime power. Here n=5 and 6 is not
a prime power. This contradiction proves that D is not solvable.

### Step 2: D is Morita equivalent to Vec\_{A5}\^omega

ENO Theorem 9.16 says that every fusion category of dimension 60 is
weakly group-theoretical. Hence D is categorically Morita equivalent
to a nilpotent fusion category N of the same dimension. Choose a
tower of faithful group extensions constructing N from Vec. The
product of the orders of the grading groups is 60.

If all grading groups are solvable, their normal series refine this
tower into cyclic-prime extensions; N and hence D are solvable,
contrary to Step 1. Thus at least one grading group is nonsolvable.
Every proper divisor of 60 is the order of only solvable finite
groups; the unique nonsolvable group of order 60 is A5. These standard
finite-group facts can also be expressed using the smallest
nonabelian simple group A5, of order 60, and a composition series.
The nonsolvable grading group therefore has order 60, and every other
extension step is trivial. Consequently N is a pointed category
Vec\_{A5}\^omega for some 3-cocycle omega. No value or trivialization of
omega is assumed.

### Step 3: all extensions of Vec\_{A5}\^omega are pointed

Let P=Vec\_{A5}\^omega, and let B be an invertible P-bimodule category.
As a left P-module category it has pointed dual (indeed the right
action identifies its dual with P, up to the harmless opposite
convention). By the classification of pointed-dual module categories,
Naidu–Nikshych Example 2.2, such a module category is described by a
normal abelian subgroup H of A5 and compatible cochain data. Since A5
has no nontrivial normal abelian subgroup, H={1}. Therefore B is the
regular left P-module category and has 60 simple objects, transitively
permuted by the 60 invertibles of P.

Each homogeneous component of a faithful group extension of P is an
invertible P-bimodule category. Consequently each component has these
60 simple objects, all with the same FP dimension d, because the
invertibles of P permute them transitively. A faithful homogeneous
component has total FP dimension FPdim(P)=60. Hence 60\*d\^2=60 and d=1.
Every simple object of the extension is invertible: the extension is
pointed. This argument applies to every omega and every coherent
extension, not merely to untwisted or preselected models.

### Step 4: transport the extension through Morita equivalence

If C is any faithful finite-group extension of D, ENO Lemma 3.4
constructs an extension of P which is Morita equivalent to C. It does
this by replacing D with its category of bimodules over an
indecomposable algebra and applying the same construction to C;
the grading survives because the algebra lies in the neutral
component. Step 3 makes the resulting category pointed. Thus C is
group-theoretical. Group-theoretical fusion categories are integral:
the standard double-coset description of simples gives dimensions

    [H : H intersect tHt\^{-1}] \* dim(projective irreducible representation),

which are integers. This proves the theorem.

## Application to two remaining rank-nine rings

Both tensors have global FP dimension 120. Their integral objects
form a dimension-60 fusion subring D and the other three objects

grading with D as neutral component. In the notation (1,g,a,b,u,v)

# Cyclic generalized Tambara--Yamagami constructions


## Result

The rings R9\_2\_746 and R9\_2\_1209 have explicit unitary categorifications.
They are the two parity-coherence inversion equivariantizations of a
12-simple-object generalized Tambara–Yamagami category with invertible
group C9 and three simple objects of dimension sqrt(3). This category is
constructed below by an explicit central C3 condensation, rather than
inferred from vanishing of a group cohomology obstruction.

All half-braidings in this note use the orientation
`c_z(X): z tensor X -> X tensor z`. This is inverse to the orientation
used for central structures in GNN Section 2.2; keeping this convention
explicit avoids a sign reversal in the bicharacter.

## Starting unitary categories and central object

Let omega=exp(2 pi i/3), zeta=exp(2 pi i/9). Take the unitary category
T=TY(C3,chi,1/sqrt(3)), chi(a,b)=omega\^(ab), with the skeletal associators
of GNN Section 4.1. Its objects are a in C3 and m, with m\^2=sum\_a a.
The central lift z of the invertible a=1 has right half-braiding

    c\_z(b)=omega\^b,   c\_z(m)=omega.

The equations are `c_z(m)^2=chi(1,1)^(-1)` and
`c_(z tensor z')(m)=c_z(m)c_z'(m)chi(a,a')^(-1)`.
Thus z has order3 as a central object, and c\_z(z)=omega.
These formulas follow by substitution in the displayed TY associators;
they are also the inverse-half-braiding form of the central invertibles in
GNN Proposition 4.1. No braiding on T itself is asserted.

Let P=Vec(C9,Omega), with representatives0,...,8 and

    Omega(a,b,c)=zeta\^(3a floor((b+c)/9)).

This is a normalized unitary 3-cocycle. The underlying invertible h=3
has right half-braiding beta(x)=zeta\^(2x). Its central square has
underlying object6 and half-braiding zeta\^(4x). Its central cube has
half-braiding

    zeta\^(6x) Omega(x,6,3)=zeta\^(9x)=1.

In the product category B=T boxtimes P, the central object

    g=(z,3)

has order3. Its self-braiding is omega\*zeta\^6=1. The associator on its
three powers is identically1, since Omega(3a,3b,3c)=1. Their braiding is
also identically1, because the self-braiding of a generator is1 and this
is a pointed category with trivial associator. They therefore give an
explicit central Tannakian Rep(C3). Set A=1+g+g\^2, with the ordinary
group-algebra multiplication (all nonzero multiplication scalars1).
It is a commutative separable central algebra with the usual unitary
normalization. The right A-module category D=B\_A is a unitary fusion
category of dimension (6\*9)/3=18.

The action of g on every simple of B is free: its C9 charge shifts by3.
Hence every free simple A-module is simple, and these are indexed by the
12 orbits. Writing the nine invertibles as u\^a, a in C9, and the three
other simples as X\_r, r in C3, gives

    u\^a u\^b = u\^(a+b),
    u\^a X\_r = X\_r u\^a = X\_(r+a),
    X\_r X\_s = sum\_(h=0,1,2) u\^(r+s-3h).

For example, use orbit representatives (0,a) and (m,r); the relation
(1,3)=1 in the quotient identifies (h,r+s) with (0,r+s-3h).
The dimensions are1\^9,(sqrt3)\^3.

## A coherent involution preserving the algebra

On T use the canonical orthogonal inversion autoequivalence
F(a)=-a,F(m)=m. The tensorator is the canonical identity map, including
the permutation of summands of m\^2. This is an actual involutive tensor
autoequivalence, as in GNN Proposition 4.8.

On P use F(x)=-x with tensorator

    J(a,b)=zeta\^mu(a,b),  mu(a,b)=-3a\*1\_(b>0) modulo 9.

Writing w for the exponent of Omega, direct substitution gives

    mu(a,b)+mu(a+b,c)-mu(b,c)-mu(a,b+c)
      = w(-a,-b,-c)-w(a,b,c) modulo 9,
    mu(a,b)+mu(-a,-b)=0 modulo 9.

Consequently F\^2=Id as a tensor functor with identity square coherence.
On T it sends the central z to z\^2. On P the transported half-braiding
of h=3 at the new argument x is

    beta(-x) J(3,-x)/J(-x,3) = zeta\^(4x),

the half-braiding of the inverse central object h\^2. Thus F(g)=g\^2 as
central objects. On powers of g the tensorator is identically1 because
mu(3a,3b)=0. The permutation g\^i -> g\^(-i) therefore defines an actual
algebra isomorphism F(A)->A whose square is identity, respecting both
multiplication and the central structure. Hence F descends to a coherent
unitary C2 action on D. It acts by

    u\^a -> u\^(-a),    X\_r -> X\_(-r).

There is a second coherent action with the same tensor functor: multiply
the square coherence by the parity natural automorphism, +1 on pointed
simples and -1 on X\_r. Parity is monoidal, invariant under F, and trivial
on A; hence it obeys the C2 action coherence before and after descent.
Both actions consist entirely of unitary structure maps.

## Uniform family

The same construction works with3 replaced by any odd n>1. Put
zeta=exp(2 pi i/n\^2), chi(a,b)=zeta\^(n ab) on Cn, choose
`c_z(m)=chi(1,1)^((n-1)/2)` (whose square is chi(1,1)\^(-1)), and use

    Omega(a,b,c)=zeta\^(n a floor((b+c)/n\^2)),
    h=n, beta(x)=zeta\^((n-1)x),
    J(a,b)=zeta\^(-n a 1\_(b>0)).

`beta(x)^n zeta^(n x)=1`. The diagonal generator's self-braiding is1.

# An order-four cover and a coherent descended action


## Result and outline

The ring R9\_2\_1254 is realized by a coherent C2 equivariantization of a
unitary generalized Moore–Read category of dimension 16. The new action
comes from a mixed order-four tensor functor on the dimension 32 covering
category. Its square becomes tensor-isomorphic to the identity only after
central boson condensation. Requiring the cover functor to have square
identity would incorrectly discard this construction.

The certificate is `mr_order4_descent_2_3_1_4.json`: it gives every
root-of-unity tensorator scalar and every component of the square
isomorphism. `mr_order4_descent.py` checks all required equations. The
categorical argument below explains why those equations suffice.

## The existing unitary cover and its central algebra

Use a unitary Ising category I with simples 1,psi,sigma, and P=Vec(C16,Omega),

    Omega(a,b,c)=zeta\^(2a floor((b+c)/16)), zeta=exp(2 pi i/16).

Let B be the parity subcategory of I boxtimes P: its simples are(i,a),
with i=0,1,2 for1,psi,sigma, and a even for i=0,1 and odd for i=2.
There are24 simples and FPdim(B)=32. The invertible

    g=(psi,8)

has right half-braiding g tensor(i,a)->(i,a)tensor g of scalar

    beta(i,a)=zeta\^(7a+e\_i),  (e\_0,e\_1,e\_2)=(0,8,12).

It has order2 in Z(B), self-braiding1, and trivial associator on its powers.
Indeed the pointed square correction is zeta\^(2a), giving
beta\_pointed(a)\^2\*zeta\^(2a)=1; the Ising central fermion squares to1.
The regular algebra A=1+g is commutative, separable and central. Its
multiplication g tensor g->1 is the identity in the chosen skeletal basis.
Thus D=B\_A is a unitary fusion category of dimension 16.

These are precisely the explicitly verified cover and central algebra in

## The order-four mixed tensor functor

On simple objects define

    F(i,a)=(i xor (a/2 mod 2),3a mod 16),   i=0,1;
    F(2,a)=(2,3a mod 16).

This preserves every fusion coefficient and the unit. The file lists the
scalar J\_F(a,b;c) in mu\_16 on every multiplicity-one fusion channel,
with tensorator orientation F(a)tensor F(b)->F(a tensor b).
F is linear on each chosen elementary morphism and permutes channel
labels in the obvious way. Its tensor-functor equations are verified
entry by entry against the cover's actual F-symbols, not only the fusion
rules. All nonzero Ising F-symbol magnitudes are preserved: the only
nontrivial magnitude1/sqrt2 occurs for three sigma inputs, and F fixes
the Ising type of those inputs. Consequently checking the phase of every
nonzero entry checks the entire tensor-functor diagram.

The actual F-symbol exponent is the standard Ising sign times8, plus
`2 a floor((b+c)/16)` from the charge factor. The script checks all 18432
nonzero associator entries and the unit constraints. Since J is unitary
and the object map is a permutation, F is a unitary tensor autoequivalence.

F(g)=g. The certificate additionally verifies every central transport
identity

    J\_F(g,a;ga)/J\_F(a,g;ga)=beta(Fa)/beta(a).

## Squaring before and after condensation

On B, F\^2 is not the identity object map. It is

    K(a)=g\^p(a) tensor a,

where p(a)=0 for an even Ising object and1 for a sigma object. Equip K
with its canonical tensorator: move the central g factors to the left
using the right half-braiding, and multiply g\^2=1. More explicitly,
reassociate

    (g\^p a)(g\^q b) -> g\^p(a(g\^q b))
      -> g\^p((a g\^q)b) -> g\^p((g\^q a)b)
      -> g\^p(g\^q(ab)) -> (g\^p g\^q)(ab) -> g\^(p+q)(ab).

The middle interchange contributes beta(a)\^(-q). All other contributions
are specified cover associators. The script independently verifies the
resulting J\_K against all 18432 tensor-functor equations.

The certificate supplies a natural isomorphism d:F\^2->K on the common
underlying simple objects. Its phases satisfy for every channel a b->c

    d(a)d(b)/d(c)
      =J\_F(a,b;c) J\_F(Fa,Fb;Fc)/J\_K(a,b;c).

This is exactly monoidality of d. It also satisfies

    d(g)=J\_F(g,g;1)=c\^2.

Thus d intertwines the algebra map phi F(phi):F\^2(A)->A with the
identity algebra map K(A)->A. It descends to the module categories.

On D, K is canonically tensor-isomorphic to the identity. On a free
module q(a), use q(g)\^p tensor q(a)->q(a), where q(g)->1\_D is supplied by
the regular algebra multiplication. The tensorator of K was defined
by precisely the central movements appearing in this map. Its
monoidality uses beta(g)=1 and the group-algebra relation g\^2=1. Hence
f\^2 is tensor-isomorphic to Id\_D.

## Coherent C2 action, with no unresolved obstruction

For completeness there are two ways to finish coherence.

First, the canonical universal grading of D is C8: writing its simples
as u\^a(a in C8) and X\_r(r in C4), their degrees are2a and2r+1 modulo 8.
Its adjoint subcategory has basis1,u\^4, as follows from
X\_r X\_r\^\*=1+u\^4. Therefore Aut\_tensor(Id\_D)=Hom(C8,C\*)=C8. The functor f
acts on this group by multiplication3. The obstruction to upgrading an
order-two tensor-equivalence class to a C2 action lies in

    H\^3(C2,C8\_3)=ker(1+3)/(3-1)C8=2C8/2C8=0.

Thus the action coherence can always be corrected. This is the standard
monoidal-action obstruction (a direct cyclic coherence calculation), not
an assertion that H4 alone supplies an extension.

Second, for the displayed certificate the correction is explicit. If

# Fermion parity and solvable descent in dimension 120


## A fermion-parity criterion

Proposition. Let C be an integral fusion category with commutative
Grothendieck ring, trivial universal grading, and dimension N divisible by
four. Write the induction of the unit into Z(C) as a sum of distinct simple
objects. Suppose exactly one nonunit summand has odd dimension, and that its
dimension is a positive power of an odd prime. Then Z(C) contains a
nontrivial Tannakian subcategory.

Proof. Integrality gives a canonical positive spherical structure and
makes Z(C) an integral nondegenerate braided category. The odd-prime-power
summand of I(1) and ENO Corollary 7.2 produce a nontrivial symmetric fusion
subcategory. If that symmetric category has dimension larger than two,
Deligne's theorem gives a nontrivial Tannakian subcategory. A symmetric
category of dimension two is either Tannakian or sVec. It remains to handle
sVec, generated by a fermion chi in Z(C).

Here is a short proof of the required dimension count. By Ostrik
Proposition 2.8, for the unnormalized modular S-matrix,

    S [I(1)] = N [I(1)].

The fermion chi has twist -1, whereas every summand of A=I(1) has twist +1
(Ostrik Theorem 2.5); hence chi is absent from A. The chi entry of this
identity is zero. For each simple Z in A, monodromy with the order-two
invertible chi is a scalar epsilon\_Z in {+1,-1}, and
`S_{chi,Z}=epsilon_Z FPdim(Z)`. Thus the total dimension of the
constituents of A centralizing chi equals that of the other constituents,
namely N/2. This uses no braiding on C.

For completeness the same dimension count has the following independent
relative-center derivation. Let F:Z(C)->C be the forgetful functor and
g=F(chi). Because the universal grading of C is trivial, the forgetful homomorphism on invertible
objects is injective: its kernel is the group of tensor automorphisms of
Id\_C, namely the characters of the universal grading group. Thus g is a
nontrivial order-two invertible, and D=<g> has dimension two.

Use the relative-center construction from the proof of ENO Lemma 9.17.
The forgetful functor Z(C)->Z\_D(C), which remembers the half-braiding only
against D, is dominant. Its right adjoint on the unit gives a connected
algebra B contained in A, of dimension N/FPdim(D)=N/2. This follows either
from that lemma's dimension calculation or from the dominant-functor
index formula and FPdim(Z\_D(C))=FPdim(C) FPdim(D).

We claim that B is precisely the sum of those constituents of A that
centralize chi. Here is the explicit morphism check, so this identification
does not require a fixed-algebra theorem or a choice of associator.
For a central simple Z occurring in A, take a nonzero map f:F(Z)->1.
It is a morphism to the unit in the relative center precisely when

    (id\_g tensor f) c\_{Z,g} = f tensor id\_g.

The naturality of chi's half-braiding gives

    (f tensor id\_g) c\_{g,Z} = id\_g tensor f.

Consequently the relative-center condition is equivalent to

    (f tensor id\_g) (c\_{g,Z} c\_{Z,g}) = f tensor id\_g.

In Z(C), Z tensor chi is simple, so this monodromy is scalar. Since chi
has order two, its monodromy eigenvalue is +1 or -1. The displayed condition
therefore holds exactly when Z centralizes chi. This proves the claim,
including multiplicities (which are one because the based ring is
commutative).

The dimension of B is N/2, which is even. B contains the unit, and every
nonunit summand of A except the specified odd-prime-power object has even
dimension. Parity therefore forces that odd object to occur in B. It lies
in the centralizer E=<chi>' inside Z(C). By nondegeneracy of Z(C), the
Muger center of E is exactly <chi>=sVec, so E is slightly degenerate.
ENO Proposition 7.4 says that a slightly degenerate integral braided
category containing a simple object of odd-prime-power dimension has a
nontrivial Tannakian subcategory. This proves the proposition. QED.

The argument explicitly treats a possible central fermion. It does not
infer a Tannakian category merely from an unspecified symmetric category.

## An auxiliary dimension-30 obstruction

Lemma. An integral fusion category of dimension 30 has no simple
object of dimension three.

Proof. ENO Theorem 9.2 makes every integral fusion category of
square-free dimension 30 group-theoretical. Every group G of order 30
has a normal cyclic subgroup P of order 15. One elementary proof is:
if its Sylow-five subgroup were not normal, there would be 24 nonidentity
order-five elements. Counting forces the Sylow-three subgroup to be
normal. The inverse image of the normal Sylow-five subgroup in the
order-ten quotient is then a normal subgroup of order 15, necessarily
cyclic, making its Sylow-five subgroup normal after all. Thus Sylow five
is normal. In the order-six quotient, the Sylow-three subgroup is normal;
its inverse image is the desired cyclic subgroup of order 15.

In a group-theoretical presentation C(G,omega,H,psi), simples over HgH are
indexed by projective irreducible representations of K=H intersect gHg\^{-1},
with dimension `[H:K] dim(pi)`. Every such K has square-free order, so
H\^2(K,C\*)=0: restriction to a Sylow subgroup is injective on the
corresponding primary part by restriction/corestriction, while every
Sylow subgroup is cyclic and has trivial complex Schur multiplier.
Thus all projective degrees coincide with ordinary character degrees.

Set Q=H intersect P. It is cyclic and, being a subgroup of the cyclic
normal subgroup P, is normal in G. We have [H:Q]<=2, and Q lies in every
intersection K. Hence [H:K]<=2. If [H:K]=2, K=Q is cyclic, so every
projective irreducible of K has dimension one. If [H:K]=1, K=H has a
cyclic normal subgroup of index at most two. Restriction of an ordinary
irreducible representation to that cyclic subgroup is a sum of characters.
Clifford theory gives irreducible dimensions at most two: an orbit of size
two induces a two-dimensional representation, while an invariant character
extends across the cyclic quotient and gives one-dimensional representations.
Thus all resulting simple dimensions are one or two.
In particular no dimension-three simple occurs. QED.

This argument allows arbitrary three-cocycles and subgroup cochains; an
untwisted character-table search alone would not suffice. The elementary
group-of-order-30 fact can equivalently be checked on the four groups of
order 30. It is also a consequence of the classification of groups of
square-free order at this particular order.

## No solvable category has dimension type (1\^6,3\^6)

Theorem. A fusion category D with six invertibles and six simple
objects of dimension three is not solvable.

Proof. Its dimension is 60. For each dimension-three simple X,
G[X] has order dividing both |G(D)|=6 and FPdim(X)\^2=9. In the decomposition
of X tensor X\*, all noninvertible summands have dimensions divisible by
three, so |G[X]| is divisible by three. Hence |G[X]|=3. The group of
six invertibles has a unique subgroup H of order three, so this same H
fixes all six noninvertible objects. It lies in D\_ad. The remainder of
X tensor X\* has dimension six, so D\_ad is nonpointed.

Consider a nontrivial faithful grading with group order u. Each component
has dimension 60/u. The neutral component contains H and at least one
three-dimensional object. A component with a invertibles and b
three-dimensional simples has dimension a+9b. There are six invertibles
and six three-dimensional simples in all. These constraints leave only
u=2, with both components of type `(1^3,3^3)`. This elementary enumeration
is replayed exhaustively by the verifier. The neutral category would
have dimension 30 and a three-dimensional simple, contrary to Section 3.
Thus U(D)=1.

If D were solvable, the final step of a cyclic-prime extension/
equivariantization construction could not be an extension. It must be
an equivariantization by C\_p. Its central Rep(C\_p) embeds into D and
supplies p invertibles, so p=2 or p=3.

For p=2, its nontrivial character acts freely on the six dimension-three
objects: their stabilizers under G(D) have order three. It also acts
freely on all six invertibles. De-equivariantization has type `(1^3,3^3)`
and dimension 30, again impossible.

For p=3, its character subgroup is H and fixes all six noninvertible
objects. The six invertibles form two free orbits. De-equivariantization
has dimension 20 and is pointed: each fixed three-dimensional simple
splits into three invertibles, and each free orbit of invertibles gives
one invertible. To justify the splitting, the endomorphism algebra of
the free module of a fixed simple is a twisted group algebra of C3;
H\^2(C3,C\*)=0, so it has three one-dimensional simple modules. Each has
underlying object X and normalized dimension 3/3=1.

Write this pointed category as Vec\_K\^omega, |K|=20. The equivariantizing
C3 acts on K by group automorphisms. Its invariant invertibles each
contribute exactly three invertibles in the equivariantization, again
because H\^2(C3,C\*)=0. Six invertibles of D would require exactly two
fixed elements of K. But K has a unique, hence characteristic, Sylow-five
subgroup, and the C3 action is trivial there because Aut(C5)=C4.
There are at least five fixed elements, a contradiction. QED.

This theorem does not exclude categories of that dimension type in
general: the group-theoretical category C(A5,1,C3,1) supplies that type.
The conclusion is nonsolvability, which is the needed restriction.

## Nonsolvable weakly group-theoretical categories of dimension 120

Lemma. Every nonsolvable weakly group-theoretical fusion category of
dimension 120 is group-theoretical.

Proof. By definition it is Morita equivalent to a nilpotent category N,
built from Vec by successive faithful extensions by finite groups G\_i.
The product of the orders |G\_i| is 120. If every G\_i is solvable, refine
each extension by a composition series into cyclic-prime extensions.
Then N, and hence the original category, is solvable, a contradiction.

Consequently one grading group is nonsolvable. A nonsolvable finite group
whose order divides 120 has a nonabelian composition factor A5: the only
possible proper nonsolvable order is 60, and its group is A5. If a grading
group has order 120, every other stage is trivial and N is pointed.
Otherwise an A5 extension uses a factor 60, leaving only a single factor
two. Up to deleting trivial stages, there are two possible orders.

First suppose Vec is extended by A5 and then by C2. The intermediate
category is P=Vec\_{A5}\^omega. Every invertible P-bimodule category, as a
left P-module category, has pointed dual. The classification of such
module categories (Naidu--Nikshych, Example 2.2) requires a normal abelian
subgroup H of A5. Its only possibility is H=1, so the left module is the
regular P-module, with 60 simples permuted transitively by A5. Each
component of any extension of P is such a bimodule, and has FP dimension
60. Its 60 equally dimensional simples therefore all have dimension one.
The extension N is pointed, for every omega and all coherent extension
data.

Second suppose Vec is extended by C2 and then by A5. The intermediate
category is P=Vec\_{C2}\^omega. Its center is pointed of rank four: its
simples are labeled by the two elements of C2 and projective irreducible
